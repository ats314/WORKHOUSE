### 1. Solution for the Haar Mass Term (Correction to Document A / Section 2)

The errors stemmed from an inconsistent parametrization and subsequent sign confusion. We correct this by rigorously applying the conventions defined in the documents.

#### Corrected Derivation

**1. Conventions and Parametrization:**
We adhere to the following definitions:
*   Lie Algebra $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{su}(N)$: Skew-Hermitian matrices ($X^\dagger = -X$).
*   Inner Product: $\langle X, Y \rangle = -\Tr(XY)$. The norm is $\|X\|^2 = -\Tr(X^2) > 0$.
*   Parametrization: For $U_b \in SU(N)$ (Unitary), the parametrization near the identity must be $U_b = \exp(X_b)$, where $X_b \in \mathfrak{su}(N)$. In the lattice context, we set $X_b = ag A_b$, where $A_b \in \mathfrak{su}(N)$.

**2. Haar Action Expansion:**
The Haar action is $S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(X) = -\log J(X)$, where $J(X)$ is the Jacobian of the exponential map:
$$
J(X) = \det_{\mathfrak{g}}\left( \frac{\sinh(\mathrm{ad}_X/2)}{\mathrm{ad}_X/2} \right).
$$
The action is:
$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(X) = -\Tr_{\mathfrak{g}} \log\left( \frac{\sinh(\mathrm{ad}_X/2)}{\mathrm{ad}_X/2} \right).
$$
Using the expansion $\log(\frac{\sinh z}{z}) = \frac{z^2}{6} + O(z^4)$:
$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(X) = -\Tr_{\mathfrak{g}}\left( \frac{(\mathrm{ad}_X/2)^2}{6} + O(X^4) \right) = -\frac{1}{24} \Tr_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{ad}_X^2).
$$

**3. Sign Resolution and the Constant $c_0$:**
We use the identity for the Killing form of $\mathfrak{su}(N)$: $\Tr_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{ad}_X^2) = 2N \Tr(X^2)$.
$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(X) = -\frac{1}{24} (2N \Tr(X^2)) = -\frac{N}{12} \Tr(X^2).
$$
Substituting the norm definition $\|X\|^2 = -\Tr(X^2)$:
$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(X) = \frac{N}{12} \|X\|^2.
$$
This rigorously confirms that the Haar measure provides a **positive** mass term (convexity).

Substituting the lattice variables $X_b = ag A_b$ and summing over links:
$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A) = \frac{N a^2 g^2}{12} \sum_b \|A_b\|^2.
$$
The Hessian of this quadratic form is $\mathrm{Hess}(S_{\mathrm{Haar}}) = 2 \times \frac{N a^2 g^2}{12} I = \frac{N a^2 g^2}{6} I$.

In Document I, the Hessian lower bound is defined as $\rho_*(a) I = (c_0 a^2 g^2 - \beta C_V(N)) I$. Matching the positive contribution from the Haar Hessian, the mathematically consistent value for $c_0$ based on the defined inner product is:
$$
c_0 = \frac{N}{6}.
$$

### 2. Solution for the Wilson Hessian Bound (Correction to Document B / Section 3)

The proof in the original documents was incomplete because it ignored the contribution of mixed derivatives (off-diagonal blocks) to the operator norm of the Hessian.

#### Corrected Derivation

We aim to bound the Hessian quadratic form $H(X) = \langle X, \mathrm{Hess} S_p(U) X \rangle$ for a single plaquette $p$ at an arbitrary configuration $U=(V_1, \dots, V_4)$, acting on a tangent vector $X=(X_1, \dots, X_4) \in \mathfrak{g}^4$. We use left-trivialized variations $V_i(t) = e^{tX_i}V_i$ to compute the Riemannian Hessian.

**1. The Full Plaquette Hessian:**
The action is $S_p = 1 - \frac{1}{N}\Re \Tr(U_p(t))$. The Hessian is $H(X) = S_p''(0) = -\frac{1}{N}\Re \Tr(U_p''(0))$.

The second derivative $U_p''(0)$ is obtained via the product rule:
$$
U_p''(0) = \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^4 V_1\dots(X_i^2 V_i)\dots V_4}_{\text{D (Diagonal)}} + \underbrace{\sum_{i\neq j} V_1\dots(X_i V_i)\dots(X_j V_j)\dots V_4}_{\text{M (Mixed)}}.
$$
The Hessian splits into $H(X) = H_D(X) + H_M(X)$.

**2. Bounding the Diagonal Terms ($H_D$):**
$$
H_D(X) = -\frac{1}{N}\Re \Tr(D) = \sum_i -\frac{1}{N}\Re\Tr(V_1\dots X_i^2 V_i \dots V_4).
$$
As correctly shown in Document B, using the cyclic property of the trace and von Neumann's trace inequality (since $-X_i^2$ is positive semi-definite):
$$
|H_D(X)| \le \frac{1}{N} \sum_i \|X_i\|^2.
$$

**3. Bounding the Mixed Terms ($H_M$):**
$$
H_M(X) = -\frac{1}{N}\Re \Tr(M) = \sum_{i\neq j} -\frac{1}{N}\Re\Tr(V_1\dots X_i V_i \dots X_j V_j \dots V_4).
$$
We must bound terms of the form $|\Tr(A X_i B X_j C)|$, where $A, B, C$ are unitary. We use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the trace (Frobenius) norm: $|\Tr(P^\dagger Q)| \le \|P\|_F \|Q\|_F$. (The Frobenius norm $\|X\|_F$ coincides with our norm $\|X\|$).
By the unitary invariance of the norm:
$$
|\Tr(A X_i B X_j C)| \le \|A X_i B\|_F \|X_j C\|_F = \|X_i\| \|X_j\|.
$$
The total bound for the mixed part is:
$$
|H_M(X)| \le \sum_{i\neq j} \frac{1}{N} \|X_i\| \|X_j\|.
$$

**4. Total Plaquette Bound:**
Combining the bounds:
\begin{align*}
|H(X)| &\le |H_D(X)| + |H_M(X)| \\
&\le \frac{1}{N} \left( \sum_i \|X_i\|^2 + \sum_{i\neq j} \|X_i\| \|X_j\| \right) \\
&= \frac{1}{N} \left( \sum_{i=1}^4 \|X_i\| \right)^2.
\end{align*}
We use the inequality between L1 and L2 norms in 4 dimensions: $(\sum_{i=1}^4 a_i)^2 \le 4 \sum_{i=1}^4 a_i^2$.
$$
|H(X)| \le \frac{4}{N} \sum_i \|X_i\|^2 = \frac{4}{N} \|X\|^2.
$$
The rigorous single-plaquette Hessian bound is $4/N$.

**5. Global Bound $C_V(N)$:**
In $d=4$ dimensions, each link is shared by 6 plaquettes. Summing the bound over all plaquettes:
$$
\big|\langle A, \mathrm{Hess} S_W(U) A\rangle\big| \le \sum_p \frac{4}{N} \|A^{(p)}\|^2 = \frac{4}{N} \cdot 6 \|A\|^2 = \frac{24}{N} \|A\|^2.
$$
The corrected rigorous global Wilson Hessian bound is $C_V(N) = 24/N$.
