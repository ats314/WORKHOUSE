# Title

Global Hessian Bound for the Wilson Plaquette Action in Four Dimensions

# Abstract

We consider the Wilson action for \(SU(N)\) lattice gauge theory on a four‑dimensional hypercubic lattice. For a configuration \(U = (U_b)_{b \in B} \in SU(N)^{|B|}\), the action is
\[
S_W(U) = \sum_p S_p(U_p), \qquad S_p(U_p) = 1 - \frac{1}{N}\Re\operatorname{Tr}(U_p),
\]
where \(U_p\) is the ordered product of link variables around plaquette \(p\). We compute the full Hessian of \(S_W\) with respect to variations of the link variables, including all mixed second derivatives between links in a plaquette. Using the Frobenius norm on \(\mathfrak{su}(N)\) and standard inequalities for traces of products with unitary matrices, we derive a sharp plaquette‑level bound
\[
\bigl|\operatorname{Hess} S_p(A^{(p)},A^{(p)})\bigr| \le \frac{4}{N}\,\|A^{(p)}\|^2,
\]
where \(A^{(p)}\) is the restriction of a tangent vector \(A\) to the four links of \(p\). Summing over plaquettes and using that in \(d=4\) each link belongs to \(2(d-1)=6\) plaquettes, we obtain the global bound
\[
\bigl|\langle A, \operatorname{Hess} S_W(U)\,A\rangle\bigr| \le \frac{24}{N}\,\|A\|^2
\]
for all configurations \(U\) and all tangent vectors \(A\). This corrects the earlier omission of mixed terms and the associated combinatorial factor, and establishes the constant \(C_V(N)=24/N\) in four dimensions.

# Verified Derivation

## 1. Setup: Wilson Action, Plaquettes, and Metric

Let \(B\) denote the set of oriented links (bonds) of a four‑dimensional hypercubic lattice, and let
\[
U = (U_b)_{b\in B} \in SU(N)^{|B|}
\]
be a gauge field configuration. For each plaquette \(p\), let \(\partial p = (l_1,l_2,l_3,l_4)\) be an ordered list of its four oriented links, and define the plaquette variable
\[
U_p = U_{l_1} U_{l_2} U_{l_3}^\dagger U_{l_4}^\dagger \in SU(N),
\]
with the precise pattern of daggers determined by the relative orientations.

The Wilson action is
\[
S_W(U) = \sum_p S_p(U_p), \qquad
S_p(U_p) = 1 - \frac{1}{N}\Re\operatorname{Tr}(U_p).
\]

At each link \(b\), the tangent space is identified with the Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{su}(N)\). We equip \(\mathfrak{su}(N)\) with the inner product
\[
\langle X, Y \rangle = -\operatorname{Tr}(XY), \qquad X,Y \in \mathfrak{su}(N),
\]
so that the induced norm is
\[
\|X\|^2 = -\operatorname{Tr}(X^2).
\]
For \(X \in \mathfrak{su}(N)\), we have \(X^\dagger = -X\), hence \(X^2\) is Hermitian and negative semi‑definite, and \(\|X\|^2 = \operatorname{Tr}(X^\dagger X)\) is the Frobenius norm squared.

For a global tangent vector \(A = (A_b)_{b\in B}\) with \(A_b \in \mathfrak{su}(N)\), we define
\[
\|A\|^2 = \sum_{b\in B} \|A_b\|^2.
\]
For a given plaquette \(p\) with boundary links \(\partial p = (l_1,\dots,l_4)\), we write
\[
A^{(p)} = (X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4), \qquad X_i := A_{l_i},
\]
and
\[
\|A^{(p)}\|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^4 \|X_i\|^2.
\]

## 2. Plaquette‑Level Hessian

### 2.1. Variation of the Links

Fix a plaquette \(p\). We consider a one‑parameter variation of the four links in \(\partial p\) along a tangent vector \(A^{(p)} = (X_1,\dots,X_4)\), using left‑invariant vector fields:
\[
U_k(\varepsilon) = e^{\varepsilon X_k} U_k, \qquad k=1,\dots,4.
\]
All other links are held fixed. The corresponding plaquette variable is
\[
U_p(\varepsilon) = U_{l_1}(\varepsilon)\, U_{l_2}(\varepsilon)\, U_{l_3}(\varepsilon)^\dagger\, U_{l_4}(\varepsilon)^\dagger.
\]

The Hessian quadratic form of the plaquette action in the direction \(A^{(p)}\) is
\[
H_p(A^{(p)}) := \left.\frac{d^2}{d\varepsilon^2} S_p\bigl(U_p(\varepsilon)\bigr)\right|_{\varepsilon=0}.
\]
Since
\[
S_p(U_p(\varepsilon)) = 1 - \frac{1}{N}\Re\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(U_p(\varepsilon)\bigr),
\]
we have
\[
H_p(A^{(p)}) = -\frac{1}{N}\Re\left.\frac{d^2}{d\varepsilon^2}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(U_p(\varepsilon)\bigr)\right|_{\varepsilon=0}.
\]

### 2.2. Second‑Order Expansion and Structure of Terms

Expand each varied link to second order:
\[
U_k(\varepsilon) = \bigl(I + \varepsilon X_k + \tfrac{\varepsilon^2}{2}X_k^2 + O(\varepsilon^3)\bigr) U_k.
\]
Similarly, for a daggered link,
\[
U_k(\varepsilon)^\dagger = U_k^\dagger \bigl(I - \varepsilon X_k + \tfrac{\varepsilon^2}{2}X_k^2 + O(\varepsilon^3)\bigr),
\]
since \((e^{\varepsilon X_k} U_k)^\dagger = U_k^\dagger e^{-\varepsilon X_k}\).

Multiplying these factors in the fixed plaquette order and collecting terms up to order \(\varepsilon^2\), the second derivative \(\frac{d^2}{d\varepsilon^2}U_p(\varepsilon)\big|_{\varepsilon=0}\) consists of:

1. **Diagonal terms** where the second derivative acts twice on the same link, producing contributions of the form
   \[
   X_i^2 V_i,
   \]
   where \(V_i\) is a product of the original link matrices (and possibly their daggers) independent of \(\varepsilon\). Each such \(V_i\) is unitary.

2. **Cross‑terms** where the first derivative acts once on link \(i\) and once on link \(j\) with \(i\neq j\). These yield contributions of the form
   \[
   X_i V_{ij} X_j W_{ij},
   \]
   where \(V_{ij}, W_{ij}\) are again products of original link matrices and hence unitary.

Thus, schematically,
\[
\left.\frac{d^2}{d\varepsilon^2}U_p(\varepsilon)\right|_{\varepsilon=0}
= \sum_{i=1}^4 X_i^2 V_i \;+\; \sum_{i\neq j} X_i V_{ij} X_j W_{ij},
\]
with all \(V_i, V_{ij}, W_{ij}\) unitary.

Taking the trace and real part, we obtain
\[
H_p(A^{(p)}) = -\frac{1}{N}\Re\operatorname{Tr}\left(
\sum_{i=1}^4 X_i^2 V_i + \sum_{i\neq j} X_i V_{ij} X_j W_{ij}
\right).
\]

The precise forms of \(V_i, V_{ij}, W_{ij}\) are irrelevant for the bounds; only their unitarity matters.

### 2.3. Norm Inequalities for the Diagonal Terms

For each diagonal term, we need to bound
\[
\left|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(X_i^2 V_i)\right|.
\]
Since \(|\Re z| \le |z|\),
\[
\left|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(X_i^2 V_i)\right| \le \left|\operatorname{Tr}(X_i^2 V_i)\right|.
\]

Let \(X \in \mathfrak{su}(N)\) and \(V\) unitary. Because \(X^\dagger = -X\), we have \(X^2\) Hermitian and negative semi‑definite. Write \(X^2 = -H\) with \(H\) Hermitian positive semi‑definite. Then
\[
\|X\|^2 = -\operatorname{Tr}(X^2) = \operatorname{Tr}(H).
\]
Diagonalize \(H = U D U^\dagger\) with \(D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_N)\), \(d_k \ge 0\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(X^2 V) = -\operatorname{Tr}(H V)
= -\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(D (U^\dagger V U)\bigr).
\]
Let \(W = U^\dagger V U\), which is unitary. Then
\[
\left|\operatorname{Tr}(X^2 V)\right|
= \left|\sum_{k=1}^N d_k W_{kk}\right|
\le \sum_{k=1}^N d_k |W_{kk}|
\le \sum_{k=1}^N d_k
= \operatorname{Tr}(H)
= \|X\|^2,
\]
since \(|W_{kk}|\le 1\) for a unitary matrix.

Thus, for each \(i\),
\[
\left|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(X_i^2 V_i)\right|
\le \|X_i\|^2.
\]

### 2.4. Norm Inequalities for the Cross‑Terms

For each cross‑term, we need to bound
\[
\left|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(X_i V_{ij} X_j W_{ij})\right|
\le \left|\operatorname{Tr}(X_i V_{ij} X_j W_{ij})\right|.
\]

We use the Frobenius inner product \(\langle A,B\rangle_F = \operatorname{Tr}(A^\dagger B)\) and its Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:
\[
|\operatorname{Tr}(AB)| \le \|A\|_F \|B\|_F.
\]
Set \(A = X_i V_{ij}\), \(B = X_j W_{ij}\). Then
\[
\left|\operatorname{Tr}(X_i V_{ij} X_j W_{ij})\right|
\le \|X_i V_{ij}\|_F \,\|X_j W_{ij}\|_F.
\]
Unitary matrices preserve the Frobenius norm, so
\[
\|X_i V_{ij}\|_F = \|X_i\|_F, \qquad \|X_j W_{ij}\|_F = \|X_j\|_F.
\]
With our choice of metric, \(\|X\|_F = \|X\|\). Hence
\[
\left|\operatorname{Tr}(X_i V_{ij} X_j W_{ij})\right|
\le \|X_i\|\,\|X_j\|.
\]
Therefore,
\[
\left|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(X_i V_{ij} X_j W_{ij})\right|
\le \|X_i\|\,\|X_j\|.
\]

### 2.5. Bounding the Plaquette Hessian

Combining the diagonal and cross‑term bounds, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
|H_p(A^{(p)})|
&= \left|\,-\frac{1}{N}\Re\operatorname{Tr}\left(
\sum_{i=1}^4 X_i^2 V_i + \sum_{i\neq j} X_i V_{ij} X_j W_{ij}
\right)\right| \\
&\le \frac{1}{N}\left(
\sum_{i=1}^4 \left|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(X_i^2 V_i)\right|
+ \sum_{i\neq j} \left|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(X_i V_{ij} X_j W_{ij})\right|
\right) \\
&\le \frac{1}{N}\left(
\sum_{i=1}^4 \|X_i\|^2
+ \sum_{i\neq j} \|X_i\|\,\|X_j\|
\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Note that
\[
\sum_{i\neq j} \|X_i\|\,\|X_j\|
= 2\sum_{1\le i<j\le 4} \|X_i\|\,\|X_j\|.
\]
Thus,
\[
|H_p(A^{(p)})|
\le \frac{1}{N}\left(
\sum_{i=1}^4 \|X_i\|^2
+ 2\sum_{1\le i<j\le 4} \|X_i\|\,\|X_j\|
\right).
\]

The expression in parentheses is exactly the square of the sum:
\[
\left(\sum_{i=1}^4 \|X_i\|\right)^2
= \sum_{i=1}^4 \|X_i\|^2
+ 2\sum_{1\le i<j\le 4} \|X_i\|\,\|X_j\|.
\]
Therefore,
\[
|H_p(A^{(p)})|
\le \frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^4 \|X_i\|\right)^2.
\]

We now relate this to \(\|A^{(p)}\|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^4 \|X_i\|^2\). By Cauchy–Schwarz in \(\mathbb{R}^4\), for the vectors
\[
v = (\|X_1\|,\|X_2\|,\|X_3\|,\|X_4\|), \quad w = (1,1,1,1),
\]
we have
\[
\left(\sum_{i=1}^4 \|X_i\|\right)^2
= (v\cdot w)^2
\le \|v\|_2^2 \,\|w\|_2^2
= \left(\sum_{i=1}^4 \|X_i\|^2\right)\cdot 4
= 4\,\|A^{(p)}\|^2.
\]

Hence,
\[
|H_p(A^{(p)})|
\le \frac{4}{N}\,\|A^{(p)}\|^2.
\]

This yields the desired plaquette‑level Hessian bound:
\[
\boxed{
\bigl|\operatorname{Hess} S_p(A^{(p)},A^{(p)})\bigr|
\le \frac{4}{N}\,\|A^{(p)}\|^2.
}
\]

## 3. Global Hessian Bound in Four Dimensions

Let \(A = (A_b)_{b\in B}\) be an arbitrary global tangent vector. The Hessian of the full Wilson action is the sum of plaquette Hessians:
\[
\langle A, \operatorname{Hess} S_W(U)\,A\rangle
= \sum_p \operatorname{Hess} S_p\bigl(A^{(p)},A^{(p)}\bigr).
\]
Taking absolute values and applying the plaquette bound,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl|\langle A, \operatorname{Hess} S_W(U)\,A\rangle\bigr|
&\le \sum_p \bigl|\operatorname{Hess} S_p(A^{(p)},A^{(p)})\bigr| \\
&\le \sum_p \frac{4}{N}\,\|A^{(p)}\|^2 \\
&= \frac{4}{N}\sum_p \sum_{b\in\partial p} \|A_b\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

We now interchange the order of summation. For each link \(b\), let \(n_b\) be the number of plaquettes \(p\) such that \(b\in\partial p\). Then
\[
\sum_p \sum_{b\in\partial p} \|A_b\|^2
= \sum_{b\in B} \left(\sum_{p\ni b} 1\right)\|A_b\|^2
= \sum_{b\in B} n_b \|A_b\|^2.
\]

On a four‑dimensional hypercubic lattice, each link belongs to exactly
\[
n_b = 2(d-1) = 2(4-1) = 6
\]
plaquettes: for each of the three directions transverse to the link, there are two plaquettes sharing that link (one on each side).

Thus,
\[
\sum_p \sum_{b\in\partial p} \|A_b\|^2
= 6 \sum_{b\in B} \|A_b\|^2
= 6\,\|A\|^2.
\]

Substituting back, we obtain
\[
\bigl|\langle A, \operatorname{Hess} S_W(U)\,A\rangle\bigr|
\le \frac{4}{N} \cdot 6\,\|A\|^2
= \frac{24}{N}\,\|A\|^2.
\]

This bound holds for all configurations \(U\) and all tangent vectors \(A\).

## 4. Theorem

**Theorem (Wilson Hessian bound in \(d=4\)).**  
Consider \(SU(N)\) lattice gauge theory on a four‑dimensional hypercubic lattice with the Wilson action
\[
S_W(U) = \sum_p \left(1 - \frac{1}{N}\Re\operatorname{Tr}(U_p)\right).
\]
Equip the tangent space at each link with the inner product \(\langle X,Y\rangle = -\operatorname{Tr}(XY)\) on \(\mathfrak{su}(N)\), and let \(\|\cdot\|\) be the associated norm. Then for every configuration \(U\) and every tangent vector \(A\),
\[
\bigl|\langle A, \operatorname{Hess} S_W(U)\,A\rangle\bigr|
\le C_V(N)\,\|A\|^2,
\]
with
\[
\boxed{C_V(N) = \frac{24}{N}.}
\]

The factor of 4 in \(C_V(N)\) relative to the naive estimate arises from the mixed second derivatives (cross‑terms) between the four links in each plaquette, and the factor of 6 reflects the number of plaquettes incident on each link in four dimensions.
