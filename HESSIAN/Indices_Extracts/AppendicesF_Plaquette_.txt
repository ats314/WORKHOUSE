# Appendix F — Plaquette proxy derivative bounds and diffusion-term closure

**File status:** **Established** (all statements are proved here from smoothness/compactness + lattice locality + bi-invariance isometries).

**Scope:** This appendix isolates the *constant-hygiene* estimates needed whenever the diffusion generator \(L_\Lambda\) is applied to plaquette-local functionals built from a globally smooth proxy \(\widetilde z_p\). The goal is to guarantee: **no hidden \(|P(\Lambda)|\)** factors appear from Laplacians/Hessians/carré-du-champ terms; every such contribution is controlled by the aggregate “badness” \(\mathcal D_\Lambda=\sum_p \widetilde z_p\), with constants independent of the volume.

**Non-scope:** Any coercive **lower bound** for the drift “pairing term” is **not** in this file.

---

## F.1 Setup: product geometry and linkwise differential operators

### F.1.1 Configuration manifold and product metric
Let \(G\) be a compact Lie group with a fixed **bi-invariant** Riemannian metric \(g_G\), Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g\), and an \(\mathrm{Ad}\)-invariant inner product \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak g}\).

Let \(\Lambda\) be a finite periodic hypercubic lattice. Write:
- \(E(\Lambda)\): oriented link set,
- \(P(\Lambda)\): oriented plaquette set,
- configuration manifold \(M_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)}\) with product metric \(g_\Lambda\).

Each plaquette has perimeter
\[
m_\partial := |\partial p| = 4.
\]
Fix an overlap constant \(\nu\) such that each link lies in the boundary of at most \(\nu\) plaquettes.

### F.1.2 Linkwise orthonormal frame, carré du champ, and Laplacian
For each link \(\ell\in E(\Lambda)\), choose an orthonormal basis \(\{X^a\}_{a=1}^{\dim\mathfrak g}\) of \(\mathfrak g\) and let \(\{X_\ell^a\}\) be the corresponding right-invariant vector fields acting in the \(\ell\)-coordinate of \(M_\Lambda\).

For \(f\in C^\infty(M_\Lambda)\),
\[
\Gamma_\Lambda(f)
:=\langle \nabla f,\nabla f\rangle_{g_\Lambda}
=\sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}\sum_{a=1}^{\dim\mathfrak g} (X_\ell^a f)^2,
\]
and
\[
\Delta_\Lambda f
=\sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}\sum_{a=1}^{\dim\mathfrak g} X_\ell^aX_\ell^a f.
\]
Write \(\nabla_\ell f\) and \(\Delta_\ell f\) for the gradient/Laplacian in the \(\ell\)-coordinate (so \(\Gamma_\Lambda(f)=\sum_\ell |\nabla_\ell f|^2\) and \(\Delta_\Lambda=\sum_\ell \Delta_\ell\)).

## F.2 The smooth proxy on \(G\): definition and global derivative constants

### F.2.1 Trace-defect proxy
Fix a faithful unitary representation \(\rho:G\to U(n)\). Define the **trace defect**
\[
\widetilde z(g)
:=1-\frac{1}{n}\Re\mathrm{Tr}\big(\rho(g)\big),
\qquad g\in G.
\]
This is smooth and conjugation-invariant.

#### Lemma F.1 (range and unique minimum) — **Established**
For all \(g\in G\),
\[
0\le \widetilde z(g)\le 2,
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\widetilde z(g)=0 \iff g=\mathbf 1.
\]

*Proof.* Since \(\rho(g)\in U(n)\), its eigenvalues lie on the unit circle, hence \(\Re\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(g))\in[-n,n]\), which gives \(0\le \widetilde z\le 2\). If \(\widetilde z(g)=0\), then \(\Re\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(g))=n\), forcing \(\rho(g)=I_n\); faithfulness implies \(g=\mathbf 1\). ∎

### F.2.2 Global \(C^2\) bounds by compactness
Define the constants
\[
C^{(1)}_{\widetilde z}:=\sup_{g\in G} |\nabla_G\widetilde z(g)|,
\qquad
C^{(2)}_{\widetilde z}:=\sup_{g\in G} \|\nabla_G^2\widetilde z(g)\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\qquad
C_\Delta:=\sup_{g\in G} |\Delta_G \widetilde z(g)|.
\]

#### Lemma F.2 (uniform \(C^2\) bounds on \(G\)) — **Established**
The constants \(C^{(1)}_{\widetilde z},C^{(2)}_{\widetilde z},C_\Delta\) are finite.

*Proof.* \(\widetilde z\in C^\infty(G)\), so \(\nabla_G\widetilde z\) and \(\nabla_G^2\widetilde z\) are continuous tensor fields. Compactness of \(G\) implies their norms attain maxima. Since \(\Delta_G\widetilde z=\mathrm{tr}(\nabla_G^2\widetilde z)\), the Laplacian is also bounded. ∎

### F.2.3 Global gradient domination
Define on \(G\setminus\{\mathbf 1\}\) the ratio
\[
Q(g):=\frac{|\nabla_G \widetilde z(g)|^2}{\widetilde z(g)}.
\]

#### Lemma F.3 (global gradient domination) — **Established**
There exists \(C_\nabla<\infty\) such that for all \(g\in G\),
\[
|\nabla_G\widetilde z(g)|^2 \le C_\nabla\,\widetilde z(g).
\]
Moreover, \(Q\) extends continuously to \(g=\mathbf 1\).

*Proof.* By Lemma F.1, \(\widetilde z(g)>0\) for \(g\neq\mathbf 1\), so \(Q\) is continuous on \(G\setminus\{\mathbf 1\}\). It remains to control \(Q(g)\) as \(g\to\mathbf 1\).

Work in normal coordinates \(g=\exp(Y)\) near \(\mathbf 1\). The Taylor expansion of the single-plaquette potential at \(\mathbf 1\) (equivalently, of \(\widetilde z(\exp Y)\)) has:
- no linear term (since \(\mathbf 1\) is a minimum),
- a strictly positive quadratic term proportional to \(|Y|^2\).

Concretely, one has
\[
\widetilde z(\exp Y)=\frac{1}{2n\lambda_\rho}|Y|^2+O(|Y|^3),
\qquad
\nabla_G\widetilde z(\exp Y)=\frac{1}{n\lambda_\rho}Y+O(|Y|^2),
\]
hence
\[
\frac{|\nabla_G\widetilde z(\exp Y)|^2}{\widetilde z(\exp Y)}
\longrightarrow
\frac{2}{n\lambda_\rho}
\quad\text{as }Y\to 0.
\]
Thus \(Q\) extends continuously to \(g=\mathbf 1\). By compactness, the continuous extension has a finite supremum; call it \(C_\nabla\). ∎

## F.3 Lifting to plaquettes: locality + bi-invariant isometries

### F.3.1 Plaquette lift
For each plaquette \(p\in P(\Lambda)\), define the plaquette holonomy \(U_p(U)\in G\) as the ordered product of the four boundary link variables (with inverses on oppositely oriented edges), and define
\[
\widetilde z_p(U):=\widetilde z\big(U_p(U)\big),
\qquad U\in M_\Lambda.
\]

### F.3.2 Link support and one-link factorization

#### Lemma F.4 (support and one-link factorization) — **Established**
Fix a plaquette \(p\) with boundary \(\partial p\subset E(\Lambda)\). For a link \(\ell\in E(\Lambda)\):

1. If \(\ell\notin\partial p\), then \(\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p\equiv 0\) and \(\Delta_\ell \widetilde z_p\equiv 0\).
2. If \(\ell\in\partial p\), then holding the other three boundary links fixed, the map \(g:=U_\ell\mapsto \widetilde z_p(U)\) can be written as
   \[
   g\longmapsto \widetilde z(A\,g^\sigma\,B)
   \]
   for some \(A,B\in G\) and \(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\). The map \(g\mapsto A g^\sigma B\) is an isometry of \((G,g_G)\).

*Proof.* If \(\ell\notin\partial p\), the plaquette word \(U_p(U)\) is independent of \(U_\ell\), so all \(\ell\)-derivatives vanish.

If \(\ell\in\partial p\), the plaquette word has the schematic form
\[
U_p = U_{b_1}\,U_{b_2}\,U_{b_3}^{-1}\,U_{b_4}^{-1}.
\]
Fixing the other three link variables, the dependence on \(U_\ell\) is either by left/right multiplication or by inversion (depending on whether \(\ell\) appears with exponent \(+1\) or \(-1\)), hence \(U_p(U)=A g^\sigma B\).

Left/right multiplication are isometries for any bi-invariant metric, and inversion is also an isometry for a bi-invariant metric. Therefore their composition \(g\mapsto A g^\sigma B\) is an isometry. ∎

### F.3.3 Derivative bounds for \(\widetilde z_p\)

#### Lemma F.5 (uniform linkwise derivative bounds for \(\widetilde z_p\)) — **Established**
With the constants from §F.2, for every plaquette \(p\), every link \(\ell\), and every configuration \(U\in M_\Lambda\),
\[
|\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p(U)| \le C^{(1)}_{\widetilde z}\,\mathbf 1_{\{\ell\in\partial p\}},
\]
\[
\big\|\nabla_\ell^2 \widetilde z_p(U)\big\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C^{(2)}_{\widetilde z}\,\mathbf 1_{\{\ell\in\partial p\}},
\]
\[
|\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p(U)|^2 \le C_\nabla\,\widetilde z_p(U)\,\mathbf 1_{\{\ell\in\partial p\}}.
\]
Moreover, the full product Laplacian satisfies
\[
|\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p(U)|\le m_\partial\,C_\Delta = 4C_\Delta.
\]

*Proof.* If \(\ell\notin\partial p\), everything is \(0\) by Lemma F.4(1).

If \(\ell\in\partial p\), write \(\widetilde z_p = \widetilde z\circ \Psi\) in the \(\ell\)-coordinate with \(\Psi(g)=A g^\sigma B\), an isometry by Lemma F.4(2). For any smooth \(f\) and isometry \(\Psi\), one has
\[
|\nabla(f\circ\Psi)|(g)=|\nabla f|(\Psi(g)),
\qquad
\Delta(f\circ\Psi)(g)=(\Delta f)(\Psi(g)),
\]
and similarly \(\|\nabla^2(f\circ\Psi)\|_{\mathrm{op}}(g)=\|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{op}}(\Psi(g))\).
Applying this with \(f=\widetilde z\) yields the first three bounds.

Finally, \(\Delta_\Lambda=\sum_\ell \Delta_\ell\), and only the four links in \(\partial p\) contribute; each contribution is bounded in magnitude by \(C_\Delta\), hence \(|\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p|\le 4C_\Delta\). ∎

#### Corollary F.6 (plaquette carré du champ bound) — **Established**
For all \(p\) and \(U\),
\[
\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)(U)
=\sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)} |\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p(U)|^2
\le m_\partial\,C_\nabla\,\widetilde z_p(U)
=4C_\nabla\,\widetilde z_p(U).
\]

*Proof.* Only \(\ell\in\partial p\) contribute; each such link contributes at most \(C_\nabla \widetilde z_p(U)\) by Lemma F.5. ∎

## F.4 Uniform closure of diffusion-generated terms for \(\Phi(s)=s^2\)

### F.4.1 Aggregate functionals
Define
\[
\mathcal D_\Lambda(U):=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \widetilde z_p(U),
\qquad
V_\Lambda(U):=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \widetilde z_p(U)^2.
\]
The intended Lyapunov function elsewhere is \(W_\Lambda=e^{\kappa V_\Lambda}\); this appendix only supplies bounds for the diffusion-generated pieces that arise from differentiating \(V_\Lambda\) and \(W_\Lambda\).

### F.4.2 Chain-rule reminder (diffusion part only)
For smooth \(\Phi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) and smooth \(f\) on \(M_\Lambda\),
\[
\Delta_\Lambda(\Phi\circ f)=\Phi'(f)\,\Delta_\Lambda f+\Phi''(f)\,\Gamma_\Lambda(f).
\]
For \(\Phi(s)=s^2\), \(\Phi'(s)=2s\) and \(\Phi''(s)=2\).

### F.4.3 The uniform closure proposition

#### Proposition F.7 (diffusion-term closure by \(\mathcal D_\Lambda\) for \(\Phi(s)=s^2\)) — **Established**
There exist constants \(A_1,A_2,A_3<\infty\), depending only on \((G,g_G)\), \(\rho\), and \(\nu\) (not on \(\Lambda\)), such that for all \(U\in M_\Lambda\),
\[
\Big|\sum_{p} 2\widetilde z_p(U)\,\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p(U)\Big|
\le A_1\,\mathcal D_\Lambda(U),
\]
\[
2\sum_{p}\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)(U)
\le A_2\,\mathcal D_\Lambda(U),
\]
\[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)(U)\le A_3\,\mathcal D_\Lambda(U).
\]
One explicit choice is
\[
A_1=8C_\Delta,
\qquad
A_2=8C_\nabla,
\qquad
A_3=64\,\nu\,C_\nabla.
\]

*Proof.*

1. **Laplacian/Hessian term.** By Lemma F.5, \(|\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p|\le 4C_\Delta\). Therefore
   \[
   \Big|\sum_{p} 2\widetilde z_p\,\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p\Big|
   \le \sum_p 2\widetilde z_p\cdot 4C_\Delta
   =8C_\Delta\sum_p \widetilde z_p
   =A_1\,\mathcal D_\Lambda.
   \]

2. **Chain-rule \(\Gamma\)-term.** By Corollary F.6,
   \[
   2\sum_p \Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)
   \le 2\sum_p 4C_\nabla \widetilde z_p
   =8C_\nabla\,\mathcal D_\Lambda,
   \]
   so \(A_2=8C_\nabla\).

3. **Carré du champ of \(V_\Lambda\).** For each link \(\ell\),
   \[
   \nabla_\ell V_\Lambda
   =\sum_{p:\,\ell\in\partial p}\nabla_\ell(\widetilde z_p^2)
   =\sum_{p:\,\ell\in\partial p} 2\widetilde z_p\,\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p.
   \]
   Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the finite sum over \(p\) meeting \(\ell\):
   \[
   |\nabla_\ell V_\Lambda|^2
   \le \Big(\#\{p:\ell\in\partial p\}\Big)\sum_{p:\ell\in\partial p} 4\widetilde z_p^2\,|\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p|^2.
   \]
   Since \(\#\{p:\ell\in\partial p\}\le \nu\), summing over links gives
   \[
   \Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)
   =\sum_\ell |\nabla_\ell V_\Lambda|^2
   \le 4\nu \sum_\ell\sum_{p:\ell\in\partial p}\widetilde z_p^2\,|\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p|^2
   =4\nu \sum_p \widetilde z_p^2\sum_{\ell\in\partial p}|\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p|^2.
   \]
   The inner sum is \(\le \Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)\), so Corollary F.6 gives
   \[
   \Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)
   \le 4\nu \sum_p \widetilde z_p^2\cdot (4C_\nabla \widetilde z_p)
   =16\nu C_\nabla \sum_p \widetilde z_p^3.
   \]
   Using the range bound \(0\le \widetilde z_p\le 2\) (Lemma F.1), we have \(\widetilde z_p^3\le 4\widetilde z_p\). Therefore
   \[
   \Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)
   \le 16\nu C_\nabla \sum_p 4\widetilde z_p
   =64\nu C_\nabla\,\mathcal D_\Lambda,
   \]
   i.e. \(A_3=64\nu C_\nabla\). ∎

## F.5 What this appendix guarantees (and what it does not)

**Guaranteed here (Established):**
- global bounds \(C^{(1)}_{\widetilde z},C^{(2)}_{\widetilde z},C_\Delta\) by compactness;
- global gradient domination \(|\nabla\widetilde z|^2\le C_\nabla \widetilde z\);
- locality/isometry lift implies the same bounds for plaquette observables \(\widetilde z_p\) without volume factors;
- for \(\Phi(s)=s^2\), every diffusion-generated term in the chain rule closes with constants times \(\mathcal D_\Lambda\), uniformly in \(\Lambda\).

**Not addressed here:**
- any lower bound (coercivity) for drift pairing/cross terms.
