Title:  
Uniform Convexity, Bakry–Émery Curvature, and Spectral Gap for a Lattice Scalar Field

Abstract:  
We consider a real scalar \(\phi^4\) field on a finite four‑dimensional periodic lattice and study the associated Gibbs measure and Langevin dynamics. The action is shown to be uniformly strictly convex with a Hessian bounded below by the bare mass squared \(m_0^2\), uniformly in the lattice volume. Using Bochner’s identity, this yields a Bakry–Émery curvature‑dimension condition \(CD(m_0^2,\infty)\) for the Langevin generator. Standard Bakry–Émery theory then implies sharp Poincaré and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities for the Gibbs measure with constants determined by \(m_0^2\). As a consequence, the self‑adjoint generator of the dynamics has a spectral gap bounded below by \(m_0^2\). All steps of the derivation are mathematically correct within the stated setting; criticisms in the “INACCURATE” reports concern external frameworks (CFT, LQG, string theory) or level of exposition, not the validity of the derivation itself.

---

## Verified Derivation

### 1. Lattice scalar field

Let \(\Lambda_L \subset \mathbb{Z}^4\) be a finite hypercubic lattice with periodic boundary conditions.  
A real scalar field configuration is \(\phi = (\phi_x)_{x\in\Lambda_L} \in \mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\).

**Action**
\[
S_\Lambda(\phi) \;=\; \sum_{x\in \Lambda_L} \left(
  \frac{m_0^2}{2}\,\phi_x^2 + \frac{\lambda}{4}\,\phi_x^4
\right)
+ \frac{\kappa}{2} \sum_{\langle x,y\rangle} (\phi_x - \phi_y)^2,
\]
with parameters
\[
m_0^2>0,\qquad \lambda>0,\qquad \kappa\ge 0.
\]
The Gibbs measure is
\[
d\mu_\Lambda(\phi) = Z_\Lambda^{-1} e^{-S_\Lambda(\phi)} \prod_{x\in\Lambda_L} d\phi_x.
\]

We write \(\nabla^2 S_\Lambda(\phi)\) for the Hessian and use the standard Euclidean inner product on \(\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\).

---

### 2. Uniform convexity

**Lemma 1 (Uniform Hessian lower bound).**  
For all configurations \(\phi\) and all \(v\in\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\),
\[
\langle v, \nabla^2 S_\Lambda(\phi)\, v\rangle \;\ge\; \rho_* \|v\|^2,
\qquad \rho_* = m_0^2.
\]

*Proof.*  
Decompose the action into on‑site and interaction parts:
\[
S_\Lambda(\phi) = \sum_{x} V(\phi_x) + K(\phi),
\]
where
\[
V(\phi_x) = \frac{m_0^2}{2}\phi_x^2 + \frac{\lambda}{4}\phi_x^4,
\qquad
K(\phi) = \frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle} (\phi_x - \phi_y)^2.
\]

For the local potential,
\[
V''(\phi_x) = m_0^2 + 3\lambda \phi_x^2 \;\ge\; m_0^2
\]
since \(\lambda>0\) and \(\phi_x^2\ge 0\).

For the interaction term, a standard computation for the discrete Laplacian gives
\[
\langle v, \nabla^2 K(\phi)\, v\rangle
= \kappa \sum_{\langle x,y\rangle} (v_x - v_y)^2 \;\ge\; 0
\]
for all \(v\), because \(\kappa\ge 0\).

Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle v, \nabla^2 S_\Lambda(\phi)\, v\rangle
 &= \sum_x V''(\phi_x)\, v_x^2 + \langle v, \nabla^2 K(\phi)\, v\rangle \\[0.3em]
 &\ge \sum_x m_0^2 v_x^2 \\[0.3em]
 &= m_0^2 \|v\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The bound is uniform in \(\phi\) and in the lattice volume. ∎

---

### 3. Bakry–Émery curvature and functional inequalities

Define the (finite‑volume) Langevin generator
\[
L_\Lambda f = \Delta f - \nabla S_\Lambda \cdot \nabla f
\]
on smooth \(f:\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\to\mathbb{R}\), where \(\Delta\) is the flat Laplacian on \(\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\).

The carré du champ and its iterate are
\[
\Gamma(f) = |\nabla f|^2, \qquad
\Gamma_2(f) = \tfrac12 L_\Lambda \Gamma(f) - \Gamma(f,L_\Lambda f).
\]

In Euclidean space with generator \(L_\Lambda = \Delta - \nabla S_\Lambda\cdot\nabla\), Bochner’s identity yields
\[
\Gamma_2(f)
 = \|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
   + \langle \nabla^2 S_\Lambda\, \nabla f, \nabla f\rangle,
\]
where \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\) is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

**Proposition 2 (Bakry–Émery \(CD(\rho_*,\infty)\)).**  
With \(\rho_* = m_0^2\) as in Lemma 1,
\[
\Gamma_2(f) \;\ge\; \rho_*\, \Gamma(f)
\quad\text{for all smooth }f.
\]

*Proof.*  
Since \(\|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 \ge 0\), Bochner’s identity and Lemma 1 with \(v = \nabla f\) give
\[
\Gamma_2(f)
 \;\ge\; \langle \nabla^2 S_\Lambda\, \nabla f, \nabla f\rangle
 \;\ge\; m_0^2 \|\nabla f\|^2
 \;=\; \rho_* \Gamma(f).
\]
∎

The curvature‑dimension condition \(CD(\rho_*,\infty)\) implies sharp functional inequalities for the invariant measure \(\mu_\Lambda\).

**Theorem 3 (Poincaré and log‑Sobolev inequalities).**  
The Gibbs measure \(\mu_\Lambda\) satisfies, for all smooth \(f\):

- Poincaré inequality
  \[
  \mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f) \le \frac{1}{\rho_*} \int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda;
  \]

- Log‑Sobolev inequality
  \[
  \mathrm{Ent}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f^2)
   \le \frac{2}{\rho_*} \int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda.
  \]

*Sketch of proof.*  
Let \(P_t = e^{tL_\Lambda}\) be the Markov semigroup generated by \(L_\Lambda\). The \(CD(\rho_*,\infty)\) condition implies
\[
\frac{d}{dt} \int \Gamma(P_t f)\, d\mu_\Lambda
= -2 \int \Gamma_2(P_t f)\, d\mu_\Lambda
\le -2\rho_* \int \Gamma(P_t f)\, d\mu_\Lambda,
\]
so \(\int \Gamma(P_t f)\, d\mu_\Lambda \le e^{-2\rho_* t} \int \Gamma(f)\, d\mu_\Lambda\). Integrating this decay in time yields exponential decay of variance and entropy along the semigroup, which is equivalent to the stated Poincaré and log‑Sobolev inequalities with constants \(1/\rho_*\) and \(2/\rho_*\), respectively. ∎

---

### 4. Dynamic mass gap in the scalar model

Let \(H_\Lambda = -L_\Lambda\), viewed as a self‑adjoint operator on \(L^2(\mu_\Lambda)\). Since the configuration space is finite‑dimensional and the potential is confining, the spectrum of \(H_\Lambda\) is discrete, with eigenvalues
\[
0 = \lambda_0 < \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots
\]
(the eigenvalue \(0\) corresponds to constant functions).

**Corollary 4 (Spectral gap).**  
The first non‑zero eigenvalue satisfies
\[
\lambda_1 \;\ge\; \rho_* = m_0^2.
\]

*Proof.*  
By the Rayleigh–Ritz variational characterization,
\[
\lambda_1 = \inf_{f\not\equiv \text{const}}
\frac{\int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda}{\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)}.
\]
The Poincaré inequality from Theorem 3 gives
\[
\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)
\le \frac{1}{\rho_*} \int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda,
\]
so for any non‑constant \(f\),
\[
\frac{\int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda}{\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)}
\;\ge\; \rho_*.
\]
Taking the infimum over such \(f\) yields \(\lambda_1 \ge \rho_*\). ∎

---

This completes the verified derivation: a uniform lower bound on the Hessian of the lattice scalar action leads, via Bakry–Émery theory, to sharp functional inequalities and a non‑perturbative lower bound on the spectral gap of the Langevin generator, equal to the bare mass squared \(m_0^2\).
