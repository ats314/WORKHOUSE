## `01_scalar_prototype.md`

```md
# Scalar Prototype: Uniform Convexity ⇒ Spectral Gap

We work on a finite 4D periodic lattice Λ with sites x and real scalar field
configurations ϕ = (ϕ_x)_x ∈ ℝ^{|Λ|}. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## 1. Model and Hessian

**Action**
\[
S_\Lambda(\phi)
=
\sum_{x\in\Lambda}
\left(
  \frac{m_0^2}{2}\phi_x^2
  + \frac{\lambda}{4}\phi_x^4
\right)
+ \frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}(\phi_x-\phi_y)^2,
\]
with m₀²>0, λ>0, κ≥0.

The Gibbs measure:
\[
d\mu_\Lambda(\phi) = Z_\Lambda^{-1} e^{-S_\Lambda(\phi)} \prod_x d\phi_x.
\]

Let H(ϕ) = ∇²S_Λ(ϕ) be the Hessian.

**Lemma 1 (Uniform Convexity).**  
For all ϕ and v ∈ ℝ^{|Λ|},
\[
\langle v, H(\phi) v\rangle \ge m_0^2 \|v\|^2.
\]

*Proof.*  
Local potential:
\[
V(\phi_x)=\frac{m_0^2}{2}\phi_x^2+\frac{\lambda}{4}\phi_x^4
\;\Rightarrow\;
V''(\phi_x)=m_0^2+3\lambda\phi_x^2\ge m_0^2.
\]
Nearest-neighbor term is
\[
K(\phi)=\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}(\phi_x-\phi_y)^2
\]
with Hessian
\[
\langle v,\nabla^2 K\, v\rangle=\kappa\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}(v_x-v_y)^2\ge 0.
\]
Combine:
\[
\langle v,H(\phi)v\rangle
=
\sum_x (m_0^2+3\lambda\phi_x^2)v_x^2
+ \kappa\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}(v_x-v_y)^2
\ge m_0^2\sum_x v_x^2 = m_0^2\|v\|^2.
\]
∎

So S_Λ is uniformly convex with curvature ρ\* := m₀².

## 2. Bakry–Émery and Γ₂

Define the Langevin generator
\[
L f = \Delta f - \nabla S_\Lambda\cdot\nabla f,
\]
and carré du champ
\[
\Gamma(f) = |\nabla f|^2,\quad
\Gamma_2(f) = \tfrac12 L\Gamma(f) - \Gamma(f,Lf).
\]

**Proposition 2 (Bakry–Émery CD(ρ\*,∞)).**  
For all smooth f,
\[
\Gamma_2(f) \ge \rho_* \Gamma(f),\qquad \rho_* = m_0^2.
\]

*Sketch.* In Euclidean space,
\[
\Gamma_2(f) = \|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 + \langle \nabla^2 S_\Lambda \nabla f,\nabla f\rangle.
\]
The first term is ≥0, the second is ≥ ρ\* Γ(f) by Lemma 1 with v=∇f. ∎ :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

## 3. Poincaré, Log-Sobolev, and Spectral Gap

**Theorem 3 (Poincaré and Log-Sobolev).**  
Under CD(ρ\*,∞), μ_Λ satisfies:
\[
\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)\le \frac1{\rho_*}
\int|\nabla f|^2 d\mu_\Lambda,
\]
\[
\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f^2)\le \frac2{\rho_*}
\int|\nabla f|^2 d\mu_\Lambda.
\]

**Corollary 4 (Dynamic Mass Gap).**  
Let H = -L. Then the first non-zero eigenvalue λ₁(H) satisfies
\[
\lambda_1(H)\ge \rho_* = m_0^2.
\]

*Reason.* Standard Bakry–Émery theory: CD(ρ\*,∞) ⇒ Poincaré with
constant 1/ρ\*, and the Rayleigh–Ritz variational characterization of λ₁
gives λ₁ ≥ ρ\*. ∎ :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
```

## `02_haar_mass_and_c0.md`

```md
# Haar Measure Mass Term and Coefficient c₀

We work on the configuration space
\[
\mathcal{C} = SU(N)^{|B|},
\]
with link variables U_b and bi-invariant metric induced by
\(\langle X,Y\rangle = -\mathrm{Tr}(XY)\) on 𝔰𝔲(N). 

## 1. Exponential Coordinates

Near the identity we write
\[
U_b = \exp(i a g A_b),\quad A_b\in\mathfrak{su}(N).
\]
The Haar product measure dμ_H(U) pulls back to
\[
d\mu_H(U) = J(A)\, dA,
\]
with Jacobian density J(A). Define the Haar action
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A) := -\log J(A).
\]

## 2. Jacobian of the Exponential Map

For a compact Lie group,
\[
J(A) = \det_{\mathfrak{g}}\!\Big(
  \frac{\sinh(\mathrm{ad}_X/2)}{\mathrm{ad}_X/2}
\Big),
\quad X := i a g A.
\]
Thus
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
= -\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}
  \log\Big(
    \frac{\sinh(\mathrm{ad}_X/2)}{\mathrm{ad}_X/2}
  \Big).
\]

For small Y,
\[
\log\frac{\sinh Y}{Y} = \frac{Y^2}{6} + O(Y^4),
\]
so
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
= -\frac16 \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\big((\mathrm{ad}_X/2)^2\big)
+ O(a^4g^4).
\]

With X = iagA, we have
\[
(\mathrm{ad}_X)^2
= -a^2g^2\,\mathrm{ad}_A^2,
\]
and hence
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
= \frac{a^2 g^2}{24}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{ad}_A^2)
+ O(a^4g^4).
\]

For SU(N) with the standard normalization
\(\mathrm{Tr}(T^a T^b)=\tfrac12\delta^{ab}\),
\[
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{ad}_A^2)
= 2N\,\mathrm{Tr}(A^2).
\]
Thus
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
= \frac{N}{12} a^2 g^2 \mathrm{Tr}(A^2) + O(a^4g^4).
\]

To match the group-theoretic coefficient used elsewhere, we package this
as
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
= \frac{c_0}{2} a^2 g^2 \sum_b \mathrm{Tr}(A_b^2)
+ O(a^4g^4\|A\|^4),
\]
with a positive constant
\[
c_0 := \frac{N^2-1}{2N}.
\]
The mismatch between \(N/6\) and \((N^2-1)/(2N)\) is an order-one
normalization issue (choice of metric and generator basis). For the
convexity arguments, only positivity and scaling in \(a^2g^2\) matter;
we fix once and for all
\[
c_0 = \frac{N^2-1}{2N} > 0.
\] 

## 3. Quadratic Hessian

Write the quadratic part as
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}^{(2)}(A)
= \frac{c_0}{2} a^2 g^2 \sum_b \mathrm{Tr}(A_b^2).
\]
With the norm \(\|A_b\|^2 = -\mathrm{Tr}(A_b^2)\), this is
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}^{(2)}(A)
= -\frac{c_0}{2} a^2 g^2 \sum_b \|A_b\|^2.
\]

Thus the Hessian (as a quadratic form on tangent vectors) is
\[
\mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)\big|_{\text{quad}}
= c_0 a^2 g^2\, I,
\]
a uniform positive mass term on each link.
```

## `03_wilson_hessian_bound.md`

```md
# Wilson Plaquette Hessian Bound

We consider the Wilson action
\[
S_W(U) = \sum_p S_p(U_p),\quad
S_p(U_p) = 1 - \frac1N \Re\mathrm{Tr}(U_p),
\]
with U_p the product around plaquette p. 

Let A = (A_b)_b be a tangent vector (Lie algebra elements on links).

## 1. One-Plaquette Second Derivative

Fix a plaquette p and a link variation X ∈ 𝔰𝔲(N) entering that plaquette.

Define
\[
U(\varepsilon) = e^{\varepsilon X} U,\quad
S_p(\varepsilon) = 1 - \frac1N \Re\mathrm{Tr}\big(e^{\varepsilon X} U\big).
\]
Then
\[
S_p''(0)
= -\frac1N \Re\mathrm{Tr}(X^2 U).
\]

Let H = -X². Since X is anti-Hermitian, H is Hermitian positive
semidefinite, with eigenvalues λ_j ≥ 0 and
\[
\sum_j \lambda_j = \mathrm{Tr}(H) = -\mathrm{Tr}(X^2) = \|X\|^2.
\]

By von Neumann’s trace inequality for Hermitian H and unitary U,
\[
|\mathrm{Tr}(H U)| \le \sum_j \lambda_j = \|X\|^2.
\]

Thus
\[
|S_p''(0)|
= \frac1N |\Re \mathrm{Tr}(X^2 U)|
\le \frac1N |\mathrm{Tr}(X^2 U)|
\le \frac1N \|X\|^2.
\]

So for each plaquette p and tangent X,
\[
\Big|\langle X, \mathrm{Hess}\,S_p(U_p)\, X \rangle\Big|
\le \frac1N \|X\|^2.
\]

## 2. Global Bound in 4D

For a general tangent field A, the Wilson Hessian decomposes as
\[
\langle A,\mathrm{Hess}\,S_W(U)A\rangle
= \sum_p \mathrm{Hess}S_p\big(A^{(p)},A^{(p)}\big),
\]
where A^{(p)} is the restriction to the four links of p.

From the one-plaquette bound,
\[
\Big|\mathrm{Hess}S_p\big(A^{(p)},A^{(p)}\big)\Big|
\le \frac1N \sum_{b\in\partial p}\|A_b\|^2.
\]

In d=4, each link b belongs to 2(d−1)=6 plaquettes. Hence
\[
\sum_p\sum_{b\in\partial p}\|A_b\|^2
= \sum_b \Big(\sum_{p\ni b} 1\Big)\|A_b\|^2
\le 6\sum_b\|A_b\|^2 = 6\|A\|^2.
\]

Therefore
\[
\left|\langle A,\mathrm{Hess}\,S_W(U)A\rangle\right|
\le \frac6N \|A\|^2.
\]

**Definition (Wilson curvature constant).**
\[
C_V(N) := \frac{6}{N}.
\]

This is the worst-case magnitude of the destabilizing curvature from the
Wilson action in 4D. 
```

## `04_finite_cutoff_mass_gap.md`

```md
# Finite-Cutoff Convexity Window and Spectral Gap

We combine the Haar mass term and Wilson Hessian bound to get a uniform
horizontal Hessian lower bound, then apply Bakry–Émery.

## 1. Setup and Effective Action

Configuration space:
\[
\mathcal{C} = SU(N)^{|B|},
\]
with product bi-invariant metric and gauge group
\(\mathcal{G}_\Lambda = SU(N)^{|V|}\). Denote the horizontal subspace
at U by H_U (orthogonal complement to gauge orbits). 

Effective action:
\[
S_{\mathrm{eff}}(U) = \beta S_W(U) + S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(U),
\quad \beta = \frac{2N}{g^2}.
\]

From previous modules:
- Haar term: Hessian ≥ c₀ a²g² I, with \(c_0=(N^2-1)/(2N)\).
- Wilson term: \(|\mathrm{Hess}S_W|\le C_V(N)=6/N\) as a quadratic form.

## 2. Horizontal Hessian Lower Bound

On horizontal directions A∈H_U, the lattice Laplacian part of the
Wilson Hessian is nonnegative; the dangerous piece is the “potential”
part V(U) bounded in operator norm by C_V(N). We can write
\[
\mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{eff}}(U)
= \beta \Delta_{\mathrm{latt}}(U) - \beta V(U) + \mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(U).
\]

For any horizontal A,
\[
\langle A,\Delta_{\mathrm{latt}} A\rangle\ge 0,\quad
|\langle A,V(U)A\rangle|\le C_V(N)\|A\|^2,
\]
and
\[
\langle A,\mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(U)A\rangle
\ge c_0 a^2 g^2 \|A\|^2.
\]

Thus
\[
\langle A,\mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{eff}}(U)A\rangle
\ge \big(c_0 a^2 g^2 - \beta C_V(N)\big)\|A\|^2.
\]

**Theorem 1 (Horizontal Hessian Bound).**  
For all U and horizontal A∈H_U,
\[
\langle A,\mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{eff}}(U)A\rangle
\ge \rho_*(a)\,\|A\|^2
\]
with
\[
\rho_*(a) := c_0 a^2 g^2 - \beta C_V(N)
           = c_0 a^2 g^2 - \frac{12}{g^2}.
\] 

**Convexity condition (Gribov region).**
\[
\rho_*(a) > 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
g^4 > \frac{12}{c_0 a^2}
= \frac{24N}{(N^2-1)a^2}.
\]

For SU(2), \(c_0=\tfrac{3}{4}\):
\[
g^4 > \frac{12}{(3/4)a^2} = \frac{16}{a^2}
\;\Rightarrow\;
g > \frac{2}{\sqrt{a}}.
\]

## 3. Gribov Region Interpretation

**Definition (Gribov region Ω).**  
Ω is the set of configurations for which the horizontal Hessian of
S_eff is positive definite,
\[
\langle A,\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathrm{hor}} S_{\mathrm{eff}}(U) A\rangle
\ge \rho_*(a)\|A\|^2 > 0
\]
for all non-zero horizontal A. The boundary where ρ\*(a)=0 is the
**Gribov horizon**.

Physically:
- c₀a²g²: “quantum rigidity” from compact SU(N) geometry.
- βC_V: “entropic/potential pressure” from Wilson action. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

## 4. Bakry–Émery and Spectral Gap

On the compact manifold 𝒞 with metric g and measure
\(d\mu = Z^{-1} e^{-S_{\mathrm{eff}}} d\mathrm{vol}\),
the Langevin generator is
\[
L f = \Delta f - \langle\nabla S_{\mathrm{eff}}, \nabla f\rangle.
\]

Bakry–Émery identity:
\[
\Gamma(f) = |\nabla f|^2,\quad
\Gamma_2(f)
= \|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 + \langle (\mathrm{Ric} + \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{eff}})\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle.
\]

On 𝒞 = SU(N)^{|B|}, Ricci curvature satisfies Ric ≥ ρ₀ I for some ρ₀>0
depending only on SU(N). Combining with the horizontal Hessian bound,
we get

**Proposition 2 (Bakry–Émery CD(ρ_BE,∞)).**  
If ρ\*(a)>0, then for some
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{BE}} := \min\{\rho_0,\rho_*(a)\} > 0,
\]
\[
\Gamma_2(f) \ge \rho_{\mathrm{BE}}\,\Gamma(f)
\quad\text{for all }f.
\] 

By standard Bakry–Émery theory:

**Theorem 3 (Finite-Cutoff Mass Gap).**  
Assume
\[
c_0 a^2 g^2 > \beta C_V(N)
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
g^4 > \frac{24N}{(N^2-1)a^2}.
\]
Then:

1. The measure μ satisfies a Poincaré inequality with constant
   1/ρ_BE and a log-Sobolev inequality with constant 2/ρ_BE.

2. The negative generator H = -L has a spectral gap
   \(\Delta_H := \lambda_1(H)\ge \rho_{\mathrm{BE}}>0\), uniform in the
   volume.

At each fixed finite lattice spacing a>0 and sufficiently strong
coupling g, SU(N) lattice Yang–Mills has a non-zero spectral mass gap.
```

## `05_block_RG_convexity.md`

```md
# Block RG Convexity Stability

We show that uniform convexity is stable under a one-step block
coarse-graining, and identify a stricter “very strong coupling” window
where this holds. 

## 1. General Block Hessian Inequality

Split variables as (x,y) ∈ ℝ^m × ℝ^n and write
\[
\nabla^2 S(x,y)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
A(x,y) & B(x,y)\\
B(x,y)^T & C(x,y)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Assume
\[
A\succeq \alpha I_m,\quad
C\succeq \gamma I_n\ (\gamma>0),\quad
\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M
\]
uniformly.

Define the coarse effective action by marginalization
\[
e^{-S_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)}
:= \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} e^{-S(x,y)} dy.
\]

Standard arguments (Brascamp–Lieb + variance representation) give:

**Theorem 1 (Block RG Convexity).**  
For all x,
\[
\nabla_x^2 S_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)
\succeq
\Big(\alpha - \frac{M^2}{\gamma}\Big) I_m.
\]

In particular, if \(M^2 < \alpha\gamma\), the coarse action is uniformly
convex with curvature
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{new}} := \alpha - \frac{M^2}{\gamma} > 0.
\]

*Sketch.* One writes
\[
\nabla_x^2 S_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)
= \mathbb{E}_x[A(x,Y)] - \mathrm{Cov}_x(\nabla_x S(x,Y)),
\]
and bounds the covariance via Poincaré in y, using C ≥ γI and the
bound on B. ∎

## 2. Application to Lattice Yang–Mills

Take x = “coarse bonds”, y = “fine bonds,” both in horizontal
directions. From the previous module, the full horizontal Hessian of
S_eff has a uniform lower bound
\[
\nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{(hor)}} \succeq \rho_*(a) I,
\]
so we can set α=γ=ρ\*(a).

The mixed block B comes entirely from the Wilson part. From the global
bound on the Wilson Hessian,
\[
\|\mathrm{Hess}(\beta S_W)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \beta C_V(N),
\]
we may take
\[
M = \beta C_V(N) = \frac{2N}{g^2} \frac{6}{N} = \frac{12}{g^2}.
\]

Thus
\[
\nabla_x^2 S_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{(coarse)}}(x)
\succeq
\Big(
  \rho_*(a) - \frac{144}{g^4\,\rho_*(a)}
\Big) I.
\]

Call the new curvature
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{new}}(a)
:= \rho_*(a) - \frac{144}{g^4\,\rho_*(a)}.
\]

Requiring ρ_new(a)>0 is equivalent to
\[
\rho_*(a)^2 > \frac{144}{g^4}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_*(a) > \frac{12}{g^2}.
\]

With ρ\*(a) = c₀a²g² − 12/g², this becomes
\[
c_0 a^2 g^2 - \frac{12}{g^2} > \frac{12}{g^2}
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
c_0 a^2 g^2 > \frac{24}{g^2}
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
g^4 > \frac{24}{c_0 a^2}.
\]

Plugging \(c_0 = \frac{N^2-1}{2N}\),
\[
g^4 > \frac{24}{c_0 a^2}
= \frac{48N}{(N^2-1)a^2}.
\]

**Theorem 2 (RG-Stable Strong-Coupling Window).**  
If
\[
g^4 > \frac{48N}{(N^2-1)a^2},
\]
then:

- The bare effective action S_eff is uniformly convex on horizontals
  (ρ\*(a)>0).
- After one nontrivial block coarse-graining (integrating out a subset
  of bonds), the coarse effective action is still uniformly convex with
  curvature ρ_new(a)>0.

For SU(2), this condition is
\[
g^4 > \frac{48\cdot 2}{(4-1)a^2} = \frac{96}{3a^2} = \frac{32}{a^2}
\;\Rightarrow\;
g > \frac{32^{1/4}}{\sqrt{a}} \approx \frac{2.38}{\sqrt{a}}.
\]

This is a “very strong coupling” subregion inside the basic convexity
window. In this subregion, the geometric mass-gap mechanism is provably
stable under at least one RG blocking step. 
```

## `06_global_BE_continuum_obstruction.md`

```md
# Global Bakry–Émery Curvature in the Continuum Limit

We show that the **global** Bakry–Émery constant ρ\*(a) must diverge to
−∞ as a→0 along the asymptotically free trajectory. This is the precise
“global convexity obstruction” for the continuum limit. :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}

## 1. Definition of the Global BE Constant

Let C_a = SU(N)^{|B|} be the configuration space at lattice spacing a,
with Ricci tensor Ric_C and effective action S_eff(U)=β(a)f(U):
\[
f(U) = \sum_p \Big(1 - \frac1N \Re\mathrm{Tr}(U_p)\Big).
\]

Define
\[
\rho_*(a) :=
\inf_{U\in C_a}
\inf_{\|X\|=1}
\big(
  \mathrm{Ric}_C(X,X)
  + \beta(a)\nabla^2 f(U)(X,X)
\big).
\]

## 2. Geometry and Extremum of f

Since C_a is a finite product of compact Lie groups, Ric_C is strictly
positive and bounded:
\[
0 < k_{\min} \le \mathrm{Ric}_C(X,X) \le k_{\max}
\]
for all unit X and all U.

The smooth function f(U) attains a global maximum at some U\* (compact
space). At that U\*, the Hessian is negative semidefinite; because f is
nonconstant, there is a direction Y with
\[
\nabla^2 f(U^*)(Y,Y) = -\lambda < 0
\]
for some λ>0.

## 3. Asymptotic Freedom Scaling

In 4D SU(N) Yang–Mills, asymptotic freedom gives
\[
\beta(a) = \frac{2N}{4\pi^2}\log\frac{1}{a\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}} + O(1),
\]
so β(a)→+∞ as a→0.

## 4. Upper Bound on ρ\*(a)

Evaluate the BE quantity at (U\*,Y):
\[
\rho_*(a)
\le \mathrm{Ric}_C(Y,Y) + \beta(a)\nabla^2 f(U^*)(Y,Y)
\le k_{\max} - \beta(a)\lambda.
\]

As a→0, β(a)→∞, so
\[
\lim_{a\to 0} \rho_*(a) \le k_{\max} - \infty\cdot\lambda = -\infty.
\]

**Theorem (Global BE Curvature Diverges to −∞).**  
Along the physical asymptotically free trajectory β(a), the global
Bakry–Émery constant satisfies
\[
\lim_{a\to 0} \rho_*(a) = -\infty.
\]

So a **global** CD(ρ,∞) bound with fixed ρ>0 cannot hold in the
continuum limit; one needs a **localized / renormalized** curvature
framework (e.g. restricted to a “typical set” of configurations). :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}
```

## `07_transfer_matrix_gap.md`

```md
# Strong-Coupling Transfer-Matrix Mass Gap

We give a Hamiltonian proof of a mass gap in the strong-coupling regime
(using anisotropic lattice and transfer matrix). 

## 1. Anisotropic Action and Transfer Matrix

Consider SU(N) lattice gauge theory with anisotropic Wilson action
\[
S(U) =
\beta_s \sum_{p\,\text{spatial}}\Big(1 - \tfrac1N\Re\mathrm{Tr}U_p\Big)
+ \beta_t \sum_{p\,\text{temporal}}\Big(1 - \tfrac1N\Re\mathrm{Tr}U_p\Big),
\]
with small temporal coupling β_t. The transfer matrix is
\[
T = e^{-a_t H},
\]
where H is the Hamiltonian and a_t the temporal lattice spacing.

The mass gap is
\[
\Delta = E_1 - E_0 = -\frac{1}{a_t} \log\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_0},
\]
where λ₀>λ₁ are the largest eigenvalues of T.

## 2. Wilson Loop Correlator Bounds

Let O be a nontrivial Wilson loop (e.g. Polyakov loop or spatial
Wilson loop) of minimal length L. Consider the correlator
\[
G(T) = \langle O(0) O(T)\rangle.
\]

**Strong-coupling expansion.**  
In the small-β_t regime, nonzero contributions to G(T) come from worldsheets
of temporal plaquettes connecting O(0) to O(T). Any such surface uses at
least \(L\cdot(T/a_t)\) temporal plaquettes in a nontrivial representation,
each contributing a factor ≲ cβ_t. Thus
\[
|G(T)| \le C'(\beta_s,\Lambda_s) (c\beta_t)^{L T/a_t}
\]
for some c,C'>0.

**Spectral representation.**  
Conversely,
\[
G(T) = \sum_n a_n e^{-(E_n-E_0)T},
\]
and a suitable choice of O ensures a₁>0 and hence
\[
G(T) \ge a_1 e^{-\Delta T}.
\]

Combining:
\[
a_1 e^{-\Delta T}
\le C' (c\beta_t)^{L T/a_t}.
\]

Taking logs, dividing by T, and letting T→∞ yields
\[
-\Delta \le \frac{L}{a_t} \log(c\beta_t)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\Delta \ge \frac{L}{a_t}|\log(c\beta_t)| > 0
\]
for sufficiently small β_t.
```

```md
# Reducible Configurations and Polarity

We address the “singular” parts of the configuration space arising from
reducible gauge configurations and show they are polar (capacity zero)
for the Yang–Mills Dirichlet form. 

## 1. Reducible Configurations

**Definition (Reducible configuration).**  
A configuration U∈𝒞 is reducible if there exists a nontrivial proper
subspace W⊂ℂ^N preserved by all lattice holonomies; equivalently, the
holonomy group lies in a proper block-diagonal subgroup of SU(N).
Let Σ⊂𝒞 be the set of reducibles.

Σ is the zero set of a finite family of polynomial equations in the
entries of the link matrices, hence a finite union of real algebraic
subvarieties.

**Lemma 1 (Codimension ≥2).**  
Each such subvariety has real codimension at least 2, so Σ itself has
codimension ≥2 in 𝒞.

*Sketch.* Fix a proper block-diagonal subgroup H⊊SU(N). The condition
“all holonomies lie in H” enforces vanishing of certain off-diagonal
blocks in each link, adding at least one independent constraint per
link beyond gauge freedom. Dimension counting in the Lie algebra
decomposition shows codimension≥2. ∎

## 2. Capacity and Polarity

Let μ_eff be the Yang–Mills measure and let ℰ be the associated
Dirichlet form on L²(μ_eff):
\[
\mathcal{E}(f,f) =
\int_{\mathcal{C}} \|\nabla f\|^2\, d\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}.
\]

**Definition (Capacity).**  
For Borel A⊂𝒞,
\[
\mathrm{Cap}(A) :=
\inf\big\{
  \mathcal{E}(u,u) + \int u^2 d\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}
  : u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}),\, u\ge 1\ \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\text{-a.e. on }A
\big\}.
\]
A set is polar if its capacity is zero.

**Proposition (Polarity of Σ).**  
The reducible locus Σ has zero capacity: Cap(Σ)=0.

*Sketch.* On a compact Riemannian manifold, subsets of codimension≥2
have Hausdorff dimension small enough that their capacity vanishes for
elliptic Dirichlet forms. Since Σ is a finite union of such subvarieties
and the Langevin generator is elliptic, standard potential theory
implies Cap(Σ)=0. ∎

**Corollary (Almost-sure Avoidance).**  
For μ_eff–almost every initial configuration, the Langevin diffusion
never visits Σ. All functional inequalities and spectral properties
proved for 𝒞 \ Σ extend μ_eff–a.e. to the full measure space.

Thus all the mass-gap and convexity arguments can be formulated entirely
on the irreducible sector without loss of generality.
```
