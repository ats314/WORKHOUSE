# Part 11 — Core Curvature Theorem (updated + fully expanded, self‑contained)

This part is the geometric “hinge” in your program: it proves that **near the trivial lattice connection** the **Bakry–Émery tensor** of the finite‑volume lattice Yang–Mills Gibbs measure has a **strictly positive lower bound** in the **physical (horizontal)** directions, uniformly in the volume. This is the local curvature input that later feeds the local‑to‑global Poincaré / log‑Sobolev machinery.

The draft Part 11 already states this as Lemma 11.1 + Theorem 11.3 and explains the continuity argument and the role of gauge‑invariant gradients . Here I rewrite the whole part from first principles and expand every proof. I also include (as an optional strengthening) the “heat‑kernel curvature bound” mechanism that appears in the UNIFIED notes .

## 11.0 Standing data, notation, and what we will prove

### 11.0.1 Lattice combinatorics (finite volume)

Fix a **finite lattice** (\Lambda). Concretely you can take a finite box in (\mathbb Z^d) with periodic or fixed boundary conditions, but the proofs below only use:

* a finite vertex set (V(\Lambda)),
* a finite oriented edge set (E(\Lambda)),
* a finite oriented plaquette (2‑cell) set (P(\Lambda)),
* each plaquette boundary (\partial p) consists of finitely many edges (for a square plaquette: 4 edges),
* **bounded local degree:** there is a constant
  [
  \nu := \sup_{e\in E(\Lambda)} #{p\in P(\Lambda): e\in \partial p}
  ]
  that depends only on the lattice dimension/local geometry, not on (|\Lambda|).
  For the standard (d)-dimensional hypercubic lattice, (\nu = 2(d-1)); in particular in (d=4) one has (\nu=6).

This “(\nu) is volume‑independent” is the only place where lattice structure enters the uniformity.

### 11.0.2 Group geometry

Let (G) be a **compact, connected Lie group** equipped with a **bi‑invariant Riemannian metric** (g_G). The Part‑11 draft assumes a **uniform Ricci lower bound**
[
\operatorname{Ric}_G ;\ge; \kappa_G, g_G,
\qquad \kappa_G>0,
\tag{11.0}
]
which is precisely the hypothesis used in Theorem 11.3 .
Below I derive the standard curvature formulas for bi‑invariant metrics and explain when (\kappa_G>0) holds.

### 11.0.3 Configuration manifold, gauge symmetry, and effective action

For each finite lattice (\Lambda), define the configuration manifold
[
M_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)}.
\tag{11.1}
]
Equip it with the **product metric**
[
g_\Lambda := \bigoplus_{e\in E(\Lambda)} g_G
\tag{11.2}
]
and product Riemannian volume (d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}).

The **lattice gauge group** is
[
\mathcal G_\Lambda := G^{V(\Lambda)}
\tag{11.3}
]
acting by
[
(g\cdot U)*e := g*{s(e)}, U_e, g_{t(e)}^{-1},
\qquad g\in \mathcal G_\Lambda,\ U\in M_\Lambda,
\tag{11.4}
]
where (s(e)) and (t(e)) are the source/target vertices of the oriented edge (e).

We consider effective actions of the form
[
S_\Lambda = S_W + S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda},
\tag{11.5}
]
with the three hypotheses stated in the Part‑11 draft:

1. (S_W) = Wilson plaquette action (smooth, gauge‑invariant, local),
2. (S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}) smooth, gauge‑invariant, local, and **uniformly Hessian‑bounded below**
   [
   \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U) ;\ge; -C_{\mathrm{add}}, g_\Lambda(U)
   \quad \forall U\in M_\Lambda,
   \tag{11.6}
   ]
   with (C_{\mathrm{add}}\ge 0) independent of (\Lambda),
3. strict “curvature margin”
   [
   C_{\mathrm{add}} < \kappa_G.
   \tag{11.7}
   ]
   These are exactly the assumptions listed in §11.1.3 of the draft  and Theorem 11.3 .

The corresponding Gibbs measure is
[
d\mu_\Lambda(U) := Z_\Lambda^{-1},e^{-S_\Lambda(U)},d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U),
\tag{11.8}
]
as in Theorem 11.3 , and the reversible diffusion generator is
[
L_\Lambda f := \Delta_{g_\Lambda} f - \langle \nabla S_\Lambda, \nabla f\rangle_{g_\Lambda}.
\tag{11.9}
]

### 11.0.4 The target statements (Lemma 11.1 + Theorem 11.3)

Let (U^{(0)}\in M_\Lambda) be the **trivial configuration** (all links equal to the identity) .

We will prove:

* There exists (r>0) and (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}>0), both **independent of (\Lambda)**, such that on the ball
  [
  B_r(U^{(0)}) := {U\in M_\Lambda:\ d_{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)})\le r}
  \tag{11.10}
  ]
  one has a **horizontal** Bakry–Émery curvature lower bound
  [
  \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)(v,v)\ \ge\ \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2
  \quad \forall U\in B_r(U^{(0)}),\ \forall v\in H_U,
  \tag{11.11}
  ]
  where (H_U) is the horizontal subspace (orthogonal complement to gauge orbit tangent space).

* Equivalently, for gauge‑invariant observables (f), one has a local curvature‑dimension inequality
  [
  \Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(U)\ \ge\ \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U)
  \quad \forall U\in B_r(U^{(0)}),
  \tag{11.12}
  ]
  matching Definition 11.2 and Theorem 11.3 in the draft .

## 11.1 Geometry of (M_\Lambda) and gauge orbits

### 11.1.1 Differential geometry of a bi‑invariant metric on (G)

Let (\mathfrak g := T_eG) be the Lie algebra. A bi‑invariant metric (g_G) corresponds to an (\mathrm{Ad})-invariant inner product (\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle) on (\mathfrak g). We use the standard identification of tangent spaces via left translation:
[
T_g G \cong \mathfrak g,\qquad v\in T_gG \mapsto (L_{g^{-1}})_* v\in\mathfrak g.
]

#### Lemma 11.1.1 (Levi‑Civita connection for bi‑invariant metrics)

If (X,Y\in\mathfrak g) and (X^L,Y^L) are the corresponding **left‑invariant** vector fields on (G), then
[
\nabla_{X^L} Y^L ;=; \tfrac12 [X,Y]^L.
\tag{11.13}
]

**Proof.** Use the Koszul formula for the Levi‑Civita connection:
[
2\langle \nabla_U V, W\rangle
= U\langle V,W\rangle + V\langle W,U\rangle - W\langle U,V\rangle

* \langle[U,V],W\rangle - \langle[V,W],U\rangle - \langle[W,U],V\rangle.
  ]
  Take (U=X^L), (V=Y^L), (W=Z^L), all left‑invariant. Then the metric pairings (\langle Y^L,Z^L\rangle) are constant (left‑invariance), so the first three derivative terms vanish. Also ([X^L,Y^L]=[X,Y]^L). Hence
  [
  2\langle \nabla_{X^L}Y^L, Z^L\rangle
  = \langle[X,Y]^L,Z^L\rangle - \langle[Y,Z]^L,X^L\rangle - \langle[Z,X]^L,Y^L\rangle.
  ]
  Using (\mathrm{Ad})-invariance of (\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle) (equivalently (\langle [A,B],C\rangle = -\langle B,[A,C]\rangle)), the last two terms combine to (\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle). Thus
  [
  2\langle \nabla_{X^L}Y^L, Z^L\rangle = \langle[X,Y],Z\rangle,
  ]
  for all (Z), which gives (\nabla_{X^L}Y^L = \frac12 [X,Y]^L). ∎

#### Lemma 11.1.2 (Curvature tensor for bi‑invariant metrics)

For (X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak g),
[
R(X^L,Y^L)Z^L ;=; -\tfrac14 [[X,Y],Z]^L.
\tag{11.14}
]

**Proof.** By definition,
[
R(U,V)W = \nabla_U\nabla_V W - \nabla_V\nabla_U W - \nabla_{[U,V]}W.
]
Apply Lemma 11.1.1:

* (\nabla_{Y^L} Z^L = \tfrac12[Y,Z]^L), then (\nabla_{X^L}\nabla_{Y^L}Z^L = \tfrac14 [X,[Y,Z]]^L).
* Symmetrically (\nabla_{Y^L}\nabla_{X^L}Z^L = \tfrac14 [Y,[X,Z]]^L).
* ([X^L,Y^L]=[X,Y]^L), so (\nabla_{[X^L,Y^L]}Z^L = \tfrac12[[X,Y],Z]^L).

Therefore
[
R(X^L,Y^L)Z^L
= \tfrac14\big([X,[Y,Z]] - [Y,[X,Z]]\big)^L - \tfrac12[[X,Y],Z]^L.
]
By the Jacobi identity, ([X,[Y,Z]] - [Y,[X,Z]] = [[X,Y],Z]). Hence
[
R(X^L,Y^L)Z^L = \Big(\tfrac14 - \tfrac12\Big)[[X,Y],Z]^L = -\tfrac14[[X,Y],Z]^L.
]
∎

#### Lemma 11.1.3 (Ricci curvature formula)

Let ({e_i}_{i=1}^{\dim G}) be an orthonormal basis of (\mathfrak g). Then for (X\in\mathfrak g),
[
\operatorname{Ric}*G(X,X) ;=; \tfrac14\sum*{i=1}^{\dim G} |[X,e_i]|^2.
\tag{11.15}
]

**Proof.** Using Lemma 11.1.2 and the definition of Ricci as a trace,
[
\operatorname{Ric}_G(X,X) = \sum_i \langle R(X,e_i)e_i, X\rangle.
]
Now (R(X,e_i)e_i = -\tfrac14[[X,e_i],e_i]). By (\mathrm{Ad})-invariance,
[
\langle [[X,e_i],e_i],X\rangle
= -\langle [X,e_i],[X,e_i]\rangle
= -|[X,e_i]|^2.
]
Hence
[
\langle R(X,e_i)e_i, X\rangle
= -\tfrac14(-|[X,e_i]|^2) = \tfrac14|[X,e_i]|^2,
]
and summing gives (11.15). ∎

**Consequences.**

* (\operatorname{Ric}_G\ge 0) for any compact group with bi‑invariant metric.
* (\operatorname{Ric}*G) is strictly positive in every direction iff (G) has no flat (torus/central) directions. For a compact **semisimple** group (e.g. (SU(N))), one indeed has a positive lower bound (\kappa_G>0) as assumed in Theorem 11.3 . One convenient choice is
  [
  \kappa_G := \inf*{|X|=1} \operatorname{Ric}_G(X,X),
  ]
  which is (>0) because the unit sphere is compact and the expression (11.15) is continuous and strictly positive on that sphere for semisimple (\mathfrak g).

### 11.1.2 Product configuration manifold (M_\Lambda=G^{E(\Lambda)})

A point (U\in M_\Lambda) is (U=(U_e)*{e\in E(\Lambda)}). A tangent vector (v\in T_U M*\Lambda) decomposes as (v=(v_e)*{e\in E(\Lambda)}) with (v_e\in T*{U_e}G).

#### Lemma 11.1.4 (Product Ricci lower bound)

Assume (\operatorname{Ric}*G\ge \kappa_G g_G) on (G). Then on ((M*\Lambda,g_\Lambda)),
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda}\ \ge\ \kappa_G, g_\Lambda,
\tag{11.16}
]
with the **same** (\kappa_G), hence independent of (\Lambda). This is the “product Ricci lower bound” used in the draft .

**Proof.** For a Riemannian product (M=M_1\times\cdots\times M_n), the Levi‑Civita connection splits and the Ricci tensor is the direct sum of the factor Ricci tensors. Concretely here:
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda}(v,v) = \sum_{e\in E(\Lambda)} \operatorname{Ric}*G(v_e,v_e)
\ \ge\ \sum*{e} \kappa_G |v_e|^2
= \kappa_G |v|*{g*\Lambda}^2.
]
∎

In the draft you sometimes state the stronger identity (\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda}=\kappa_G g_\Lambda) ; this holds when (G) itself is Einstein with (\operatorname{Ric}_G=\kappa_G g_G) (e.g. (G) compact simple with metric proportional to (-)Killing).

### 11.1.3 Gauge group action, vertical/horizontal spaces, and gauge‑invariant gradients

#### Definition 11.1 (Gauge action)

Define (\mathcal G_\Lambda := G^{V(\Lambda)}) and the action (g\cdot U) by (11.4). This is the standard lattice gauge action.

#### Lemma 11.1.5 (Gauge action is isometric)

The action of (\mathcal G_\Lambda) on ((M_\Lambda,g_\Lambda)) is by isometries.

**Proof.** Fix (g\in\mathcal G_\Lambda). For each edge (e), the map (U_e\mapsto g_{s(e)}U_eg_{t(e)}^{-1}) is a composition of a left translation and a right translation on (G). Because (g_G) is **bi‑invariant**, both left and right translations are isometries; hence each coordinate map is an isometry and so is the product map on (M_\Lambda). ∎

#### Definition 11.2 (Vertical and horizontal subspaces)

Fix (U\in M_\Lambda). The **gauge orbit** through (U) is (\mathcal O_U := {g\cdot U:\ g\in\mathcal G_\Lambda}). Define:

* the **vertical subspace**
  [
  V_U := T_U \mathcal O_U \subset T_U M_\Lambda;
  \tag{11.17}
  ]
* the **horizontal subspace** as orthogonal complement
  [
  H_U := V_U^\perp \subset T_U M_\Lambda.
  \tag{11.18}
  ]
  This agrees with the repeated definition across the draft (e.g. Part 14) .

#### Definition 11.3 (Gauge‑invariant observables)

Let
[
\mathcal A_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}
:= {f\in C^\infty(M_\Lambda): f(g\cdot U)=f(U)\ \forall g\in\mathcal G_\Lambda,\ \forall U\in M_\Lambda},
\tag{11.19}
]
as in the draft .

#### Lemma 11.1.6 (Gradients of gauge‑invariant functions are horizontal)

If (f\in\mathcal A_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}), then
[
\nabla f(U) \in H_U\quad \forall U\in M_\Lambda.
\tag{11.20}
]
This is stated in the draft as a key geometric fact  and is used throughout Parts 11–14 .

**Proof.** Fix (U\in M_\Lambda) and any vertical vector (v\in V_U). By definition of (V_U), there exists a smooth path (g(t)\in\mathcal G_\Lambda) with (g(0)=\mathrm{id}) such that (v = \frac{d}{dt}\big|*{0} g(t)\cdot U). Since (f) is gauge‑invariant,
[
f(g(t)\cdot U) = f(U)\quad \forall t,
]
hence differentiating at (t=0) gives
[
df_U(v) = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|*{0} f(g(t)\cdot U) = 0.
]
But the Riemannian gradient is characterized by (df_U(w)=\langle \nabla f(U), w\rangle_{g_\Lambda}) for all (w\in T_U M_\Lambda). Thus (\langle \nabla f(U), v\rangle_{g_\Lambda}=0) for all (v\in V_U), i.e. (\nabla f(U)\in V_U^\perp = H_U). ∎

## 11.2 Bakry–Émery calculus from first principles

This section expands the “Bochner–Bakry–Émery identity” step that the draft uses in one line .

### 11.2.1 Carré du champ (\Gamma) and (\Gamma_2)

Let ((M,g)) be any smooth Riemannian manifold and let (\mu) be a Gibbs measure
[
d\mu = Z^{-1}e^{-S},d\mathrm{vol}_g
]
with (S\in C^2(M)). Consider the diffusion generator
[
Lf = \Delta_g f - \langle \nabla S, \nabla f\rangle_g.
]

#### Definition 11.4 (Carré du champ)

For smooth (f,g), define
[
\Gamma(f,g) := \tfrac12\big(L(fg) - f,Lg - g,Lf\big), \qquad \Gamma(f):=\Gamma(f,f).
\tag{11.21}
]

#### Lemma 11.2.1 ((\Gamma(f,g)=\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle))

For (L=\Delta_g - \langle\nabla S,\nabla\cdot\rangle) as above,
[
\Gamma(f,g) = \langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle_g.
\tag{11.22}
]

**Proof.** Use the product rule for the Laplacian and the Leibniz rule for directional derivatives:
[
\Delta_g(fg) = f\Delta_g g + g\Delta_g f + 2\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle_g,
]
and
[
\langle\nabla S,\nabla(fg)\rangle = f\langle\nabla S,\nabla g\rangle + g\langle\nabla S,\nabla f\rangle.
]
Subtracting (fLg+gLf) from (L(fg)) cancels the drift terms and leaves (2\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle), hence (11.22). ∎

#### Definition 11.5 (Iterated carré du champ)

Define
[
\Gamma_2(f,g) := \tfrac12\Big(L\Gamma(f,g) - \Gamma(f,Lg) - \Gamma(g,Lf)\Big),\qquad \Gamma_2(f):=\Gamma_2(f,f).
\tag{11.23}
]

### 11.2.2 The Bakry–Émery tensor and Bochner identity

#### Definition 11.6 (Bakry–Émery Ricci tensor)

Define the (weighted) Bakry–Émery tensor
[
\operatorname{Ric}*\mu := \operatorname{Ric}*g + \nabla^2 S,
\tag{11.24}
]
matching the draft’s formula (\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}=\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda}+\nabla^2S_\Lambda) .

Here (\nabla^2 S) is the Riemannian Hessian: ((\nabla^2 S)(X,Y):= \langle \nabla_X\nabla S, Y\rangle).

#### Theorem 11.2.2 (Bochner–Bakry–Émery identity)

For all smooth (f),
[
\Gamma_2(f)
= |\nabla^2 f|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + \operatorname{Ric}*\mu(\nabla f,\nabla f),
\tag{11.25}
]
where (|\nabla^2 f|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2) is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm square of the Hessian.

**Proof (expanded).**
First recall the classical Bochner formula on a Riemannian manifold:
[
\tfrac12 \Delta_g |\nabla f|^2
= |\nabla^2 f|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + \langle \nabla f, \nabla(\Delta_g f)\rangle + \operatorname{Ric}_g(\nabla f,\nabla f).
\tag{11.26}
]
This is a standard identity; but we can derive it quickly in a normal coordinate chart at a point (x\in M):

1. Choose local orthonormal frame ({e_i}) geodesic at (x), so ((\nabla_{e_i}e_j)(x)=0).
2. Write (\nabla f = \sum_i (e_i f)e_i).
3. Compute derivatives at (x) using the vanishing of Christoffel symbols at (x), while curvature enters via commutators (\nabla_{e_i}\nabla_{e_j}-\nabla_{e_j}\nabla_{e_i}) acting on vector fields.
4. The resulting trace identity yields (11.26).

Now incorporate the drift term in (L=\Delta_g - \langle\nabla S,\nabla\cdot\rangle).

Compute (\Gamma_2(f)=\frac12(L|\nabla f|^2 - 2\langle\nabla f,\nabla Lf\rangle)) using (11.22) and (11.23). Indeed,
[
\Gamma_2(f)=\tfrac12\big(L\Gamma(f) - 2\Gamma(f,Lf)\big)
= \tfrac12\big(L|\nabla f|^2 - 2\langle\nabla f,\nabla Lf\rangle\big).
\tag{11.27}
]
Expand (L|\nabla f|^2 = \Delta_g|\nabla f|^2 - \langle\nabla S,\nabla|\nabla f|^2\rangle). Using (11.26) for the (\Delta_g) term gives
[
\tfrac12\Delta_g|\nabla f|^2
= |\nabla^2 f|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + \langle\nabla f,\nabla\Delta_g f\rangle + \operatorname{Ric}*g(\nabla f,\nabla f).
]
Also (\nabla Lf = \nabla\Delta_g f - \nabla\langle\nabla S,\nabla f\rangle). Insert these into (11.27):
[
\Gamma_2(f)
= |\nabla^2 f|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + \operatorname{Ric}_g(\nabla f,\nabla f)

* \underbrace{\langle\nabla f,\nabla\Delta_g f\rangle - \langle\nabla f,\nabla\Delta_g f\rangle}_{\text{cancels}}
* \tfrac12\Big(-\langle\nabla S,\nabla|\nabla f|^2\rangle + 2\langle\nabla f,\nabla\langle\nabla S,\nabla f\rangle\rangle\Big).
  ]
  So it remains to simplify the drift correction:
  [
  \mathcal D := \tfrac12\Big(-\langle\nabla S,\nabla|\nabla f|^2\rangle + 2\langle\nabla f,\nabla\langle\nabla S,\nabla f\rangle\rangle\Big).
  \tag{11.28}
  ]
  Use the identity (\nabla|\nabla f|^2 = 2\nabla^2 f(\nabla f,\cdot)) (a standard differential consequence of ( |\nabla f|^2 = \langle\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle)). Then
  [
  \langle\nabla S,\nabla|\nabla f|^2\rangle
  = 2\nabla^2 f(\nabla f,\nabla S).
  \tag{11.29}
  ]
  On the other hand, expand
  [
  \langle\nabla f,\nabla\langle\nabla S,\nabla f\rangle\rangle
  = \nabla^2 S(\nabla f,\nabla f) + \nabla^2 f(\nabla f,\nabla S),
  \tag{11.30}
  ]
  which is a product rule for covariant derivatives:
  (\nabla\langle A,B\rangle = \langle\nabla A,B\rangle + \langle A,\nabla B\rangle) applied to (A=\nabla S), (B=\nabla f).

Plugging (11.29)–(11.30) into (11.28) gives
[
\mathcal D
= \tfrac12\Big(-2\nabla^2 f(\nabla f,\nabla S) + 2\big(\nabla^2 S(\nabla f,\nabla f) + \nabla^2 f(\nabla f,\nabla S)\big)\Big)
= \nabla^2 S(\nabla f,\nabla f).
]
Therefore
[
\Gamma_2(f) = |\nabla^2 f|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + \big(\operatorname{Ric}*g+\nabla^2 S\big)(\nabla f,\nabla f)
= |\nabla^2 f|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + \operatorname{Ric}*\mu(\nabla f,\nabla f),
]
which is (11.25). ∎

#### Corollary 11.2.3 (Curvature–dimension (CD(\rho,\infty)))

If (\operatorname{Ric}_\mu \ge \rho g) as quadratic forms, then
[
\Gamma_2(f)\ \ge\ \rho,\Gamma(f)\qquad \forall f\in C^\infty(M).
\tag{11.31}
]
This is exactly the basic “Ricci lower bound (\Rightarrow CD(\rho,\infty))” engine emphasized in the early strategy part .

## 11.3 Pointwise Bakry–Émery curvature bound at the vacuum (U^{(0)})

The Part‑11 draft states the pointwise bound schematically and attributes the Wilson Hessian positivity to the “Hodge‑type decomposition” developed earlier . Here is the full derivation.

### 11.3.1 Bakry–Émery tensor for lattice YM

By Definition (11.24), on ((M_\Lambda,g_\Lambda)) with potential (S_\Lambda),
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}
= \operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} + \nabla^2 S_\Lambda
= \operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} + \nabla^2 S_W + \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda},
\tag{11.32}
]
matching the draft .

By Lemma 11.1.4,
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} \ge \kappa_G g_\Lambda
\tag{11.33}
]
uniformly in (\Lambda).

By assumption (11.6),
[
\nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U) \ge -C_{\mathrm{add}} g_\Lambda(U)
\qquad \forall U.
\tag{11.34}
]

So the only nontrivial part is understanding (\nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})) and its sign.

### 11.3.2 Hessian of the Wilson plaquette action at (U^{(0)})

I will derive the standard discrete‑Maxwell form of the Wilson Hessian at the trivial configuration—the statement summarized in your Part 9: (\nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)}) = 2c_W, d_1^*d_1) .

#### 11.3.2.1 Linearizing (M_\Lambda) at (U^{(0)})

At (U^{(0)}), each link variable is (e\in G), so
[
T_{U^{(0)}}M_\Lambda \cong \bigoplus_{e\in E(\Lambda)} T_eG \cong \bigoplus_{e\in E(\Lambda)}\mathfrak g.
\tag{11.35}
]
Thus a tangent vector (A\in T_{U^{(0)}}M_\Lambda) can be viewed as a (\mathfrak g)-valued 1‑cochain:
[
A = (A_e)_{e\in E(\Lambda)},\qquad A_e\in\mathfrak g.
]
This is exactly the “linearization = (\mathcal C^1(\Lambda;\mathfrak g))” summary in Part 9 .

The product metric gives the inner product
[
\langle A,B\rangle := \sum_{e\in E(\Lambda)} \langle A_e, B_e\rangle_{\mathfrak g},
\qquad |A|^2=\langle A,A\rangle.
\tag{11.36}
]

#### 11.3.2.2 Discrete exterior derivative (d_1)

Fix an orientation convention for plaquettes. For each plaquette (p), its boundary is a signed sum of oriented edges:
[
\partial p = \sum_{e\in E(\Lambda)} \sigma_{p,e}, e,
\qquad \sigma_{p,e}\in{-1,0,+1},
\tag{11.37}
]
where (\sigma_{p,e}=0) unless (e) is in the boundary; for a square plaquette, exactly 4 coefficients are nonzero.

Define the discrete coboundary (exterior derivative)
[
(d_1 A)*p := \sum*{e} \sigma_{p,e} A_e \in \mathfrak g.
\tag{11.38}
]
This is the usual (d_1:\mathcal C^1\to\mathcal C^2).

Define the inner product on 2‑cochains by
[
\langle F,G\rangle_{2} := \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \langle F_p,G_p\rangle_{\mathfrak g}.
\tag{11.39}
]
Then (d_1^*) is the adjoint defined by
[
\langle d_1A, F\rangle_2 = \langle A, d_1^*F\rangle_1.
\tag{11.40}
]

#### 11.3.2.3 Quadratic expansion of Wilson action

Write a nearby configuration in exponential coordinates:
[
U_e(\varepsilon) := \exp(\varepsilon A_e),
\tag{11.41}
]
for a fixed (A=(A_e)) and small (\varepsilon).

For each plaquette (p), define the plaquette holonomy (U_p(\varepsilon)) as the ordered product around the boundary. In a square plaquette case, for example,
[
U_p(\varepsilon) = U_{e_1}(\varepsilon),U_{e_2}(\varepsilon),U_{e_3}(\varepsilon)^{-1},U_{e_4}(\varepsilon)^{-1}
\tag{11.42}
]
with appropriate orientation.

Using the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula, one has the standard linearization:
[
U_p(\varepsilon) = \exp\Big(\varepsilon (d_1A)_p + O(\varepsilon^2)\Big).
\tag{11.43}
]
(Reason: the first‑order term in the product is exactly the signed sum of the edge Lie algebra elements.)

Now fix the Wilson plaquette potential (\Phi:G\to\mathbb R). For (SU(N)) one commonly takes (\Phi(U)=\frac{\beta}{N}\mathrm{Re,Tr}(I-U)). The only structural property we need is:

* (\Phi) is smooth and conjugation‑invariant,
* (\Phi(e)=0) and (e) is a minimum,
* the second derivative (D^2\Phi(e)) is a positive multiple of (\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle) on (\mathfrak g).

Equivalently, there is a constant (c_W>0) such that for (X\in\mathfrak g),
[
\Phi(\exp X) = \tfrac{c_W}{2}|X|^2 + O(|X|^3).
\tag{11.44}
]

Define the Wilson action
[
S_W(U) := \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \Phi(U_p).
\tag{11.45}
]

Plug (11.43) into (11.44):
[
\Phi(U_p(\varepsilon))
= \Phi\Big(\exp(\varepsilon(d_1A)_p + O(\varepsilon^2))\Big)
= \tfrac{c_W}{2}\varepsilon^2 |(d_1A)*p|^2 + O(\varepsilon^3).
]
Summing over plaquettes,
[
S_W(U(\varepsilon))
= \tfrac{c_W}{2}\varepsilon^2 \sum*{p\in P(\Lambda)} |(d_1A)_p|^2 + O(\varepsilon^3)
= \tfrac{c_W}{2}\varepsilon^2 |d_1A|_2^2 + O(\varepsilon^3).
\tag{11.46}
]

Therefore, the Hessian at (\varepsilon=0) (i.e. at (U^{(0)})) is:
[
\nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})(A,A)
= \frac{d^2}{d\varepsilon^2}\Big|_{0} S_W(U(\varepsilon))
= c_W |d_1A|_2^2.
\tag{11.47}
]

Since (|d_1A|_2^2=\langle d_1A,d_1A\rangle_2=\langle A,d_1^*d_1A\rangle_1), we can rewrite (11.47) as the operator identity
[
\nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)}) = 2c_W, d_1^*d_1
\quad\text{(as a bilinear form)}.
\tag{11.48}
]
This matches the “Hessian = discrete Maxwell” summary in Part 9 .

#### Corollary 11.3.1 (Nonnegativity and kernel)

For all (A\in T_{U^{(0)}}M_\Lambda),
[
\nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})(A,A) = c_W|d_1A|_2^2 \ge 0,
\tag{11.49}
]
and the kernel is
[
\ker\nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)}) = {A:\ d_1A=0}=\ker(d_1),
\tag{11.50}
]
the space of discrete closed 1‑forms, as in the Part‑9 summary .

### 11.3.3 Horizontal restriction and the Hodge/gauge decomposition (at (U^{(0)}))

This subsection is not strictly needed for the curvature bound (since we already have nonnegativity on all directions), but the draft explicitly references “horizontal/co‑exact physical modes” and the Hodge decomposition , so I include it.

At the vacuum (U^{(0)}), the gauge action linearizes to the discrete gradient map (d_0):

* A gauge parameter is a 0‑cochain (\phi=(\phi_x)_{x\in V(\Lambda)}\in \mathcal C^0(\Lambda;\mathfrak g)).
* The induced infinitesimal change on an oriented edge (e:x\to y) is
  [
  (d_0\phi)*e := \phi_x - \phi_y.
  \tag{11.51}
  ]
  Thus the **vertical subspace** at (U^{(0)}) is
  [
  V*{U^{(0)}} = \mathrm{im}(d_0).
  \tag{11.52}
  ]
  The horizontal subspace is (H_{U^{(0)}} = (\mathrm{im}(d_0))^\perp = \ker(d_0^*)).

Define the discrete Laplacians
[
\Delta_1 := d_0d_0^* + d_1^*d_1.
\tag{11.53}
]
Then one has the standard Hodge decomposition
[
\mathcal C^1
= \mathrm{im}(d_0)\ \oplus\ \ker(\Delta_1)\ \oplus\ \mathrm{im}(d_1^*),
\tag{11.54}
]
and correspondingly the “physical/horizontal” space can be identified as
[
H_{U^{(0)}} = \ker(\Delta_1)\ \oplus\ \mathrm{im}(d_1^*),
\tag{11.55}
]
which is precisely what Part 9 records .

On (\mathrm{im}(d_1^*)) (co‑exact modes), (d_1^*d_1) is strictly positive (no kernel), while (\ker(\Delta_1)) is “harmonic” and sits in the kernel of (d_1), hence in the kernel of the Wilson Hessian. That is the rigorous version of the draft’s statement “nonnegative on horizontal, strictly positive on co‑exact, zero only on harmonic” .

### 11.3.4 Pointwise Bakry–Émery bound at (U^{(0)})

Define
[
\rho_0 := \kappa_G - C_{\mathrm{add}} > 0,
\tag{11.56}
]
as in the draft’s Lemma 11.1 discussion .

#### Proposition 11.3 (Pointwise horizontal curvature at the vacuum)

For every (v\in H_{U^{(0)}}),
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v)
\ \ge\ \rho_0,|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2.
\tag{11.57}
]
This is the same statement as “Proposition 10.2” in Part 10  and the §11.2 discussion of Part 11 .

**Proof.** Using (11.32),
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v)
= \operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v)

* \nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})(v,v)
* \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v).
  ]
  Now apply:

- product Ricci lower bound (11.33): (\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v)\ge \kappa_G|v|^2),
- Wilson Hessian nonnegativity (11.49): (\nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})(v,v)\ge 0),
- Hessian bound (11.34): (\nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v)\ge -C_{\mathrm{add}}|v|^2).

Hence
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v)
\ge (\kappa_G - C_{\mathrm{add}})|v|^2
= \rho_0 |v|^2.
]
∎

**Remark (analogy with the scalar convex case).**
In the convex scalar prototype, (\mathrm{Ric}*g\equiv 0) and a uniform Hessian lower bound (\nabla^2S\ge m_0^2 I) gives (CD(m_0^2,\infty)) globally . Here, the group geometry supplies a built‑in positive Ricci term (\kappa_G) which plays the same “mass‑like” role, while (C*{\mathrm{add}}) measures possible negative curvature from extra local terms, exactly as noted in Part 10 .

## 11.4 Uniform extension to a small‑field ball (Lemma 11.1, expanded)

The draft proves Lemma 11.1 by a continuity/compactness argument . That is correct, but if we want the **volume‑independence** to be completely transparent, it is helpful to give an explicit uniform modulus of continuity for the Wilson Hessian using locality.

I will prove a slightly stronger version: the curvature lower bound on (B_r(U^{(0)})) holds for **all** tangent vectors, hence in particular for horizontal ones. (This avoids delicate discussion about continuity of (H_U) at singular points—though the draft addresses it on the regular set .)

### 11.4.1 A uniform Lipschitz bound for the Wilson Hessian near (U^{(0)})

We need an estimate of the form
[
\big|\nabla^2 S_W(U) - \nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})\big|*{\mathrm{op}}
\ \le\ L_W, d*{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)}),
\tag{11.58}
]
with (L_W) depending only on (G), the chosen plaquette potential (\Phi), and local lattice constants such as (\nu), but **not** on (|\Lambda|).

#### Lemma 11.4.1 (Uniform operator bound for the third derivative)

There exists a constant (C^{(3)}*W<\infty), depending only on (G) and (\Phi), such that for all finite (\Lambda), all (U\in M*\Lambda), and all tangent vectors (\eta,v,w\in T_U M_\Lambda),
[
\big|\nabla^3 S_W(U)(\eta,v,w)\big|
\ \le\ C^{(3)}_W,\nu^{3/2},|\eta|,|v|,|w|.
\tag{11.59}
]

**Proof.** Write (S_W=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} S_{W,p}), where each (S_{W,p}(U)=\Phi(U_p)) depends only on the finitely many links in (\partial p). Since (\Phi) is smooth on compact (G), all its derivatives are bounded; and since the holonomy map (U\mapsto U_p) is smooth on (G^{\partial p}), the third covariant derivative of each (S_{W,p}) is uniformly bounded (by a constant depending only on (\Phi) and (G), not on (\Lambda)). Thus there is a constant (K_3) such that
[
|\nabla^3 S_{W,p}(U)(\eta,v,w)|
\le K_3,|\eta|*{E(p)},|v|*{E(p)},|w|*{E(p)},
\tag{11.60}
]
where (|\cdot|*{E(p)}) denotes the norm restricted to the edge subset (E(p)=\partial p).

Summing over plaquettes,
[
|\nabla^3 S_W(U)(\eta,v,w)|
\le K_3 \sum_{p} |\eta|*{E(p)},|v|*{E(p)},|w|*{E(p)}.
\tag{11.61}
]
Apply Hölder (Cauchy–Schwarz in triple form):
[
\sum_p a_pb_pc_p \le \Big(\sum_p a_p^2\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_p b_p^2\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_p c_p^2\Big)^{1/2}
]
with (a_p=|\eta|*{E(p)}), etc. So it suffices to bound (\sum_p |\eta|*{E(p)}^2). But
[
\sum*{p}|\eta|*{E(p)}^2
= \sum_p\sum*{e\in E(p)} |\eta_e|^2
= \sum_{e\in E(\Lambda)} \Big(#{p:\ e\in E(p)}\Big),|\eta_e|^2
\le \nu \sum_e |\eta_e|^2
= \nu |\eta|^2.
\tag{11.62}
]
Thus (\sum_p|\eta|_{E(p)}^2\le \nu|\eta|^2), and similarly for (v,w). Insert into (11.61) to get
[
|\nabla^3 S_W(U)(\eta,v,w)|
\le K_3(\nu^{1/2}|\eta|)(\nu^{1/2}|v|)(\nu^{1/2}|w|)
= K_3\nu^{3/2}|\eta||v||w|.
]
Set (C^{(3)}_W:=K_3). ∎

#### Corollary 11.4.2 (Uniform Lipschitz continuity of the Hessian)

For all (U\in M_\Lambda),
[
\big|\nabla^2 S_W(U) - \nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})\big|*{\mathrm{op}}
\le C^{(3)}*W,\nu^{3/2}, d*{g*\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)}).
\tag{11.63}
]

**Proof.** Let (\gamma:[0,1]\to M_\Lambda) be a minimizing geodesic from (U^{(0)}) to (U). Then (|\dot\gamma(t)|\equiv d_{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)})). By the fundamental theorem of calculus for covariant derivatives,
[
\nabla^2 S_W(U) - \nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})
= \int_0^1 \nabla^3 S_W(\gamma(t))[\dot\gamma(t),,\cdot,,\cdot]\ dt.
]
Taking operator norm and using Lemma 11.4.1 with (|\dot\gamma(t)|=d_{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)})) yields (11.63). ∎

Define
[
L_W := C^{(3)}_W,\nu^{3/2}.
\tag{11.64}
]

### 11.4.2 Choosing the radius (r) and obtaining a uniform local curvature lower bound

Recall from §11.3 that (\rho_0=\kappa_G-C_{\mathrm{add}}>0).

Choose
[
r := \min\Big{1,\ \frac{\rho_0}{2L_W}\Big}.
\tag{11.65}
]
(Any small positive choice works; the point is to make (L_W r\le \rho_0/2).)

Then for any (U\in B_r(U^{(0)})) and any (v\in T_U M_\Lambda), we estimate:

1. Ricci term:
   [
   \operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda}(U)(v,v)\ge \kappa_G |v|^2.
   ]
2. Additional term:
   [
   \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U)(v,v)\ge -C_{\mathrm{add}}|v|^2.
   ]
3. Wilson term: by Corollary 11.4.2 and nonnegativity of (\nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})) (Corollary 11.3.1),
   [
   \nabla^2 S_W(U)(v,v)
   \ge \nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})(v,v) - |\nabla^2S_W(U)-\nabla^2S_W(U^{(0)})|*{\mathrm{op}}|v|^2
   \ge -L_W r,|v|^2
   \ge -\tfrac{\rho_0}{2}|v|^2.
   \tag{11.66}
   ]
   Summing:
   [
   \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu_\Lambda}(U)(v,v)
   \ge \kappa_G|v|^2 - C_{\mathrm{add}}|v|^2 - \tfrac{\rho_0}{2}|v|^2
   = \tfrac{\rho_0}{2}|v|^2.
   ]

Define
[
\rho_{\mathrm{loc}} := \frac{\rho_0}{2} = \frac{\kappa_G-C_{\mathrm{add}}}{2},
\tag{11.67}
]
exactly as in the draft .

We have shown:

#### Lemma 11.1 (Uniform local curvature bound; strengthened form)

There exist (r>0) and (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}>0), depending only on (\kappa_G), (C_{\mathrm{add}}), and local lattice structure (through (\nu)), such that for every finite lattice (\Lambda),
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)(v,v)\ \ge\ \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2
\quad \forall U\in B_r(U^{(0)}),\ \forall v\in T_U M_\Lambda.
\tag{11.68}
]
In particular, (11.68) holds for all horizontal vectors (v\in H_U), which is exactly the Lemma 11.1 statement in the draft .

**Proof.** The computation above is the proof. ∎

**Why the constants are independent of (\Lambda).**
Everything entering (r) and (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}) is volume‑independent:

* (\kappa_G) depends only on (G) and (g_G),
* (C_{\mathrm{add}}) is assumed uniform in (\Lambda) ,
* the Wilson third‑derivative constant depends only on (\Phi) and (G),
* (\nu) depends only on local lattice connectivity, not on lattice size.

This makes precise the draft’s statement that “(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}) and (r) are independent of (\Lambda)” .

## 11.5 From local BE curvature to local (CD(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)) for gauge‑invariant observables

The draft packages this as Definition 11.2 + Theorem 11.3(2) . Here is the full derivation.

### 11.5.1 Local horizontal (CD(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty))

Recall Definition 11.2 in the draft:

> The system satisfies local horizontal (CD(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)) on (B_r(U^{(0)})) if
> (\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(U)\ge \rho_{\mathrm{loc}}\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U)) for all gauge‑invariant (f) and all (U\in B_r(U^{(0)})). 

We now prove it.

#### Proposition 11.5.1 (Local (CD(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)) on (B_r(U^{(0)})))

Assume the hypotheses of Lemma 11.1. Then for every (f\in\mathcal A_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) and every (U\in B_r(U^{(0)})),
[
\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(U)\ \ge\ \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U).
\tag{11.69}
]

**Proof.** Fix (f\in\mathcal A_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) and (U\in B_r(U^{(0)})).

1. By Lemma 11.1.6, (\nabla f(U)\in H_U) (horizontal) .
2. By Theorem 11.2.2 (Bochner–Bakry–Émery),
   [
   \Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(U)
   = |\nabla^2 f(U)|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu_\Lambda}(U)(\nabla f(U),\nabla f(U)).
   ]
3. The first term is nonnegative; the second term is bounded below by Lemma 11.1:
   [
   \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)(\nabla f,\nabla f)\ \ge\ \rho_{\mathrm{loc}}|\nabla f|^2
   =\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U).
   ]
   Thus (\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(U)\ge \rho_{\mathrm{loc}}\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U)). ∎

So Definition 11.2 holds with (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}) independent of (\Lambda), exactly as stated in the draft .

## 11.6 The Core Curvature Theorem (Theorem 11.3, now with full proof)

I restate the theorem in essentially the same form as the draft , but the proof is now the combination of the fully expanded arguments above.

### Theorem 11.3 (Core curvature theorem for lattice Yang–Mills)

Let (G) be a compact, connected Lie group equipped with a bi‑invariant Riemannian metric (g_G) such that (\operatorname{Ric}*G\ge \kappa_G g_G) with (\kappa_G>0). For each finite lattice (\Lambda), let (M*\Lambda=G^{E(\Lambda)}) be the configuration manifold with product metric (g_\Lambda) and product Haar volume, and let (\mathcal G_\Lambda=G^{V(\Lambda)}) act by lattice gauge transformations.

Consider effective actions (S_\Lambda=S_W+S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}) satisfying:

1. (S_W) is the Wilson plaquette action, smooth and gauge‑invariant.
2. (S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}) is smooth, gauge‑invariant, local, and satisfies (\nabla^2S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U)\ge -C_{\mathrm{add}}g_\Lambda(U)) for all (U), with (C_{\mathrm{add}}) independent of (\Lambda).
3. (C_{\mathrm{add}}<\kappa_G).

Let (d\mu_\Lambda = Z_\Lambda^{-1}e^{-S_\Lambda}d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}) be the Gibbs measure and (L_\Lambda) the associated generator. Let (\mathcal A_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) be the smooth gauge‑invariant observables.

Then there exist constants (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}>0) and (r>0), depending only on (\kappa_G), (C_{\mathrm{add}}), and local lattice structure, such that for every finite lattice (\Lambda):

1. (**Horizontal Bakry–Émery curvature bound.**) For all (U\in M_\Lambda) with (d_{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)})\le r) and all (v\in H_U),
   [
   \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)(v,v)\ \ge\ \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2.
   ]
2. (**Local (CD(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)) for gauge‑invariant observables.**) For all smooth (f\in\mathcal A_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}),
   [
   \Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(U)\ \ge\ \rho_{\mathrm{loc}}\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U)
   \qquad \forall U\in B_r(U^{(0)}).
   ]

Moreover, the constants (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},r) are independent of (\Lambda). This is precisely the statement recorded in the draft .

**Proof.**
Item (1) is Lemma 11.1 (as stated in the draft ), proved in §11.4; it uses: product Ricci lower bound, Wilson Hessian nonnegativity at (U^{(0)}), the global Hessian bound on (S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}), and locality to choose (r) independent of (\Lambda).

Item (2) follows from (1) + Lemma 11.1.6 (gauge‑invariant gradients are horizontal) + Theorem 11.2.2 (Bochner–Bakry–Émery identity), as in §11.5. ∎

## 11.7 Optional strengthening: a **global** curvature constant for heat‑kernel lattice actions (UNIFIED mechanism)

Your UNIFIED “Rigorous Lattice Yang‑Mills” note contains a different curvature mechanism: for certain **heat‑kernel** (or heat‑kernel‑regularized) lattice actions, one can bound the negative part of the action Hessian by an explicit constant involving heat kernel derivative bounds (M_2(t)), and obtain a curvature constant of the form
[
\kappa_A(a)=\rho_G - 6\big(M_2(t_0(a))+M_2(t_1(a))\big),
\tag{11.70}
]
with a corresponding (CD(\kappa_A(a),\infty)) estimate . This is *not* automatically “better” than the Wilson small‑field theorem—it depends on parameter regimes—but it is mathematically clean and worth recording because it gives a route to **global** CD bounds when (\kappa_A(a)>0).

I’ll restate it here in the same Bakry–Émery language.

### 11.7.1 Heat kernel potentials and derivative bounds

Let (K_t(g)) be the heat kernel on (G) (w.r.t. Haar volume) at time (t>0), and define the potential
[
V_t(g) := -\log K_t(g).
\tag{11.71}
]
For fixed (t>0), (V_t) is smooth on compact (G). Define the second‑derivative bound
[
M_2(t) := \sup_{g\in G}\ \big|\nabla^2 V_t(g)\big|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{11.72}
]
(Your UNIFIED note introduces such (M_i(t)) bounds and uses them to control the action Hessian; see Lemma 2.3 / Prop. 2.4 discussion .)

### 11.7.2 A global curvature bound template for local plaquette actions

Consider a plaquette action of the form
[
A(U) = \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} V_{t}(U_p),
\tag{11.73}
]
or more generally a sum with two heat‑kernel times (t_0(a),t_1(a)) appearing in different plaquette families (as in the UNIFIED construction; the resulting bound uses (M_2(t_0(a))+M_2(t_1(a))) ).

The key observation is: if (\nabla^2V_t) is bounded in operator norm, then the plaquette action Hessian is bounded below by a negative multiple of the product metric, with the multiple controlled by the number of plaquettes touching a link.

In (d=4), (\nu=6), hence the factor (6) in (11.70) .

### 11.7.3 The global CD bound (as in UNIFIED Proposition 2.4)

A faithful rephrasing of the UNIFIED statement is:

> For the generator associated to such an action, one has
> (\Gamma_2(f) \ge \kappa_A(a)\Gamma(f)) with (\kappa_A(a)=\rho_G-6(M_2(t_0(a))+M_2(t_1(a)))), and (\Gamma_2) decomposes into a sum of squares plus a Ricci term .

The underlying proof is the same Bochner identity we proved in §11.2 plus an explicit Hessian bound on the action using (11.72) and locality. In other words, this global mechanism is a specific instance of the general principle:
[
\operatorname{Ric}*\mu = \operatorname{Ric}*{g_\Lambda} + \nabla^2 A
\ \ge\ \rho_G g_\Lambda - \nu\cdot (\text{Hessian upper bounds per plaquette})\cdot g_\Lambda,
]
and then (11.31) gives (CD(\kappa,\infty)).
