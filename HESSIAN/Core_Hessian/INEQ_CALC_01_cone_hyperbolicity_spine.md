# 0. Purpose

This note formalizes a compact *inequality calculus* in which:

- **Reflection positivity (RP)** and  
- **spectral gap permanence (GP)**

are consequences of a single structural hypothesis: **convex-cone invariance + (quadratic) hyperbolicity preservation**.

The intent is not to replace model-specific estimates (those still matter),
but to isolate a *common invariant* that is transported by admissible maps (RG steps,
gauge fixing, external-field insertions), so that “positivity + gap” behaves like a functorial property.

---

# 1. Basic objects

## 1.1 Observable space

Let $\mathfrak A$ be a real Banach space of observables.  
In lattice gauge settings, one may take $\mathfrak A$ to be the completion of bounded
cylindrical functions with respect to $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ or $\|\cdot\|_{L^2(\mu)}$.

Let $\Theta:\mathfrak A\to\mathfrak A$ be a bounded involution (reflection): $\Theta^2=\mathrm{Id}$.

Let $\mathfrak A_+\subset \mathfrak A$ be a distinguished “positive-time” (or “positive-side”) subalgebra.

## 1.2 Positivity cone

Fix a closed, pointed, generating convex cone $C\subset \mathfrak A$.
Write its dual cone in $\mathfrak A^\*$ as
\[
C^\* := \{\ell\in \mathfrak A^\*: \ell(c)\ge 0 \ \forall c\in C\}.
\]

### Two canonical cone choices (useful in QFT)

**(i) Pointwise positivity.**  
If $\mathfrak A$ is a function space, define
\[
C_{\ge 0}:=\{f\in \mathfrak A: f(x)\ge 0\ \text{a.e.}\}.
\]

**(ii) OS (reflection-square) cone.**  
Define the *OS cone* generated by reflection-squares:
\[
C_{\mathrm{OS}} := \overline{\mathrm{cone}}\{\, \Theta(F)\,F : F\in \mathfrak A_+ \,\}.
\]
Then reflection positivity of a state $\mu$ is exactly: $\mu\in C_{\mathrm{OS}}^\*$.

In practice one may work with the *sum cone*
\[
C := C_{\mathrm{OS}} + C_{\ge 0},
\]
which is large enough to contain both reflection squares and Lyapunov functions.

# 2. Quadratic hyperbolicity preservation

The strongest “stability polynomial” definition can be overkill.
For the applications here, it suffices to formalize a **quadratic test class**.

## 2.1 Quadratic tests

Let $\mathcal Q(C)$ denote a family of quadratic forms on $\mathfrak A$ that are nonnegative on $C$:
\[
Q(f) = \langle f,Af\rangle,\qquad A\succeq_C 0,
\]
meaning $Q(c)\ge 0$ for all $c\in C$.

From $Q$ we build the univariate test polynomial
\[
p_Q(t):=Q(f+t g),
\]
or, in operator form, characteristic polynomials associated to finite-dimensional compressions.

## 2.2 Hyperbolicity-preserving maps (quadratic version)

A bounded linear map $T:\mathfrak A\to\mathfrak A$ is **cone-hyperbolic (quadratic)** if:

1. **Cone invariance:** $T(C)\subseteq C$.
2. **No new null directions in $C$:** for every $Q\in \mathcal Q(C)$,
   \[
   Q(c)=0\ \text{and}\ c\in C \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q(Tc)=0\ \Rightarrow\ c\ \text{was already forced by symmetries}.
   \]
   Equivalently: $T$ does not create fresh flat directions for the quadratic tests used to certify coercivity.

This captures the operative meaning of “hyperbolicity” in the gap-permanence logic:
**admissible maps cannot manufacture zero-curvature directions inside the cone**.

# 3. Admissible model

A **model object** is
\[
\mathrm{Mod}=(\mathfrak A,\ C,\ \Theta,\ \mathcal B,\ K;\ \|\cdot\|,\ \mathrm{Loc},\ \mathrm{Mon})
\]
where:

- $K:\mathfrak A\to\mathfrak A$ is the transfer operator (or Euclidean semigroup element),
- $\mathcal B$ is a boundary/conditioning operator (restriction to finite volume + insertion maps),
- $\mathrm{Loc}$ records locality structure (finite range / quasi-locality),
- $\mathrm{Mon}$ records monotonicity used by $\mathcal B$.

**Admissibility (structural).**  
$\mathrm{Mod}$ is admissible if
\[
K(C)\subseteq C,\qquad \Theta(C)\subseteq C,\qquad \mathcal B(C)\subseteq C,
\]
and $K$ is cone-hyperbolic (quadratic) on the chosen test class.

# 4. RP and GP as consequences

## 4.1 Reflection positivity as dual-cone positivity

Let $\mu\in \mathfrak A^\*$ be the expectation functional (state).
Assume $\mu\in C^\*$.

If $C$ contains $\Theta(F)F$ for all $F\in \mathfrak A_+$ (e.g. $C\supseteq C_{\mathrm{OS}}$),
then
\[
\langle F,\Theta F\rangle_\mu := \mu(\Theta(F)F) \ge 0,\qquad \forall F\in \mathfrak A_+,
\]
i.e. **reflection positivity** is a direct dual-cone consequence.

## 4.2 Gap permanence as “no new flats”

Let $L$ be a (symmetric) generator on $L^2(\mu)$ with Dirichlet form
\[
\mathcal E_\mu(f,f)=\int \Gamma(f)\,d\mu.
\]
Suppose a **certificate** exists:
\[
\mathrm{Cert}=(\Phi,\kappa,m),\qquad \Phi\in C,
\]
meaning:
- (curvature floor) $\Gamma_2^\Phi \ge \kappa\,\Gamma^\Phi$ on the certificate region (often a SAFE core),
- (drift) $L\Phi \le -m\Phi + b$ (Foster–Lyapunov shape).

Then standard local-to-global engines (Bakry–Émery + Lyapunov drift patching) yield a global
Poincaré constant and hence a spectral gap $\lambda\gtrsim \lambda(\kappa,m,\dots)>0$.

If $A:\mathrm{Mod}_1\to \mathrm{Mod}_2$ is an admissible morphism (next section),
cone-hyperbolicity ensures that the quadratic forms encoding curvature/coercivity cannot
acquire new null directions on the image: thus the gap does not collapse.

# 5. Admissible morphisms and certificate transport

An **admissible morphism**
\[
A:\mathrm{Mod}_1\to \mathrm{Mod}_2
\]
satisfies:

1. **Cone-preserving:** $A_\# C_1 \subseteq C_2$.
2. **Norm control:** $\|Af\|\le L\|f\|$.
3. **Cone-hyperbolic (quadratic)** on the test class.
4. **Reflection compatibility:** $A\circ \Theta_1=\Theta_2\circ A$.

### Certificate transport (template)

Under mild regularity (compatibility of generators / Dirichlet forms), certificates transport as
\[
(\Phi,\kappa,m)\ \mapsto\ (A\Phi,\ \underline\alpha\,\kappa,\ \underline\beta\,m),
\]
where the losses satisfy, in typical gradient-based settings,
\[
\underline\alpha \ \gtrsim\ L^{-2},\qquad \underline\beta\ \gtrsim\ L^{-1},
\]
with the precise constants depending on how $\Gamma,\Gamma_2$ pull back under $A$ and on locality overlap constants.

# 6. Entry: external-field linearization

Let $h\in C$ be reflection-even, and deform the action by
\[
E_h = E-\langle h,\mathsf J\rangle.
\]
Let $K_h=e^{-H_h}$ be the associated transfer operator and assume the Fréchet derivative at $h=0$
\[
DK[h]\big|_{0}
=\int_0^1 e^{-tH}\,(\partial_h H)\,e^{-(1-t)H}\,dt
\]
is cone-preserving and cone-hyperbolic on $\mathfrak A_+$.

**Transport theorem (linear response form).**  
If $\mathrm{Cert}_0=(\Phi,\kappa,m)$ certifies $\mathrm{Mod}_0$, then for sufficiently small $\|h\|$,
\[
\mathrm{Cert}_h=\Big(\Phi,\ \kappa - c_1\|h\|,\ m - c_2\|h\|\Big)
\]
certifies $\mathrm{Mod}_h, with explicit $c_{1,2}$ controlled by
$\|DK[h]\|_{C\to C}$, locality, and reflection compatibility.

---

# 7. Minimal “block-check” interface

To use this calculus in proofs and simulations:

1. Choose a concrete $\Phi\in C$ (typically quadratic curvature / Hessian energy on a gauge-fixed slice).
2. Verify on finite blocks:
   \[
   \Gamma_2^\Phi \ge \kappa\,\Gamma^\Phi,\qquad L\Phi \le -m\Phi + b.
   \]
3. For each intertwiner $A$ (RG step, gauge projection, external-field map),
   verify cone preservation and compute Lipschitz/locality constants.
4. Read off transported $(\kappa,m)$ and conclude uniform RP + gap on the image model.

This is the intended “inequality calculus” workflow: **structural admissibility + certificate transport**.
