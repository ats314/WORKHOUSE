# 1. Why this note exists

A recurring annoyance in nonperturbative gauge theory is that the *configuration space itself* is curved and compact (a product of $SU(N)$s), yet most intuitive “mass-gap” stories are told in flat field coordinates. The project documents develop a finite-cutoff (finite volume, nonzero lattice spacing) module where that curvature is not a nuisance but a *source* of convexity and spectral gaps.

Two linked claims are extracted here:

1. **A geometric seed**: the Haar measure Jacobian in exponential coordinates produces a **strictly positive quadratic term** (“Haar mass”) in the effective action.

2. **A stability narrative**: although the explicit Haar mass scales like $a^2$ and appears to vanish as $a\to 0$, a conjectural renormalisation/smoothing flow could transfer (“hand off”) responsibility to **intrinsic curvature** and to **topological/spectral locking** via Wilson loops.

This note distills the derivations and turns them into a research-program skeleton.

# 2. Setup: lattice configuration manifold and effective action

Let $\Lambda$ be a finite hypercubic lattice (periodic b.c.). Let $B$ be the set of oriented bonds, $N_b=|B|$.
The configuration space is the compact Lie group manifold

$$
\mathcal C_\Lambda \;=\; SU(N)^{N_b},
$$

with product bi-invariant Riemannian metric and product Haar probability measure $d\mu_H$.

The Wilson action (schematically) is

$$
S_W(U) \;=\; \sum_{p} \Bigl(1 - \frac1N \Re\operatorname{Tr} U_p\Bigr),
\qquad
U_p=\prod_{b\in \partial p} U_b .
$$

The **effective action** used by the geometric module is

$$
S_{\mathrm{eff}}(U) \;=\; S_W(U) \;+\; S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(U),
$$

where $S_{\mathrm{Haar}}$ is the measure-action coming from writing the Haar measure in exponential coordinates.

# 3. Derivation: the Haar-measure induces a quadratic “mass term” in exponential coordinates

## 3.1 Exponential coordinates

On links close to the identity, write each link variable as

$$
U_b = \exp(i a g A_b),\qquad A_b\in \mathfrak{su}(N),
$$

with lattice spacing $a$ and bare coupling $g$.

The change of variables $U\mapsto A$ has a Jacobian:

$$
d\mu_H(U) \;=\; J(A)\, dA,
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A) := -\log J(A).
$$

A standard Lie-group computation yields

$$
J(A)
=
\det\!\left(
\frac{\sinh\!\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{iagA}/2\bigr)}{\operatorname{ad}_{iagA}/2}
\right),
$$

so

$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
=
-\operatorname{Tr}
\log\!\left(\frac{\sinh X}{X}\right),
\qquad
X:=\frac{\operatorname{ad}_{iagA}}{2}.
$$

## 3.2 Small-field expansion and positivity

Using the series
$\log(\sinh X / X)=\frac16 X^2 + O(X^4)$ near $X=0$, we get

$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
= -\frac16 \operatorname{Tr}(X^2) + O(\|X\|^4).
$$

Now $X^2 = -\frac{a^2 g^2}{4} \operatorname{ad}_A^2$ and (with an $SU(N)$-invariant inner product)
$\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{ad}_A^2) = -C_2(\mathrm{ad})\langle A,A\rangle$.
Hence

$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
=
\frac{a^2 g^2}{24} C_2(\mathrm{ad})\langle A,A\rangle \;+\; O(a^4\|A\|^4).
$$

This is the key point: **the quadratic term is strictly positive**. To quadratic order, it looks like

$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
\approx
\frac{m_H^2}{2}\sum_{b}\operatorname{Tr}(A_b^2),
\qquad m_H^2>0,
$$

with $m_H^2\propto a^2 g^2$ (and group-dependent constants).

**Interpretation.** This is a gauge-invariant “mass” generated not by the bare action but by the **geometry of $SU(N)$**.

# 4. Global horizontal Hessian gap on the irreducible sector

Gauge invariance forces degeneracy along gauge orbits (“vertical directions”). The module therefore works with the **horizontal Hessian**.

## 4.1 Horizontal/vertical splitting

At $U\in\mathcal C_\Lambda$ let $V_U$ be the tangent space generated by infinitesimal gauge transformations.
Let $H_U$ be the orthogonal complement (horizontal space). Define the restricted Hessian

$$
\operatorname{Hess}^{\perp} S_{\mathrm{eff}}(U)
:=
\operatorname{Hess} S_{\mathrm{eff}}(U)\big|_{H_U\times H_U},
$$

and let $\lambda_{\min}(U)$ be its smallest eigenvalue.

## 4.2 Statement and proof idea

**Claim (global horizontal gap).** On the irreducible sector $\mathcal C_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{irr}}$,
there exists a constant $c_\*>0$ independent of the lattice volume such that

$$
\lambda_{\min}(U)\ge c_\*
\qquad
\forall\, U\in \mathcal C_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{irr}}.
$$

**Proof idea skeleton.**

1. **Local convexity near identity.** The Haar quadratic term provides strict positivity of the horizontal Hessian in a neighborhood of the identity configuration.

2. **Compactness + gauge covariance.** $\mathcal C_\Lambda^{\mathrm{irr}}$ is compact modulo gauge, so it can be covered by finitely many gauge-translated neighborhoods where the bound holds.

3. **Irreducibility prevents horizontal zero modes.** Away from the “small field” region, continuity plus the exclusion of reducible points prevents the smallest horizontal eigenvalue from touching zero.

This is the main finite-cutoff convexity input for functional inequalities and spectral gaps.

# 5. Functional inequalities and a Langevin spectral gap (Bakry--Émery)

Consider the overdamped Langevin dynamics on $\mathcal C_\Lambda$ with generator

$$
Lf = \Delta_{\mathcal C_\Lambda} f
-\langle \nabla S_{\mathrm{eff}}, \nabla f\rangle ,
$$

with invariant measure $d\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\propto e^{-S_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,d\mathrm{vol}$.

A uniform lower bound on the *horizontal* Hessian, combined with the manifold Ricci curvature,
is packaged into a Bakry--Émery-type condition

$$
\mathrm{Ric} + \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{eff}} \;\ge\; \rho_\*\, g,
\qquad \rho_\*>0.
$$

Consequences (standard $\Gamma$-calculus):

- Poincaré inequality and logarithmic Sobolev inequality with constants controlled by $\rho_\*$.
- A **spectral gap** for $-L$ on $L^2(\mu_{\mathrm{eff}})$ bounded below by $\rho_\*$, uniform in volume.

This gives a clean, nonperturbative “mass-at-finite-cutoff” notion: exponential relaxation of the stochastic dynamics and exponential decay of correlations for the corresponding semigroup.

# 6. A second (complementary) gap: strong-coupling transfer-matrix spectral gap

In the Hamiltonian formulation (transfer matrix $T=e^{-a_t H}$), a strong-coupling/cluster expansion yields

$$
\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_0} \le e^{-m_0 a_t} < 1
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\Delta \ge m_0 >0,
$$

where the leading excited states can be created by Wilson loop operators and bounded via an induced string tension.

The geometric (Hessian/Bakry--Émery) gap and the strong-coupling transfer-matrix gap are distinct handles on “gapped-ness,” and the documents emphasize using them together.

# 7. Conjectural continuum handoff: geometric--spectral stability via a Riccati mechanism

The geometric seed $m_H^2\propto a^2$ seems to die as $a\to 0$.
The extracted proposal is:

1. The Haar seed is only needed to **prime** convexity at finite cutoff.
2. Under an appropriate renormalisation/smoothing flow (modeled on viscous Hamilton--Jacobi / heat-flow evolution of effective actions),
   the smallest horizontal Hessian eigenvalue should satisfy a **Riccati-type inequality** with a **strictly positive source term**
   that survives the limit.
3. The Wilson-loop/flux-tube sector (“string tension”) provides a kind of **topological locking**: if the confining phase connects continuously to the continuum theory, the gap cannot smoothly collapse without a phase transition.

A stylized form is:

$$
\dot \lambda_{\min}(t)
\;\ge\;
\sigma_{\mathrm{geom}} \;-\; 2\lambda_{\min}(t)^2
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\lambda_{\min}(t)\to \lambda_\infty\ge \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{geom}}/2}>0.
$$

The novelty here is the *division of labor*:
finite-cutoff Haar convexity starts the process; intrinsic group curvature and RG-flow dynamics maintain it; Wilson-loop physics prevents the gap from “escaping” through topological channels.

# 8. Concrete next steps (research tasks)

1. **Make the RG/smoothing flow explicit.** Identify a mathematically controlled flow on measures/effective actions on $\mathcal C_\Lambda$ whose generator reproduces coarse-graining and yields a Hessian evolution equation.

2. **Prove a matrix Riccati inequality in a gauge setting.** In scalar convex models, Bakry--Émery plus vHJ flow gives clean Riccati bounds; adapt that technology to the horizontal Hessian on gauge orbit space.

3. **Quantify the “geometric source” $\sigma_{\mathrm{geom}}$.** Determine what survives as $a\to 0$: is it purely group curvature, or does it mix with renormalised topological data (an “anomaly source”)?

4. **Bridge to physical (Minkowski) mass gap.** Relate the Dirichlet-form / Langevin gap and/or transfer-matrix gap to OS-reconstructed Minkowski spectral data, with precise scaling.

# 9. Meta-comment

This module is attractive because it converts deep QFT angst into geometric analysis on compact manifolds:
if you can keep a lower bound on an appropriate horizontal Hessian *through the continuum limit*, the usual functional inequalities do the rest.
The hard part is exactly that: proving the “handoff” rather than assuming it.
