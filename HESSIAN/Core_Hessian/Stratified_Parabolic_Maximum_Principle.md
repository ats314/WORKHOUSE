# Stratified parabolic maximum principle with polar singular set

**Scope.** This document extracts the parabolic comparison / maximum-principle mechanism proposed for curvature flows on stratified configuration spaces, where the singular stratum (e.g. reducibles) is assumed *polar* (capacity zero) and therefore does not act as a boundary that can spoil positivity.

**Primary source.**
- `SYNTH_P20_stratified_parabolic_principle.md` (statement, proof mechanism, and “bridging proposition”).

---

## Stratified setting and polarity assumption

Let \(\mathcal M\) be a stratified space with regular stratum \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}\) and singular set \(\Sigma:=\mathcal M\setminus\mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}\).

Assume \(\Sigma\) is **polar** with respect to a reference measure \(\mu\) and generator \(L\):
\[
\mathrm{Cap}_\mu(\Sigma)=0.
\]
Heuristically, the Markov process associated to \(L\) started in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}\) almost surely never hits \(\Sigma\) in finite time.

---

## Parabolic comparison theorem (extracted statement)

### Theorem (Parabolic comparison on stratified spaces)

Let \(u(t,x)\) be a supersolution on \((0,T]\times \mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}\) of a semilinear parabolic inequality
\[
\partial_t u \;\ge\; L u \;+\; F(u),
\]
where \(F\) is non-decreasing. If
\[
u(0,x)\ge 0\quad\text{for all }x\in \mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}},
\]
then
\[
u(t,x)\ge 0\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T],\;x\in \mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}.
\]

### Proof mechanism (as extracted)

The source frames the argument as:

1. **Stochastic representation.** Represent \(u\) via a Feynman–Kac formula (or semigroup / stochastic flow) along sample paths \(X_t\) of the diffusion generated by \(L\).

2. **Avoidance by polarity.** Since \(\Sigma\) has capacity zero, almost all paths \(X_t\) starting in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}\) remain in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}\) for all finite times.

3. **Standard maximum principle.** On the smooth manifold \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}\), the standard parabolic maximum principle applies (no singular boundary contribution is encountered along the paths).

4. **Conclusion.** Positivity is preserved by the dynamics: the sign of \(u\) is determined by the initial data and the monotone nonlinearity \(F\), not by boundary conditions on \(\Sigma\).

\(\square\)

## Bridging proposition: PDE-to-ODE reduction for a curvature eigenvalue

The extracted “application” is a PDE-to-ODE reduction for a quantity \(\lambda(t,x)\) that plays the role of a smallest eigenvalue of a curvature/Hessian tensor.

### Proposition (curvature forcing implies a positive floor)

Assume \(\lambda(t,x)\) satisfies a differential inequality on \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}\):
\[
\partial_t \lambda \;\ge\; L\lambda \;-\;2\lambda^2 \;+\;\sigma_*,
\]
and assume polarity of \(\Sigma\).

Then \(\lambda(t,x)\) is bounded below by the solution \(\underline\lambda(t)\) of the scalar ODE
\[
\dot{\underline\lambda} \;=\;-2\underline\lambda^2 + \sigma_*,
\qquad \underline\lambda(0)=\inf_{x\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}}\lambda(0,x).
\]
In particular, if \(\underline\lambda(0)=0\) and \(\sigma_*>0\), then \(\underline\lambda(t)\to\sqrt{\sigma_*/2}>0\).

## Why this is “interesting physics/mathematics”

If curvature positivity is the analytic proxy for a “mass scale,” then the stratified maximum principle is a way of saying:

- **Singular strata cannot destroy positivity if they are polar.**
- Curvature positivity is then governed by a bulk PDE plus forcing, and can be compared to a scalar Riccati barrier ODE.

This is a clean separation of *geometric PDE mechanism* from *physical input* (\(\sigma_*>0\)).
