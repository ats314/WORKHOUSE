## Appendix J. A globally smooth plaquette “energy” and the missing uniform second–derivative bound

This appendix supplies the technical estimate singled out as the Lyapunov–drift bottleneck in the analytic engine: in the plaquette-based Lyapunov ansatz (W_\Lambda=\exp(\eta\sum_p \Phi(z_p))), the drift computation is “clean except for the term (X^a_\ell X^a_\ell z_p), which must be bounded uniformly to close the inequality.” 

The cure proposed in the main analytic note is to **replace the non-smooth plaquette distance-square (d_G(U_p,\mathbf 1)^2)** by a **globally smooth** conjugation-invariant proxy (z(g)). 
Here we implement that replacement for (G=\mathrm{SU}(N)) using
[
z(g);:=;1-\frac1N\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Tr}(g),
\qquad z_p(U):=z!\big(U_p(U)\big),
]
and we prove the two uniform estimates needed to remove the obstruction:

1. **Uniform bound on second derivatives:** (|X^a_\ell X^a_\ell z_p(U)|\le C) for all (\Lambda,p,\ell,U).
2. **“No constant leakage” after choosing (\Phi'(0)=0):** for the canonical choice (\Phi(s)=s^2) (so (\Phi'(s)=2s)), the potentially dangerous term (\Phi'(z_p),X^a_\ell X^a_\ell z_p) is pointwise bounded by (C,z_p) and hence cannot contribute an (O(|P(\Lambda)|)) constant.

The rest of the Lyapunov drift estimate (how the (-\langle\nabla S,\nabla\cdot\rangle) part of (L_\Lambda) yields a coercive negative term) is logically separate; Appendix J is only about the **global smoothness/second-derivative** obstruction that was explicitly identified as *the* missing estimate. 

---

### J.1. Lattice and configuration-space conventions

Fix a finite lattice region (\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z^d) (in applications (d=4)). Let (E(\Lambda)) be the set of oriented nearest-neighbor links and (P(\Lambda)) the set of oriented plaquettes. The configuration manifold is
[
M_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)}\cong \prod_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}G,
]
as in the Wilson-action setup. 

For an oriented plaquette (p=(x;\mu,\nu)) (with (\mu<\nu)), its holonomy is
[
U_p(U) := U_{x,\mu},U_{x+\hat\mu,\nu},U_{x+\hat\nu,\mu}^{-1},U_{x,\nu}^{-1}.
\tag{J.1}
]
This is exactly the standard plaquette product. 

We work with (G=\mathrm{SU}(N)) and an (\mathrm{Ad})-invariant inner product on (\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{su}(N)) of the form
[
\langle A,B\rangle_\lambda := \lambda,(-\mathrm{Tr}(AB)),
\qquad \lambda>0,
\tag{J.2}
]
as used in the project notes. 
This induces a bi-invariant Riemannian metric on each group factor and the product metric on (M_\Lambda). 

---

### J.2. The smooth plaquette observable (z(g)=1-\frac1N\mathrm{ReTr}(g))

Define for (g\in \mathrm{SU}(N)),
[
z(g):=1-\frac1N\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Tr}(g).
\tag{J.3}
]
This is smooth and conjugation-invariant. It vanishes at the identity and is bounded:
[
0\le z(g)\le 2.
\tag{J.4}
]
(The upper bound comes from (\mathrm{ReTr}(g)\ge -|\mathrm{Tr}(g)|\ge -N) for unitary (g).)

For each plaquette (p), define the plaquette energy
[
z_p(U):= z(U_p(U)).
\tag{J.5}
]

A key algebraic identity relates (z(g)) to the Hilbert–Schmidt distance from (\mathbf 1).

**Lemma J.1 (Hilbert–Schmidt form of (z)).**
For (g\in \mathrm{SU}(N)),
[
z(g)=\frac{1}{2N},|g-\mathbf 1|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2,
\tag{J.6}
]
where (|A|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2:=\mathrm{Tr}(A^\ast A)).

*Proof.* Expand
[
|g-\mathbf 1|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=\mathrm{Tr}\big((g-\mathbf 1)^\ast(g-\mathbf 1)\big)
=\mathrm{Tr}(g^\ast g)-\mathrm{Tr}(g^\ast)-\mathrm{Tr}(g)+\mathrm{Tr}(\mathbf 1).
]
Since (g\in \mathrm{SU}(N)\subset U(N)), (g^\ast g=\mathbf 1) and (\mathrm{Tr}(g^\ast)=\overline{\mathrm{Tr}(g)}). Thus
[
|g-\mathbf 1|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2
= N-\overline{\mathrm{Tr}(g)}-\mathrm{Tr}(g)+N
=2N-2,\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Tr}(g).
]
Divide by (2N) to get (J.6). ∎

This identity is the reason (z(g)) behaves like a squared distance near (\mathbf 1) but stays **globally smooth** (no cut locus issues).

### J.3. Differential operators on (M_\Lambda) via right-invariant vector fields

Following the standard right-translation trivialization (T_gG\simeq\mathfrak g), any tangent vector at (g) is written as ((dR_g)_eX) for a unique (X\in\mathfrak g). 

Fix an orthonormal basis ({T^a}*{a=1}^{\dim\mathfrak g}) of ((\mathfrak g,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle*\lambda)). For each link (\ell\in E(\Lambda)) and each (a), define the right-invariant derivative in the (\ell)-coordinate by
[
(X_\ell^a f)(U)
:=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|*{t=0} f\big(U^{(\ell,a)}(t)\big),
\quad
U^{(\ell,a)}(t)*{\ell'}=
\begin{cases}
U_\ell,\exp(tT^a), & \ell'=\ell,\
U_{\ell'}, & \ell'\neq \ell.
\end{cases}
\tag{J.7}
]

On the product manifold with product bi-invariant metric, the linkwise Laplacian is the sum of squares,
[
\Delta_\Lambda f = \sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}\sum_a X_\ell^aX_\ell^a f.
\tag{J.8}
]

Given an effective action (S_\Lambda\in C^2(M_\Lambda)), the symmetric diffusion generator has the form
[
L_\Lambda f = \Delta_\Lambda f - \langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla f\rangle,
\tag{J.9}
]
as in the analytic engine. 
In coordinates (using the orthonormal frame (X_\ell^a)), one may write equivalently
[
L_\Lambda f
=\sum_{\ell,a}\Big(X_\ell^aX_\ell^a f - (X_\ell^a S_\Lambda),(X_\ell^a f)\Big).
\tag{J.10}
]

The carré du champ is
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(f)=\sum_{\ell,a}(X_\ell^a f)^2 = |\nabla f|^2,
\tag{J.11}
]
matching the general identity (\Gamma(f)=|\nabla f|^2) in Part 15.

### J.4. Uniform derivative bounds for (z(g)) on (\mathrm{SU}(N))

We first bound derivatives on the group itself; then we lift them to plaquettes.

#### J.4.1 First derivative: ((Xz)^2 \lesssim z)

For (X\in\mathfrak{su}(N)), define the right-invariant differential operator ((\widetilde X f)(g):=\frac{d}{dt}|_{0} f(g\exp(tX))).

**Lemma J.2 (first-derivative bound).**
For every (g\in\mathrm{SU}(N)) and (X\in\mathfrak{su}(N)),
[
|\widetilde X z(g)|;\le;\sqrt{\frac{2}{N\lambda}};|X|*\lambda;\sqrt{z(g)}.
\tag{J.12}
]
In particular, if (|X|*\lambda=1) then ((\widetilde X z(g))^2\le \frac{2}{N\lambda}z(g)).

*Proof.* Start from the definition (z(g)=1-\frac1N\mathrm{ReTr}(g)). Along (g(t)=g\exp(tX)),
[
\frac{d}{dt}\Big|*{0}\mathrm{Tr}(g\exp(tX))
=\mathrm{Tr}(gX),
]
so
[
\widetilde X z(g)= -\frac1N \mathrm{ReTr}(gX).
\tag{J.13}
]
Because (X\in\mathfrak{su}(N)) has (\mathrm{Tr}(X)=0), we may rewrite
[
\mathrm{Tr}(gX)=\mathrm{Tr}\big((g-\mathbf 1)X\big).
\tag{J.14}
]
Thus
[
|\widetilde X z(g)|
\le \frac1N,|\mathrm{Tr}((g-\mathbf 1)X)|
\le \frac1N,|g-\mathbf 1|*{\mathrm{HS}};|X|_{\mathrm{HS}}
\tag{J.15}
]
by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product.

Now use Lemma J.1: (|g-\mathbf 1|*{\mathrm{HS}}=\sqrt{2Nz(g)}). Also, for skew-Hermitian (X),
[
|X|*\lambda^2=\langle X,X\rangle_\lambda
=\lambda,\mathrm{Tr}(X^\ast X)=\lambda,|X|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
|X|*{\mathrm{HS}}=\frac{|X|_\lambda}{\sqrt\lambda}.
\tag{J.16}
]
Substituting into (J.15) gives exactly (J.12). ∎

Consequently, with an orthonormal basis ({T^a}),
[
\Gamma_G(z)(g):=\sum_a (\widetilde T^a z(g))^2
;\le;\frac{2,\dim\mathfrak g}{N\lambda};z(g).
\tag{J.17}
]

#### J.4.2 Second derivative: (|\widetilde X^2 z|\lesssim |X|^2)

**Lemma J.3 (second-derivative bound).**
For every (g\in\mathrm{SU}(N)) and (X\in\mathfrak{su}(N)),
[
|\widetilde X^2 z(g)|\le \frac{1}{\sqrt{N},\lambda},|X|*\lambda^2.
\tag{J.18}
]
In particular, if (|X|*\lambda=1) then (|\widetilde X^2 z(g)|\le \frac{1}{\sqrt N,\lambda}) uniformly in (g).

*Proof.* Differentiate (J.13) once more along (g(t)=g\exp(tX)):
[
\widetilde X^2 z(g)
= -\frac1N,\mathrm{Re},\frac{d}{dt}\Big|*0 \mathrm{Tr}(g\exp(tX)X)
= -\frac1N,\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Tr}(gX^2).
\tag{J.19}
]
Hence
[
|\widetilde X^2 z(g)|
\le \frac1N,|\mathrm{Tr}(gX^2)|
\le \frac1N,|g|*{\mathrm{HS}};|X^2|*{\mathrm{HS}}.
\tag{J.20}
]
For unitary (g), (|g|*{\mathrm{HS}}=\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(g^\ast g)}=\sqrt N). Also,
[
|X^2|*{\mathrm{HS}}
\le |X|*{\mathrm{op}};|X|*{\mathrm{HS}}
\le |X|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=\frac{|X|*\lambda^2}{\lambda},
\tag{J.21}
]
where we used (|X|*{\mathrm{op}}\le |X|_{\mathrm{HS}}) and (J.16). Plugging into (J.20) yields (J.18). ∎

A direct corollary is a uniform bound on (\Delta_G z):
[
|\Delta_G z(g)|
=\left|\sum_a \widetilde T^a\widetilde T^a z(g)\right|
\le \sum_a \frac{1}{\sqrt N,\lambda}
=\frac{\dim\mathfrak g}{\sqrt N,\lambda}.
\tag{J.22}
]

### J.5. Lifting the derivative bounds to plaquettes: (z_p(U)=z(U_p(U)))

Fix a plaquette (p). By construction (J.1), (U_p(U)) is a product of four link variables (or inverses). For any fixed link (\ell\in\partial p), we can factor the plaquette holonomy as
[
U_p(U)=A(U),U_\ell^{\sigma},B(U),
\tag{J.23}
]
where:

* (\sigma\in{+1,-1}) indicates whether (U_\ell) appears as (U_\ell) or (U_\ell^{-1}) in the oriented boundary product,
* (A(U),B(U)\in G) are products of the **other three** link variables (and inverses) in the plaquette.

This is just regrouping of the four-factor product (J.1). 

We now compute (X_\ell^a z_p) and (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p).

#### J.5.1 First derivative bound: ((X_\ell^a z_p)^2 \lesssim z_p)

**Lemma J.4 (first derivative of plaquette energy).**
Fix (\ell\in\partial p) and (a). Then for all (U\in M_\Lambda),
[
|X_\ell^a z_p(U)|
;\le;
\sqrt{\frac{2}{N\lambda}};\sqrt{z_p(U)}.
\tag{J.24}
]
Equivalently, ((X_\ell^a z_p(U))^2\le \frac{2}{N\lambda}z_p(U)).

*Proof.* Consider the (t)-perturbed configuration (U^{(\ell,a)}(t)) from (J.7), so only (U_\ell) changes to (U_\ell\exp(tT^a)). We analyze separately (\sigma=+1) and (\sigma=-1) in (J.23).

**Case 1: (\sigma=+1).** Then
[
U_p(t):=U_p(U^{(\ell,a)}(t)) = A,U_\ell e^{tT^a},B
=(A,U_\ell,B),(B^{-1}e^{tT^a}B)
=U_p(0),e^{t,\mathrm{Ad}*{B^{-1}}T^a}.
\tag{J.25}
]
Hence (t\mapsto U_p(t)) is exactly a right-translation curve through (U_p(0)) with tangent (\mathrm{Ad}*{B^{-1}}T^a\in\mathfrak{su}(N)). Since the metric is (\mathrm{Ad})-invariant, (\mathrm{Ad}*{B^{-1}}T^a) has the same (\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle*\lambda)-norm as (T^a), i.e. norm (1). 
Therefore Lemma J.2 applied at (g=U_p(0)) with (X=\mathrm{Ad}_{B^{-1}}T^a) gives exactly (J.24).

**Case 2: (\sigma=-1).** Then (U_\ell) appears as (U_\ell^{-1}). Under the perturbation (U_\ell(t)=U_\ell e^{tT^a}),
[
U_\ell(t)^{-1} = (U_\ell e^{tT^a})^{-1} = e^{-tT^a},U_\ell^{-1}.
\tag{J.26}
]
Thus
[
U_p(t)=A,e^{-tT^a},U_\ell^{-1},B.
\tag{J.27}
]
Differentiate (\mathrm{Tr}(U_p(t))) at (t=0):
[
\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_0 \mathrm{Tr}(U_p(t))
=======================================

-\mathrm{Tr}(A,T^a,U_\ell^{-1},B).
\tag{J.28}
]
Rewrite the right-hand side by factoring out (U_p(0)=A U_\ell^{-1}B):
[
A,T^a,U_\ell^{-1},B
=(A U_\ell^{-1}B),\big(B^{-1}U_\ell T^a U_\ell^{-1}B\big)
=U_p(0),\mathrm{Ad}*{B^{-1}U*\ell}T^a.
\tag{J.29}
]
Therefore
[
\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_0 \mathrm{Tr}(U_p(t))
=======================================

-\mathrm{Tr}\big(U_p(0),\mathrm{Ad}*{B^{-1}U*\ell}T^a\big),
\tag{J.30}
]
and again (\mathrm{Ad}*{B^{-1}U*\ell}T^a) has norm (1). Applying Lemma J.2 to (g=U_p(0)) and (X=\mathrm{Ad}*{B^{-1}U*\ell}T^a) yields (J.24). ∎

Summing (J.24) over (a) and then over the four links (\ell\in\partial p) gives a uniform “energy controls gradient” estimate on the configuration space:

**Corollary J.5 (carré du champ bound for (z_p)).**
There exists a constant (C_{\nabla}=C_{\nabla}(N,\lambda,d)) such that for all lattices (\Lambda), plaquettes (p\in P(\Lambda)), and configurations (U\in M_\Lambda),
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(z_p)(U)
=\sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}\sum_a (X_\ell^a z_p(U))^2
;\le;C_{\nabla},z_p(U).
\tag{J.31}
]
Moreover the sum over (\ell) actually only runs over (\ell\in\partial p) (four links), hence (C_{\nabla}) is independent of (|\Lambda|).

*Proof.* If (\ell\notin\partial p) then (z_p) does not depend on (U_\ell), so (X_\ell^a z_p=0). For (\ell\in\partial p), apply (J.24) and sum over the finitely many (a)’s and links. ∎

#### J.5.2 Second derivative bound: (|X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p|\lesssim 1)

**Lemma J.6 (uniform second derivative of (z_p)).**
There exists (C_2=C_2(N,\lambda)) such that for every lattice (\Lambda), plaquette (p), link (\ell\in E(\Lambda)), basis index (a), and configuration (U\in M_\Lambda),
[
|X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p(U)|\le C_2.
\tag{J.32}
]

*Proof.* If (\ell\notin\partial p), the function (z_p) does not depend on (U_\ell) and the left-hand side is (0). So assume (\ell\in\partial p), and use the factorization (U_p(t)) as in Lemma J.4.

* In the (\sigma=+1) case, (J.25) shows (U_p(t)=U_p(0)\exp(tY)) for (Y:=\mathrm{Ad}*{B^{-1}}T^a) of norm (1). Then (t\mapsto z(U_p(t))) is exactly the one-dimensional restriction (t\mapsto z(g\exp(tY))), and Lemma J.3 (with (|Y|*\lambda=1)) gives a bound (|\frac{d^2}{dt^2}|_0 z(U_p(t))|\le \frac{1}{\sqrt N,\lambda}).

* In the (\sigma=-1) case, we can compute directly from (J.27):
  [
  U_p(t)=A,e^{-tT^a},U_\ell^{-1},B.
  ]
  Then
  [
  \frac{d^2}{dt^2}\Big|*0 \mathrm{Tr}(U_p(t))
  =\mathrm{Tr}(A,(T^a)^2,U*\ell^{-1},B).
  \tag{J.33}
  ]
  Hence
  [
  \left|\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\Big|*0 z(U_p(t))\right|
  \le \frac1N,\left|\mathrm{Tr}(A,(T^a)^2,U*\ell^{-1},B)\right|
  \le \frac1N,|A|*{\mathrm{HS}};|(T^a)^2|*{\mathrm{HS}};|U_\ell^{-1}B|*{\mathrm{HS}}.
  \tag{J.34}
  ]
  Since all group factors are unitary, (|A|*{\mathrm{HS}}=|U_\ell^{-1}B|*{\mathrm{HS}}=\sqrt N). As in Lemma J.3, (|(T^a)^2|*{\mathrm{HS}}\le 1/\lambda) because (|T^a|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=1/\lambda). Thus (J.34) is bounded by (1/\lambda).

Combining the two cases gives a uniform constant (C_2) (e.g. (C_2=\max{1/(\sqrt N,\lambda),1/\lambda}=1/\lambda)). ∎

This is precisely the uniform control that was missing when (z_p) was taken to be a geodesic distance square (which loses smoothness at the cut locus), and it is the control flagged as necessary in Part 15.10.2.

### J.6. The “no (O(|P|)) leakage” mechanism when (\Phi'(0)=0)

We now formalize the key bookkeeping point: if one uses the smooth (z_p) above and chooses (\Phi) with (\Phi'(0)=0) (e.g. (\Phi(s)=s^2)), then the second-derivative term in the Lyapunov drift cannot contribute a raw (|P(\Lambda)|) factor.

Let (\Phi\in C^2([0,2])) satisfy

* (\Phi(0)=0),
* (\Phi'(0)=0),
* and (\Phi') has linear growth near (0), i.e. there is (C_\Phi) such that
  [
  0\le \Phi'(s)\le C_\Phi,s\qquad \forall s\in[0,2].
  \tag{J.35}
  ]
  For (\Phi(s)=s^2), (C_\Phi=2).

Define the plaquette Lyapunov potential
[
V_\Lambda(U):=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \Phi(z_p(U)).
\tag{J.36}
]

The dangerous piece in the drift computation is the Laplacian part (sum of linkwise second derivatives)
[
\sum_{\ell,a} X_\ell^a X_\ell^a V_\Lambda
= \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\sum_{\ell\in\partial p}\sum_a X_\ell^aX_\ell^a \Phi(z_p).
\tag{J.37}
]
Using the one-variable chain rule with (\Phi\in C^2),
[
X_\ell^aX_\ell^a \Phi(z_p)
= \Phi'(z_p),X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p + \Phi''(z_p),(X_\ell^a z_p)^2.
\tag{J.38}
]

We bound the two terms separately.

#### J.6.1 Bounding (\Phi'(z_p),X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p) without (|P|)

By Lemma J.6, (|X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p|\le C_2) uniformly. Then by (J.35),
[
\big|\Phi'(z_p),X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p\big|
\le C_2,\Phi'(z_p)
\le C_2,C_\Phi,z_p.
\tag{J.39}
]
Summing over (\ell\in\partial p) (four links) and over (a) (finitely many), we get
[
\sum_{\ell\in\partial p}\sum_a \big|\Phi'(z_p),X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p\big|
\le C,z_p,
\tag{J.40}
]
where (C) depends only on (N,\lambda) (through (C_2) and the basis size) and on the fixed plaquette combinatorics (four links), but **not** on (|\Lambda|).

Crucially, the right-hand side is proportional to (z_p), not to (1). Thus summing (J.40) over all plaquettes yields a bound proportional to (\sum_p z_p), not (|P(\Lambda)|).

This is exactly the bookkeeping fix described informally in Part 15.10.2: the obstruction was the inability to bound (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p) uniformly (and hence to prevent a per-plaquette constant); with a smooth proxy, that uniform bound holds and (\Phi'(0)=0) ensures the resulting term is weighted by (z_p). 

#### J.6.2 Bounding the carré-du-champ term (\Phi''(z_p)(X_\ell^a z_p)^2)

Assume (\Phi'') is bounded on ([0,2]): (|\Phi''|_\infty<\infty). (For (\Phi(s)=s^2), (\Phi''\equiv 2).)

Then by Lemma J.4,
[
\Phi''(z_p),(X_\ell^a z_p)^2
\le |\Phi''|_\infty \cdot \frac{2}{N\lambda},z_p.
\tag{J.41}
]
Again the right-hand side is proportional to (z_p), not to (1), so after summing over the finitely many (\ell\in\partial p) and (a), this contributes at most (C,z_p) per plaquette.

### J.7. Application to the Lyapunov candidate (W_\Lambda=\exp(\eta V_\Lambda))

Define for (\eta>0),
[
W_\Lambda(U):=\exp(\eta V_\Lambda(U)),
\qquad V_\Lambda(U):=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\Phi(z_p(U)).
\tag{J.42}
]

A standard computation (and explicitly used throughout Part 15’s Lyapunov discussion) gives
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
= \eta,L_\Lambda V_\Lambda + \eta^2,\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda).
\tag{J.43}
]
(One can check this directly from (L(e^{\eta V}) = e^{\eta V}(\eta LV + \eta^2\Gamma(V))) using the derivation property of (\Gamma); see the general Part 15 diffusion setup. )

Now split (L_\Lambda V_\Lambda = \Delta_\Lambda V_\Lambda - \langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla V_\Lambda\rangle). Using (J.37)–(J.41) and the fact that only links in (\partial p) contribute to derivatives of (z_p), we obtain:

**Proposition J.7 (uniform control of the diffusion part of (L_\Lambda V_\Lambda)).**
Let (z_p(U)=1-\frac1N\mathrm{ReTr}(U_p(U))) and let (\Phi) satisfy (J.35) with (|\Phi''|*\infty<\infty). Then there exists (C=C(N,\lambda,d,\Phi)) such that for all finite lattices (\Lambda) and all (U\in M*\Lambda),
[
\Delta_\Lambda V_\Lambda(U);\le;C,\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} z_p(U).
\tag{J.44}
]
In particular, **no term of the form (C,|P(\Lambda)|)** appears on the right-hand side.

*Proof.* Expand (\Delta_\Lambda V_\Lambda) as in (J.37)–(J.38). Bound the (\Phi'(z_p)X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p) term using (J.39)–(J.40) and the (\Phi''(z_p)(X_\ell^a z_p)^2) term using (J.41). All sums over (\ell\in\partial p) and (a) are finite with constants independent of (\Lambda). Summing over (p) yields (J.44). ∎

This proposition is the formal version of the “(\Phi'(0)=0) prevents an (O(|P|)) leakage from the Laplacian/Hessian term” heuristic you wanted to install.

Finally, combining (J.43) and (J.44) yields the explicit “fixed bottleneck” inequality:

**Corollary J.8 (the former bottleneck term is now uniformly bounded).**
With the same hypotheses,
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
;\le;
\eta,C\sum_{p} z_p
;-;\eta,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla V_\Lambda\rangle
;+;\eta^2,\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda),
\tag{J.45}
]
where the constant (C) is independent of (\Lambda). In particular, the contribution coming from the second derivatives (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p) is now uniformly controlled, exactly addressing the obstruction identified in Part 15.10.2. 

*Proof.* Combine (J.43) with the estimate (\Delta_\Lambda V_\Lambda\le C\sum_p z_p). ∎

### J.8. Concrete choice (\Phi(s)=s^2)

The choice explicitly suggested in your instruction is (\Phi(s)=s^2), for which (\Phi'(s)=2s) and (\Phi''(s)=2). Then (V_\Lambda=\sum_p z_p^2) and (J.44) becomes especially transparent:

**Corollary J.9 (explicit bound for (\Phi(s)=s^2)).**
Let (V_\Lambda(U):=\sum_{p} z_p(U)^2). Then there exists (C=C(N,\lambda,d)) such that
[
\Delta_\Lambda V_\Lambda(U)\le C,\sum_{p} z_p(U).
\tag{J.46}
]
Equivalently, for each plaquette,
[
\Delta_\Lambda (z_p^2);\lesssim; z_p,
\tag{J.47}
]
uniformly in (U) and (\Lambda).

*Proof.* With (\Phi(s)=s^2), equation (J.38) reads
[
X_\ell^aX_\ell^a(z_p^2)=2z_p,X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p + 2(X_\ell^a z_p)^2.
]
Use Lemma J.6 to bound (|X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p|) uniformly and Lemma J.4 to bound ((X_\ell^a z_p)^2\le c,z_p). Summing over (\ell,a) and then over (p) yields (J.46). ∎

This is exactly the mechanism you asked for: the “Laplacian/Hessian term” is automatically weighted by (z_p) because of the (2z_p) factor, and therefore it cannot produce an unweighted (|P(\Lambda)|) contribution.
