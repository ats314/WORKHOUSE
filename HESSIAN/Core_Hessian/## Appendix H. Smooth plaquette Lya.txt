## Appendix H. Smooth plaquette Lyapunov potential and the drift computation with volume‑uniform constants

This appendix replaces the nonsmooth plaquette potential (z_p=d_G(U_p,\mathbf 1)^2) by a globally smooth, conjugation‑invariant proxy and re-runs the Lyapunov drift computation, isolating all constants and verifying that the previously uncontrolled second‑derivative term is uniformly bounded, as required by the bottleneck diagnosis in the analytic engine module. 

### H.1. Configuration diffusion and (\Gamma)–calculus identities

Let (G=\mathrm{SU}(N)). For a finite lattice (\Lambda), write
[
\Omega_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)}
]
for link configurations, equipped with the product Riemannian metric (g_\Lambda) induced from a fixed bi‑invariant metric (g_G) on (G). (A choice of Ad‑invariant inner product on (\mathfrak g) fixes (g_G); left and right multiplications are isometries for (g_G).) 

Fix an orthonormal basis ({T^a}*{a=1}^{\dim G}) of (\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{su}(N)) with respect to the chosen inner product. For each link (\ell\in E(\Lambda)), define the right‑invariant vector field (X*\ell^a) acting on smooth (F:\Omega_\Lambda\to\mathbb R) by
[
(X_\ell^a F)(U)\ :=\ \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|*{t=0}F(U^{\ell,a}(t)),
\qquad
U^{\ell,a}(t)*{\ell'} :=
\begin{cases}
U_\ell,\exp(tT^a), & \ell'=\ell,\
U_{\ell'}, & \ell'\neq \ell,
\end{cases}
]
i.e. infinitesimal right multiplication on the (\ell)-coordinate. 

Define the configuration Laplacian
[
\Delta_\Lambda := \sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}\sum_{a=1}^{\dim G}(X_\ell^a)^2.
]
(Equivalently, (\Delta_\Lambda) is the Laplace–Beltrami operator on ((\Omega_\Lambda,g_\Lambda)) because the frame ({X_\ell^a}) is orthonormal and the metric is a product of bi‑invariant metrics.) 

Let (S_\Lambda\in C^2(\Omega_\Lambda)) be the (gauge‑invariant) action defining the finite‑volume Gibbs measure (d\mu_\Lambda\propto e^{-S_\Lambda},d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}). The associated reversible diffusion generator is
[
L_\Lambda F\ :=\ \Delta_\Lambda F\ -\ \langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla F\rangle_{g_\Lambda}.
\tag{H.1}
]
This is the standard “Laplacian minus gradient drift” form. 

The carré du champ is
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(F,G)
:=\frac12\Big(L_\Lambda(FG)-F,L_\Lambda G-G,L_\Lambda F\Big)
=\sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}\langle\nabla_\ell F,\nabla_\ell G\rangle_{\mathfrak g}
=\sum_{\ell,a}(X_\ell^aF)(X_\ell^aG),
\tag{H.2}
]
and in particular (\Gamma_\Lambda(F):=\Gamma_\Lambda(F,F)=|\nabla F|^2_{g_\Lambda}). 

A key chain rule that will be used repeatedly is the exponential identity (Lemma 14.3 in the project):
[
L_\Lambda(e^{\eta V})
=e^{\eta V}\Big(\eta,L_\Lambda V+\eta^2,\Gamma_\Lambda(V)\Big),
\qquad V\in C^2(\Omega_\Lambda),\ \eta\in\mathbb R.
\tag{H.3}
]


For completeness, a proof of (H.3) is given in §H.4.

### H.2. A globally smooth conjugation‑invariant plaquette proxy

Define the class function
[
\widetilde z:G\to\mathbb R,\qquad
\widetilde z(g):=1-\frac1N\operatorname{ReTr}(g).
\tag{H.4}
]
This is explicitly proposed in the proof stack as a replacement for (d_G(g,\mathbf 1)^2) to avoid cut‑locus nonsmoothness and to make the second derivatives uniformly bounded. 

The same function appears as the canonical gauge‑invariant “plaquette energy” used to define the good set in the fused outline. 

#### Lemma H.1 (Basic properties of (\widetilde z))

For (G=\mathrm{SU}(N)), (\widetilde z) satisfies:

1. (\widetilde z\in C^\infty(G)) and is conjugation invariant: (\widetilde z(hgh^{-1})=\widetilde z(g)) for all (g,h\in G).
2. (\widetilde z(g)\ge 0) for all (g\in G), and (\widetilde z(g)=0) iff (g=\mathbf 1).
3. (\widetilde z) is bounded: (0\le \widetilde z(g)\le 2) for all (g\in G).

**Proof.**

1. Smoothness: (g\mapsto \operatorname{Tr}(g)) is a polynomial in the matrix entries, hence smooth on the embedded submanifold (G\subset \mathbb C^{N\times N}). Taking real part preserves smoothness. Conjugation invariance follows from (\operatorname{Tr}(hgh^{-1})=\operatorname{Tr}(g)).

2. Let (g\in \mathrm{SU}(N)). Since (g) is unitary, it is diagonalizable with eigenvalues ({e^{i\theta_j}}*{j=1}^N) on the unit circle. Then
   [
   \operatorname{ReTr}(g)=\sum*{j=1}^N \cos\theta_j \le \sum_{j=1}^N 1 = N,
   ]
   hence (\widetilde z(g)=1-\frac1N\operatorname{ReTr}(g)\ge 0). Equality (\widetilde z(g)=0) forces (\operatorname{ReTr}(g)=N), which implies (\cos\theta_j=1) for all (j), so all eigenvalues are (1), hence (g=\mathbf 1).

3. Since (\cos\theta_j\ge -1), we have (\operatorname{ReTr}(g)\ge -N), so (\widetilde z(g)\le 1-\frac1N(-N)=2). ∎

### H.3. Uniform first/second derivative bounds and their lift to plaquettes

The obstruction identified in the analytic engine is the term (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p) in the drift computation when (z_p=d_G(U_p,\mathbf 1)^2), because distance‑squared is not globally smooth on compact manifolds and can develop unbounded second derivatives near the cut locus. 

Replacing (z_p) by (\widetilde z_p:=\widetilde z(U_p)) eliminates this specific analytic obstruction, because (\widetilde z) is (C^\infty) on compact (G). 

We now record the uniform derivative bounds in a form directly usable in the configuration‑space drift estimate.

#### Lemma H.2 (Uniform derivative bounds on (G))

Let ((G,g_G)) be (\mathrm{SU}(N)) with a fixed bi‑invariant metric. There exist finite constants (C_1,C_2\in(0,\infty)), depending only on ((G,g_G)), such that for all (g\in G),
[
|\nabla_G \widetilde z(g)|*{g_G}\le C_1,
\qquad
|\nabla_G^2 \widetilde z(g)|*{\mathrm{op}}\le C_2.
\tag{H.5}
]
Consequently, the group Laplacian satisfies (|\Delta_G\widetilde z(g)|\le (\dim G)C_2) for all (g\in G).

**Proof.**
(\widetilde z\in C^\infty(G)) by Lemma H.1, hence (\nabla_G\widetilde z) and (\nabla_G^2\widetilde z) are continuous tensor fields on (G). The functions
[
g\mapsto |\nabla_G \widetilde z(g)|*{g_G},
\qquad
g\mapsto |\nabla_G^2 \widetilde z(g)|*{\mathrm{op}}
]
are continuous. Since (G) is compact, each attains its maximum, defining finite constants (C_1,C_2).

For the Laplacian bound, take an orthonormal basis ({e_a}) of (T_gG); then (\Delta_G \widetilde z(g)=\sum_a \nabla_G^2 \widetilde z(g)[e_a,e_a]), so
[
|\Delta_G\widetilde z(g)|
\le \sum_a |\nabla_G^2\widetilde z(g)|*{\mathrm{op}}|e_a|^2
=(\dim G),|\nabla_G^2\widetilde z(g)|*{\mathrm{op}}
\le (\dim G)C_2.
]
∎

#### Lemma H.3 (Uniform derivative bounds for plaquette lifts)

Let (p\in P(\Lambda)) be a plaquette and define (\widetilde z_p:\Omega_\Lambda\to\mathbb R) by
[
\widetilde z_p(U):=\widetilde z(U_p(U)),
\tag{H.6}
]
where (U_p(U)\in G) is the oriented plaquette holonomy. Then:

1. If (\ell\notin \partial p), then (X_\ell^a\widetilde z_p\equiv 0) and (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a\widetilde z_p\equiv 0).
2. If (\ell\in\partial p), then for every (U\in\Omega_\Lambda) and every (a),
   [
   |X_\ell^a \widetilde z_p(U)|\le C_1,
   \qquad
   |X_\ell^aX_\ell^a \widetilde z_p(U)|\le C_2,
   \tag{H.7}
   ]
   with the same constants (C_1,C_2) as in Lemma H.2, hence independent of (\Lambda), (p), (\ell).

**Proof.**

1. If (\ell\notin\partial p), then (U_p(U)) does not depend on (U_\ell); by definition of (X_\ell^a) as differentiation in (U_\ell), the first and second derivatives vanish.

2. Fix (\ell\in\partial p). Write the plaquette holonomy as an ordered product of four link variables and inverses. Along the curve (t\mapsto U^{\ell,a}(t)) that right‑multiplies (U_\ell) by (\exp(tT^a)), the plaquette holonomy evolves as
   [
   U_p\big(U^{\ell,a}(t)\big)=A(U),\exp(\pm tT^a),B(U)
   \tag{H.8}
   ]
   for some smooth (A(U),B(U)\in G) independent of (t) (the sign depends on whether (U_\ell) appears as (U_\ell) or (U_\ell^{-1}) in the plaquette word). Left and right multiplications (g\mapsto A g B) are isometries of ((G,g_G)) because (g_G) is bi‑invariant. 

Therefore (t\mapsto U_p(U^{\ell,a}(t))) is a unit‑speed (up to the fixed normalization of (T^a)) curve in (G) obtained by composing a one‑parameter subgroup with isometries, and differentiation of (\widetilde z) along this curve is bounded by the global sup norms of (|\nabla_G\widetilde z|) and (|\nabla_G^2\widetilde z|). Concretely,
[
X_\ell^a \widetilde z_p(U)
=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|*{0}\widetilde z!\left(U_p(U^{\ell,a}(t))\right)
]
is the directional derivative of (\widetilde z) at the point (U_p(U)) along a unit tangent vector (again, because of bi‑invariance). Hence
[
|X*\ell^a \widetilde z_p(U)|\le \sup_{g\in G}|\nabla_G\widetilde z(g)|=C_1.
]
Similarly, the second derivative (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a\widetilde z_p(U)) is bounded in absolute value by (\sup_{g\in G}|\nabla_G^2\widetilde z(g)|_{\mathrm{op}}=C_2). ∎

### H.4. The drift computation for (W_\Lambda=\exp(\eta V_\Lambda))

We now compute (L_\Lambda W_\Lambda) for a standard exponential Lyapunov candidate and isolate precisely how the choice (\Phi'(0)=0) prevents a volume‑proportional constant term from appearing.

#### H.4.1. Chain rule for (L_\Lambda) and (e^{\eta V})

We restate and prove (H.3).

#### Lemma H.4 (Exponential chain rule)

Let (L_\Lambda) be of the form (H.1) with carré du champ (\Gamma_\Lambda) as in (H.2). For (V\in C^2(\Omega_\Lambda)) and (\eta\in\mathbb R),
[
L_\Lambda(e^{\eta V})
=e^{\eta V}\Big(\eta,L_\Lambda V+\eta^2,\Gamma_\Lambda(V)\Big).
\tag{H.9}
]

**Proof.**
Write (W=e^{\eta V}). For each vector field (X_\ell^a),
[
X_\ell^a W=\eta,W,X_\ell^a V,\qquad
(X_\ell^a)^2W=\eta,W,(X_\ell^a)^2V+\eta^2,W,(X_\ell^aV)^2.
]
Summing over ((\ell,a)) gives
[
\Delta_\Lambda W
=\eta,W,\Delta_\Lambda V+\eta^2,W,\sum_{\ell,a}(X_\ell^aV)^2
=\eta,W,\Delta_\Lambda V+\eta^2,W,\Gamma_\Lambda(V).
]
For the drift term, using (\nabla W=\eta W \nabla V),
[
\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla W\rangle
=\eta,W,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla V\rangle.
]
Substituting into (L_\Lambda W=\Delta_\Lambda W-\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla W\rangle) yields
[
L_\Lambda W
=W\Big(\eta(\Delta_\Lambda V-\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla V\rangle)+\eta^2\Gamma_\Lambda(V)\Big)
=W\Big(\eta L_\Lambda V+\eta^2\Gamma_\Lambda(V)\Big).
]
∎

This is the same identity recorded in the project’s Lyapunov template.

### H.5. Choice of (\Phi) with (\Phi'(0)=0) and elimination of the (\mathcal O(|P(\Lambda)|)) constant

#### H.5.1. The Lyapunov candidate

Fix (\Phi\in C^2([0,2])) with
[
\Phi(0)=0,\qquad \Phi'(0)=0,\qquad \Phi(s)\ge 0.
\tag{H.10}
]
The concrete choice requested in the task is
[
\Phi(s)=s^2,\qquad \Phi'(s)=2s,\qquad \Phi''(s)=2.
\tag{H.11}
]

Define
[
V_\Lambda(U)\ :=\ \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\Phi(\widetilde z_p(U)),
\qquad
W_\Lambda(U)\ :=\ \exp(\eta V_\Lambda(U)),
\quad \eta>0.
\tag{H.12}
]

The good region (used elsewhere for local functional inequalities) is typically defined by averaged plaquette badness; e.g.
[
K_\Lambda(\varepsilon)\ :=\ \Big{U:\ \frac1{|P(\Lambda)|}\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\widetilde z_p(U)\le \varepsilon\Big}.
\tag{H.13}
]
Such “averaged” definitions are explicitly recommended for volume‑stable concentration estimates. 

#### H.5.2. Computation of (L_\Lambda V_\Lambda)

We compute (L_\Lambda V_\Lambda) term‑by‑term.

Fix a single plaquette (p) and write (u=\widetilde z_p). Since (u\in C^\infty(\Omega_\Lambda)), (\Phi\circ u\in C^2), and for each ((\ell,a)),
[
X_\ell^a(\Phi(u))=\Phi'(u),X_\ell^a u,
\qquad
(X_\ell^a)^2(\Phi(u))=\Phi''(u),(X_\ell^a u)^2+\Phi'(u),(X_\ell^a)^2u.
\tag{H.14}
]
Summing gives
[
\Delta_\Lambda(\Phi(u))
=\Phi''(u),\Gamma_\Lambda(u)+\Phi'(u),\Delta_\Lambda u.
\tag{H.15}
]
Moreover, (\nabla(\Phi(u))=\Phi'(u)\nabla u), hence
[
\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla(\Phi(u))\rangle
=\Phi'(u),\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla u\rangle.
\tag{H.16}
]
Combining (H.15)–(H.16) yields
[
L_\Lambda(\Phi(u))
=\Phi''(u),\Gamma_\Lambda(u)+\Phi'(u),L_\Lambda u.
\tag{H.17}
]
Therefore, summing over (p),
[
L_\Lambda V_\Lambda
=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\Big(\Phi''(\widetilde z_p),\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)+\Phi'(\widetilde z_p),L_\Lambda\widetilde z_p\Big).
\tag{H.18}
]

This is the precise version of the schematic drift expansion recorded in the analytic engine discussion of the obstruction term. 

#### H.5.3. Where the (\mathcal O(|P(\Lambda)|)) leakage occurs and how (\Phi'(0)=0) removes it

Write
[
L_\Lambda \widetilde z_p=\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p-\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla \widetilde z_p\rangle.
\tag{H.19}
]
The “leakage” issue is the Laplacian part in (H.18):
[
\sum_{p}\Phi'(\widetilde z_p),\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p.
\tag{H.20}
]

* If one takes (\Phi(s)=s), then (\Phi'(s)\equiv 1) and
  [
  \sum_p \Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p
  ]
  is bounded only by a constant times (|P(\Lambda)|), because (\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p) is uniformly bounded but does not vanish pointwise. This is the mechanism by which a volume‑proportional constant can appear in (L_\Lambda W_\Lambda/W_\Lambda).

* If one takes (\Phi) with (\Phi'(0)=0), and in particular (\Phi(s)=s^2) so that (\Phi'(s)=2s), then each term in (H.20) carries a factor (\widetilde z_p), and thus (H.20) is controlled by (\sum_p \widetilde z_p), not by (|P(\Lambda)|) alone.

We now record this as a clean uniform estimate.

#### Proposition H.5 (Uniform bound on the Laplacian/Hessian contribution; no (|P(\Lambda)|)-constant)

Let (\Phi(s)=s^2). Then for all (\Lambda) and all (U\in\Omega_\Lambda),
[
\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\Phi'(\widetilde z_p(U)),\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p(U)
===============================================================================

2\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\widetilde z_p(U),\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p(U)
\le 2C_\Delta \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\widetilde z_p(U),
\tag{H.21}
]
where
[
C_\Delta := 4,(\dim G),C_2
\tag{H.22}
]
and (C_2) is the global Hessian bound in Lemma H.2. In particular, no term of the form (c,|P(\Lambda)|) appears in (H.21).

**Proof.**
By Lemma H.3, ((X_\ell^a)^2\widetilde z_p\equiv 0) unless (\ell\in\partial p), and for (\ell\in\partial p),
(|(X_\ell^a)^2\widetilde z_p|\le C_2).
Hence
[
|\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p|
=\Big|\sum_{\ell,a}(X_\ell^a)^2\widetilde z_p\Big|
=\Big|\sum_{\ell\in\partial p}\sum_{a}(X_\ell^a)^2\widetilde z_p\Big|
\le |\partial p|,(\dim G),C_2
=4(\dim G)C_2
=:C_\Delta.
]
Since (\widetilde z_p\ge 0), we obtain pointwise
[
2,\widetilde z_p,\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p
\le 2,\widetilde z_p,|\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p|
\le 2C_\Delta,\widetilde z_p.
]
Summing over (p) yields (H.21). ∎

This is exactly the requested “no (\mathcal O(|P|)) leakage” mechanism: with (\Phi'(0)=0) the Laplacian/Hessian term is automatically weighted by (\widetilde z_p), not by (1).

### H.6. Full expression for (L_\Lambda W_\Lambda/W_\Lambda) and uniform control of the previously missing term

Using Lemma H.4 and (H.18),
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
=====================================

\eta,L_\Lambda V_\Lambda+\eta^2,\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda).
\tag{H.23}
]

For (\Phi(s)=s^2), (H.18) becomes
[
L_\Lambda V_\Lambda
=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\Big(
2,\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)
+
2,\widetilde z_p,L_\Lambda\widetilde z_p
\Big),
\tag{H.24}
]
and inserting (L_\Lambda\widetilde z_p=\Delta_\Lambda\widetilde z_p-\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla\widetilde z_p\rangle) gives the exact decomposition
[
L_\Lambda V_\Lambda
===================

\underbrace{2\sum_{p}\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)}*{\text{(I) gradient-square}}
+
\underbrace{2\sum*{p}\widetilde z_p,\Delta_\Lambda\widetilde z_p}_{\text{(II) Laplacian/Hessian}}
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\underbrace{2\sum_{p}\widetilde z_p,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla\widetilde z_p\rangle}_{\text{(III) drift alignment}}.
\tag{H.25}
]

The term (II) is the location of the formerly uncontrolled obstruction (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p) when (z_p=d_G^2). 
By Lemma H.3, for the smooth proxy (\widetilde z_p) the second derivatives (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a\widetilde z_p) are uniformly bounded by (C_2), hence (II) is controlled by Proposition H.5. This closes the specific analytic gap identified in the Part 15 bottleneck statement. 

It remains to control (\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)) uniformly (for the (\eta^2)-term). This can be bounded using only locality and the uniform first-derivative bound (C_1).

#### Lemma H.6 (Uniform bound on (\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)) in terms of local quantities)

Let (V_\Lambda=\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2) as above. Let
[
n_*:=\max_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}#{p\in P(\Lambda):\ell\in\partial p}.
\tag{H.26}
]
On the 4D hypercubic lattice, (n_*\le 6). Then for all (U),
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)(U)
\le 4n_*,\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\widetilde z_p(U)^2,\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)(U),
\tag{H.27}
]
and in particular, using (\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)\le 4(\dim G)C_1^2),
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)(U)\le C_V,V_\Lambda(U),
\qquad
C_V := 16,n_*,(\dim G),C_1^2.
\tag{H.28}
]

**Proof.**
Differentiate (V_\Lambda=\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2):
[
X_\ell^a V_\Lambda=\sum_{p:\ell\in\partial p} 2\widetilde z_p,X_\ell^a\widetilde z_p.
]
By Cauchy–Schwarz, for a sum of at most (n_*) terms,
[
(X_\ell^a V_\Lambda)^2
\le n_* \sum_{p:\ell\in\partial p} \big(2\widetilde z_p,X_\ell^a\widetilde z_p\big)^2
=4n_*\sum_{p:\ell\in\partial p}\widetilde z_p^2,(X_\ell^a\widetilde z_p)^2.
]
Sum over ((\ell,a)):
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)
=\sum_{\ell,a}(X_\ell^a V_\Lambda)^2
\le 4n_*\sum_{p}\widetilde z_p^2\sum_{\ell\in\partial p,a}(X_\ell^a\widetilde z_p)^2
=4n_*\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2,\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p),
]
which is (H.27). The bound (H.28) follows from Lemma H.3:
for each (p), (\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)) is a sum over 4 links and (\dim G) directions, each term bounded by (C_1^2), hence (\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)\le 4(\dim G)C_1^2). ∎

Combining (H.23), (H.25), Proposition H.5, and Lemma H.6 yields the explicit, uniform drift bound
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
\le
\eta\Big(
2\sum_{p}\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)
+
2C_\Delta\sum_{p}\widetilde z_p
-------------------------------

2\sum_{p}\widetilde z_p,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla\widetilde z_p\rangle
\Big)
+\eta^2 C_V,V_\Lambda.
\tag{H.29}
]

All constants in (H.29) are uniform in (\Lambda):
[
C_\Delta = 4(\dim G)C_2,\qquad C_V=16n_*(\dim G)C_1^2,
]
where (C_1,C_2) depend only on the group metric, and (n_*) depends only on the lattice dimension.

### H.7. What is proved here, and what remains to obtain a full Foster–Lyapunov inequality

The program’s Lyapunov requirement is to produce (W_\Lambda\ge 1) and (\lambda,b>0) uniform in (\Lambda) such that
[
L_\Lambda W_\Lambda \le -\lambda W_\Lambda + b,\mathbf 1_{K_\Lambda},
\tag{H.30}
]
or, for entropy control, a strengthened version of the form
[
L_\Lambda W_\Lambda \le (-c,\mathcal D_\Lambda+b),W_\Lambda
\quad\text{with }\mathcal D_\Lambda\sim \sum_p \widetilde z_p \text{ or }\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2,
\tag{H.31}
]
as stated in the analytic engine module. 

**Proved in this appendix (unconditional).**

1. The replacement (\widetilde z(g)=1-\frac1N\operatorname{ReTr}(g)) is (C^\infty), conjugation‑invariant, nonnegative, and vanishes only at (\mathbf 1) (Lemma H.1).
2. The previously uncontrolled term (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p) becomes uniformly bounded:
   [
   \sup_{\Lambda,p,\ell,a,U}|X_\ell^aX_\ell^a\widetilde z_p(U)|\le C_2,
   ]
   so the specific analytic obstruction singled out in Part 15 is removed (Lemma H.3), exactly as suggested in the obstruction analysis. 
3. With (\Phi'(0)=0) (in particular (\Phi(s)=s^2)), the Laplacian/Hessian contribution to (L_\Lambda V_\Lambda) is weighted by (\widetilde z_p) and therefore admits the uniform bound (H.21), i.e. it does **not** generate an additive term proportional to (|P(\Lambda)|) (Proposition H.5).

**Not proved in this appendix (still required to deduce (H.30)/(H.31)).**

The remaining term in (H.29) that must provide the negative drift is the drift alignment term
[
-2\eta\sum_{p}\widetilde z_p,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla\widetilde z_p\rangle,
\tag{H.32}
]
together with absorption of the positive terms (2\eta\sum_p\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)) and (\eta^2 C_V V_\Lambda) by choosing (\eta) suitably small.

A complete Foster–Lyapunov inequality (H.30) or strengthened inequality (H.31) requires a **coercivity estimate** comparing the drift generated by (S_\Lambda) with the plaquette observable (\widetilde z_p). This coercivity input is exactly the global “measure concentration / Lyapunov” bottleneck emphasized in the analytic engine summary. 

Formally, one sufficient condition is:

> **Coercivity input needed to close drift (explicit).** There exist constants (c_0>0), (b_0\ge 0), independent of (\Lambda), and a set (K_\Lambda) (the region where local functional inequalities are proved) such that for all (U),
> [
> \sum_{p}\widetilde z_p(U),\langle\nabla S_\Lambda(U),\nabla\widetilde z_p(U)\rangle
> \ \ge\
> c_0,\mathcal D_\Lambda(U)\ -\ b_0,\mathbf 1_{K_\Lambda}(U),
> \tag{H.33}
> ]
> with (\mathcal D_\Lambda) comparable to (\sum_p\widetilde z_p) or (\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2).

Given (H.33), (H.29) immediately yields (H.31) after choosing (\eta>0) small enough (depending only on (c_0,C_\Delta,C_V) and on the comparison between (\mathcal D_\Lambda) and the positive terms), because the remaining constants are volume‑uniform by construction.

### H.8. Summary of the effect of the replacement

* The original geodesic‑distance choice (z_p=d_G(U_p,\mathbf 1)^2) fails at the level of global (C^2) control; the analytic engine isolates the precise failure as the term (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p) in the drift calculation. 
* Replacing (z_p) by the smooth class function (\widetilde z_p=1-\frac1N\operatorname{ReTr}(U_p)) makes (X_\ell^aX_\ell^a\widetilde z_p) uniformly bounded and therefore removes this obstruction. 
* Choosing (\Phi) with (\Phi'(0)=0), specifically (\Phi(s)=s^2), forces the Laplacian/Hessian part of (L_\Lambda V_\Lambda) to be weighted by (\widetilde z_p), preventing a bare (\mathcal O(|P(\Lambda)|)) constant from appearing in (L_\Lambda W_\Lambda/W_\Lambda) (Proposition H.5).

This appendix supplies the complete, volume‑uniform second‑derivative control and the corrected weighting mechanism requested in the task. The remaining step for a full global Lyapunov theorem is the coercivity estimate (H.33) for the specific Yang–Mills action (S_\Lambda), which is outside the scope of this appendix and is explicitly flagged elsewhere as the global bottleneck.
