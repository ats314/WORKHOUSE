Part 11 — Core Curvature Theorem and Interface to Part I

In Parts 6–10 we assembled the geometric ingredients needed to control the Bakry–Émery curvature of finite-volume lattice Yang–Mills in the physical (gauge-invariant) directions:

* The product configuration manifold (M_\Lambda = G^{E(\Lambda)}) with product bi-invariant metric (g_\Lambda) and product Haar volume, whose Ricci tensor satisfies (\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} = \kappa_G g_\Lambda) with (\kappa_G>0) independent of (\Lambda). 
* The Wilson action (S_W), the gauge group (\mathcal{G}*\Lambda), gauge orbits, and the horizontal (physical) subbundle (H_U\subset T_U M*\Lambda) defined as the orthogonal complement of gauge directions. Gauge-invariant observables have gradients (\nabla f(U)\in H_U).
* The Haar volume in exponential coordinates, which produces a strictly convex “Haar mass” potential with Hessian controlled from below by the group Ricci tensor. 
* The Wilson Hessian and its behavior on pure gauge, harmonic, and co-exact modes, and the local horizontal Bakry–Émery bound near the trivial configuration (U^{(0)}). 

The goal of this part is to package those ingredients into a single **Core Curvature Theorem** that matches the abstract “horizontal curvature” hypothesis (H_{\mathrm{curv}}) from Part I. 

We work throughout with the **Riemannian-volume-as-reference** picture from Part I:

* reference measure on (M_\Lambda): (d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}),
* effective action (S_\Lambda),
* Gibbs measure
  [
  d\mu_\Lambda(U) = Z_\Lambda^{-1} e^{-S_\Lambda(U)}, d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U),
  ]
* generator
  [
  L_\Lambda f = \Delta_{g_\Lambda} f - \langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla f\rangle_{g_\Lambda},
  ]
* carré du champ (\Gamma_\Lambda(f) = |\nabla f|*{g*\Lambda}^2),
* Bakry–Émery tensor (\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda} = \operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} + \nabla^2 S_\Lambda).

## 11.1. Hypotheses on the group, geometry, and effective action

We recall and freeze the structural hypotheses introduced in Part II.

### 11.1.1. Group and configuration manifold

* (G) is a compact, connected Lie group (e.g. (SU(N))) equipped with a fixed bi-invariant Riemannian metric (g_G).

* The corresponding Ricci tensor satisfies
  [
  \operatorname{Ric}_G \ge \kappa_G, g_G
  ]
  for some constant (\kappa_G>0).

* For a finite lattice (\Lambda), the configuration manifold of link variables is
  [
  M_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)},
  ]
  with product metric (g_\Lambda) and product Haar volume. The Ricci tensor of ((M_\Lambda,g_\Lambda)) is
  [
  \operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} = \kappa_G, g_\Lambda,
  ]
  so in particular
  [
  \operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} \ge \kappa_G, g_\Lambda
  ]
  with (\kappa_G) **independent of (\Lambda)**. 

### 11.1.2. Gauge group, orbits, and horizontal directions

* The lattice gauge group (\mathcal{G}*\Lambda = G^{V(\Lambda)}) acts on (M*\Lambda) by endpoint conjugation:
  [
  (g\cdot U)*{x,\mu} = g_x U*{x,\mu} g_{x+\hat\mu}^{-1}.
  ]
  This action is smooth, isometric for (g_\Lambda), and preserves Haar volume. 

* For each (U\in M_\Lambda):

  * The gauge orbit (\mathcal{O}*U = {g\cdot U : g\in\mathcal{G}*\Lambda}) is an immersed submanifold.
  * The **vertical subspace** (V_U := T_U\mathcal{O}_U) is spanned by fundamental vector fields of the gauge action.
  * The **horizontal subspace** (H_U) is defined as the orthogonal complement:
    [
    T_U M_\Lambda = H_U \oplus V_U, \qquad H_U := V_U^\perp.
    ]

* Let (\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) be the algebra of smooth gauge-invariant observables:
  [
  \mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}} := {f\in C^\infty(M_\Lambda) : f(g\cdot U) = f(U)\ \forall g,U}.
  ]
  For any (f\in\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}), one has
  [
  \nabla f(U)\in H_U \quad\forall U\in M*\Lambda,
  ]
  i.e. **gradients of gauge-invariant functions are horizontal**.

This is the geometric content that will let us restrict curvature considerations to physical directions.

### 11.1.3. Effective action and additional terms

We consider a family of effective actions
[
S_\Lambda(U) = S_W(U) + S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U)
]
with the following properties:

1. **Wilson action (S_W):**

   * Defined as a local sum of plaquette terms:
     [
     S_W(U) = \beta \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\Big(1 - \frac{1}{N}\Re\operatorname{Tr}U_p(U)\Big),
     ]
     smooth and gauge-invariant. 

   * In right-invariant exponential coordinates (X\in\mathcal{C}^1(\Lambda;\mathfrak{g})) near (U^{(0)}), its Hessian at the trivial configuration is
     [
     \nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)}) = 2c_W, d_1^* d_1
     ]
     with (c_W>0) and (d_1) the discrete edge–plaquette coboundary operator. On co-exact (physical) modes this is strictly positive; on pure gauge + harmonic modes it vanishes.

2. **Additional terms (S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}):**

   * Smooth and gauge-invariant.
   * **Local**: finite sum of translates of a fixed local function (e.g. gauge-fixing or regulator terms).
   * Hessian bounded below **uniformly in (\Lambda)**:
     [
     \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U) \ge -C_{\mathrm{add}},g_\Lambda(U)
     \qquad\forall U\in M_\Lambda,
     ]
     with (C_{\mathrm{add}}\ge 0) independent of (\Lambda).

3. **Dominance of group curvature:**

   * The metric (g_G) is chosen so that
     [
     \kappa_G > C_{\mathrm{add}},
     ]
     equivalently
     [
     \rho_0 := \kappa_G - C_{\mathrm{add}} > 0.
     ]

Under these assumptions the Bakry–Émery tensor at (U^{(0)}) is
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U^{(0)})
= \operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda}(U^{(0)}) + \nabla^2 S_\Lambda(U^{(0)}),
]
and its restriction to horizontal directions will be strictly positive with a bound of order (\rho_0).

## 11.2. Horizontal Bakry–Émery curvature at the trivial configuration

We first recall the precise horizontal curvature bound at the vacuum derived in Part II.

### 11.2.1. Splitting Ricci + Hessian in horizontal directions

By definition,
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}
= \operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} + \nabla^2 S_\Lambda
= \kappa_G,g_\Lambda + \nabla^2 S_W + \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}.
]

At (U^{(0)}), let (v\in H_{U^{(0)}}). Then
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v)
= \kappa_G |v|*{g*\Lambda}^2
+ \nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})(v,v)
+ \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v).
]

Using the Hessian bound on (S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}),
[
\nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v) \ge -C_{\mathrm{add}}|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2,
]
so we obtain the crude but robust estimate
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v)
;\ge; (\kappa_G - C_{\mathrm{add}})|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2
+ \nabla^2 S_W(U^{(0)})(v,v).
]

On horizontal directions, the Wilson Hessian is **nonnegative** (it vanishes only on vertical + harmonic components) and strictly positive on co-exact physical modes. Thus
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U^{(0)})(v,v)
;\ge;
(\kappa_G - C_{\mathrm{add}})|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2
= \rho_0,|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2
\qquad\forall v\in H_{U^{(0)}},
]
with (\rho_0>0) independent of (\Lambda).

This is the **horizontal curvature lower bound at the trivial configuration**.

### 11.2.2. Geometric role of Haar mass

In exponential coordinates on (G) and hence on (M_\Lambda), the product Haar volume appears as a Gibbs density with potential
[
S_{H,\Lambda}(X) = \sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)} S_H(X_\ell),
]
where the single-link Haar potential satisfies
[
\nabla^2 S_H(0) = \frac{1}{3}\operatorname{Ric}_G \ge \frac{\kappa_G}{3} g_G.
]
Thus near (X=0) the Haar contribution is strictly convex in **all** directions with Hessian bounded below by a positive constant. 

In the Riemannian-volume picture this strict convexity is encoded instead in the **geometric** Ricci tensor (\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} = \kappa_G g_\Lambda). Either viewpoint leads to the same lower bound (\kappa_G) in the horizontal curvature estimate above.

## 11.3. Extension to a uniform small-field neighborhood

The bound in §11.2 holds exactly at the single point (U^{(0)}). We now extend it to a **uniform small-field ball** around (U^{(0)}).

Two continuity facts are used:

1. The map
   [
   U \longmapsto \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)
   ]
   is smooth as a section of the ((2,0))-tensor bundle over (M_\Lambda); in particular it is continuous.

2. The horizontal bundle
   [
   U \longmapsto H_U\subset T_U M_\Lambda
   ]
   depends smoothly on (U) (on the open dense subset of regular configurations), because the gauge action is smooth and the vertical spaces (V_U) vary smoothly. The orthogonal complement (H_U) therefore varies continuously. 

Fix an arbitrary unit horizontal vector (v_0\in H_{U^{(0)}}). The map
[
U \mapsto \inf{\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)(v,v) : v\in H_U,\ |v|*{g*\Lambda}=1}
]
is continuous in (U) (on the regular set) because:

* (H_U) depends continuously on (U),
* (\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)) depends continuously on (U),
* the unit sphere in each (H_U) is compact.

At (U^{(0)}), this infimum is at least (\rho_0 > 0). By continuity there exists a radius (r>0) such that
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)(v,v)
;\ge; \frac{\rho_0}{2},|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2
\qquad\forall U \text{ with }d_{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)})\le r,\ \forall v\in H_U.
]

We denote
[
\rho_{\mathrm{loc}} := \frac{\rho_0}{2} = \frac{\kappa_G - C_{\mathrm{add}}}{2} > 0.
]

The key point is that **both (r) and (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}) are independent of (\Lambda)**: all the objects involved ((\kappa_G), (C_{\mathrm{add}}), the local structure of the lattice, the bi-invariant metric) are volume-independent, and the argument uses only finite-dimensional compactness/continuity. 

We summarize this as the following lemma.

> **Lemma 11.1 (Uniform local horizontal curvature bound).**
> Under the hypotheses of §11.1, there exist constants (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}>0) and (r>0), depending only on (\kappa_G), (C_{\mathrm{add}}), and local lattice structure, such that for every finite lattice (\Lambda),
> [
> \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)(v,v)
> ;\ge; \rho_{\mathrm{loc}}, |v|*{g*\Lambda}^2
> ]
> for all configurations (U\in M_\Lambda) with (d_{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)})\le r) and all horizontal vectors (v\in H_U).

This is precisely Lemma 7.1 in the original Part II formulation, now recast as Lemma 11.1 here.

## 11.4. Induced local (CD(\rho,\infty)) condition for gauge-invariant observables

We now translate Lemma 11.1 into the language of Γ–calculus and (CD(\rho,\infty)) conditions **restricted to gauge-invariant observables**.

### 11.4.1. Γ(_2) for gauge-invariant functions

For any smooth (f\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}),

* (\nabla f(U)\in H_U) for all (U\in M_\Lambda),
* the carré du champ is
  [
  \Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U) = |\nabla f(U)|*{g*\Lambda}^2,
  ]
* and the Bochner–Bakry–Émery identity gives
  [
  \Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(U)
  = |\nabla^2 f(U)|*{\mathrm{HS}}^2
  + \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu_\Lambda}(U)\big(\nabla f(U),\nabla f(U)\big).
  ] 

On the small-field ball
[
B_r(U^{(0)}) := {U\in M_\Lambda : d_{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)})\le r},
]
Lemma 11.1 applies to (\nabla f(U)), so
[
\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)\big(\nabla f(U),\nabla f(U)\big)
;\ge;
\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},|\nabla f(U)|*{g*\Lambda}^2
= \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U)
]
for all (U\in B_r(U^{(0)})).

Consequently,
[
\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(U)
;\ge; \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U)
\qquad \forall U\in B_r(U^{(0)}),\ \forall f\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}.
]

In other words, the triple (\big(B_r(U^{(0)}),,\mu_\Lambda|*{B_r},,L*\Lambda\big)) satisfies the **local curvature–dimension condition (CD(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty))** when tested on gauge-invariant functions.

This is the small-field, physical-sector version of the global (CD(\rho,\infty)) condition used in Part I to derive Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities.

### 11.4.2. Local CD((\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)) as local BE curvature

For later use in Part III, it is convenient to phrase this explicitly as:

> **Definition 11.2 (Local horizontal CD((\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)) condition).**
> We say that the finite-volume YM system satisfies the local horizontal CD((\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)) condition on (B_r(U^{(0)})) if:
> [
> \Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(U) \ge \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U)
> ]
> for all gauge-invariant (f\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) and all (U\in B_r(U^{(0)})).

Lemma 11.1 shows that this condition holds with the same constant (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}>0) **independent of (\Lambda)**.

## 11.5. The Core Curvature Theorem

We now collect the preceding results into a single theorem that will serve as the interface between Part II (geometric YM analysis) and Parts I–III (abstract curvature ⇒ functional inequalities).

> **Theorem 11.3 (Core curvature theorem for lattice Yang–Mills).**
> Let (G) be a compact, connected Lie group equipped with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric (g_G) such that (\operatorname{Ric}*G \ge \kappa_G g_G) with (\kappa_G>0). For each finite lattice (\Lambda), let
> [
> M*\Lambda = G^{E(\Lambda)}
> ]
> be the configuration manifold with product metric (g_\Lambda) and product Haar volume (d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}), and let (\mathcal{G}_\Lambda = G^{V(\Lambda)}) act by lattice gauge transformations.
>
> Consider effective actions
> [
> S_\Lambda = S_W + S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}
> ]
> satisfying:
>
> 1. (S_W) is the Wilson plaquette action, smooth and gauge-invariant.
> 2. (S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}) is smooth, gauge-invariant, local, and satisfies
>    [
>    \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}(U) \ge -C_{\mathrm{add}},g_\Lambda(U)
>    \quad\forall U\in M_\Lambda
>    ]
>    with a constant (C_{\mathrm{add}}\ge 0) independent of (\Lambda).
> 3. (C_{\mathrm{add}} < \kappa_G).
>
> Let
> [
> d\mu_\Lambda(U) = Z_\Lambda^{-1} e^{-S_\Lambda(U)}, d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U)
> ]
> be the corresponding Gibbs measures, and let (L_\Lambda) be the associated generators. Let (\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) denote the algebra of smooth gauge-invariant observables.
>
> Then there exist constants (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}>0) and (r>0), depending only on (\kappa_G), (C_{\mathrm{add}}), and local lattice structure, such that for every finite lattice (\Lambda):
>
> 1. (**Horizontal Bakry–Émery curvature bound**)
>    For all configurations (U\in M_\Lambda) with (d_{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)})\le r) and all horizontal vectors (v\in H_U),
>    [
>    \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)(v,v)
>    ;\ge; \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2.
>    ]
>
> 2. (**Local (CD(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)) condition for gauge-invariant observables**)
>    For all smooth gauge-invariant (f\in\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}),
>    [
>    \Gamma*{2,\Lambda}(f)(U)
>    ;\ge; \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U)
>    \qquad\forall U\in B_r(U^{(0)}),
>    ]
>    where (B_r(U^{(0)}) = {U\in M_\Lambda: d_{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)})\le r}).
>
> The constants (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}) and (r) are **independent of the finite volume (\Lambda)**.

*Sketch of proof.* This is simply the concatenation of:

* Product Ricci lower bound (\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} = \kappa_G g_\Lambda), independent of (\Lambda). 
* Hessian bound (\nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda} \ge -C_{\mathrm{add}} g_\Lambda).
* Positivity of the Wilson Hessian on physical modes near (U^{(0)}), and non-negativity on all horizontal modes. 
* Gauge-invariant gradients (\nabla f(U)\in H_U). 
* Continuity of (\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}) and (H_U) in (U), and compactness of the unit sphere in each (H_U), yielding a uniform small-field radius (r) and uniform curvature constant (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}). 

The inequality in (2) is then a direct application of the Bochner–Bakry–Émery identity restricted to gauge-invariant observables.

## 11.6. Interface with Part I: horizontal (H_{\mathrm{curv}})

In Part I we formulated the following **abstract hypothesis** for a family ((M_\Lambda,g_\Lambda,S_\Lambda,\mu_\Lambda)) with gauge symmetries: 

> **Hypothesis (H_{\mathrm{curv}}) (global horizontal BE bound).**
> There exists (\rho_*>0), independent of (\Lambda), such that
> [
> \big(\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} + \nabla^2 S_\Lambda\big)(v,v)
> ;\ge; \rho_*,|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2
> ]
> for all (U\in M_\Lambda) and all (v\in H_U).

Under (H_{\mathrm{curv}}), the Bakry–Émery calculus on the quotient (or on (\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}})) yields **global** Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities for gauge-invariant observables with constants (1/\rho**) and (2/\rho_*), and hence a uniform spectral gap for the gauge-invariant sector.

Theorem 11.3 shows that lattice Yang–Mills satisfies a **local** version of (H_{\mathrm{curv}}):

* The curvature bound holds on the small-field ball (B_r(U^{(0)})),
* Only horizontal directions matter, and those are exactly the gradients of gauge-invariant observables,
* Constants (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}, r) are independent of (\Lambda).

This provides the **precise geometric input** needed for the local-to-global machinery of Part III:

* From the local (CD(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)) condition on (B_r(U^{(0)})), one gets **local** Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities on that ball for gauge-invariant observables.
* Combined with Lyapunov drift estimates controlling excursions outside (B_r(U^{(0)})), one can (in principle) promote these to **global** Poincaré/LSI for the full Gibbs measures (\mu_\Lambda) with volume-independent constants. 

Thus:

* **Geometric side (Part II):** Theorem 11.3 verifies the local horizontal curvature hypothesis needed by the abstract machinery.
* **Analytic side (Part I & III):** Given local curvature and suitable Lyapunov conditions, curvature ⇒ local FI ⇒ global FI ⇒ uniform spectral gaps for the configuration-space diffusion acting on gauge-invariant observables.

In the next parts of the project, this core curvature theorem will be combined with Lyapunov estimates, OS reconstruction, and continuum-limit analysis to formulate precise scenarios under which such configuration-space spectral gaps would imply a genuine mass gap for continuum Yang–Mills.
