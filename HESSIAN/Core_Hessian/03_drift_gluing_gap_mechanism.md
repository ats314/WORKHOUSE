# Drift domination + smooth gluing as a “mass gap engine” at fixed cutoff
*(Project extraction: “Gap lemma” and the Lyapunov/strip-gluing mechanism for a uniform Poincaré constant)*

## 0. What this module is doing
A common route to a **uniform (in volume) Poincaré inequality** for a complicated Gibbs measure is:

1. Identify a **good set** $K$ where convexity/curvature is strong $\Rightarrow$ restricted Poincaré holds.
2. Identify a **bad set** $K^c$ where you can still prove restricted Poincaré (sometimes by different means).
3. Prove that the diffusion dynamics has a **negative drift across the boundary strip** separating $K$ from $K^c$.
4. Use a **smooth gluing lemma** to turn (1)–(3) into a global Poincaré inequality.

The project’s key order parameter is an averaged plaquette “badness”
\[
\mathcal B_\Lambda(U)=\frac1{|P|}\sum_{p\in P}\,b(U_p),
\]
with $b(U_p)$ measuring how far plaquette holonomy $U_p$ is from identity.

The “Gap lemma” file does two crucial things:

- computes $L\mathcal B_\Lambda$ explicitly for the Wilson generator,
- isolates the one geometric input needed to force *uniform negative drift* in a boundary strip: an **SU(2) local cancellation lemma** (a quantified “forces don’t all cancel unless you are globally Cartan-aligned”).

---

## 1. The diffusion generator
Let $(M_\Lambda,g_\Lambda)$ be the finite-dimensional lattice configuration manifold (product of copies of $G$ over links), with Wilson measure
\[
d\mu_\Lambda(U)=Z^{-1}e^{-S_W(U)}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_\Lambda}(U).
\]
The configuration-space Langevin generator is
\[
L_\Lambda = \Delta_\Lambda - \langle \nabla S_W,\nabla(\cdot)\rangle.
\]
Its Dirichlet form is
\[
\mathcal E_\Lambda(f,f)=\int_{M_\Lambda}|\nabla f|^2\,d\mu_\Lambda.
\]

A volume-uniform Poincaré constant for $\mu_\Lambda$ yields a **uniform spectral gap** for $-L_\Lambda$.

---

## 2. The order parameter and the key drift identity
Take the averaged badness $\mathcal B_\Lambda$ as above. The “Gap lemma” file asserts the structural identity
\[
L_\Lambda \mathcal B_\Lambda
=
\Delta_\Lambda \mathcal B_\Lambda
-\beta\,|P|\,|\nabla \mathcal B_\Lambda|^2.
\tag{2.1}
\]
The Laplacian term is bounded (a geometry-of-$G$ constant):
\[
|\Delta_\Lambda \mathcal B_\Lambda|\le C_\Delta.
\tag{2.2}
\]
So on any region where $|\nabla\mathcal B_\Lambda|^2$ has a uniform lower bound,
\[
L_\Lambda\mathcal B_\Lambda \le C_\Delta - \beta|P|\cdot(\text{lower bound}).
\]
For $\beta$ large enough, that becomes a **strict negative drift**.

---

## 3. The “boundary strip drift domination” hypothesis
Fix $\varepsilon$ and $\delta>0$ and define:

- good set: $K = \{\mathcal B_\Lambda\le \varepsilon\}$
- bad set: $K^c = \{\mathcal B_\Lambda\ge \varepsilon+\delta\}$
- strip: $\Sigma=\{\varepsilon<\mathcal B_\Lambda<\varepsilon+\delta\}$

The gluing lemma assumes a drift condition on $\Sigma$:
\[
L_\Lambda\mathcal B_\Lambda \le -\rho\quad\text{on }\Sigma.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Given (2.1)–(2.2), (3.1) reduces to:
\[
|\nabla\mathcal B_\Lambda|^2 \ge \frac{C_\Delta+\rho}{\beta|P|}
\quad\text{on }\Sigma.
\tag{3.2}
\]

So the “physics” is: **if you are not quite good and not quite terrible (the strip), the average badness must be changing in at least one link direction**. No flat saddle plateau is allowed there.

That is exactly where the SU(2) local cancellation lemma is used.

---

## 4. The SU(2) local cancellation lemma (geometric coercivity)
The file states a geometric coercivity statement of the form:

> If $\mathcal B_\Lambda(U)\ge\varepsilon$, then **some link** $\ell$ has a force bounded below:
> \[
> \|\nabla_\ell S_W(U)\|\ge c_0(\varepsilon,\beta) >0,
> \]
> except on a globally Cartan-aligned set of measure zero.

Intuition: in SU(2), forces generated by nearby plaquettes can only cancel “too well” if the entire configuration sits inside a common Abelian subgroup.

If quantified sharply enough, this coercivity plus (2.1) implies (3.2).

---

## 5. Smooth gluing lemma (barrier estimate across the strip)
The “Gap lemma” document includes a clean gluing statement (schematically):

Assume restricted Poincaré inequalities on $K$ and $K^c$ and drift domination in $\Sigma$. Then for any smooth $f$,
\[
\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)
\le
C_{\mathrm{mix}}\mathcal E_\Lambda(f,f)
+
C_\Sigma\int_\Sigma (f-\mu_\Lambda f)^2\,d\mu_\Lambda,
\tag{5.1}
\]
where $C_{\mathrm{mix}}=O(1/\rho)$ and $C_\Sigma=O(1)$ depend on strip regularity bounds.

The last term is then handled by (i) smallness of $\mu_\Lambda(\Sigma)$ for large $\beta$ or (ii) an additional local inequality on the strip.

The “barrier” intuition is nice: (3.1) implies that any function that significantly differs between $K$ and $K^c$ must have a large gradient somewhere in the strip, and that costs Dirichlet energy.

---

## 6. Output: a volume-uniform Poincaré constant at fixed cutoff
Once the above pieces are assembled, you get
\[
\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)\ \le\ \frac{1}{\lambda_*}\,\mathcal E_\Lambda(f,f)
\]
with $\lambda_*$ independent of $\Lambda$ (for $\beta$ large enough), hence a uniform spectral gap for $-L_\Lambda$.

This is exactly the “configuration-space gap” ingredient needed for the OS/Hamiltonian leg of the program.

---

## 7. Where the hard work actually is (and how to push it)
This module is a powerful *blueprint*, but its truth hinges on two technically delicate points:

1. **Quantitative SU(2) local cancellation**  
   You want a bound that is uniform in lattice size and robust away from a small “Abelian” exceptional set.

2. **Uniform control of strip geometry**  
   The drift inequality needs strip derivative bounds (bounds on $|\nabla \mathcal B_\Lambda|$ and $|L\mathcal B_\Lambda|$ on $\Sigma$). This is plausible because $G$ is compact and $b$ is smooth, but the dependence on how $\Sigma$ is chosen matters.

A good next step is to isolate the smallest geometric lemma that forces (3.2) and test it on:

- one cube / one plaquette toy models,
- then a few coupled cubes with explicit SU(2) parametrizations,
- then scaling arguments for general $\Lambda$.
