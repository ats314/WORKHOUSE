## Appendix A. A smooth plaquette proxy and the Lyapunov drift computation

### A.1 Motivation: where the differentiability problem enters

The Lyapunov candidates used in the drift mechanism (Parts 12–15) are exponential weights of a plaquette “energy” functional, e.g.
[
W_\Lambda(U)=\exp!\Big(\eta\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\Phi(z_p(U))\Big),
\qquad z_p(U)=\text{(plaquette energy)}.
]
In the drift computation for such a (W_\Lambda), one inevitably encounters second derivatives of (z_p) along link vector fields, schematically terms of the form ((X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p)) (one sees this explicitly in the “analytic hinge” discussion).  The issue is that the most geometric choice (z_p(U)=d_G(U_p(U),e)^2) fails to be globally (C^2) because of the cut locus, so those terms are not uniformly controlled. This is identified as the point where the drift computation “breaks.” 

A remedy already suggested in the notes is to replace the distance-square plaquette energy by a **globally smooth, conjugation-invariant** proxy, with bounded derivatives, e.g.
[
\phi(g):=1-\frac1N \mathrm{ReTr}(g)\quad (g\in SU(N)),
]
and to define (z_p(U)=\phi(U_p(U))). 

This appendix executes that replacement and re-runs the drift algebra at the level of pointwise estimates, with constants uniform in (\Lambda).

---

### A.2 The smooth plaquette observable and “small-field” sets

We adopt the same canonical plaquette observable already fixed in the main text:
[
\phi(g)=1-\frac1N\mathrm{ReTr}(g),
\qquad g\in SU(N),
]
and for a configuration (U\in M_\Lambda:=G^{E(\Lambda)}) we write
[
z_p(U):=\phi\big(U_p(U)\big)\ge 0 .
]
This is the same (\phi) used to define the canonical “good set” (K_\Lambda(\varepsilon)) and its max version:
[
K_\Lambda^{\mathrm{av}}(\varepsilon):=\Big{U:\frac1{|P(\Lambda)|}\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\phi(U_p(U))\le \varepsilon\Big},
\qquad
K_\Lambda^{\max}(\varepsilon):={U:\phi(U_p(U))\le \varepsilon\ \forall p},
]
with (K_\Lambda^{\max}(\varepsilon)\subset K_\Lambda^{\mathrm{av}}(\varepsilon)). 

This choice is also the Wilson plaquette energy density (up to the coupling (\beta)); the Wilson action is a sum over plaquettes of (\beta\phi(U_p)). 

---

### A.3 Generator, carré du champ, and the two chain rules we will use

We work with the reversible diffusion generator on ((M_\Lambda,g_\Lambda)) with Gibbs measure (d\mu_\Lambda\propto e^{-S_\Lambda},d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}):
[
L_\Lambda f=\Delta_{g_\Lambda}f-\langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla f\rangle_{g_\Lambda},
\qquad
\Gamma_\Lambda(f)=|\nabla f|*{g*\Lambda}^2.

]

Two standard diffusion identities (already recorded in the notes) drive all computations:

1. **Chain rule for (L)** (Lemma 2.6):
   [
   L(\Phi(f))=\Phi'(f),Lf+\Phi''(f),\Gamma(f).
   \tag{A.1}
   ]


2. **Exponential chain rule** (Lemma 14.3):
   [
   L(e^{\kappa V})=e^{\kappa V}\big(\kappa,LV+\kappa^2,\Gamma(V)\big).
   \tag{A.2}
   ]


---

### A.4 Uniform (C^2) control for the plaquette proxy (z_p(U)=\phi(U_p(U)))

The key point of replacing (d_G^2) by (\phi) is that (\phi) is globally smooth on the compact group, hence its derivatives of any fixed order are bounded.

#### Lemma A.1 (Uniform derivative bounds for (\phi))

Let (G=SU(N)) with a fixed bi-invariant Riemannian metric. Then there exist finite constants
[
M_1:=\sup_{g\in G}|\nabla\phi(g)|,\qquad
M_2:=\sup_{g\in G}|\nabla^2\phi(g)|_{\mathrm{op}}<\infty,
]
depending only on ((G,\phi,g_G)).

**Proof.** (\phi) is smooth and (G) is compact, so (\nabla\phi) and (\nabla^2\phi) are continuous and achieve maxima. (\square)

We also need a “quadratic control” of (|\nabla\phi|^2) by (\phi) to prevent the (\Phi'')-term from producing an (O(|P(\Lambda)|)) leakage.

#### Lemma A.2 (Global gradient-square control (|\nabla\phi|^2\lesssim \phi))

There exists a constant (C_\phi<\infty) such that for all (g\in G),
[
|\nabla\phi(g)|^2\le C_\phi,\phi(g).
\tag{A.3}
]

**Proof.**
Step 1 (local estimate near the identity). Consider (g(t)=\exp(tX)) with (X\in\mathfrak{su}(N)). Using the Taylor expansion of the matrix exponential and the fact that (\mathrm{Tr}(X)=0), one has
[
\mathrm{ReTr}(e^{tX})=N+\frac{t^2}{2}\mathrm{ReTr}(X^2)+O(t^3).
]
For (X\in\mathfrak{su}(N)) (anti-Hermitian), (\mathrm{ReTr}(X^2)=\mathrm{Tr}(X^2)) is strictly negative unless (X=0), and (with the standard inner product normalization) (-\mathrm{Tr}(X^2)=|X|^2). Thus
[
\phi(e^{tX})
=1-\frac1N\mathrm{ReTr}(e^{tX})
=\frac{t^2}{2N}|X|^2+O(t^3),
]
so (\phi) has a nondegenerate minimum at (e), with positive-definite Hessian at (e). Standard Taylor remainder estimates in normal coordinates then imply: there exists a neighborhood (U\ni e) and constants (c_1,c_2>0) such that
[
\phi(g)\ge c_1,d_G(g,e)^2,\qquad
|\nabla\phi(g)|\le c_2,d_G(g,e),
\qquad g\in U.
]
Combining gives (|\nabla\phi(g)|^2\le (c_2^2/c_1)\phi(g)) on (U).

Step 2 (global extension). On the compact set (G\setminus U), (\phi) is continuous and strictly positive (since (\phi(g)=0) forces (\mathrm{ReTr}(g)=N), i.e. all eigenvalues are (1), hence (g=e)). Let (m:=\inf_{G\setminus U}\phi>0) and (M:=\sup_G|\nabla\phi|^2<\infty). Then for (g\notin U),
[
|\nabla\phi(g)|^2\le M \le \frac{M}{m},\phi(g).
]
Taking (C_\phi:=\max{c_2^2/c_1,M/m}) yields (A.3) on all of (G). (\square)

### A.5 Lattice locality constants

Two purely combinatorial locality facts are used to keep constants uniform in (\Lambda):

* Each plaquette depends on exactly (4) links.
* Each link belongs to at most (2(d-1)) plaquettes in dimension (d) (hence at most (6) plaquettes in (d=4)). 

We denote
[
m:=4,\qquad \nu:=2(d-1)\quad(\nu=6\text{ in }d=4).
\tag{A.4}
]

---

### A.6 The modified Lyapunov candidate and its drift algebra

We now implement the second modification: choose (\Phi) with (\Phi'(0)=0). A canonical choice is
[
\Phi(s)=s^2,\qquad \Phi'(s)=2s,\qquad \Phi''(s)=2.
\tag{A.5}
]
Define
[
V_\Lambda(U):=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \Phi(z_p(U))=\sum_{p} z_p(U)^2,
\qquad
W_\Lambda(U):=e^{\eta V_\Lambda(U)},
\tag{A.6}
]
and the “linear badness” functional
[
\mathcal D_\Lambda(U):=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} z_p(U)=\sum_p \phi(U_p(U)).
\tag{A.7}
]

By the exponential chain rule (A.2),
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
=\eta,L_\Lambda V_\Lambda+\eta^2,\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda).
\tag{A.8}
]


So we need to control (L_\Lambda V_\Lambda) and (\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)).

---

### A.7 Bounding (L_\Lambda V_\Lambda) without an (O(|P|)) leakage

Apply the diffusion chain rule (A.1) to each term (z_p^2=\Phi(z_p)):
[
L_\Lambda(z_p^2)=\Phi'(z_p),L_\Lambda z_p+\Phi''(z_p),\Gamma_\Lambda(z_p)
=2z_p,L_\Lambda z_p + 2,\Gamma_\Lambda(z_p).
\tag{A.9}
]


Summing over plaquettes,
[
L_\Lambda V_\Lambda
=2\sum_p z_p,L_\Lambda z_p + 2\sum_p \Gamma_\Lambda(z_p).
\tag{A.10}
]

Now expand (L_\Lambda z_p=\Delta z_p - \langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla z_p\rangle) using the generator definition.  This gives
[
L_\Lambda V_\Lambda
=2\sum_p z_p,\Delta z_p
-2\sum_p z_p,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla z_p\rangle
+2\sum_p \Gamma_\Lambda(z_p).
\tag{A.11}
]

We control the two “geometric” (non-drift) terms by (\mathcal D_\Lambda).

#### Lemma A.3 (The Laplacian/Hessian term becomes (\mathcal D_\Lambda)-weighted)

There exists a constant (C_{\Delta}) independent of (\Lambda) such that
[
2\sum_{p} z_p,\Delta z_p\ \le\ C_\Delta,\mathcal D_\Lambda.
\tag{A.12}
]

**Proof.**
Fix a plaquette (p). The map (U\mapsto z_p(U)=\phi(U_p(U))) is smooth and depends only on the (m=4) link variables around (p). The linkwise Laplacian (\Delta) on the product manifold is a sum of second derivatives along each link factor; by locality, only those (4) link factors contribute.

Because (\phi) is (C^2) with globally bounded Hessian (Lemma A.1), and the plaquette word (U_p(\cdot)) has fixed length (4), there is a uniform bound
[
|\Delta z_p(U)|\le C_0
\quad\text{for all }U\in M_\Lambda,
\tag{A.13}
]
with (C_0) depending only on ((G,\phi,g_G)) and the plaquette word, not on (\Lambda). Therefore,
[
2\sum_p z_p,\Delta z_p
\le 2C_0\sum_p z_p
= 2C_0,\mathcal D_\Lambda.
]
Set (C_\Delta:=2C_0). (\square)

This is exactly where (\Phi'(0)=0) matters: the potentially dangerous term (\Delta z_p) is multiplied by (z_p), so the sum scales like (\sum_p z_p=\mathcal D_\Lambda), not like (|P(\Lambda)|).

#### Lemma A.4 (The (\Phi'')-term is also (\mathcal D_\Lambda)-weighted)

There exists a constant (C_{\Gamma z}) independent of (\Lambda) such that
[
2\sum_p \Gamma_\Lambda(z_p)\ \le\ C_{\Gamma z},\mathcal D_\Lambda.
\tag{A.14}
]

**Proof.**
For each plaquette (p),
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(z_p)=|\nabla z_p|^2.
]
Since (z_p=\phi\circ U_p) and the plaquette word has fixed length, (|\nabla z_p|) is controlled by (|\nabla\phi|) up to a uniform multiplicative constant. Combining this with the global gradient-square control (|\nabla\phi|^2\le C_\phi,\phi) from Lemma A.2 yields
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(z_p)\le C_1,z_p,
]
with (C_1) independent of (\Lambda). Summing gives
[
2\sum_p \Gamma_\Lambda(z_p)\le 2C_1\sum_p z_p=2C_1,\mathcal D_\Lambda.
]
Set (C_{\Gamma z}:=2C_1). (\square)

Putting Lemmas A.3–A.4 into (A.11) yields the uniform bound
[
L_\Lambda V_\Lambda
\le
C_V,\mathcal D_\Lambda
----------------------

2\sum_p z_p,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla z_p\rangle,
\qquad
C_V:=C_\Delta+C_{\Gamma z}.
\tag{A.15}
]

Up to this point, the only ingredient was that (z_p) is built from a globally smooth proxy (\phi) with bounded (C^2) norm—precisely the replacement recommended in the “analytic hinge” discussion. 

---

### A.8 Bounding (\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)) by (\mathcal D_\Lambda) with uniform constants

We now bound the quadratic term (\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)=|\nabla V_\Lambda|^2).

Write the edgewise gradient decomposition on a product manifold (one may decompose (\nabla=\bigoplus_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}\nabla_\ell)), so
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)=\sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}|\nabla_\ell V_\Lambda|^2.
\tag{A.16}
]
(See the general “sum over edges” principle for product manifolds.) 

For fixed (\ell), only plaquettes (p\ni \ell) contribute to (\nabla_\ell V_\Lambda). Using (V_\Lambda=\sum_p z_p^2),
[
\nabla_\ell V_\Lambda
=\sum_{p\ni \ell} \nabla_\ell(z_p^2)
=\sum_{p\ni\ell} 2z_p,\nabla_\ell z_p.
\tag{A.17}
]
Hence, by Cauchy–Schwarz and the incidence bound (|{p:,p\ni\ell}|\le \nu) from (A.4),
[
|\nabla_\ell V_\Lambda|^2
\le
\Big(\sum_{p\ni\ell} 2z_p,|\nabla_\ell z_p|\Big)^2
\le
\nu\sum_{p\ni\ell} 4 z_p^2,|\nabla_\ell z_p|^2.
\tag{A.18}
]
Summing over (\ell),
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)
\le
4\nu\sum_{\ell}\sum_{p\ni\ell} z_p^2,|\nabla_\ell z_p|^2.
\tag{A.19}
]
Now use two uniform facts:

1. (|\nabla_\ell z_p|^2\le|\nabla z_p|^2=\Gamma(z_p)\le C_1 z_p) from Lemma A.4’s proof.

2. (0\le z_p\le 2), since (\mathrm{ReTr}(g)\in[-N,N]) implies (\phi(g)\in[0,2]).

Thus (z_p^2|\nabla_\ell z_p|^2 \le C_1 z_p^3\le 2C_1 z_p^2\le 4C_1 z_p). Insert into (A.19):
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)
\le
4\nu\sum_{\ell}\sum_{p\ni\ell} (4C_1 z_p)
=16C_1\nu\sum_{\ell}\sum_{p\ni\ell} z_p.
\tag{A.20}
]
Each plaquette has exactly (m=4) links, so (\sum_{\ell}\sum_{p\ni\ell} z_p = m\sum_p z_p = 4\mathcal D_\Lambda). Therefore,
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)\le C_\Gamma,\mathcal D_\Lambda,
\qquad
C_\Gamma:=16C_1\nu m = 64 C_1\nu,
\tag{A.21}
]
uniformly in (\Lambda) (and in (d=4), (\nu=6)).

---

### A.9 Final uniform drift inequality: what is fixed, and what remains action-dependent

Combine (A.8), (A.15), and (A.21):
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
\le
\eta,C_V,\mathcal D_\Lambda
-,
2\eta\sum_p z_p,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla z_p\rangle
+\eta^2,C_\Gamma,\mathcal D_\Lambda.
\tag{A.22}
]
Equivalently,
[
L_\Lambda W_\Lambda
\le
\Big(
(\eta C_V+\eta^2 C_\Gamma),\mathcal D_\Lambda
---------------------------------------------

2\eta\sum_p z_p,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla z_p\rangle
\Big),W_\Lambda.
\tag{A.23}
]

This achieves the specific technical goal stated in your two-point fix:

* the previously uncontrolled “Hessian of (z_p)” contribution is now uniformly bounded (because (\phi) is globally (C^2)), and
* choosing (\Phi'(0)=0) (here (\Phi(s)=s^2)) forces these second-derivative contributions to be weighted by (z_p), hence by (\mathcal D_\Lambda=\sum_p z_p), and **not** by (|P(\Lambda)|).

This is precisely the obstruction and remedy highlighted in the Part 15.8.2–15.8.3 discussion. 

#### Corollary A.5 (A clean “no (|P|)-leakage” statement)

There exist constants (C_V,C_\Gamma<\infty), depending only on (G), the chosen metric, the dimension (d) via (\nu), and the choice (\Phi(s)=s^2), such that for all finite (\Lambda),
[
L_\Lambda W_\Lambda
\le
\Big(
(\eta C_V+\eta^2 C_\Gamma),\mathcal D_\Lambda
---------------------------------------------

2\eta\sum_p z_p,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla z_p\rangle
\Big),W_\Lambda.
\tag{A.24}
]

In particular, **every term other than the action-dependent drift pairing**
[
\sum_p z_p,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla z_p\rangle
\tag{A.25}
]
is already controlled with constants uniform in (\Lambda).

---

### A.10 How (A.24) closes a Lyapunov drift bound once the action supplies coercivity

To obtain a Lyapunov drift inequality of the form targeted in the program (schematically (L_\Lambda W_\Lambda \le (-c,\mathcal D_\Lambda+b)W_\Lambda)), it suffices to verify a pointwise lower bound on the action-gradient pairing (A.25), e.g. an estimate of the form
[
2\sum_p z_p,\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla z_p\rangle
\ \ge\
\alpha,\mathcal D_\Lambda-\beta,
\tag{A.26}
]
with constants (\alpha>0,\beta\ge 0) independent of (\Lambda). Plugging (A.26) into (A.24) gives
[
L_\Lambda W_\Lambda
\le
\Big(
-(\eta\alpha-\eta C_V-\eta^2 C_\Gamma),\mathcal D_\Lambda
+\eta\beta
\Big)W_\Lambda,
\tag{A.27}
]
and choosing (\eta>0) small enough so that (\eta\alpha-\eta C_V-\eta^2 C_\Gamma>0) yields the desired drift with uniform constants.

The key content of this appendix is that, after replacing (d_G^2) by (\phi(g)=1-\frac1N\mathrm{ReTr}(g)) and taking (\Phi'(0)=0), the drift computation has no hidden (|P(\Lambda)|)-scale leakage; all “geometric” contributions are uniformly (\mathcal D_\Lambda)-weighted, and the only remaining input is the coercivity (A.26), which is an action-specific estimate.
