## Appendix K. Smooth plaquette Lyapunov potentials and the drift calculation with volume-uniform constants

### K.1. Purpose and the specific obstruction being removed

A canonical plaquette-based Lyapunov Ansatz considered in the drafts is
[
W_\Lambda(U);=;\exp\Big(\eta\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\Phi(z_p(U))\Big),
\qquad z_p(U)=d_G(U_p(U),\mathbf 1)^2,
]
whose drift computation produces a term of the schematic form
[
\eta\sum_{p,\ell,a}\Phi'(z_p),\big(X^a_\ell X^a_\ell z_p\big),
]
and the technical bottleneck is that (z_p=d_G(\cdot,\mathbf 1)^2) is not globally smooth on a compact Lie group (cut locus), so (X^a_\ell X^a_\ell z_p) cannot be bounded uniformly without a cut-locus avoidance strategy . The same discussion explicitly proposes replacing (d_G^2) by a globally smooth conjugation-invariant proxy such as
[
\widetilde z(g);:=;1-\frac1N\operatorname{ReTr}(g)\qquad(g\in SU(N)),
]
in order to make (X^a_\ell X^a_\ell \widetilde z_p) uniformly bounded by smoothness on a compact manifold .

This appendix carries out the corresponding drift computation **with the proxy (\widetilde z)** and shows precisely where the choice (\Phi'(0)=0) (e.g. (\Phi(s)=s^2)) eliminates the unwanted (\mathrm{O}(|P(\Lambda)|)) leakage in (L_\Lambda W_\Lambda/W_\Lambda), replacing it by a term controlled by (\sum_p \widetilde z_p) with constants independent of (\Lambda).

No claim is made here that the full Yang–Mills Lyapunov drift hypothesis is proved; rather, this appendix supplies the missing uniform second-derivative control and the “no (|P|)-leak” bookkeeping required by the drift framework .

### K.2. Geometry and differential operators on (SU(N))

Fix (G:=SU(N)) with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric induced from an (\mathrm{Ad})-invariant inner product (\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak g}) on (\mathfrak g:=\mathfrak{su}(N)). Concretely one may take
[
|A|_{\mathfrak g}^2 := -\mathrm{Tr}(A^2)\quad (A\in\mathfrak{su}(N)),
]
as used in the Wilson-expansion bookkeeping (for (A) anti-Hermitian, (\mathrm{Tr}(A^2)\le 0) is real) .

Let ({e_a}*{a=1}^{\dim G}) be an orthonormal basis of (\mathfrak g). Let (X^a) denote the corresponding left-invariant vector fields on (G). The Laplace–Beltrami operator is
[
\Delta_G = \sum*{a=1}^{\dim G} (X^a)^2.
]
Gradients and Hessians are taken with respect to the fixed metric.

### K.3. The smooth conjugation-invariant proxy (\widetilde z)

Define
[
\widetilde z(g) := 1 - \frac{1}{N},\mathrm{ReTr}(g),\qquad g\in SU(N).
]

#### Lemma K.1 (basic properties of (\widetilde z))

(\widetilde z) satisfies:

1. (\widetilde z\in C^\infty(G)).
2. (\widetilde z) is conjugation-invariant: (\widetilde z(hgh^{-1})=\widetilde z(g)).
3. (0\le \widetilde z(g)\le 2) for all (g\in G), and (\widetilde z(g)=0) iff (g=\mathbf 1).
4. Near (\mathbf 1), (\widetilde z) is quadratic: for (A\in\mathfrak g) small,
   [
   \widetilde z(\exp A)=\frac{1}{2N}|A|*{\mathfrak g}^2+O(|A|*{\mathfrak g}^3).
   ]

**Proof.**

(1) Smoothness is immediate from smoothness of (\mathrm{Tr}) and matrix multiplication.

(2) (\mathrm{Tr}(hgh^{-1})=\mathrm{Tr}(g)), hence (\mathrm{ReTr}) is class-invariant.

(3) Since eigenvalues of (g\in SU(N)) lie on the unit circle, (\mathrm{ReTr}(g)\in[-N,N]). Therefore
[
\widetilde z(g)=1-\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{ReTr}(g)\in[0,2].
]
If (\widetilde z(g)=0) then (\mathrm{ReTr}(g)=N). This forces every eigenvalue to have real part (1), hence every eigenvalue equals (1), hence (g=\mathbf 1).

(4) Expand (\exp A=I+A+\frac12A^2+O(|A|^3)). Since (A\in\mathfrak{su}(N)) has (\mathrm{Tr}(A)=0), we obtain
[
\mathrm{ReTr}(I-\exp A)= -\tfrac12,\mathrm{Tr}(A^2)+O(|A|^3),
]
which is exactly the quoted Wilson/Taylor expansion statement . Dividing by (N) gives the stated quadratic asymptotic for (\widetilde z(\exp A)). ∎

### K.4. Uniform derivative bounds for (\widetilde z) on (SU(N))

Because (G) is compact and (\widetilde z\in C^\infty(G)), all derivatives are bounded. The key improvement needed for Lyapunov bookkeeping is a *weighted* gradient estimate of the form (|\nabla \widetilde z|^2 \lesssim \widetilde z), which ensures that gradient-square terms generated by chain rules do not produce an (\mathrm{O}(|P(\Lambda)|)) constant when summed over plaquettes.

#### Lemma K.2 (uniform bounds; weighted gradient bound)

There exist finite constants (M_1,M_2,M_\Delta,C_\nabla) depending only on (G) (hence only on (N) and the chosen metric normalization) such that for all (g\in G),
[
|\nabla \widetilde z(g)| \le M_1,\qquad
|\nabla^2\widetilde z(g)|*{\mathrm{op}} \le M_2,\qquad
|\Delta_G \widetilde z(g)| \le M*\Delta,
]
and moreover
[
|\nabla \widetilde z(g)|^2 \le C_\nabla,\widetilde z(g)\qquad\text{for all }g\in G.
]

**Proof.**

The first three bounds follow from compactness: the continuous functions (g\mapsto|\nabla \widetilde z(g)|), (g\mapsto|\nabla^2\widetilde z(g)|_{\mathrm{op}}), and (g\mapsto|\Delta_G\widetilde z(g)|) attain finite maxima on (G).

For the weighted gradient inequality, define on (G\setminus{\mathbf1})
[
Q(g) := \frac{|\nabla \widetilde z(g)|^2}{\widetilde z(g)}.
]
By Lemma K.1(3), (\widetilde z(g)>0) for (g\neq \mathbf1), hence (Q) is well-defined and continuous on (G\setminus{\mathbf1}).

It remains to show that (Q) extends continuously to (g=\mathbf1). Use exponential coordinates (g=\exp A) for (A) small. Lemma K.1(4) gives
[
\widetilde z(\exp A)=c|A|^2+O(|A|^3),\qquad c=\frac{1}{2N}.
]
Differentiating in these coordinates gives
[
\nabla \widetilde z(\exp A)=2c,A+O(|A|^2),
]
hence
[
|\nabla \widetilde z(\exp A)|^2 = 4c^2|A|^2 + O(|A|^3).
]
Therefore
[
Q(\exp A)=\frac{4c^2|A|^2+O(|A|^3)}{c|A|^2+O(|A|^3)};\longrightarrow;4c=\frac{2}{N}
\quad\text{as }A\to 0.
]
So (Q) extends continuously to (\mathbf1) by defining (Q(\mathbf1):=2/N). The extended (Q) is continuous on compact (G), hence bounded: (C_\nabla:=\sup_{g\in G}Q(g)<\infty). This is exactly (|\nabla \widetilde z|^2\le C_\nabla \widetilde z). ∎

### K.5. Transfer of these bounds to lattice plaquette observables

Let (\Lambda\subset \mathbb Z^4) be a finite lattice, (E(\Lambda)) its oriented edge set, and (P(\Lambda)) its oriented plaquette set. The configuration manifold is
[
M_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)}.
]
Fix the product Riemannian metric (g_\Lambda) on (M_\Lambda).

For a plaquette (p\in P(\Lambda)), let (U_p(U)\in G) be its plaquette holonomy as a product of the four link variables with appropriate inverses and orientation. (E.g. a square plaquette has (U_p = U_{e_1}U_{e_2}U_{e_3}^{-1}U_{e_4}^{-1}) in a standard orientation .) Define the plaquette proxy
[
\widetilde z_p(U) := \widetilde z(U_p(U)) = 1-\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{ReTr}(U_p(U)).
]

Let ({X^a_\ell}) denote the left-invariant vector fields acting on the (\ell)-th link coordinate (holding all other link coordinates fixed), built from the fixed orthonormal basis ({e_a}\subset\mathfrak g). Then the product Laplacian is
[
\Delta_\Lambda = \sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)} \sum_{a=1}^{\dim G} (X^a_\ell)^2
]
and the carré du champ is
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(F):=\sum_{\ell,a} (X^a_\ell F)^2 = |\nabla F|*{g*\Lambda}^2,
]
consistent with the diffusion setup used in the drift framework .

The combinatorial parameter needed below is the (uniform) maximum plaquette-incidence degree
[
\nu := \max_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)} #{p\in P(\Lambda):\ \ell\subset \partial p}.
]
In a (4)-dimensional cubic lattice, (\nu=6) (each link sits in (2(d-1)) plaquettes).

#### Lemma K.3 (uniform link-derivative bounds for (\widetilde z_p))

There exist constants (C_1,C_2,C_3) depending only on ((G,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak g})) such that for every lattice (\Lambda), every plaquette (p), every configuration (U\in M_\Lambda), every link (\ell), and every basis index (a),

1. If (\ell\not\subset\partial p) then (X_\ell^a \widetilde z_p(U)=0) and ((X_\ell^a)^2\widetilde z_p(U)=0).
2. If (\ell\subset\partial p), then
   [
   \sum_{a=1}^{\dim G}\big(X_\ell^a \widetilde z_p(U)\big)^2 ;\le; C_1,\widetilde z_p(U),
   ]
   [
   \Big|\sum_{a=1}^{\dim G}(X_\ell^a)^2 \widetilde z_p(U)\Big| ;\le; C_2.
   ]
3. Consequently,
   [
   \Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)(U)\le 4C_1,\widetilde z_p(U),
   \qquad
   |\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p(U)|\le 4C_2.
   ]

**Proof.**

(1) (\widetilde z_p(U)) depends only on the link variables along (\partial p). If (\ell\notin\partial p), varying (U_\ell) leaves (U_p) unchanged, hence the derivatives vanish.

(2) Fix (\ell\subset\partial p). View (U_p(U)) as a smooth map of (U_\ell\in G) with all other link variables frozen. Concretely, (U_p) is a product of (U_\ell) (or (U_\ell^{-1})) with fixed left/right multipliers (products of the other links). Left and right multiplication and inversion are smooth diffeomorphisms of (G) and are isometries for a bi-invariant metric. Therefore, the derivatives of (\widetilde z\circ U_p) with respect to (U_\ell) have the same norm bounds as derivatives of (\widetilde z) itself, up to universal constants depending only on the fixed metric normalization.

More explicitly, the map (U_\ell\mapsto U_p(U)) is of the form (U_\ell\mapsto A,U_\ell^{\pm 1},B) with (A,B\in G) depending on (U) but not on (U_\ell). Since (g\mapsto A g B) and (g\mapsto g^{-1}) are isometries, the gradient and Laplacian bounds from Lemma K.2 transfer with identical constants. Setting (C_1:=C_\nabla) and (C_2:=M_\Delta) from Lemma K.2 yields the claimed inequalities.

(3) A plaquette has exactly four links, so summing the linkwise bounds over the at most four links contributing to (\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)) and (\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p) produces the factors (4). ∎

### K.6. Drift computation for (W_\Lambda=\exp(\eta\sum_p\Phi(\widetilde z_p))) and elimination of the (\mathrm{O}(|P|)) leakage

Fix a (C^2) function (\Phi:[0,2]\to\mathbb R) and define
[
V_\Lambda(U):=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\Phi(\widetilde z_p(U)),
\qquad
W_\Lambda(U):=e^{\eta V_\Lambda(U)}.
]

Let (\mu_\Lambda) be a Gibbs measure with density (e^{-S_\Lambda}) with respect to the Riemannian volume (product Haar), and let (L_\Lambda) be the associated diffusion generator
[
L_\Lambda F := \Delta_\Lambda F - \langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla F\rangle_{g_\Lambda},
]
with carré du champ (\Gamma_\Lambda(F)=|\nabla F|^2) .

The diffusion chain rule for exponentials gives the fundamental identity
[
L_\Lambda(e^{\eta V_\Lambda})
=============================

e^{\eta V_\Lambda}\Big(\eta L_\Lambda V_\Lambda+\eta^2\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)\Big),
]
proved as Lemma 14.3 in the draft . Equivalently,
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
=====================================

\eta L_\Lambda V_\Lambda+\eta^2\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda).
\tag{K.1}
]

We now compute (L_\Lambda V_\Lambda) and isolate the term that would leak an (\mathrm{O}(|P(\Lambda)|)) constant if (\Phi'(0)\neq 0).

#### Lemma K.4 (explicit expansion of (L_\Lambda V_\Lambda))

For (V_\Lambda=\sum_p\Phi(\widetilde z_p)),
[
L_\Lambda V_\Lambda
===================

\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}
\Big(
\Phi'(\widetilde z_p),L_\Lambda \widetilde z_p
+
\Phi''(\widetilde z_p),\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)
\Big).
\tag{K.2}
]
Moreover,
[
L_\Lambda \widetilde z_p = \Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p - \langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla \widetilde z_p\rangle_{g_\Lambda}.
\tag{K.3}
]

**Proof.**

(K.2) is the standard diffusion chain rule (L(\Psi\circ f)=\Psi'(f)Lf+\Psi''(f)\Gamma(f)) applied plaquette-wise and summed; it is the same calculus used in Lemma 14.3 .

(K.3) is the definition of (L_\Lambda). ∎

Insert (K.2) into (K.1):
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
=====================================

\eta
\sum_p
\Big(
\Phi'(\widetilde z_p),\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p
---------------------------------------------------

\Phi'(\widetilde z_p),\langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla \widetilde z_p\rangle
+
\Phi''(\widetilde z_p),\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)
\Big)
+
\eta^2\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda).
\tag{K.4}
]

The potentially dangerous term is
[
T_\Delta(U):=\sum_p \Phi'(\widetilde z_p(U)),\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p(U),
\tag{K.5}
]
since, a priori, (|\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p|) is bounded below/above by a constant and summing over (p) would produce (\mathrm{O}(|P|)).

The bookkeeping fix is:

* choose (\widetilde z_p) globally smooth, so (|\Delta_\Lambda\widetilde z_p|) is uniformly bounded (Lemma K.3);
* choose (\Phi) with (\Phi'(0)=0), so (\Phi'(\widetilde z_p)) carries an explicit factor (\widetilde z_p) near (\mathbf1), and in particular (|\Phi'(\widetilde z_p)|\lesssim \widetilde z_p) on ([0,2]) for such (\Phi).

We now make that precise for (\Phi(s)=s^2).

### K.7. The choice (\Phi(s)=s^2): uniform control of all positive terms by (\sum_p \widetilde z_p)

Fix
[
\Phi(s)=s^2,\qquad \Phi'(s)=2s,\qquad \Phi''(s)=2.
\tag{K.6}
]
This has (\Phi'(0)=0) as requested.

Define the “energy” functional
[
\mathcal D_\Lambda(U):=\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \widetilde z_p(U),
\tag{K.7}
]
which is gauge-invariant because (\widetilde z) is conjugation-invariant.

#### Proposition K.5 (no (|P|)-leak in the Laplacian/Hessian term)

There exist constants (A_1,A_2,A_3) depending only on (G) (and dimension (d=4) through (\nu)), such that for every lattice (\Lambda) and configuration (U\in M_\Lambda):

1. Laplacian term:
   [
   \Big|\sum_{p} \Phi'(\widetilde z_p),\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p\Big|
   ;\le; A_1,\mathcal D_\Lambda(U).
   \tag{K.8}
   ]
2. Chain-rule gradient term:
   [
   \sum_p \Phi''(\widetilde z_p),\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)
   ;\le; A_2,\mathcal D_\Lambda(U).
   \tag{K.9}
   ]
3. Exponential carré-du-champ term:
   [
   \Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)(U);\le; A_3,\mathcal D_\Lambda(U).
   \tag{K.10}
   ]

In particular, the contribution of these terms to (L_\Lambda W_\Lambda/W_\Lambda) is controlled by (\mathcal D_\Lambda(U)) with constants independent of (|\Lambda|) and (|P(\Lambda)|).

**Proof.**

(1) Using (\Phi'(\widetilde z_p)=2\widetilde z_p), Lemma K.3 gives (|\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p|\le 4C_2). Hence
[
\Big|\sum_p \Phi'(\widetilde z_p),\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p\Big|
\le
\sum_p 2\widetilde z_p\cdot 4C_2
================================

# 8C_2\sum_p \widetilde z_p

A_1,\mathcal D_\Lambda,
]
with (A_1:=8C_2).

This is exactly the mechanism that replaces the forbidden bound (\mathrm{O}(|P|)) (which would arise if (\Phi'(0)\neq0)) by (\mathrm{O}(\sum_p\widetilde z_p)).

(2) With (\Phi''\equiv 2),
[
\sum_p \Phi''(\widetilde z_p),\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)
============================================================

2\sum_p \Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)
\le
2\sum_p 4C_1 \widetilde z_p
===========================

# 8C_1\sum_p \widetilde z_p

A_2,\mathcal D_\Lambda,
]
using Lemma K.3(3). Take (A_2:=8C_1).

(3) Recall (V_\Lambda=\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2). Differentiate linkwise:
[
\nabla_\ell V_\Lambda = \sum_{p:,\ell\subset\partial p} 2\widetilde z_p,\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p.
]
Using Cauchy–Schwarz and (#{p:\ell\subset\partial p}\le \nu),
[
|\nabla_\ell V_\Lambda|^2
\le
\nu\sum_{p:,\ell\subset\partial p} 4\widetilde z_p^2,|\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p|^2.
]
Summing over links (\ell),
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)
=========================

\sum_\ell |\nabla_\ell V_\Lambda|^2
\le
4\nu \sum_\ell \sum_{p:\ell\subset\partial p}\widetilde z_p^2,|\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p|^2
============================================================================================

4\nu \sum_p \widetilde z_p^2 \sum_{\ell\subset\partial p} |\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p|^2.
]
But (\sum_{\ell\subset\partial p}|\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p|^2 \le \Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)\le 4C_1\widetilde z_p) by Lemma K.3(3). Therefore
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)
\le
4\nu\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2 \cdot 4C_1 \widetilde z_p
=====================================================

16\nu C_1 \sum_p \widetilde z_p^3.
]
Since (0\le \widetilde z_p\le 2), (\widetilde z_p^3\le 4\widetilde z_p). Hence
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)
\le
16\nu C_1\sum_p 4\widetilde z_p
===============================

# 64\nu C_1 \sum_p \widetilde z_p

A_3,\mathcal D_\Lambda,
]
with (A_3:=64\nu C_1). ∎

### K.8. The final drift identity with uniform constants (what is now controlled, and what is not)

Combine (K.4) with Proposition K.5. With (\Phi(s)=s^2), we obtain the pointwise identity
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
=====================================

\eta\sum_p\Big(2\widetilde z_p,\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p
-2\widetilde z_p,\langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla \widetilde z_p\rangle
+2,\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)\Big)
+
\eta^2\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda),
\tag{K.11}
]
and therefore the pointwise bound
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
;\le;
-;2\eta\sum_p \widetilde z_p,\langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla \widetilde z_p\rangle
;+;\eta(A_1+A_2),\mathcal D_\Lambda
;+;\eta^2 A_3,\mathcal D_\Lambda,
\tag{K.12}
]
where (A_1,A_2,A_3) depend only on (G) and the lattice incidence bound (\nu), not on (|\Lambda|).

**This is the “closed” uniform-constant version of the drift computation in the sense relevant to the previously isolated obstruction**: the previously uncontrolled term (\sum_{p,\ell,a}\Phi'(\cdot),(X_\ell^aX_\ell^a z_p)) is now uniformly bounded because (\widetilde z) is globally smooth , and the choice (\Phi'(0)=0) prevents an (\mathrm{O}(|P(\Lambda)|)) constant from appearing in (L_\Lambda W_\Lambda/W_\Lambda), replacing it by (\mathrm{O}(\mathcal D_\Lambda)).

What remains (and is explicitly identified elsewhere as the substantive Yang–Mills Lyapunov/concentration bottleneck ) is the sign/coercivity control of the drift term
[
\sum_p \widetilde z_p,\langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla \widetilde z_p\rangle,
]
i.e. proving a lower bound of the form
[
\sum_p \widetilde z_p,\langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla \widetilde z_p\rangle
;\ge; c,\mathcal D_\Lambda - b
\quad\text{(or }\ge c\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2 - b\text{)}
\tag{K.13}
]
with (c,b) independent of (\Lambda). The present appendix does not establish (K.13); it establishes that once (K.13) is proved, the remaining terms in the drift calculation are already controlled with volume-uniform constants and no hidden (|P|)-dependence.
