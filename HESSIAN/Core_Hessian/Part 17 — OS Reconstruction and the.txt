Part 17 — OS Reconstruction and the Finite-Volume Hamiltonian

In this part we pass from the **Euclidean lattice Yang–Mills measure** and its OS properties (reflection positivity, Euclidean invariance) to a **Hilbert space** (\mathcal{H}*\Lambda), a **finite-volume Hamiltonian** (H*\Lambda), and a precise relation between **Euclidean-time correlation decay** and the **spectral gap** of (H_\Lambda).

We work at fixed lattice spacing (a>0) on a finite space–time lattice (\Lambda \subset \mathbb{Z}^{d+1}) with a distinguished Euclidean time direction. The OS axioms in the lattice gauge setting were spelled out in Part 16; in particular:

* The Wilson-type lattice Yang–Mills measure (d\mu_\Lambda(U)) on the configuration space (M_\Lambda = G^{E(\Lambda)}) is reflection positive with respect to a time-reflection (\theta).
* There is a *positive-time algebra* (\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+) of gauge-invariant local observables supported in (t>0). 

Using this structure, we now carry out the standard OS reconstruction at finite volume.

---

## 17.1. Positive-time algebra, reflection, and pre-inner product

Recall the setup from Part 16:

* (\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) is the *-algebra generated by bounded, gauge-invariant local observables on the space–time lattice.
* (\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^+ \subset \mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) is the subalgebra generated by observables supported entirely in the half-space (\Lambda^+ = { (t,\mathbf{x}) : t>0}). 
* (\theta) is the time-reflection map on fields, extended to observables by
  [
  (\theta \mathcal{O})(U) := \overline{\mathcal{O}(\theta U)}.
  ]
* The OS reflection positivity axiom reads: for all (F\in\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^+),
  [
  \langle (\theta F) F\rangle*\Lambda ;\ge; 0,
  ]
  and more generally the quadratic form
  [
  (F,G)\mapsto \langle (\theta F) G\rangle_\Lambda
  ]
  is positive semidefinite on (\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+).

Here (\langle \cdot\rangle_\Lambda) denotes expectation with respect to the lattice Yang–Mills measure
[
\langle F\rangle_\Lambda = \int_{M_\Lambda} F(U),d\mu_\Lambda(U).
]

We now use this to define a **pre-inner product** on (\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+).

Set
[
(F,G) := \langle \Theta F, G\rangle_{L^2(\mu_\Lambda)}
= \int (\Theta F)(U),G(U),d\mu_\Lambda(U),
]
where (\Theta) is the conjugate-linear reflection operator induced by (\theta) (we will not distinguish notationally between (\theta) and (\Theta) below). 

Then:

* ((F,G)) is conjugate symmetric:
  [
  (G,F) = \overline{(F,G)}.
  ]
* Reflection positivity implies ((F,F)\ge 0) for all (F\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+).

This makes ((\cdot,\cdot)) a **pre-inner product** on (\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+); it is positive semidefinite but may be degenerate.

Define the **null space**
[
\mathcal{N} := { F\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+ : (F,F) = 0}.
]

Reflection positivity guarantees that (\mathcal{N}) is a linear subspace. 

---

## 17.2. OS Hilbert space and vacuum vector

### 17.2.1. Pre-Hilbert space and completion

Define the quotient space
[
\mathcal{D}*\Lambda := \mathcal{A}*\Lambda^+ / \mathcal{N},
]
with equivalence relation (F\sim G) if (F-G \in \mathcal{N}). 

The pre-inner product descends to a **true inner product** on (\mathcal{D}*\Lambda) via
[
([F],[G])*{\mathcal{H}_\Lambda} := (F,G).
]

This is well-defined: if (F_1\sim F_2) and (G_1\sim G_2), then ((F_1,G_1) = (F_2,G_2)) because adding an element of (\mathcal{N}) does not change any inner product, again by reflection positivity.

We denote by (\mathcal{H}*\Lambda) the **completion** of ((\mathcal{D}*\Lambda,(\cdot,\cdot)*{\mathcal{H}*\Lambda})).

Thus:

> (\mathcal{H}*\Lambda) is the **OS Hilbert space** associated with the finite-volume lattice Yang–Mills measure (\mu*\Lambda), constructed from the positive-time algebra and reflection positivity.

We will often write the class of (F\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+) as ([!F!]), or simply (F) when no confusion is likely.

### 17.2.2. Vacuum vector

The constant observable (\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^+) defines a distinguished vector
[
\Omega*\Lambda := [\mathbf{1}] \in \mathcal{H}_\Lambda.
]

Its norm is
[
|\Omega_\Lambda|^2 = (\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})
= \langle \Theta \mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\rangle
= \int 1\cdot 1, d\mu_\Lambda = 1,
]
so (\Omega_\Lambda) is a **unit vector**. 

We interpret (\Omega_\Lambda) as the **vacuum** in the finite-volume OS Hilbert space.

## 17.3. Representation of observables

We now explain how gauge-invariant local observables act as operators on (\mathcal{H}_\Lambda).

Let (\mathcal{A}_\Lambda) be the full algebra of bounded gauge-invariant observables on the space–time lattice (not restricted to positive times). We focus on those observables whose support intersects the positive-time region in a controlled way, or which are localized at a fixed time slice and then time-translated (see §17.4 below).

### 17.3.1. Multiplication operators on (\mathcal{D}_\Lambda)

Given a bounded observable (\mathcal{O}) localized in some time slab that intersects (\Lambda^+), define an operator initially on (\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^+) by **multiplication**
[
(M*\mathcal{O} F)(U) := \mathcal{O}(U) F(U).
]

If (\mathcal{O}) is supported in strictly positive times, then (M_\mathcal{O} F \in \mathcal{A}*\Lambda^+) whenever (F\in\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^+). In that case we may attempt to define a densely defined operator on (\mathcal{D}_\Lambda) by
[
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{OS}} [F] := [,\mathcal{O} F,].
]

To see that this is well-defined (i.e. independent of the representative (F) in its equivalence class), one checks:

* If (F_1\sim F_2), then (F_1 - F_2\in\mathcal{N}) and, under mild locality hypotheses, reflection positivity implies (\mathcal{O}(F_1-F_2)\in\mathcal{N}), so ([\mathcal{O}F_1]=[\mathcal{O}F_2]).

Thus each suitably localized observable induces a densely defined (often closable, sometimes bounded) operator (\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{OS}}) on (\mathcal{H}_\Lambda).

In particular, **gauge-invariant local observables** supported in a single time slice define operators on (\mathcal{H}_\Lambda) in this way. These are the OS counterparts of physical fields at Euclidean time (t=0).

---

## 17.4. Time translations and the transfer matrix

The OS axioms (Part 16) ensure that **time translations** act as *-automorphisms on the observable algebra and are compatible with reflection positivity.* We now use this to construct the **transfer matrix** and the finite-volume Hamiltonian.

### 17.4.1. Time translation on configurations and observables

Let (\tau) be the discrete time-translation by one unit:
[
\tau(t,\mathbf{x}) := (t+1,\mathbf{x}) \mod T.
]

On configurations (U\in M_\Lambda), (\tau) acts by relabeling link variables in the time direction:
[
(\tau U)*{(t,\mathbf{x}),\mu} := U*{(t-1,\mathbf{x}),\mu},
]
with periodicity in time. 

On observables (\mathcal{O}), define the pullback
[
(\tau \mathcal{O})(U) := \mathcal{O}(\tau^{-1}U).
]

Thus (\tau) is a *-automorphism of the observable algebra; it implements Euclidean time translation.

For (F\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+), (\tau F) need not remain supported entirely in positive times. One typically restricts to a sufficiently large time slab and/or observables that stay in the positive-time region under a bounded number of translations. At finite volume this can always be arranged.

### 17.4.2. Transfer matrix on (\mathcal{H}_\Lambda)

Define an operator (T_\Lambda) on the dense subspace (\mathcal{D}*\Lambda\subset\mathcal{H}*\Lambda) by
[
T_\Lambda [F] := [\tau F],
]
whenever (\tau F \in \mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+). 

Reflection positivity and time-translation invariance of the measure imply that:

* (T_\Lambda) is **self-adjoint** with respect to the OS inner product,
* (T_\Lambda) is **positive** (all inner products ((F,T_\Lambda F)) are nonnegative),
* (|T_\Lambda|\le 1), i.e. (T_\Lambda) is a **contraction** on (\mathcal{H}_\Lambda).

These are standard OS properties: intuitively, (T_\Lambda) pushes observables forward by one Euclidean time-step in the Hilbert space picture.

By spectral theory for bounded self-adjoint operators, there exists a unique positive self-adjoint operator (H_\Lambda) on (\mathcal{H}*\Lambda) such that
[
T*\Lambda = e^{-a H_\Lambda},
]
where (a>0) is the Euclidean time lattice spacing. 

We call (H_\Lambda) the **finite-volume OS Hamiltonian**.

The vacuum vector (\Omega_\Lambda) is fixed by (T_\Lambda):

* Formally, (\tau \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}), so (T_\Lambda \Omega_\Lambda = \Omega_\Lambda), hence (H_\Lambda\Omega_\Lambda = 0).
* Thus (0) is the lowest eigenvalue of (H_\Lambda), corresponding to the vacuum.

## 17.5. Schwinger functions and spectral representation

We now connect the OS Hamiltonian (H_\Lambda) to **Euclidean Schwinger functions** and read off the **finite-volume mass gap** from the spectral representation.

### 17.5.1. Time-dependent observables and OS correspondence

Let (\mathcal{O}) be a gauge-invariant local observable supported on a single time slice (say (t=0)). Define its time-translated versions by
[
\mathcal{O}(t)(U) := \mathcal{O}(\tau^{-t} U),
]
so that (\mathcal{O}(t)) is localized at Euclidean time (t). 

The (n)-point Euclidean **Schwinger function** at times (0\le t_1\le \dots\le t_n) is
[
S_n(t_1,\dots,t_n)
:= \big\langle \mathcal{O}*1(t_1)\cdots \mathcal{O}*n(t_n)\big\rangle*{\mu*\Lambda}.
]

OS reconstruction gives the explicit Hilbert-space representation
[
S_n(t_1,\dots,t_n)
= \big\langle \Omega_\Lambda,
\mathcal{O}*1^{\mathrm{OS}}
e^{-(t_2-t_1)H*\Lambda}
\mathcal{O}*2^{\mathrm{OS}}
\cdots
e^{-(t_n-t*{n-1})H_\Lambda}
\mathcal{O}*n^{\mathrm{OS}}
\Omega*\Lambda
\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_\Lambda}.
]


Here the operators (\mathcal{O}_j^{\mathrm{OS}}) are the OS realizations (multiplication followed by projection) of the observables (\mathcal{O}_j) at time zero.

This is the OS **correspondence**: Euclidean-time correlations are matrix elements of the Euclidean evolution (e^{-t H_\Lambda}) between states created by observables from the vacuum.

### 17.5.2. Two-point function and spectral decomposition

Consider a single gauge-invariant observable (\mathcal{O}) with zero mean:
[
\langle \mathcal{O}\rangle_{\mu_\Lambda} = 0.
]

The Euclidean **two-point function** in the time direction is
[
C_\Lambda(t) := \langle \mathcal{O}(t),\mathcal{O}(0)\rangle_{\mu_\Lambda}.
]

In (\mathcal{H}*\Lambda), this is
[
C*\Lambda(t)
= \big\langle \Omega_\Lambda,
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{OS}} e^{-t H_\Lambda} \mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{OS}} \Omega_\Lambda
\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_\Lambda}. 
]

Let ((E_n,\psi_n)*{n\ge 0}) be the spectral decomposition of (H*\Lambda):
[
H_\Lambda \psi_n = E_n \psi_n,\qquad 0 = E_0 < E_1 \le E_2 \le \cdots,
]
with (\psi_0 = \Omega_\Lambda). Expanding
[
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{OS}}\Omega_\Lambda = \sum_{n\ge 1} a_n \psi_n,
]
(note that there is no (n=0) component since (\langle\Omega_\Lambda,\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{OS}}\Omega_\Lambda\rangle = \langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{\mu_\Lambda} = 0)), we find
[
\begin{aligned}
C_\Lambda(t)
&= \sum_{m,n\ge 1} \overline{a_m} a_n
\big\langle\psi_m, e^{-t H_\Lambda}\psi_n\big\rangle \
&= \sum_{n\ge 1} |a_n|^2 e^{-E_n t}.
\end{aligned}
]


Thus the two-point function is a **Laplace transform** of the spectral measure of (H_\Lambda) with respect to the state (\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{OS}}\Omega_\Lambda).

### 17.5.3. Finite-volume mass gap and exponential decay

Define the **finite-volume OS mass gap** by
[
\Delta_\Lambda := E_1(\Lambda) = \min{E_n>0 : n\ge 1}.
]

Then the spectral representation immediately yields:

* For every (t\ge 0),
  [
  |C_\Lambda(t)|
  \le e^{-\Delta_\Lambda t}
  \sum_{n\ge 1} |a_n|^2
  =  e^{-\Delta_\Lambda t}
  \big\langle \Omega_\Lambda, (\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{OS}})^2 \Omega_\Lambda\big\rangle,
  ]
  so two-point functions decay **at least** as fast as (e^{-\Delta_\Lambda t}). 

* Conversely, if for some observable (\mathcal{O}) the two-point function satisfies
  [
  |C_\Lambda(t)| \le C e^{-\delta t}
  \quad\text{for all }t\ge 0,
  ]
  with (\delta>0), then necessarily (\Delta_\Lambda \ge \delta). In particular, **any exponential decay** of (C_\Lambda(t)) with positive rate implies that (H_\Lambda) has a nonzero gap above the vacuum.

We summarize:

> **Definition 17.1 (Finite-volume OS mass gap).**
> For each finite (\Lambda), the **mass gap** of the OS Hamiltonian (H_\Lambda) is the lowest nonzero eigenvalue
> [
> \Delta_\Lambda := \inf\big(\sigma(H_\Lambda)\setminus{0}\big).
> ]
> It is equivalently characterized as the best exponential rate of decay of Euclidean two-point functions in the time direction for nontrivial gauge-invariant observables.

In this precise sense, **finite-volume mass gaps are encoded in the Euclidean-time decay of Schwinger functions.**

## 17.6. Relation to configuration-space analysis (forward look)

Part 17 has been deliberately OS-focused: starting from the finite-volume lattice Yang–Mills measure and its reflection positivity (Part 16), we have constructed:

1. A Hilbert space (\mathcal{H}*\Lambda) and vacuum (\Omega*\Lambda),
2. A positive self-adjoint Hamiltonian (H_\Lambda\ge 0),
3. A transfer matrix (T_\Lambda = e^{-a H_\Lambda}),
4. A correspondence between Euclidean Schwinger functions and matrix elements of (e^{-t H_\Lambda}). 

At the same time, Parts I–III gave **configuration-space** control for the same finite-volume measures (\mu_\Lambda):

* Curvature and Hessian structure (Part II),
* Local-to-global Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities (Part III),
* Uniform spectral gaps for the Gibbs diffusion generator (-L_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) under suitable hypotheses.

The natural question is:

> How does the spectral gap of the configuration-space generator (-L_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) (analytic side) relate to the mass gap (\Delta_\Lambda) of the OS Hamiltonian (H_\Lambda) (OS/Hamiltonian side)?

This is not automatic; it requires additional **comparison estimates** between configuration-space dynamics and transfer-matrix dynamics. The structural aspects of this comparison, together with associated conjectures (e.g. “(\Delta_\Lambda \ge c,\lambda_*)” for some (c>0) independent of (\Lambda)), are the subject of Part 18. 

For now, Part 17 completes the bridge from **lattice OS axioms** to a concrete **finite-volume Hamiltonian** whose spectral gap is the rigorous avatar of a lattice mass gap.
