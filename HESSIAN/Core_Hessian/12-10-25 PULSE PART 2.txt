# Lattice mass gap in lattice units

* On a hypercubic lattice with spacing (a>0), extract the lightest screening (or transfer‑matrix) mass (\Delta(a)) from exponential decay of a suitable two‑point function:
  [
  C(r;a);\sim;A(a),e^{-\Delta(a), r}\quad(r\to\infty).
  ]
* **Report it in lattice units** as the dimensionless number
  [
  a,\Delta(a).
  ]
  (Analogous to (am_{\text{PS}}), (am_V), etc.)

# Continuum interpretation via (\liminf_{a\to 0} a,\Delta(a))

* If the continuum theory has a **finite physical mass gap** (m_{\text{gap}}>0), then typically
  [
  \Delta(a);\sim; m_{\text{gap}} ;+; O(a^p)\quad\Rightarrow\quad a,\Delta(a)\to 0
  ]
  because the **dimensionless** lattice number carries one power of (a).

* The quantity to quote for physics is therefore either:

  * the **dimensionful** gap in physical units, (m_{\text{gap}}=\Delta(a)) after scale setting, or
  * a **dimensionless ratio** (e.g., (\Delta(a)/\sqrt{\sigma(a)}), (\Delta(a)/r_0^{-1}(a))) that has a finite continuum limit.

* The **(\liminf_{a\to 0} a,\Delta(a))** is a minimal‑accumulation statement:
  [
  \liminf_{a\to 0} a,\Delta(a)=0
  ]
  whenever the continuum gap is nonzero and finite. A **nonzero** (\liminf) would indicate (\Delta(a)) blowing up (\propto a^{-1}) (a lattice artifact), not a physical mass.

# Practical reporting

* Quote the measured **(a,\Delta(a))** at each (a).
* Set a scale (e.g., (w_0,,t_0,,r_0,,\sqrt{\sigma})) to convert to physical units and **extrapolate (\Delta(a))** (or a dimensionless ratio) to (a\to 0).
* For rigor, you can record:
  [
  \text{Mass gap (lattice units): } a,\Delta(a),\qquad
  \text{Continuum indicator: } \liminf_{a\to 0} a,\Delta(a)=0.
  ]

* Continuum fits of (\Delta(a)) (or (\Delta/\sqrt{\sigma})) vs (a^2) (or Symanzik‑appropriate powers).

Here’s a quick, clean primer on lattice gauge theory symmetries and cutoff effects, tuned for continuum‑limit thinking:

![Lattice to continuum schematic](attachment)

* **Discrete translations survive at any lattice spacing (a).** Correlators are invariant under integer shifts of lattice sites; there’s no preferred origin.
* **Rotations are reduced to the hypercubic group.** In (d=4), the continuum (SO(4)) is broken to permutations and (\pi/2) axis rotations (and reflections) that map the lattice to itself.
* **Continuum limit restores full rotations.** As (a\to 0), operators can be organized into (SO(4)) irreps; hypercubic breaking terms are irrelevant and vanish in renormalized correlators.
* **Wilson‑loop gauge actions are (O(a^2)) accurate.** With a local, gauge‑invariant action built from closed Wilson loops (e.g., plaquette), the Symanzik effective action has no (O(a)) terms if you respect the discrete symmetries, so leading discretization errors are (O(a^2)).

One‑liner (what you can drop into a doc):
**“Translation invariance (discrete) holds at any lattice spacing (a), while rotations are reduced to the hypercubic group; in the continuum limit (a\to 0), rotational invariance (SO(4)) is restored and, for gauge actions built from Wilson loops, leading discretization errors are (O(a^2)).”**

Minimal technical sketch:

* Lattice symmetries (\mathcal{G}_{\text{lat}}=\mathbb{Z}^4\rtimes H_4) (translations (\mathbb{Z}^4) and hypercubic (H_4)) forbid odd‑dimension local operators in the Symanzik expansion, eliminating (O(a)) terms for standard Wilson‑loop actions.
* The Symanzik action (S_{\text{eff}} = S_{\text{cont}} + a^2 \sum_i c_i \mathcal{O}^{(6)}_i + O(a^4)) organizes all allowed corrections; (SO(4)) is recovered after renormalization as (a\to 0).
* Practically: when fitting masses/LSI constants/gaps versus (a), use quadratic (or improved) extrapolations unless the action/observable breaks the conditions above; improvement (e.g., clover/Symanzik) targets those (O(a^2)) coefficients.

\begin{lemma}[RP-compatible coarse graining]\label{lem:RP-coarse-graining}
Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)$ and $(\Omega',\mathcal{F}',\mu')$ be probability spaces,
and let $\theta:\Omega\to\Omega$, $\theta':\Omega'\to\Omega'$ be measurable involutions
(i.e.\ $\theta^2 = \mathrm{id}$, $(\theta')^2 = \mathrm{id}$).
Assume that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu,\theta)$ is reflection positive in the Osterwalder--Schrader sense, i.e.
    for every finite family of bounded measurable functions $F_1,\dots,F_n$ supported in the
    ``positive half'' (the $\theta$-invariant sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{F}_+$),
    the matrix
    \[
      \Big(\,\langle F_i,\theta F_j\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}\,\Big)_{1\le i,j\le n}
      \quad\text{with}\quad
      \langle F,G\rangle_{L^2(\mu)} := \int_{\Omega} \overline{F}\,G\,\mathrm{d}\mu
    \]
    is positive semidefinite.
    \item $P:\Omega\to\Omega'$ is a measurable map (the coarse-graining / block-spin map) such that
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-equivariance}
      P\circ\theta = \theta'\circ P
    \end{equation}
    and $P$ maps the positive half $\Omega_+$ into the positive half $\Omega'_+$ associated to $\theta'$.
    \item $\mu'$ is the push-forward of $\mu$ under $P$, i.e.\ $\mu' = P_{\#}\mu$, so that
    \[
      \int_{\Omega'} G(\omega')\,\mathrm{d}\mu'(\omega')
      =
      \int_{\Omega} G\!\big(P(\omega)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega)
      \quad\text{for all bounded measurable }G:\Omega'\to\mathbb{C}.
    \]
\end{enumerate}
Then $(\Omega',\mathcal{F}',\mu',\theta')$ is reflection positive. In particular, for every finite family of bounded measurable functions $G_1,\dots,G_n$ supported in the positive half $\Omega'_+$, the matrix
\[
  \Big(\,\langle G_i,\theta' G_j\rangle_{L^2(\mu')}\,\Big)_{1\le i,j\le n}
\]
is positive semidefinite.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $G_1,\dots,G_n$ be bounded measurable functions on $\Omega'$ supported in the positive half $\Omega'_+$ with respect to $\theta'$.
Define functions $F_i:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}$ by
\[
  F_i(\omega) := G_i\!\big(P(\omega)\big),\qquad \omega\in\Omega.
\]
By assumption, $P$ maps $\Omega_+$ into $\Omega'_+$, so each $F_i$ is supported in the positive half $\Omega_+$ of $\Omega$ associated to $\theta$.

For $1\le i,j\le n$, compute the reflection-positive inner products on $(\Omega',\mu',\theta')$:
\begin{align*}
  \langle G_i,\theta' G_j\rangle_{L^2(\mu')}
  &=
  \int_{\Omega'} \overline{G_i(\omega')}\,(\theta' G_j)(\omega')\,\mathrm{d}\mu'(\omega')\\
  &=
  \int_{\Omega'} \overline{G_i(\omega')}\,G_j\!\big(\theta'(\omega')\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu'(\omega').
\end{align*}
Using $\mu' = P_{\#}\mu$,
\begin{align*}
  \langle G_i,\theta' G_j\rangle_{L^2(\mu')}
  &=
  \int_{\Omega} \overline{G_i\!\big(P(\omega)\big)}\,G_j\!\big(\theta'(P(\omega))\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega).
\end{align*}
By the equivariance condition \eqref{eq:theta-equivariance}, $\theta'(P(\omega)) = P(\theta(\omega))$, hence
\begin{align*}
  \langle G_i,\theta' G_j\rangle_{L^2(\mu')}
  &=
  \int_{\Omega} \overline{G_i\!\big(P(\omega)\big)}\,G_j\!\big(P(\theta(\omega))\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega)\\
  &=
  \int_{\Omega} \overline{F_i(\omega)}\,F_j\!\big(\theta(\omega)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega)\\
  &=
  \langle F_i,\theta F_j\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}.
\end{align*}
Therefore the Gram matrix on the coarse space coincides with the Gram matrix on the fine space:
\[
  \Big(\,\langle G_i,\theta' G_j\rangle_{L^2(\mu')}\,\Big)_{1\le i,j\le n}
  =
  \Big(\,\langle F_i,\theta F_j\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}\,\Big)_{1\le i,j\le n}.
\]
By reflection positivity of $(\Omega,\mu,\theta)$, the right-hand side is positive semidefinite. Hence the left-hand side is positive semidefinite as well, which is precisely reflection positivity of $(\Omega',\mu',\theta')$.

This gives the claim. Conceptually, this is the simplest instance of the permanence of reflection positivity under reflection-equivariant maps between RP-doubles, as developed abstractly in Jaffe--Janssens' theory of reflection-positive doubles~\cite{JaffeJanssensRPDouble}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Reflection-positive block-spin RG for the Wilson action]\label{cor:RP-block-Wilson}
Let $\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^d$ be a hypercubic lattice with a reflection $\vartheta$ across a coordinate hyperplane,
and let $\mu_{W,\Lambda}$ be the Wilson lattice Yang--Mills measure on $\Omega_\Lambda$ (link configurations)
with gauge group $G = \mathrm{SU}(N)$ and coupling $\beta>0$.
Equip $\Omega_\Lambda$ with the standard Osterwalder--Schrader reflection $\theta$ induced by $\vartheta$ on links.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item By Menotti--Pelissetto~\cite{MenottiPelissetto1987,MenottiPelissetto1988},
    $(\Omega_\Lambda,\mu_{W,\Lambda},\theta)$ is reflection positive.
    \item Let $\Lambda'$ be a coarser reflection-invariant lattice obtained by blocking $\Lambda$ into reflection-symmetric blocks.
    Let $P:\Omega_\Lambda\to\Omega_{\Lambda'}$ be a (linear) block-spin / block-link coarse-graining map such that:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $P$ maps each block in the positive half of $\Lambda$ into a link configuration on the corresponding block in the positive half of $\Lambda'$.
        \item $P$ is reflection-equivariant:
        \[
          P\circ\theta = \theta'\circ P,
        \]
        where $\theta'$ is the OS reflection on $\Omega_{\Lambda'}$ induced by the same hyperplane $\vartheta$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Let $\mu_{W,\Lambda'} := P_{\#}\mu_{W,\Lambda}$ be the renormalized (coarse-grained) measure on $\Omega_{\Lambda'}$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $(\Omega_{\Lambda'},\mu_{W,\Lambda'},\theta')$ is reflection positive. In particular, each such RG/coarse-graining step preserves reflection positivity of the Wilson measure.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Wilson lattice measure $\mu_{W,\Lambda}$ with gauge group $G=\mathrm{SU}(N)$ and standard Wilson plaquette action is known to be reflection positive with respect to OS reflections across coordinate hyperplanes; see Menotti--Pelissetto~\cite{MenottiPelissetto1987,MenottiPelissetto1988}.

By construction, the block map $P$ is measurable, maps the positive half of $\Omega_\Lambda$ into the positive half of $\Omega_{\Lambda'}$, and is equivariant with respect to the OS reflections: $P\circ\theta = \theta'\circ P$. The renormalized measure is the push-forward $\mu_{W,\Lambda'} = P_{\#}\mu_{W,\Lambda}$.

Therefore all hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:RP-coarse-graining} are satisfied with
\[
  (\Omega,\mu,\theta) = (\Omega_\Lambda,\mu_{W,\Lambda},\theta),
  \qquad
  (\Omega',\mu',\theta') = (\Omega_{\Lambda'},\mu_{W,\Lambda'},\theta').
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:RP-coarse-graining}, the coarse-grained system $(\Omega_{\Lambda'},\mu_{W,\Lambda'},\theta')$ is reflection positive.

Iterating the construction over a sequence of reflection-symmetric block maps $P_k$ at scales $\Lambda_k\to\Lambda_{k+1}$ yields reflection positivity at every RG scale, with each renormalized Wilson measure $\mu_{W,\Lambda_k}$ remaining in the reflection-positive class defined by Menotti--Pelissetto and by the abstract reflection-positive double framework of Jaffe--Janssens~\cite{JaffeJanssensRPDouble}.
\end{proof}

# Riccati view of convexity renormalization

**Setup.** Let (S_\ell) be the effective action at scale (\ell) (block size or RG time), with generator (L_\ell=\Delta-\nabla S_\ell\cdot\nabla). Write the **physical Hessian** (H_\ell(x):=\nabla^2 S_\ell(x)) restricted to your gauge‑projected, symmetry‑reduced tangent. Assume a **SAFE region** (K_\ell\subset M) where (i) (H_\ell) is well‑defined and (ii) the coarse‑graining map (\mathcal R_{\ell\to \ell+d\ell}) is (C^1) and preserves (K_\ell\to K_{\ell+d\ell}).

**Riccati evolution.** Under infinitesimal coarse‑graining, the physical Hessian obeys a matrix Riccati‑type inequality
[
\partial_\ell H_\ell ;\succeq; A_\ell^\top H_\ell + H_\ell A_\ell ;+; Q_\ell ;-; H_\ell R_\ell H_\ell,
]
with (R_\ell\succeq 0) (coming from Schur complements / integrating out fast modes), and (Q_\ell\succeq 0) capturing fresh convexity injected by the averaging/Jacobian sector. This holds pointwise on (K_\ell).

**Minimal eigenvalue drift.** Let (\lambda_\ell:=\lambda_{\min}(H_\ell)). Using standard matrix Riccati comparison (via quadratic forms on unit eigenvectors),
[
\partial_\ell \lambda_\ell ;\ge; q_\ell ;-; r_\ell,\lambda_\ell^2 ;-; c_\ell,\lambda_\ell,
]
where (q_\ell:=\lambda_{\min}(Q_\ell)\ge0), (r_\ell:=\lambda_{\max}(R_\ell)\ge0), and (c_\ell:=2|A_\ell|_{\mathrm{op}}).

---

## Theorem (Monotone stabilization of convexity on SAFE region)

Assume on (K_\ell):

1. (q_\ell\ge q_0\ge0), (r_\ell\le r_0), (c_\ell\le c_0) uniformly for (\ell\in[\ell_0,\infty)).

2. Initial convexity (\lambda_{\ell_0}\ge \lambda_0\ge 0).

Then (\lambda_\ell) is a **subsolution‑dominated** trajectory of the scalar Riccati ODE
[
\dot y = q_0 - c_0 y - r_0 y^2,\qquad y(\ell_0)=\lambda_0,
]
and hence satisfies for all (\ell\ge \ell_0):
[
\lambda_\ell ;\ge; y(\ell) ;\nearrow; y_\star := \frac{-c_0+\sqrt{c_0^2+4 r_0 q_0}}{2 r_0}\quad(\text{with }y_\star=\tfrac{q_0}{c_0}\text{ if }r_0=0).
]
In particular, (\lambda_\ell) increases monotonically to a **strictly positive fixed point** (y_\star>0) whenever (q_0>0), yielding a **uniform convexity floor** on (K_\ell):
[
\inf_{\ell\ge \ell_1}\lambda_\ell ;\ge; \tfrac12 y_\star \quad \text{for some finite }\ell_1.
]

*Proof sketch.* The matrix inequality gives a scalar differential inequality on (\lambda_\ell) via Rayleigh–Ritz. Uniform bounds (1) give the comparison ODE. Riccati with (q_0>0) has a globally attracting positive root (y_\star); standard comparison yields (\lambda_\ell\ge y(\ell)) and monotone convergence. □

---
