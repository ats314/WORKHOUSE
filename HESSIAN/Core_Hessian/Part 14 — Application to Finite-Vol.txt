Part 14 — Application to Finite-Volume Lattice Yang–Mills: Structural Global Functional Inequalities

In this part we explain how the abstract local-to-global machinery of Part III interacts with the **finite-volume lattice Yang–Mills** family ((M_\Lambda,\mu_\Lambda)) constructed in Part II.

The goal is *structural* rather than fully proved:

* Translate the geometric input from Part II (local horizontal Bakry–Émery curvature) into the hypotheses of the local-to-global Poincaré and LSI theorems of Part III.
* Isolate the remaining analytic tasks, primarily Lyapunov estimates and local functional inequalities.
* Record precisely what one would obtain for lattice Yang–Mills **if** those analytic hypotheses are verified with constants **uniform in the volume (\Lambda)**. 

We keep the focus on **gauge-invariant observables** and their horizontal gradients, as set up in Part II.

---

## 14.1. Finite-volume Yang–Mills data

Fix:

* A compact, connected Lie group (G) (typically (SU(N))) with bi-invariant metric (g_G).
* A finite hypercubic lattice (\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^d) with oriented edge set (E(\Lambda)).
* The configuration manifold
  [
  M_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)},
  ]
  with product Riemannian metric (g_\Lambda) and product Haar volume (\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}).
* An **effective action**
  [
  S_\Lambda = S_W + S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}
  ]
  satisfying the hypotheses of the core curvature theorem of Part II (Wilson + Haar + additional terms with globally bounded Hessian from below). 

The corresponding Gibbs measure and diffusion generator are
[
d\mu_\Lambda(U) = Z_\Lambda^{-1} e^{-S_\Lambda(U)},d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U),
\qquad
L_\Lambda = \Delta_{g_\Lambda} - \langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla\cdot\rangle,
]
with carré du champ
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U) = |\nabla f(U)|*{g*\Lambda}^2.
]

The **gauge group** (\mathcal{G}*\Lambda = G^{V(\Lambda)}) acts on (M*\Lambda) by endpoint conjugation; this action is isometric and preserves (\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}) and (\mu_\Lambda). Gauge-invariant observables satisfy (\nabla f(U)\in H_U), where (H_U) is the horizontal (physical) subspace defined in Part II. 

In this part we always restrict (\Gamma_\Lambda), (L_\Lambda), and all functional inequalities to the **gauge-invariant sector**
[
\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}} := { f\in C^\infty(M*\Lambda) : f(g\cdot U)=f(U)\ \forall g\in\mathcal{G}_\Lambda}.
]

---

## 14.2. Small-field region and local horizontal curvature bound

Part II proved a **local horizontal Bakry–Émery curvature bound**, uniform in (\Lambda). Concretely, there exist constants (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}>0) and (r>0) such that for all finite lattices (\Lambda):

* Define the **small-field region**
  [
  \Omega_\Lambda := B_r(U^{(0)}) = {U\in M_\Lambda : d_{g_\Lambda}(U,U^{(0)}) \le r},
  ]
  where (U^{(0)}) is the trivial configuration (all links (=e\in G)).
* For all (U\in \Omega_\Lambda) and all horizontal vectors (v\in H_U),
  [
  \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)(v,v)
  ;\ge; \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},|v|*{g*\Lambda}^2.
  ]


Equivalently, for every smooth gauge-invariant (f),
[
\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(U) ;\ge; \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\Gamma_\Lambda(f)(U)
\quad\text{for all }U\in\Omega_\Lambda,
]
where (\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}) is the iterated carré du champ for (L_\Lambda). 

Thus, **restricted to gauge-invariant observables**, the generator (L_\Lambda) satisfies a local curvature–dimension condition
[
CD(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)\quad\text{on }\Omega_\Lambda,
]
with constants independent of (\Lambda).

This is the Yang–Mills incarnation of the abstract hypothesis (H(_{\mathrm{loc}})) of Part III. 

---

## 14.3. Local functional inequalities in the Yang–Mills small-field region

The local Bakry–Émery bound immediately yields **local functional inequalities** on (\Omega_\Lambda), in the sense of Part I:

* On any relatively compact open (U_\Lambda\subset \Omega_\Lambda) with (\mu_\Lambda(U_\Lambda)>0), classical BE theory implies:

  * A **local Poincaré inequality**:
    [
    \int_{U_\Lambda} (f - f_{U_\Lambda})^2,d\mu_\Lambda
    ;\le; C_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Lambda) \int_{U_\Lambda} \Gamma_\Lambda(f),d\mu_\Lambda
    ]
    for all smooth gauge-invariant (f), where
    [
    f_{U_\Lambda} := \frac{1}{\mu_\Lambda(U_\Lambda)}\int_{U_\Lambda} f,d\mu_\Lambda.
    ]
  * A **local logarithmic Sobolev inequality**:
    [
    \operatorname{Ent}*{\mu*\Lambda}\big(f^2 \mathbf{1}*{U*\Lambda}\big)
    ;\le; C_{\mathrm{LS,loc}}(\Lambda)\int_{U_\Lambda} \Gamma_\Lambda(f),d\mu_\Lambda,
    ]
    whenever (\int_{U_\Lambda} f^2,d\mu_\Lambda = 1). 

The constants (C_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Lambda)) and (C_{\mathrm{LS,loc}}(\Lambda)) can be controlled in terms of:

* The local curvature lower bound (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}),
* The geometry and volume of (U_\Lambda\subset \Omega_\Lambda),
* The local dimension and interaction structure.

Because:

* (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}) is independent of (\Lambda),
* (\Omega_\Lambda) is a ball of fixed radius (r) in the product metric,
* The local geometry is **product-local** and the lattice has bounded coordination number,

it is natural (and standard in finite-dimensional BE geometry) to expect that one can choose:

* a canonical small-field region (U_\Lambda\subset\Omega_\Lambda) (e.g. a ball of radius (r/2) in geodesic or plaquette-angle norms),
* and constants (C_{\mathrm{loc}},C_{\mathrm{LS,loc}}) independent of (\Lambda) for these sets. 

Since we are not supplying the full geometric estimates here, we package this expectation as an explicit hypothesis:

> **Assumption (YM-Local-FI).**
> There exists a bounded, gauge-invariant region (U_\Lambda\subset\Omega_\Lambda) and constants (C_{\mathrm{loc}}, C_{\mathrm{LS,loc}} < \infty), **independent of (\Lambda)**, such that for all smooth gauge-invariant (f):
> [
> \int_{U_\Lambda} (f - f_{U_\Lambda})^2,d\mu_\Lambda
> ;\le; C_{\mathrm{loc}} \int_{U_\Lambda} \Gamma_\Lambda(f),d\mu_\Lambda,
> ]
> and, whenever (\int_{U_\Lambda} f^2,d\mu_\Lambda = 1),
> [
> \operatorname{Ent}*{\mu*\Lambda}\big(f^2 \mathbf{1}*{U*\Lambda}\big)
> ;\le; C_{\mathrm{LS,loc}} \int_{U_\Lambda} \Gamma_\Lambda(f),d\mu_\Lambda.
> ] 

This is exactly the “local functional inequality” hypothesis needed to invoke the local-to-global theorems of Part III.

## 14.4. Lyapunov functions for lattice Yang–Mills

The local-to-global results of Part III also require **Lyapunov drift conditions** that force the process back towards the small-field region. Abstractly, one needs functions (W_\Lambda : M_\Lambda\to[1,\infty)) such that:

[
L_\Lambda W_\Lambda ;\le; -\alpha W_\Lambda + \beta,\mathbf{1}*{K*\Lambda}
]
for some constants (\alpha,\beta>0) and sets (K_\Lambda \subset U_\Lambda), with all of these bounds **independent of (\Lambda)**. 

### 14.4.1. Structural form of (W_\Lambda)

Heuristically, good candidates for (W_\Lambda) should grow with the **total field strength**, measured for example by plaquette angles. A typical ansatz is
[
W_\Lambda(U) := 1 + \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \Phi\big(\operatorname{dist}_G(U_p(U),e)\big),
]
where:

* (U_p(U)\in G) is the plaquette holonomy at plaquette (p),
* (\operatorname{dist}_G(\cdot,e)) is the Riemannian distance on (G),
* (\Phi : [0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)) is some convex, increasing function, e.g. (\Phi(r)=r^2) or a truncated power.

Such a (W_\Lambda):

* is gauge-invariant, since (U_p) is conjugation invariant under (\mathcal{G}_\Lambda),
* measures “distance from the vacuum” in a way that is additive over plaquettes and hence well adapted to locality,
* naturally grows large on configurations with many large plaquette angles.

Other choices might incorporate link-based energies or norms of discrete field strengths, but the common theme is **locality plus growth with field strength**.

### 14.4.2. Drift inequality and volume-uniformity

The drift inequality
[
L_\Lambda W_\Lambda \le -\alpha W_\Lambda + \beta 1_{K_\Lambda}
]
should be understood as follows:

* Outside a small set (K_\Lambda\subset U_\Lambda) (e.g. where all plaquettes are “small”), the generator (L_\Lambda) produces a **negative drift** in (W_\Lambda), pulling the process towards smaller total field strength.
* On (K_\Lambda) the drift may be positive but is bounded (so the dynamics is not explosive there).

The constants (\alpha,\beta) and a normalized description of (K_\Lambda) must be **independent of (\Lambda)** when expressed in lattice units. This is plausible because:

* (S_\Lambda) is a finite sum of **local** terms (plaquettes and possible local regulators),
* Each link participates in only finitely many plaquettes,
* The generator (L_\Lambda) is built from local derivatives of (S_\Lambda) and the Laplace–Beltrami operator on a product manifold of bounded local geometry.

The detailed verification of such Lyapunov inequalities is a separate analytic problem, involving careful control of large-field contributions. In the present part we encode this requirement as a second explicit hypothesis.

> **Assumption (YM-Lyapunov).**
> There exists a family of smooth, gauge-invariant Lyapunov functions (W_\Lambda : M_\Lambda\to[1,\infty)) and constants (\alpha,\beta>0), independent of (\Lambda), and compact sets (K_\Lambda \subset U_\Lambda\subset\Omega_\Lambda) such that:
> [
> L_\Lambda W_\Lambda(U) ;\le; -\alpha W_\Lambda(U) + \beta,\mathbf{1}*{K*\Lambda}(U)
> \quad \text{for all }U\in M_\Lambda.
> ]

Assumptions (YM-Local-FI) and (YM-Lyapunov) are precisely the inputs that allow us to plug the YM family into the **local-to-global functional inequality** results of Part III.

## 14.5. Local-to-global theorems specialized to lattice Yang–Mills

The local-to-global Poincaré and LSI theorems of Part III (Section 2 and Section 3) say, roughly:

* Local curvature (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}>0) on a region (\Omega),
* Local Poincaré / super-Poincaré (or local LSI) on a smaller set (U\subset\Omega),
* A Lyapunov drift condition back towards (U),

together imply **global** Poincaré and LSI with explicit constants. 

We now specialize these to the Yang–Mills family.

### 14.5.1. Global Poincaré inequality for finite-volume Yang–Mills

Let (\Lambda) be fixed and consider the YM measure (\mu_\Lambda) on (M_\Lambda). Assume:

1. (**Local curvature**) The horizontal Bakry–Émery tensor satisfies
   [
   \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}(U)(v,v) \ge \rho_{\mathrm{loc}},|v|^2
   \quad\forall U\in\Omega_\Lambda,\ \forall v\in H_U,
   ]
   with (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}>0) independent of (\Lambda), as in Part II. 

2. (**Local functional inequalities**) Assumption (YM-Local-FI) holds on some (U_\Lambda\subset\Omega_\Lambda) with constants (C_{\mathrm{loc}},C_{\mathrm{LS,loc}}) independent of (\Lambda). 

3. (**Lyapunov drift**) Assumption (YM-Lyapunov) holds with Lyapunov functions (W_\Lambda) and constants (\alpha,\beta>0) independent of (\Lambda).

Then the Poincaré local-to-global theorem (Part III, §2) implies:

> **Theorem 14.1 (Global Poincaré for finite-volume YM, structural).**
> Under the hypotheses above, there exists a finite constant (C_P>0), depending only on (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}, C_{\mathrm{loc}}, \alpha,\beta) and geometric data **but not on (\Lambda)**, such that for all smooth gauge-invariant observables (f\in\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}),
> [
> \operatorname{Var}*{\mu_\Lambda}(f)
> ;\le; C_P \int_{M_\Lambda} \Gamma_\Lambda(f),d\mu_\Lambda.
> ]

Equivalently, the generator (L_\Lambda), restricted to (\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}), has a spectral gap (\lambda*{1,\Lambda}^{\mathrm{inv}} \ge C_P^{-1} > 0) independent of (\Lambda).

The proof is exactly the one in Part III §2, applied with:

* (M = M_\Lambda),
* (\mu = \mu_\Lambda),
* (\Gamma = \Gamma_\Lambda),
* local curvature set (V = \Omega_\Lambda),
* small set (K_\Lambda\subset U_\Lambda\subset\Omega_\Lambda),
* Lyapunov function (W_\Lambda). 

The point is that **all constants entering the theorem’s conclusion are controlled uniformly in (\Lambda)** by our assumptions.

### 14.5.2. Global log-Sobolev inequality for finite-volume Yang–Mills

Similarly, the log-Sobolev local-to-global theorem of Part III §3 says that:

* local curvature (CD(\rho_{\mathrm{loc}},\infty)) on (\Omega_\Lambda),
* a local super-Poincaré or LSI on (U_\Lambda),
* a Lyapunov drift condition with appropriate growth of (W_\Lambda),

imply a global LSI for (\mu_\Lambda). Specializing this to the YM case (again under (YM-Local-FI) and (YM-Lyapunov)) yields:

> **Theorem 14.2 (Global log-Sobolev for finite-volume YM, structural).**
> Under the same hypotheses as Theorem 14.1, plus the local LSI part of (YM-Local-FI), there exists a finite constant (C_{\mathrm{LS}}>0), independent of (\Lambda), such that
> [
> \operatorname{Ent}*{\mu*\Lambda}(f^2)
> ;\le; C_{\mathrm{LS}} \int_{M_\Lambda} \Gamma_\Lambda(f),d\mu_\Lambda
> \quad \forall f\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}.
> ]

Again, we do not repeat the proof: it is a direct application of the general theorem in Part III, with YM plugged into the abstract symbols. The crucial feature is that the **local curvature and drift data are volume-uniform**, so the resulting LSI constant (C_{\mathrm{LS}}) is also uniform.

## 14.6. Consequences for finite-volume spectral gaps and mixing

Assuming Theorems 14.1 and 14.2 have been made rigorous by verifying (YM-Local-FI) and (YM-Lyapunov), we obtain the following consequences for the YM Langevin generator (L_\Lambda) and its semigroup (P_t^{(\Lambda)} = e^{tL_\Lambda}), restricted to the gauge-invariant sector.

### 14.6.1. Spectral gap and exponential (L^2)-mixing

The **global Poincaré inequality** implies that:

* The operator (-L_\Lambda), acting on (L^2(\mu_\Lambda)) and restricted to (\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}), has a spectral gap (\lambda*{1,\Lambda}^{\mathrm{inv}}\ge C_P^{-1}>0) independent of (\Lambda).
* Consequently, for every (f\in\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) with (\mu*\Lambda(f)=0),
  [
  |P_t^{(\Lambda)} f|*{L^2(\mu*\Lambda)}
  ;\le; e^{-\lambda_{1,\Lambda}^{\mathrm{inv}} t} |f|*{L^2(\mu*\Lambda)},
  ]
  with (\lambda_{1,\Lambda}^{\mathrm{inv}}\ge C_P^{-1}) uniform in (\Lambda). 

This is the **configuration-space analogue of a mass gap**: at fixed lattice spacing, the Langevin dynamics in field space relaxes exponentially fast to equilibrium with a rate bounded away from zero uniformly in the spatial volume.

### 14.6.2. Entropy decay and hypercontractivity

The global LSI strengthens this to control **entropy decay** and hypercontractivity:

* For any (f\in\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) with (\mu*\Lambda(f^2)=1),
  [
  \operatorname{Ent}*{\mu*\Lambda}\big( (P_t^{(\Lambda)} f)^2\big)
  ;\le; e^{-2t/C_{\mathrm{LS}}},\operatorname{Ent}*{\mu*\Lambda}(f^2),
  ]
  with decay rate independent of (\Lambda).
* The semigroup (P_t^{(\Lambda)}) is hypercontractive in the sense that it maps (L^2(\mu_\Lambda)) into (L^p(\mu_\Lambda)) with (p>2) for all (t>0), with (p-2) controlled by (t/C_{\mathrm{LS}}) uniformly in (\Lambda).

These estimates are the configuration-space analogues of **finite-volume mass scales**: they constrain how correlations in field space can spread and make precise how curvature and Lyapunov control translate into strong mixing properties.

## 14.7. Summary and open analytic tasks

Structurally, after Parts I–III and this chapter, the situation can be summarized as follows. 

1. **Geometry (Part II):**
   Haar geometry + Wilson action + gauge structure give a **local horizontal Bakry–Émery curvature bound** on a small-field region (\Omega_\Lambda), with curvature constant (\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}>0) and radius (r>0) independent of the finite volume (\Lambda). 

2. **Analysis (Part III §2–3):**
   Abstract local-to-global theorems show that:

   * Local curvature + local Poincaré/LSI + Lyapunov drift (\Rightarrow) global Poincaré and LSI, with constants depending only on those local/Drift data.

3. **Yang–Mills structural translation (this part):**
   For lattice Yang–Mills, the abstract analytic hypotheses become the two concrete conditions:

   * **(YM-Local-FI)**: local Poincaré and LSI on a bounded, gauge-invariant small-field region (U_\Lambda\subset\Omega_\Lambda) with constants independent of (\Lambda),
   * **(YM-Lyapunov)**: existence of volume-uniform Lyapunov functions (W_\Lambda) encoding drift back to (U_\Lambda).

4. **What one gets if (YM-Local-FI) and (YM-Lyapunov) hold:**
   For each finite (\Lambda), in the gauge-invariant sector,

   * a **global Poincaré inequality** with constant (C_P) independent of (\Lambda),
   * a **global LSI** with constant (C_{\mathrm{LS}}) independent of (\Lambda),
   * hence **volume-uniform spectral gaps** and strong mixing for the configuration-space dynamics. 

These are exactly the analytic inputs needed in later parts to connect to:

* **Spectral gaps of OS Hamiltonians** via reflection positivity and OS reconstruction, and
* **Continuum mass gaps** via suitable scaling limits.

The **hard analytic work that remains** is to actually prove (or at least provide convincing bounds for) the two Yang–Mills-specific hypotheses:

* Local functional inequalities on a canonical small-field region with volume-uniform constants,
* Volume-uniform Lyapunov drift estimates for interacting non-abelian gauge fields.

The next part (Part 15) will turn this structural picture into a precise **checklist** of analytic tasks for the Yang–Mills mass-gap program, distinguishing clearly between what is already proved in this project and what is still conjectural.
