# Part 7 — Wilson Action, Gauge Symmetry, and Reflection Positivity (Configuration Level)

In this part we finally put a Yang–Mills interaction on the configuration manifold constructed in Part 6 and record two structural facts that are used repeatedly later:

1. The Wilson plaquette action is a smooth, local, gauge-invariant potential on
   ((M_\Lambda,g_\Lambda)), and, together with the product Haar measure, defines a finite-dimensional Gibbs system.

2. On a spacetime lattice, the same Wilson action is reflection positive with respect to Euclidean time reflection in the classical Osterwalder–Seiler sense.

The first point is what allows us to feed lattice Yang–Mills into the Bakry–Émery curvature machinery (via (\operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} + \nabla^2 S_\Lambda)). The second point is the key OS input for constructing a transfer matrix and Hamiltonian in Parts 16–17.

We work first on a purely spatial lattice (for curvature and Bakry–Émery) and then extend to a spacetime lattice to discuss time reflection and reflection positivity.

---

## 7.1. Spatial lattice and configuration manifold (recap)

Let (\Lambda \subset \mathbb{Z}^d) be a finite hypercubic lattice (e.g. a periodic box).

* Vertices (sites): (V(\Lambda)).
* Oriented edges (links): (E(\Lambda)).
* Oriented plaquettes: (P(\Lambda)), i.e. unit squares with an orientation.

The configuration manifold is the product of one group copy per oriented edge,
[
M_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)},
]
where (G) is a fixed compact, connected Lie group such as (SU(N)). A configuration (U\in M_\Lambda) is a collection of link variables
[
U = (U_{x,\mu})*{(x,\mu)\in E(\Lambda)}, \quad U*{x,\mu} \in G.
]

We equip each factor (G) with a fixed bi-invariant Riemannian metric (g_G), and (M_\Lambda) with the product metric
[
g_\Lambda = \bigoplus_{\ell \in E(\Lambda)} g_G,
]
and the corresponding product Haar volume form (\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}). This is exactly the structure built in Part 6; we recall it here only to fix notation and emphasize that ((M_\Lambda,g_\Lambda)) is a compact, positively curved Riemannian manifold with product Haar measure as reference. 

In this chapter we keep the metric and volume fixed and add an interaction potential — the Wilson action.

---

## 7.2. Plaquette holonomies on the spatial lattice

The Wilson action is built from oriented plaquette holonomies, which discretize the curvature.

### 7.2.1. Orientation conventions

Fix once and for all an orientation convention for plaquettes: for each spatial plaquette (p \in P(\Lambda)), pick a basepoint (x\in V(\Lambda)) and an ordered pair of coordinate directions (\mu,\nu\in{1,\dots,d}) so that (p) is the unit square with corners
[
x,\quad x+\hat\mu,\quad x+\hat\mu+\hat\nu,\quad x+\hat\nu,
]
traversed counterclockwise, say.

Different choices of orientation or basepoint differ only by conjugation or inversion of the resulting holonomy; since the action will use a class function of the holonomy (trace or character), these conventions are immaterial.

### 7.2.2. Plaquette holonomy

Given a configuration (U = (U_{x,\mu})\in M_\Lambda), define the plaquette holonomy (U_p(U)\in G) as the ordered product of link variables around the boundary of (p):
[
U_p(U) := U_{x,\mu}, U_{x+\hat\mu,\nu}, U^{-1}*{x+\hat\nu,\mu}, U^{-1}*{x,\nu}.
]

This is the discrete analogue of the path-ordered exponential of the gauge field around a small loop. The map
[
M_\Lambda \longrightarrow G,\quad U\longmapsto U_p(U)
]
is smooth and depends only on the finitely many links on the boundary of (p). 

---

## 7.3. Wilson plaquette action as a smooth local potential

We now define the Wilson action on the spatial lattice and record its basic analytic properties.

### 7.3.1. Single-plaquette contribution

Fix a coupling parameter (\beta > 0). For definiteness, take (G = SU(N)); the general compact case follows with a character.

The single-plaquette Wilson term is
[
S_p(U) := \beta\left( 1 - \frac{1}{N},\Re\operatorname{Tr} U_p(U) \right).
]

Properties:

1. **Smoothness.**
   (S_p) is smooth as a function of (U), being a composition of smooth group operations and the matrix trace.

2. **Non-negativity and boundedness.**
   For (U_p\in SU(N)),
   [
   \Re \operatorname{Tr} U_p \le N,
   ]
   hence (S_p(U)\ge 0). On the compact space (M_\Lambda), all (S_p) are bounded above as well.

3. **Locality.**
   (S_p(U)) depends only on the four link variables around (p): the interaction is strictly local at the lattice scale. 

For a general compact Lie group (G), one can replace the normalized trace with a fixed real-valued class function (\chi) on (G) and define
[
S_p(U) := \beta \big( c_0 - \chi(U_p(U))\big)
]
for a suitable constant (c_0); the geometric and analytic features we use (smoothness, locality, gauge invariance) are unchanged. 

### 7.3.2. Total Wilson action

The Wilson action on the spatial lattice is the sum of these plaquette contributions:
[
S_W(U) := \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} S_p(U)
= \beta \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{N},\Re\operatorname{Tr} U_p(U)\right).
]

This defines a smooth function (S_W : M_\Lambda \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}), local at the lattice scale (finite-range interaction), and gauge-invariant in a sense we now make precise. 

---

## 7.4. Lattice gauge group and its action

The lattice gauge group is the product of one copy of (G) per site:
[
\mathcal{G}*\Lambda := G^{V(\Lambda)},\quad g = (g_x)*{x\in V(\Lambda)}.
]

It acts on (M_\Lambda) by “endpoint conjugation”:
[
(U^g)*{x,\mu} := g_x, U*{x,\mu}, g_{x+\hat\mu}^{-1}.
]

This action satisfies:

* **Isometry.**
  Each map (U\mapsto U^g) is a product of left and right translations in the group (G); since the metric on each factor is bi-invariant, the action is isometric for (g_\Lambda).

* **Volume preservation.**
  Haar measure on each factor is left- and right-invariant, so the product Haar volume (\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}) is invariant under (\mathcal{G}_\Lambda).

* **Gauge invariance of the action.**
  Under this action the plaquette holonomy transforms by conjugation:
  [
  U_p(U^g) = h_p, U_p(U), h_p^{-1}
  ]
  for some site-dependent element (h_p) built from the (g_x)’s at the plaquette corners. Because (S_p) depends on (U_p) only through a class function (trace / character), we have
  [
  S_p(U^g) = S_p(U),\quad \text{hence}\quad S_W(U^g) = S_W(U)
  ]
  for all (g\in \mathcal{G}_\Lambda).

Thus the Wilson action is gauge-invariant and the pair ((M_\Lambda,g_\Lambda)) carries a smooth isometric action of the gauge group.

---

## 7.5. Wilson Gibbs measure on configuration space

The finite-volume Wilson Gibbs measure on the spatial configuration manifold is
[
d\mu_\Lambda^W(U) := \frac{1}{Z_\Lambda^W}, e^{-S_W(U)}, d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U),
\qquad Z_\Lambda^W = \int_{M_\Lambda} e^{-S_W(U)}, d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U).
]

Because (M_\Lambda) is compact and (S_W) is continuous, (Z_\Lambda^W < \infty) and (\mu_\Lambda^W) is a probability measure.

The structural properties we need later are:

1. **Gauge invariance.**
   The action and volume are invariant under (\mathcal{G}*\Lambda), so (\mu*\Lambda^W) is invariant. Expectation values only depend on the orbit of (U), and gauge-invariant observables can be viewed as functions on the quotient (M_\Lambda / \mathcal{G}_\Lambda).

2. **Compatibility with Bakry–Émery.**
   In Part 6 we saw that (\mathrm{Ric}*{g*\Lambda}) is uniformly positive. The total Bakry–Émery tensor for (\mu_\Lambda^W) is
   [
   \operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda^W}
   = \operatorname{Ric}*{g*\Lambda} + \nabla^2 S_W,
   ]
   so the Wilson action contributes a Hessian term that we can combine with the Haar “mass” term in later parts. The detailed Hessian analysis is done in Part 9; here we only record the smooth potential structure and gauge invariance.

This gives a perfectly good finite-dimensional Gibbs system in the sense of Bakry–Émery: compact configuration space, smooth convex-ish potential, and a natural diffusion generator associated with (\mu_\Lambda^W).

---

## 7.6. From spatial to spacetime lattice

To talk about reflection positivity and OS reconstruction, we upgrade the spatial lattice to a Euclidean spacetime lattice with one distinguished time direction.

Fix a finite spatial lattice (\Lambda_S \subset \mathbb{Z}^d) and a time range ({-T,\dots,T}). The spacetime lattice is
[
\Lambda := {(x_0,x): x_0\in{-T,\dots,T},\ x\in\Lambda_S}.
]

Edges (E(\Lambda)) are now labelled by a spacetime point ((x_0,x)) and a direction (\mu\in{0,1,\dots,d}):

* (\mu=0) is the temporal direction.
* (\mu=1,\dots,d) are spatial directions.

On each oriented edge ((x_0,x;\mu)) we place a link variable
[
U(x_0,x;\mu) \in G,
]
and the configuration space is again
[
M_\Lambda = G^{E(\Lambda)},
]
with product Haar volume and Wilson action built from plaquette holonomies in this ((d+1))-dimensional lattice, defined exactly as before but now including time-like plaquettes. 

### 7.6.1. Time reflection on sites and links

Define a time reflection on sites:
[
\theta(x_0,x) := (-x_0,x),
]
which flips Euclidean time and leaves spatial coordinates unchanged.

This induces a map (\Theta) on link variables:

* **Spatial links** ((\mu = i \in {1,\dots,d})):
  [
  \Theta\big(U(x_0,x;i)\big) := U(\theta(x_0,x); i) = U(-x_0,x;i).
  ]

* **Temporal links** ((\mu = 0)), oriented from ((x_0,x)) to ((x_0+1,x)):
  [
  \Theta\big(U(x_0,x;0)\big)
  := U(\theta(x_0+1,x);0)^{-1}
  = U(-x_0-1,x;0)^{-1}.
  ]

This choice has three key properties:

1. (\Theta) is an involution: (\Theta^2 = \mathrm{Id}) on (M_\Lambda).
2. Plaquette structure is respected: plaquettes are mapped to plaquettes, with orientation reversals paired with group inversion in a way compatible with the holonomy definition.
3. Spatial and temporal directions are treated in a way consistent with usual OS reflection.

We extend (\Theta) to all of (M_\Lambda) componentwise. On observables (F), the OS reflection is the conjugate-linear map
[
(\theta F)(U) := \overline{F(\Theta U)}.
]

## 7.7. Reflection positivity of the Wilson weight

We can now formulate reflection positivity for the Wilson action.

### 7.7.1. Positive-time algebra

Split the spacetime lattice into negative-time, time-zero, and positive-time slices. Define the **strictly positive-time region**
[
\Lambda_+ := {(x_0,x)\in\Lambda : x_0 > 0}.
]

Let (A_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) be the algebra of gauge-invariant local observables (functions depending on finitely many link variables and invariant under (\mathcal{G}*\Lambda)). Define the **positive-time algebra** (A*\Lambda^+\subset A_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) to be the *-subalgebra generated by gauge-invariant local observables supported entirely in (\Lambda_+); that is, each factor depends only on link variables whose spacetime support lies in (\Lambda_+).

Elements (F\in A_\Lambda^+) are finite linear combinations of finite products of such local observables; these are the test functions at positive times in the OS formalism.

Reflection positivity for a measure (\mu_\Lambda) on (M_\Lambda) means that
[
\int (\theta F)(U), F(U), d\mu_\Lambda(U) \ge 0
\quad\text{for all } F\in A_\Lambda^+.
]

In our case, (\mu_\Lambda = \mu_\Lambda^W) is the Gibbs measure defined by the spacetime Wilson action and product Haar volume.

### 7.7.2. “Square of a norm” representation (Osterwalder–Seiler mechanism)

At this point we invoke the classical Osterwalder–Seiler reflection-positivity argument for Wilson-type lattice actions. Structurally, one proceeds as follows:

1. **Decomposition of the action.**
   Write the Wilson action as
   [
   S_W(U) = S_-(U) + S_0(U) + S_+(U),
   ]
   where:

   * (S_-) depends only on link variables in negative times;
   * (S_+) depends only on link variables in positive times;
   * (S_0) consists of “straddling” plaquette terms that touch both time (x_0\le 0) and (x_0\ge 0).

2. **Kernel representation of the straddling factor.**
   The key step is to rewrite
   [
   e^{-S_0(U)} = \sum_\alpha \overline{K_\alpha(\Theta U)}, K_\alpha(U),
   ]
   for suitable functionals (K_\alpha) that depend only on positive-time link variables (and possibly the time-zero layer). The existence of such a representation is a nontrivial but standard combinatorial fact for Wilson plaquette actions: it uses locality, the product structure of Haar integration, and the fact that each “crossing” plaquette can be expanded into a sum of products of positive-time functionals and their reflections. 

3. **Factorization of the measure.**
   The product Haar measure factorizes between negative-time and nonnegative-time variables, and the negative-time Wilson contributions (e^{-S_-(U)}) can be integrated out explicitly as an overall positive weight on the kernel representation.

Putting this together, one obtains
[
e^{-S_W(U)}
= e^{-S_-(U)};
\sum_\alpha \overline{K_\alpha(\Theta U)}, K_\alpha(U);
e^{-S_+(U)}.
] 

### 7.7.3. Positivity of the quadratic form

Let (\mu_\Lambda^W) be the Wilson measure on (M_\Lambda). For any (F\in A_\Lambda^+), we can compute
[
\int (\theta F)(U), F(U), d\mu_\Lambda^W(U)
]
by first integrating out negative-time variables, using the kernel representation.

One finds
[
\int (\theta F) F, d\mu_\Lambda^W
= \sum_\alpha
\left|
\int F(U), K_\alpha(U), e^{-S_+(U)}, d\mu_+(U)
\right|^2 \ge 0,
]
where (d\mu_+) is a measure on positive-time link variables incorporating the Haar measure and (e^{-S_+(U)}). The right-hand side is a sum of squared moduli, hence nonnegative. 

This proves **reflection positivity of the Wilson weight**:
[
\int (\theta F)(U), F(U), d\mu_\Lambda^W(U) \ge 0
\quad\text{for all } F\in A_\Lambda^+.
]

In this project we treat this as a structural theorem: the detailed combinatorial construction of the (K_\alpha) is recorded once in the reflection-positivity section of your OS part and is not repeated in later arguments.

## 7.8. Role of reflection positivity and outlook

At the level of this part, we have established three structural facts that will be used repeatedly:

1. The spatial Wilson Gibbs measure (\mu_\Lambda^W) is a well-defined finite-dimensional Gibbs system on ((M_\Lambda,g_\Lambda)), with smooth local potential (S_W). This is the starting point for curvature and Bakry–Émery analysis once we add Haar mass and gauge-fixing contributions.

2. The lattice gauge group (\mathcal{G}*\Lambda) acts isometrically on ((M*\Lambda,g_\Lambda)), preserves the Haar volume and the Wilson action, and hence preserves (\mu_\Lambda^W). Gauge-invariant observables form a natural sector that will later be identified with functions on the physical configuration space.

3. The spacetime extension of the Wilson action satisfies reflection positivity with respect to Euclidean time reflection. This is the minimal OS input needed, in Part 16–17, to construct a transfer matrix and a positive self-adjoint Hamiltonian (H_\Lambda) whose spectral gap is the lattice “mass gap”.

From here the story branches:

* Part 8 implements the abstract gauge-invariant curvature interface (Part 5) in the concrete lattice setting by analyzing gauge orbits, vertical and horizontal directions, and physical gradients.
* Part 9 analyzes the Wilson Hessian and combines it with Haar mass to obtain positivity of the total Bakry–Émery tensor in physical directions near the vacuum.
* Later OS parts (16–17) use the reflection-positivity structure recorded here to construct the OS Hilbert space and Hamiltonian.

That’s the entire “Wilson + gauge + reflection positivity” chapter in one place; everything beyond this is curvature and dynamics built on top of that skeleton.
