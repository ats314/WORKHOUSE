# Title

Haar Measure Contribution to the Effective Action for \(SU(N)\) and Positivity of the Quadratic Term

# Abstract

We consider the contribution of the Haar measure to the effective action for a lattice gauge link \(U\in SU(N)\) parameterized via the exponential map \(U = \exp(a g A_b)\) with \(A_b \in \mathfrak{su}(N)\) anti‑Hermitian. Using the standard Jacobian of the exponential map for compact Lie groups, we show that the measure is governed by the operator-valued function \(\sin(\mathcal{L}/2)/(\mathcal{L}/2)\), where \(\mathcal{L} = -i a g\,\mathrm{ad}_{A_b}\) has real eigenvalues. Expanding the resulting effective Haar action for small lattice spacing and coupling, we derive the quadratic term
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}^{(2)} = \frac{N}{12}\,a^2 g^2 \,\|A_b\|^2,
\]
with \(\|A_b\|^2 = -\mathrm{Tr}(A_b^2)\). This term is strictly positive for \(N\ge 2\), implying a positive definite Hessian at the origin and a Gaussian suppression of fluctuations in the Euclidean path integral. The corrected derivation resolves the sign error of the original treatment and is consistent with the compactness of \(SU(N)\).

# Verified Derivation

## 1. Setup and Haar Measure Jacobian

We consider a single lattice gauge link variable \(U\in SU(N)\), parameterized by an element of the Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{su}(N)\) via the exponential map
\[
U = \exp(a g A_b),
\]
where
- \(a\) is the lattice spacing,
- \(g\) is the gauge coupling,
- \(A_b \in \mathfrak{su}(N)\) is anti‑Hermitian: \(A_b^\dagger = -A_b\).

The Haar measure on the group induces a measure on the algebra through the Jacobian of the exponential map:
\[
dU = J(A_b)\, dA_b.
\]

For a left-invariant Haar measure, the Jacobian of the exponential map at \(X\in\mathfrak{g}\) is
\[
J(X) = \det\!\left(\frac{1 - e^{-\mathrm{ad}_X}}{\mathrm{ad}_X}\right),
\]
where \(\mathrm{ad}_X(Y) = [X,Y]\) is the adjoint action on the Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{g}\).

We are interested in the effective action associated with the Haar measure,
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A_b) = -\log J(A_b).
\]

For compact groups, it is convenient to isolate the part of the Jacobian that depends on the spectrum of the adjoint action in a manifestly real form. Define the dimensionless operator
\[
\mathcal{L} \equiv -i\,a g\,\mathrm{ad}_{A_b}.
\]

Since \(A_b\) is anti‑Hermitian, \(\mathrm{ad}_{A_b}\) is anti‑Hermitian with respect to the Killing form, and its eigenvalues are purely imaginary. Writing these eigenvalues as \(i\mu_j\) with \(\mu_j\in\mathbb{R}\), the eigenvalues of \(\mathcal{L}\) are real:
\[
\mathcal{L} \sim \mu_j \in \mathbb{R}.
\]

The modulus of the eigenvalues of \(\frac{1 - e^{-\mathrm{ad}_{ag A_b}}}{\mathrm{ad}_{ag A_b}}\) can be expressed in terms of \(\sin\) of these real eigenvalues. One finds
\[
\left|\frac{1 - e^{-\mathrm{ad}_{ag A_b}}}{\mathrm{ad}_{ag A_b}}\right|
\;\sim\;
\left|\frac{\sin(\mathcal{L}/2)}{\mathcal{L}/2}\right|.
\]

Thus, up to an irrelevant phase and overall constant, the Jacobian relevant for the effective action is
\[
J(A_b) = \det\!\left(\frac{\sin(\mathcal{L}/2)}{\mathcal{L}/2}\right).
\]

Accordingly, the Haar contribution to the effective action is
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A_b)
= -\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}} \log\!\left(\frac{\sin(\mathcal{L}/2)}{\mathcal{L}/2}\right),
\]
where \(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\) denotes the trace over the Lie algebra (adjoint representation).

This is the compact‑group analogue of the non‑compact expression involving \(\sinh\), and the replacement \(\sinh \to \sin\) for real eigenvalues of \(\mathcal{L}\) is the key structural correction.

## 2. Small‑Field Expansion of the Haar Action

We now expand \(S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A_b)\) for small \(a g\), i.e. for small \(\mathcal{L}\).

The Taylor expansion of \(\sin x / x\) around \(x=0\) is
\[
\frac{\sin x}{x} = 1 - \frac{x^2}{6} + O(x^4).
\]

Substituting \(x = \mathcal{L}/2\), we obtain
\[
\frac{\sin(\mathcal{L}/2)}{\mathcal{L}/2}
= \mathbb{I} - \frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}}{2}\right)^2 + O(\mathcal{L}^4)
= \mathbb{I} - \frac{1}{24}\mathcal{L}^2 + O(\mathcal{L}^4).
\]

For an operator close to the identity, we use
\[
\log(\mathbb{I} - \epsilon) = -\epsilon + O(\epsilon^2),
\]
valid when \(\|\epsilon\|\ll 1\). Here \(\epsilon = \frac{1}{24}\mathcal{L}^2 + O(\mathcal{L}^4)\), so to quadratic order in \(A_b\) we may neglect \(O(\mathcal{L}^4)\) and write
\[
\log\!\left(\frac{\sin(\mathcal{L}/2)}{\mathcal{L}/2}\right)
= \log\!\left(\mathbb{I} - \frac{1}{24}\mathcal{L}^2 + O(\mathcal{L}^4)\right)
\approx -\frac{1}{24}\mathcal{L}^2.
\]

Therefore, to quadratic order,
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A_b)
&= -\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}} \log\!\left(\frac{\sin(\mathcal{L}/2)}{\mathcal{L}/2}\right) \\
&\approx -\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\!\left(-\frac{1}{24}\mathcal{L}^2\right) \\
&= \frac{1}{24}\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{L}^2).
\end{aligned}
\]

This establishes the sign of the quadratic term in terms of \(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{L}^2)\).

## 3. Expressing the Trace in Terms of \(A_b\)

Using the definition \(\mathcal{L} = -i a g\,\mathrm{ad}_{A_b}\), we have
\[
\mathcal{L}^2 = (-i a g)^2\,\mathrm{ad}_{A_b}^2 = -a^2 g^2\,\mathrm{ad}_{A_b}^2.
\]

Substituting into the action,
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A_b)
= \frac{1}{24}\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{L}^2)
= \frac{1}{24}\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\!\left(-a^2 g^2\,\mathrm{ad}_{A_b}^2\right)
= -\frac{a^2 g^2}{24}\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{ad}_{A_b}^2).
\]

For \(X\in\mathfrak{su}(N)\), the Killing form can be expressed in terms of the fundamental representation trace. With the usual normalization of the generators in the fundamental representation, one has the standard identity
\[
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{ad}_X^2)
= 2N\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{fund}}(X^2).
\]

Applying this with \(X = A_b\),
\[
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{ad}_{A_b}^2)
= 2N\,\mathrm{Tr}(A_b^2),
\]
where \(\mathrm{Tr}\) denotes the trace in the fundamental representation.

Thus,
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A_b)
= -\frac{a^2 g^2}{24}\,(2N)\,\mathrm{Tr}(A_b^2)
= -\frac{N a^2 g^2}{12}\,\mathrm{Tr}(A_b^2).
\]

This is the quadratic contribution to the Haar effective action expressed directly in terms of \(A_b\).

## 4. Norm, Sign, and Positivity of the Quadratic Term

Because \(A_b\) is anti‑Hermitian, its eigenvalues are purely imaginary, and \(\mathrm{Tr}(A_b^2)\) is non‑positive. It is convenient to define a positive definite norm on the algebra by
\[
\|A_b\|^2 \equiv -\mathrm{Tr}(A_b^2).
\]

Then \(\mathrm{Tr}(A_b^2) = -\|A_b\|^2\), and the Haar action becomes
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A_b)
= -\frac{N a^2 g^2}{12}\,\bigl(-\|A_b\|^2\bigr)
= \frac{N a^2 g^2}{12}\,\|A_b\|^2.
\]

Equivalently, we can write the quadratic part of the action as
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}^{(2)}(A_b)
= \frac{c_0}{2}\,a^2 g^2\,\|A_b\|^2,
\quad
c_0 = \frac{N}{6}.
\]

For \(SU(N)\) with \(N\ge 2\), we have \(c_0>0\). Thus the quadratic form is strictly positive for nonzero \(A_b\), and the Hessian of \(S_{\mathrm{Haar}}\) at \(A_b=0\) is positive definite and proportional to the identity in the space of algebra components.

## 5. Hessian and Physical Interpretation

Near \(A_b = 0\), the Haar contribution to the Euclidean action is
\[
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A_b)
= \frac{N a^2 g^2}{12}\,\|A_b\|^2 + O(A_b^4).
\]

In the Euclidean path integral
\[
\int \mathcal{D}A_b \,\exp\bigl(-S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A_b) - S_{\text{other}}(A_b)\bigr),
\]
this term provides a Gaussian suppression of fluctuations in \(A_b\) around the origin. The positive definite Hessian corresponds to a stabilizing, “mass‑like” contribution induced purely by the compactness of the group manifold and the nontrivial Jacobian of the exponential map.

This resolves the earlier sign error (which had led to an apparent tachyonic instability) and yields a consistent, stable quadratic effective action from the Haar measure for \(SU(N)\).
