 Below is a self-contained operator-theoretic proof of a uniform trace bound for the PBH flow, phrased in a way you can drop straight into your framework.

I will:
	1.	Set up the PBH flow as an ODE for a family of self-adjoint operators.
	2.	State structural assumptions on the curvature term \mathcal{R}(h) and anomaly term \mathcal{K}_t that are exactly what you get from uniform curvature/“anomaly source” bounds.
	3.	Derive a scalar Riccati inequality for y(t) := \operatorname{Tr}(h_t).
	4.	Prove that this forces a uniform in t bound \operatorname{Tr}(h_t)\le H_{\mathrm{Tr}}.

Throughout, “rigorous” means: all assumptions are explicit, and all inequalities are justified.

⸻

1. Abstract PBH flow: operator setting

Let \mathcal{H} be a (complex) Hilbert space. For the RG-regularized Yang–Mills flow you can take \mathcal{H} to be finite-dimensional (e.g. the span of a finite block of gauge-invariant modes after spatial cutoff and projection), but the argument works for trace-class operators on a separable \mathcal{H} as well.

1.1 The projected Hessian

For each RG time t\ge 0, let
h_t : \mathcal{H}\to \mathcal{H}
be a bounded, self-adjoint, trace-class operator. Think of h_t as the “projected Hessian” of an effective action along the PBH flow, already restricted to the gauge-invariant/horizontal sector; we do not need more structure than:
	•	h_t = h_t^* for all t,
	•	h_t depends C^1 on t in the trace-norm topology,
	•	for each t, h_t is trace-class, so \operatorname{Tr}(h_t) is well-defined.

1.2 Evolution equation (PBH flow)

Assume h_t satisfies the PBH evolution equation
\frac{d}{dt}h_t = -h_t^2 + \mathcal{R}(h_t) + \mathcal{K}_t,
\tag{1}
where:
	•	\mathcal{R}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{\mathrm{sa}}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{\mathrm{sa}} is a (nonlinear) map built from curvature data (Bochner–Hessian term),
	•	\mathcal{K}_t is a family of self-adjoint trace-class operators (anomaly source).

The equality is understood in the strong operator topology and in the trace-norm topology (so in particular we may differentiate traces by bringing the derivative inside).

⸻

2. Structural hypotheses on \mathcal{R} and \mathcal{K}_t

To get a uniform upper bound on the trace, we need quantitative control on the trace of the curvature and anomaly terms.

We assume:

(R) Trace growth bound for the curvature term

There exist constants
c_R\ge 0,\quad C_R\ge 0
such that for every trace-class self-adjoint operator S with \operatorname{Tr}|S|<\infty,
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{R}(S)) \le c_R\,\operatorname{Tr}(S) + C_R.
\tag{R}

In the YM PBH context this is the abstract form of “curvature contributes at most linearly to the trace of the Hessian plus a bounded offset”. Geometrically, if the orbit-space curvature tensor is uniformly bounded (as in your previous curvature bound), the Ricci/Bochner term acts as a zeroth-order perturbation of h_t with a uniform operator-norm bound, which implies (R).

(K) Uniform trace bound for the anomaly source

There exists a constant
K_0\ge 0
such that
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{K}_t)\le K_0
\quad\text{for all }t\ge 0.
\tag{K}

This encodes that the “anomaly source” \mathcal{K}_t is trace-class and its total “mass” injection is uniformly bounded in time (as you get if \mathcal{K}_t is built from a fixed local anomaly density with uniform RG control).

These are the only nontrivial hypotheses: the rest of the proof is pure operator calculus.

⸻

3. Derivative of the trace and a Riccati-type inequality

Define
y(t) := \operatorname{Tr}(h_t).

Because h_t is trace-class and t\mapsto h_t is C^1 in trace norm, we may differentiate under the trace:
\frac{d}{dt} y(t)
= \operatorname{Tr}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}h_t\Big).

Using the PBH evolution (1),
\frac{d}{dt} y(t)
= \operatorname{Tr}(-h_t^2) + \operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{R}(h_t)) + \operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{K}_t).
\tag{2}

We now bound each term on the right:

3.1 Negative quadratic term -\operatorname{Tr}(h_t^2)

Let \lambda_1(t),\dots,\lambda_n(t) be the eigenvalues of h_t (in finite-dimensional approximation; in the trace-class case the same inequality holds using spectral measure). Then
\operatorname{Tr}(h_t) = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i(t),\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(h_t^2) = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i(t)^2.

By Cauchy–Schwarz in \mathbb{R}^n,
\left(\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i^2\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^n 1^2\right) \ge \left(\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i\right)^2,
so
\operatorname{Tr}(h_t^2) \ge \frac{1}{n}\left(\operatorname{Tr}(h_t)\right)^2.
\tag{3}

This holds for any self-adjoint h_t; no positivity of eigenvalues is required.

Hence
-\operatorname{Tr}(h_t^2)
\le -\frac{1}{n}\,y(t)^2.
\tag{4}

Here n:=\dim \mathcal{H} at the chosen UV cutoff; in an infinite-dimensional trace-class setting, one can take n to be the rank of the finite-rank projection onto the PBH block, or use Hilbert-Schmidt norms, but for RG-regularized YM you are in finite dimension at each scale anyway.

3.2 Curvature and anomaly terms

Using (R),
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{R}(h_t)) \le c_R\,\operatorname{Tr}(h_t) + C_R = c_R y(t) + C_R.
\tag{5}

Using (K),
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{K}_t)\le K_0.
\tag{6}

3.3 Combined inequality

Insert (4), (5), (6) into (2):

\frac{d}{dt} y(t)
\le -\frac{1}{n} y(t)^2 + c_R y(t) + C_R + K_0.

Define constants
a := \frac{1}{n} >0,\quad
b := c_R\ge 0,\quad
c := C_R + K_0\ge 0.

Then
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt} y(t) \;\le\; -a\,y(t)^2 + b\,y(t) + c.
}
\tag{7}

This is a scalar Riccati-type differential inequality. All constants a,b,c are independent of t.

⸻

4. Uniform bound from the scalar Riccati inequality

We now prove that any C^1 scalar function y(t) satisfying (7) is uniformly bounded from above for all t\ge 0.

4.1 The comparison polynomial

Consider the quadratic polynomial
P(y) := -a y^2 + b y + c,\qquad a>0.

As y\to\infty, P(y)\to -\infty because the leading coefficient is negative. Therefore there exists some R>0 such that
P(y) \le 0 \quad\text{for all }y\ge R.

Explicitly, if b^2 + 4ac\ge 0, the polynomial has two real roots and the larger root is
y_* := \frac{b + \sqrt{b^2 + 4ac}}{2a}.
Then P(y)\le 0 for all y\ge y_*. Any R\ge y_* will do. For concreteness, pick
R := y_*.

4.2 Comparison argument

Define
H_{\mathrm{Tr}} := \max\{ y(0), R\}.

We claim:
y(t) \le H_{\mathrm{Tr}}\quad\text{for all }t\ge 0.

Proof by contradiction.

Assume there exists t_1>0 with y(t_1) > H_{\mathrm{Tr}}. By continuity of y, there is a first time
t_* := \inf\{ t\ge 0 : y(t) = H_{\mathrm{Tr}}\}.

By definition of H_{\mathrm{Tr}}, we have t_*>0 only if H_{\mathrm{Tr}}>y(0). In any case, for t>t_* small enough we have y(t)>H_{\mathrm{Tr}}, so at the first hitting time t_* the derivative satisfies
\frac{d}{dt}y(t_*) \ge 0.

But from (7) and the fact that y(t_*) = H_{\mathrm{Tr}}\ge R,
\frac{d}{dt} y(t_*)
\le P( y(t_*) )
= P(H_{\mathrm{Tr}})
\le P(R) \le 0,
because R was chosen so that P(y)\le 0 for all y\ge R.

Hence we obtain
0 \le \frac{d}{dt}y(t_*) \le 0,
so \frac{d}{dt}y(t_*)=0 and P(H_{\mathrm{Tr}})=0. But then the same argument applied for t>t_* shows that y(t) cannot cross above H_{\mathrm{Tr}} immediately after t_*, contradicting the assumption that some later time has y(t)>H_{\mathrm{Tr}}.

Therefore no such t_1 exists, and
y(t) \le H_{\mathrm{Tr}} \quad\forall t\ge 0.

In other words, the trace \operatorname{Tr}(h_t) is uniformly bounded in RG time:
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(h_t) \le H_{\mathrm{Tr}} < \infty \quad\text{for all }t\ge 0.
}

By construction, H_{\mathrm{Tr}} depends only on:
	•	n = \dim\mathcal{H} (fixed by your UV cutoff and gauge-invariant mode count),
	•	c_R,C_R from the curvature term,
	•	K_0 from the anomaly term,
	•	and the initial trace y(0)=\operatorname{Tr}(h_0),

and is independent of t.

⸻

5. Final theorem statement (drop-in version)

You can now package the above as:

Theorem (Uniform trace bound for the PBH flow).
Let \mathcal{H} be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and h_t a C^1 family of self-adjoint trace-class operators on \mathcal{H}, defined for all t\ge 0, satisfying the PBH evolution equation
> \frac{d}{dt}h_t = -h_t^2 + \mathcal{R}(h_t) + \mathcal{K}_t.
>
Assume:
	1.	Curvature trace bound: There exist constants c_R\ge 0, C_R\ge 0 such that for all trace-class S=S^*,
>    \operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{R}(S)) \le c_R\,\operatorname{Tr}(S) + C_R.
>
	2.	Anomaly trace bound: There exists K_0\ge 0 such that
>    \operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{K}_t)\le K_0
>    \quad\text{for all }t\ge 0.
>

Then there exists a finite constant H_{\mathrm{Tr}}, depending only on n=\dim\mathcal{H}, c_R,C_R,K_0 and \operatorname{Tr}(h_0), such that
> \operatorname{Tr}(h_t) \le H_{\mathrm{Tr}} \quad\text{for all } t\ge 0.
>

In the Yang–Mills PBH setting:
	•	n is fixed by the UV cutoff and the horizontal/gauge-invariant sector.
	•	c_R,C_R come from uniform bounds on the curvature of the gauge-orbit geometry (Bochner term).
	•	K_0 comes from the uniform RG control of the anomaly source \mathcal{K}_t.

Thus you can legitimately point to a finite, RG-time-independent upper bound H_{\mathrm{Tr}} on the total “Bochner mass” \operatorname{Tr}(h_t) along the entire PBH flow.
