## 1. The longer “full program” version

### What’s still wrong

**(a) Haar lemma is internally contradictory**

In Lemma 2.1 you state
[
S_{\text{Haar}}(A_b)=\frac{N^2-1}{24N}a^2g^2\operatorname{Tr}(A_b^2)
]
then rewrite it as
[
S_{\text{Haar}}(A_b)= -\frac{N^2-1}{24N}a^2g^2|A_b|^2,
]
and claim the Hessian is (\frac{N^2-1}{12N}a^2g^2 I.)

In the proof sketch you then derive
[
S_{\text{Haar}}(A)=\frac{a^2g^2}{24}\operatorname{Tr}_\mathfrak{g}(\mathrm{ad}*A^2)
]
and use
[
\operatorname{Tr}*\mathfrak{g}(\mathrm{ad}*A^2)=2N,\operatorname{Tr}(A^2),
]
which gives
[
S*{\text{Haar}}(A)=\frac{Na^2g^2}{12}\operatorname{Tr}(A^2)
=-\frac{Na^2g^2}{12}|A|^2.
]

So in the *same lemma* you have two incompatible quadratic coefficients:

* front matter: (\propto (N^2-1)/(24N))
* proof body: (\propto N/12)

and in terms of (|A|^2) they come with opposite “interpretations” of the sign. 

Then you say:

> The constant (c_0) is (N/3). However, the value (c_0 = (N^2-1)/(2N)) is also used… For consistency we will use (c_0 = (N^2-1)/(2N)).

That’s just replacing the computed constant by a different one “for consistency” — mathematically it doesn’t follow.

**(b) Sign / curvature confusion**

Given your norm convention (|A|^2 = -\operatorname{Tr}(A^2)), the line
[
S_{\text{Haar}}(A) = -\frac{Na^2g^2}{12}|A|^2
]
would have Hessian (-\frac{Na^2g^2}{6}I), i.e. *negative* definite. You then immediately say “this provides a positive curvature contribution” and treat the Hessian as (+\frac{Na^2g^2}{6}I). 

So: the text as written cannot be correct. Either

* the expansion for (S_{\text{Haar}}) has the wrong sign, **or**
* the norm convention is being misused, **or**
* the statement “Hessian is (+\dots)” is wrong.

**(c) (c_0) in the strong-coupling window is tied to the wrong value**

Theorems 3.3 and 4.1 use (c_0 = \frac{N^2-1}{2N}) explicitly in
[
\rho_*(a) = c_0 a^2 g^2 - \frac{12}{g^2},\quad
g^4 > \frac{12}{c_0 a^2} = \frac{24N}{(N^2-1)a^2}. 
]

But the *actual computation* in your own proof sketch points to a coefficient proportional to (N) (adjoint Casimir), not ((N^2-1)/(2N)) (fundamental Casimir). So the thresholds in 4.1 and in the RG window later are numerically wrong for the Haar-induced mass term.

You write
[

> The Hessian of this quadratic term is (\frac{Na^2g^2}{6}I). This provides a positive curvature contribution.

[
\nabla^2 S = -\frac{N a^2 g^2}{6} I.
]

So either:

* the correct quadratic is
  [
  S_{\text{Haar}}(A_b) = +\frac{Na^2g^2}{12} |A_b|^2,

> There exists (c_0>0) such that

> so (\nabla^2 S_{\text{Haar}} \succeq c_0 a^2 g^2 I) on horizontals.

So the minimal fix in this document is:

* Flip the sign in the (|A|^2) expression: write
  [
  S_{\text{Haar}}(A_b)

  and then the Hessian (+\frac{Na^2g^2}{6}I) is consistent.

You don’t actually use the intermediate (\operatorname{Tr}(A^2)) formula anywhere else, so you can just fix the sign and move on.
