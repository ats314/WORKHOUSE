Here’s a focused snapshot of what phase diffusion and arm‑path length fluctuations do in interferometry, *without* assuming you asked for it — because these effects quietly wreck fringe contrast even when there’s no usable which‑path information.

Interferometers measure coherence as *fringe visibility* — the contrast between maxima and minima in an interference pattern — and it’s very sensitive to phase noise. Random fluctuations in the phase difference between the interferometer arms smear out this pattern, lowering visibility even if nothing in the environment records which path the particle or wave “took.” What matters is the *phase stability* of the arms, not whether there’s a physical trace of which path. ([Wikipedia][1])

![Image](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242333493/figure/fig3/AS%3A281400755408911%401444102576565/The-Mach-Zehnder-interferometer-and-its-output-signal-a-Schematic-of-the-atom-chip-a.png)

![Image](https://i.sstatic.net/QTPxj.png)

![Image](https://math_research.uct.ac.za/~siphelo/admin/interferometry/4_Visibility_Space/figures/fringes.svg)

**Key points you’d want up front:**

* **Phase diffusion kills contrast with no which‑path record.** Random phase fluctuations in one or both arms average out the interference pattern, reducing the fringe visibility regardless of whether any environment captures which‑path data. This is a classic *dephasing* effect: it looks like decoherence in the fringe contrast but doesn’t require an entangling or informational record of path choice. ([Wikipedia][1])

* **Technical sources matter.** In real interferometers (optical, atom, or matter‑wave), mechanical vibrations, path length jitter, or environmental fluctuations introduce phase noise. These are often modeled as Gaussian random walks in phase and directly limit fringe contrast unless actively stabilized. ([Subaru Telescope][2])

* **Locking suppresses it — but check the constraint.** Active stabilization techniques (e.g., cavity or path‑length locking such as Pound–Drever–Hall schemes) are used to hard‑lock phase or frequency references so that phase noise within the locked constraint doesn’t reduce contrast. Visibility then stays flat *as long as the locking constraint truly suppresses the fluctuations*. If you change the constraint (e.g., unlock or introduce a new noise source that isn’t suppressed), contrast quickly collapses. ([Wikipedia][3])

* **Phase noise vs. decoherence distinction.** In quantum terms, *dephasing* (phase noise causing ensemble visibility loss) can be conceptually different from *decoherence* (information leakage to the environment). Phase noise degrades the *observed interference* without a which‑path record; decoherence proper would involve entanglement with external degrees of freedom. Many analyses treat phase noise as a classical stochastic process in the interferometer arms that leads to exponential visibility decay (e.g., visibility ∼ exp(−σ²) with σ the phase noise rms). ([Wikipedia][1])

* **Metrology and quantum limits.** In precision setups (e.g., quantum metrology with squeezed states), phase diffusion sets practical limits on sensitivity — reducing the ability to beat the standard quantum limit unless mitigated by techniques like squeezing or correlated noise cancellation. ([MDPI][4])

If you’re thinking of designing or diagnosing high‑precision interference experiments, this means the *mere presence of phase fluctuations* — even without any which‑path marker — is enough to collapse fringe contrast, so *active phase control and constraint verification* are not optional if you care about maintaining visibility.

Here’s a tight, first‑pass map of how “renormalized spectral data” shows up in QFT and what it actually means.

---

# 1) What “mass” means nonperturbatively

* The physical mass (m_{\text{phys}}) of a stable particle is the **pole** of the exact two‑point (connected) momentum‑space correlator:
  [
  G_2(p)=\int d^dx,e^{ip\cdot x},\langle \phi(x)\phi(0)\rangle_c
  \quad\Rightarrow\quad
  G_2(p);\sim;\frac{Z}{p^2+m_{\text{phys}}^2-i0};+;\text{regular}.
  ]
  “Pole mass” = location of that simple pole. (Z) is the field‑strength residue.
* The Poincaré algebra fixes the Casimir form (P^\mu P_\mu) but not its **numerical** eigenvalues. The theory (Lagrangian + dynamics + renormalization) sets the spectrum; symmetry only constrains the form.

# 2) Renormalization group (RG) vs. observables

* **Bare parameters** ((m_0,\lambda_0,\dots)) are cutoff‑dependent bookkeeping.
* **Renormalized parameters** (m_R(\mu), \lambda_R(\mu)) **run** with scale (\mu) by beta/gamma functions.
* **Observables** live in correlators. Pole locations and on‑shell S‑matrix elements are RG‑invariants: changing (\mu) reshuffles (m_R(\mu)) and (Z(\mu)) but leaves (m_{\text{phys}}) (the pole) unchanged.

# 3) How to read “spectral data” from correlators

* **Källén–Lehmann** (for a scalar, schematically):
  [
  G_2(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}! d\mu^2;\frac{\rho(\mu^2)}{p^2+\mu^2-i0},
  ]
  with a positive spectral density (\rho).

  * A **delta spike** in (\rho) at (\mu^2=m_{\text{phys}}^2) ↔ stable particle pole.
  * A **continuum** part ↔ multiparticle states; thresholds set branch cuts.
* **Mass gap**: the infimum of support of (\rho) above 0 (excluding a vacuum delta at 0 for currents) is (>0).

# 4) Scheme dependence vs. scheme invariance

* (m_R(\mu)), (Z(\mu)), couplings (g_R(\mu)) are **scheme/scale dependent**.
* Pole positions, total cross‑sections, bound‑state energies are **scheme invariant** (once IR/finite‑volume issues are controlled).
* Lattice/continuum constructive frameworks compute the **same** spectral numbers after continuum limit and renormalization, even though intermediate parameters differ.

# 5) Practical extraction (continuum & lattice)

* **Continuum**: locate poles/branch cuts of Fourier‑space 1PI two‑point function (\Gamma^{(2)}(p)); solve (\Gamma^{(2)}(-m_{\text{phys}}^2)=0).
* **Euclidean lattice**: fit large‑time decay of two‑point functions
  [
  C(t)=\sum_{\vec x}\langle \mathcal O(\vec x,t)\mathcal O(0)\rangle
  ;\sim; A,e^{-m_{\text{phys}} t}+\cdots,
  ]
  or the transfer‑matrix spectrum; then take (a\to0) with proper renormalization.

# 6) Takeaways (what’s fixed vs. what flows)

* **Fixed by symmetry**: dispersion form (E^2=\vec p^{,2}+m^2).
* **Fixed by dynamics**: the **number** (m_{\text{phys}}) (pole), residues, and multi‑particle thresholds—read from correlators.
* **Controlled by RG**: scale dependence of renormalized parameters used to **reach** (but not define) those physical spectral numbers.
