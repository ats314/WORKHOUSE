This document presents a revised, rigorous mathematical statement concerning the persistence of the Yang-Mills mass gap, formulated as a conditional theorem based on the Projected Bochner-Hessian (PBH) flow. This addresses the previous shortcomings by explicitly stating the necessary assumptions required for the dominance of the anomaly source over geometric corrections.

### Prong B.2 – Conditional Persistence of the Mass Gap under PBH Flow

We now formulate a conditional theorem that makes precise the intuitive statement “the anomaly source dominates the geometric corrections in the asymptotically free regime.”

We work at a fixed finite cutoff (e.g., on a lattice or a Galerkin truncation) where the regular quotient $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}} = \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}/\mathcal{G}$ is a finite-dimensional Riemannian manifold with metric induced from the $L^2$ inner product.

Let $S_t : \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}\to\mathbb{R}$ be a time-dependent effective action solving the horizontal viscous Hamilton–Jacobi equation
$$
\partial_t S_t = \Delta_H S_t - |\nabla_H S_t|^2 + J_t,
$$
with horizontal gradient $V_t=\nabla_H S_t$ and horizontal Hessian $h_t=\nabla_H^2 S_t$. The Projected Bochner–Hessian (PBH) flow reads
$$
\partial_t h_t = \Delta_H h_t - 2\nabla_{V_t}h_t - 2 h_t^2 + S_{\mathrm{anom}}(t) + \mathfrak{G}(S_t,h_t),
$$
where $S_{\mathrm{anom}}(t)=\nabla_H^2 J_t$ and $\mathfrak{G}$ is the geometric correction coming from the curvature of $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}$ and the non-integrability of the horizontal distribution.

Let $\lambda_{\min}(t)$ denote the lowest eigenvalue of the symmetric bilinear form $h_t$.

#### Hypotheses

We impose the following conditions:

*   **(Curv)** There exists a constant $C_0>0$ and a running coupling $g(t)$ such that the sectional curvature satisfies
    $$
    |K_t(X,Y)| \le C_0\, g(t)^2 \quad\text{for all unit horizontal }X,Y\in T\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}},\;\forall t\ge 0.
    $$
*   **(Trace bound)** There exists $H_{\mathrm{Tr}}<\infty$ such that the trace of the positive part of $h_t$ is uniformly bounded:
    $$
    \sum_i \max\{\lambda_i(t),0\} \le H_{\mathrm{Tr}} \quad\text{for all }t\ge 0,
    $$
    where $\{\lambda_i(t)\}$ are the eigenvalues of $h_t(t)$ (counted with multiplicity).
*   **(Anom)** There exists $\sigma_A>0$ such that the anomaly source has a uniform positive lower bound in the lowest eigen-direction:
    $$
    \inf_{x\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}}\;\inf_{\|v\|=1} \langle v, S_{\mathrm{anom}}(t,x)v\rangle \;\ge\; \sigma_A \quad\text{for all }t\ge 0.
    $$
*   **(Asymptotic freedom)** The running coupling satisfies
    $$
    \lim_{t\to\infty} g(t) = 0,
    $$
    and the effective actions $S_t$ stay in a regime where (Curv) and (Trace bound) hold.
*   **(Initial gap)** There exists $T_0\ge 0$ and $\lambda_* > 0$ such that
    $$
    \lambda_{\min}(T_0) \ge \lambda_*.
    $$
    In practice, $T_0$ is chosen at a scale where the lattice/constructive arguments already provide a positive mass gap.

#### Geometric bound on $\mathfrak{G}$

Under (Curv) and (Trace bound), there exists a constant $C_1>0$ such that, for each $t$,
$$
|\langle \mathfrak{G}(S_t,h_t), v\otimes v\rangle| \;\le\; C_1\, g(t)^2\, \|h_t\|_{\mathrm{Tr}} \quad\text{for all unit }v\in T\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}.
$$
In particular, evaluating on an eigenvector $v_0(t)$ corresponding to $\lambda_{\min}(t)$ and using (Trace bound) gives
$$
|\langle \mathfrak{G}(S_t,h_t), v_0\otimes v_0\rangle| \le C_1\, g(t)^2 H_{\mathrm{Tr}}.
$$

*Sketch.* The PBH derivation shows that $\mathfrak{G}$ is a linear combination of curvature components contracted with $h_t$ and $V_t$. Bounded sectional curvature implies $|\mathfrak{G}(S_t,h_t)|\lesssim \sup|K_t|\cdot\|h_t\|$, and in a finite-dimensional setting the operator norm can be bounded by the trace norm: $\|h_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le \|h_t\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}$. This yields the stated inequality.

#### Differential inequality for $\lambda_{\min}(t)$

Using the PBH flow and a standard parabolic maximum-principle argument for symmetric tensors, one obtains a scalar inequality of the form
$$
\partial_t \lambda_{\min}(t) \;\ge\; -2\lambda_{\min}(t)^2 + \sigma_A - C_1\, g(t)^2 H_{\mathrm{Tr}}.
$$

By (Asymptotic freedom), there exists $T_1\ge T_0$ such that
$$
C_1\, g(t)^2 H_{\mathrm{Tr}} < \frac{\sigma_A}{2} \quad\text{for all }t\ge T_1.
$$
For such $t$ we have the **Riccati-type inequality**
$$
\partial_t \lambda_{\min}(t) \;\ge\; -2\lambda_{\min}(t)^2 + \frac{\sigma_A}{2}.
$$

#### Theorem (Conditional persistence of the mass gap)

Under hypotheses (Curv), (Trace bound), (Anom), (Asymptotic freedom), and (Initial gap), there exists $\sigma_{\min}>0$ such that
$$
\lambda_{\min}(t) \;\ge\; \sigma_{\min} \quad\text{for all }t\ge T_1.
$$
In particular, the horizontal Hessian of the effective action has a strictly positive minimal eigenvalue along the PBH flow for all sufficiently large RG times, and the local-sector mass gap persists.

*Proof.* For $t\ge T_1$ we have
$$
\partial_t \lambda_{\min}(t) \;\ge\; -2\lambda_{\min}(t)^2 + \frac{\sigma_A}{2}.
$$
The scalar ODE
$$
\dot\lambda = -2\lambda^2 + \frac{\sigma_A}{2}
$$
has a stable positive equilibrium at $\lambda_\infty = \tfrac12\sqrt{\sigma_A/2}$. A standard comparison argument for scalar ODEs (using that $\lambda_{\min}(T_1)\ge\lambda_*>0$) implies that $\lambda_{\min}(t)$ stays bounded away from zero for all $t\ge T_1$ and in fact converges to a limit $\lambda_{\mathrm{IR}}\ge \lambda_\infty/2>0$ after possibly reducing the constant. Set $\sigma_{\min}:=\lambda_\infty/2$.

Thus the minimal Hessian eigenvalue cannot close once it is open at $T_0$, provided the anomaly source remains uniformly positive while the geometric corrections decay like $g(t)^2$ in the asymptotically free regime. ∎
