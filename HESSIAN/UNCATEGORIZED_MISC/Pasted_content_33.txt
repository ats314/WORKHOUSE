1. Setup: finite-dimensional truncated, gauge-fixed YM model

We keep your setting almost verbatim, but crystallize the assumptions.

1.1 Configuration space and truncation

Let T^4 = (\mathbb{R}/L\mathbb{Z})^4 with normalized Fourier basis
e_k(x) = L^{-2} e^{i 2\pi k\cdot x/L}, \quad k\in\mathbb{Z}^4,
so that \{e_k\} is orthonormal in L^2(T^4).

Fix a cutoff \Lambda>0 and define
K_\Lambda := \{k\in\mathbb{Z}^4 : |k|\le\Lambda\}.

A truncated gauge field is given by coefficients
A_\mu^a(k),\quad \mu=1,\dots,4,\quad a=1,\dots,N^2-1,\quad k\in K_\Lambda,
so we set
V_\Lambda := \Bigl\{A = (A_\mu^a(k))_{\mu,a,k\in K_\Lambda}\Bigr\} \cong \mathbb{R}^{M_\Lambda},
\quad M_\Lambda = 4(N^2-1)\,\#K_\Lambda.

Equip V_\Lambda with the Euclidean inner product
\langle A,B\rangle_\Lambda = \sum_{\mu,a}\sum_{k\in K_\Lambda} A_\mu^a(k)\,B_\mu^a(k),\quad
\|A\|_\Lambda^2 := \langle A,A\rangle_\Lambda.

We assume a global linear gauge fixing has been imposed (e.g. Fourier Coulomb gauge k_\mu A_\mu^a(k)=0 for all k\neq 0), and that the Faddeev–Popov sector produces a strictly convex quadratic contribution
S_{\mathrm{FP},\Lambda}(A) = \frac{\rho}{2}\,\|A\|_\Lambda^2,
with \rho>0 a fixed constant independent of \Lambda.

Let S_{W,\Lambda}:V_\Lambda\to\mathbb{R} denote the truncated Wilson–type action (gauge-invariant part), assumed:
	•	S_{W,\Lambda}\in C^2(V_\Lambda),
	•	(Convexity hypothesis)
\mathrm{Hess}\,S_{W,\Lambda}(A)\ge 0 as a quadratic form for all A\in V_\Lambda.

This is the main idealization: we are replacing “convex in a small-field region plus control of large fields” by global convexity.

Define the effective potential
S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}(A)
:= S_{W,\Lambda}(A) + S_{\mathrm{FP},\Lambda}(A)
= S_{W,\Lambda}(A) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\,\|A\|_\Lambda^2.

Then for all A,
\mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}(A)
= \mathrm{Hess}\,S_{W,\Lambda}(A) + \rho I
\;\ge\; \rho I.
\tag{1.1}

This uniform convexity with constant \rho is the key structural input.

1.2 Gibbs measure and diffusion generator

Define the Gibbs measure
\mu_\Lambda(dA)
:= Z_\Lambda^{-1} \exp\big(-S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}(A)\big)\, dA,
where dA is Lebesgue measure on \mathbb{R}^{M_\Lambda} and
Z_\Lambda = \int e^{-S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}} dA < \infty (obvious from the quadratic term).

On L^2(\mu_\Lambda), define:
	•	Gradient \nabla f(A)\in V_\Lambda.
	•	Laplacian \Delta f(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{M_\Lambda} \partial_{A_i}^2 f(A).
	•	Generator
L_\Lambda f(A)
:= \Delta f(A) - \langle \nabla S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}(A),\nabla f(A)\rangle_\Lambda.

L_\Lambda is the standard reversible diffusion with invariant measure \mu_\Lambda.

Dirichlet form:
\mathcal{E}_\Lambda(f,f)
:= \int \|\nabla f(A)\|_\Lambda^2\,\mu_\Lambda(dA),
initially on smooth compactly supported functions and then closed.

Carré du champ:
\Gamma_\Lambda(f,g)(A)
:= \tfrac12\big(L_\Lambda(fg) - fL_\Lambda g - gL_\Lambda f\big)
= \langle \nabla f(A),\nabla g(A)\rangle_\Lambda,
\Gamma_\Lambda(f):=\Gamma_\Lambda(f,f) = \|\nabla f\|_\Lambda^2.

Iterated carré du champ:
\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f,g)
:= \tfrac12\big(L_\Lambda\Gamma_\Lambda(f,g) - \Gamma_\Lambda(f,L_\Lambda g) - \Gamma_\Lambda(g,L_\Lambda f)\big),
\quad
\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f):=\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f,f).

2. Bakry–Émery curvature bound BE(\rho,\infty)

Lemma 2.1 (Bochner formula in the finite-dimensional model)

For smooth f:V_\Lambda\to\mathbb{R},
\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)(A)
= \|\nabla^2 f(A)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
+ \langle \mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}(A)\,\nabla f(A),\nabla f(A)\rangle_\Lambda,
where \nabla^2 f is the matrix of second derivatives and \|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{HS}} the Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

Proof sketch. This is the standard Bakry–Émery computation for
L = \Delta - \nabla V\cdot\nabla on \mathbb{R}^d. Expanding the definitions of L, \Gamma, \Gamma_2 in coordinates gives the formula. The Hessian of V here is \mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}. ∎

Lemma 2.2 (CD/BE(\rho,\infty) with \rho independent of \Lambda)

Assume (1.1). Then for all smooth f,
\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f) \;\ge\; \rho\,\Gamma_\Lambda(f),
i.e. the pair (L_\Lambda,\Gamma_\Lambda) satisfies the curvature–dimension condition BE(\rho,\infty), with the same \rho>0 for all \Lambda.

Proof. By Lemma 2.1,
\Gamma_{2,\Lambda}(f)
= \|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
+ \langle \mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}\,\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle_\Lambda.
Using (1.1),
\langle \mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}\,\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle_\Lambda
\ge \rho\,\|\nabla f\|_\Lambda^2 = \rho\,\Gamma_\Lambda(f),
and \|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge 0. ∎

So you’ve fully nailed the “uniform curvature” part: BE(\rho,\infty) with a constant \rho that does not depend on the cutoff \Lambda.

3. Dimension-free functional inequalities and concentration

From BE(\rho,\infty), you get the standard Bakry–Émery package.

Proposition 3.1 (Uniform Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities)

Under the hypotheses above, for each \Lambda and all smooth f:V_\Lambda\to\mathbb{R},
	1.	Poincaré (spectral gap):
\int f^2\,d\mu_\Lambda - \Bigl(\int f\,d\mu_\Lambda\Bigr)^2
\le \frac{1}{\rho}\int \|\nabla f\|_\Lambda^2\,d\mu_\Lambda.
	2.	Log-Sobolev:
\int f^2\log\frac{f^2}{\int f^2 d\mu_\Lambda} \, d\mu_\Lambda
\le \frac{2}{\rho} \int \|\nabla f\|_\Lambda^2\,d\mu_\Lambda.

The constants 1/\rho, 2/\rho are independent of \Lambda.

Proof. This is exactly the Bakry–Émery theorem: BE(\rho,\infty) implies Poincaré and LSI with constants 1/\rho and 2/\rho, respectively, and the constants depend only on \rho. Lemma 2.2 provides BE(\rho,\infty) with \rho independent of \Lambda. ∎

Corollary 3.2 (Uniform Gaussian concentration)

Let F:V_\Lambda\to\mathbb{R} be 1-Lipschitz with respect to \|\cdot\|_\Lambda. Then, after centering F so that \int F\,d\mu_\Lambda = 0,
\int e^{\lambda F} d\mu_\Lambda \le \exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{2\rho}\right)
\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\mathbb{R},
and thus, for all R>0,
\mu_\Lambda(|F|>R) \le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\rho R^2}{2}\right).

The constants on the right are uniform in \Lambda.

Proof. Standard Herbst argument from LSI (3.1); BE(\rho,\infty) gives LSI with constant 2/\rho independent of \Lambda. ∎

This is the “dimension-free concentration” you want: no dependence on M_\Lambda.

4. Tightness as \Lambda\to\infty

We now embed all truncated spaces into a common Hilbert space.

4.1 Embedding into a fixed Hilbert space

Let H := \ell^2(\mathbb{N}) with canonical orthonormal basis \{e_i\}. For each \Lambda, fix an orthonormal identification V_\Lambda\cong\mathbb{R}^{M_\Lambda} and define
J_\Lambda : V_\Lambda \hookrightarrow H,\quad
J_\Lambda(A)_i =
\begin{cases}
A_i,& i\le M_\Lambda,\\
0,& i>M_\Lambda.
\end{cases}

Define the pushforward measures
\tilde\mu_\Lambda := (J_\Lambda)_\# \mu_\Lambda
on H. For notational simplicity, write \mu_\Lambda for \tilde\mu_\Lambda and view each A as an element of H.

The Hilbert norm \|\cdot\|_H restricted to V_\Lambda coincides with \|\cdot\|_\Lambda.

The function
F(A) := \|A\|_H
is 1-Lipschitz on H, hence 1-Lipschitz on each V_\Lambda.

4.2 Uniform bounds on \|A\| and tightness

By Corollary 3.2 applied to F after centering, we get for each \Lambda and R>0,
\mu_\Lambda\Big(\|A\|_H - \mathbb{E}_{\mu_\Lambda}\|A\|_H > R\Big)
\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\rho R^2}{2}\right).

It remains to bound \mathbb{E}_{\mu_\Lambda}\|A\|_H uniformly in \Lambda. Because the potential satisfies
S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}(A)
\ge \tfrac{\rho}{2}\|A\|_H^2,
and S_{W,\Lambda}\ge 0, we can compare \mu_\Lambda to a centered Gaussian N(0,\rho^{-1}I) on V_\Lambda. Standard log-concave measure estimates yield
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_\Lambda}\|A\|_H \le C_0
for a constant C_0 depending only on \rho (and mild structure of S_{W,\Lambda} near 0), not on \Lambda.

Thus, for R\ge C_0,
\mu_\Lambda(\|A\|_H > R)
\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\rho (R-C_0)^2}{2}\right)
=: \varepsilon(R),
with \varepsilon(R)\to 0 as R\to\infty, uniformly in \Lambda.

For any \varepsilon>0, choose R large so that \varepsilon(R)<\varepsilon. Then
\mu_\Lambda(B_R) \ge 1-\varepsilon
\quad\text{for all }\Lambda,
where B_R:=\{A\in H:\|A\|_H\le R\}.

Since closed balls are weakly compact in H, this shows that \{\mu_\Lambda\} is tight on (H,\text{weak topology}).

By Prokhorov, any sequence \mu_{\Lambda_n} with \Lambda_n\to\infty has a weakly convergent subsequence, say
\mu_{\Lambda_{n_k}} \Rightarrow \mu
for some probability measure \mu on H.

So you get exactly what you wanted: tightness and existence of subsequential limits.

5. Stability of functional inequalities / BE in the limit

The last step is the subtle one. There are two levels:
	1.	The “safe” level: LSI and spectral gap survive in the limit with the same constant.
	2.	The stronger level: the BE(\rho,\infty) curvature bound survives for the limiting semigroup.

You should be careful how you state the second, but the first is robust.

5.1 LSI and spectral gap in the limit

Assume:
	•	\mu_{\Lambda_n}\Rightarrow\mu weakly on H.
	•	The Dirichlet forms \mathcal{E}_{\Lambda_n} converge in the Mosco sense to a Dirichlet form (\mathcal{E},\mathcal{D}) on L^2(H,\mu).
	•	Cylinder functions (depending on finitely many coordinates) lie in \mathcal{D} and are dense in \mathcal{D} for the \mathcal{E}^{1/2}-norm.

Then for a bounded cylinder function f,
	•	Entropy is lower semicontinuous under weak convergence:
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\int f^2 \log\frac{f^2}{\int f^2 d\mu_{\Lambda_n}}\,d\mu_{\Lambda_n}
\ge
\int f^2 \log\frac{f^2}{\int f^2 d\mu}\,d\mu.
	•	Mosco convergence implies
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\int \|\nabla (f|_{V_{\Lambda_n}})\|^2\,d\mu_{\Lambda_n}
= \int \|\nabla f\|^2\,d\mu,
where \nabla is the gradient on H.

From the log-Sobolev inequality (with constant 2/\rho) for each \mu_{\Lambda_n},
\int f^2\log\frac{f^2}{\int f^2 d\mu_{\Lambda_n}} \, d\mu_{\Lambda_n}
\le \frac{2}{\rho}\int\|\nabla(f|_{V_{\Lambda_n}})\|^2\,d\mu_{\Lambda_n},
taking \limsup on the right and \liminf on the left yields
\int f^2\log\frac{f^2}{\int f^2 d\mu} \, d\mu
\le \frac{2}{\rho}\int\|\nabla f\|^2\,d\mu
for cylinder f. By density, this extends to all f\in\mathcal{D}.

Proposition 5.1 (Limit LSI and spectral gap)

Under the assumptions above, the limiting measure \mu and Dirichlet form \mathcal{E} satisfy:
	•	Log-Sobolev inequality
\int f^2\log\frac{f^2}{\int f^2 d\mu} \, d\mu
\le \frac{2}{\rho}\int\|\nabla f\|^2\,d\mu,
for all f\in\mathcal{D}.
	•	Poincaré inequality
\int f^2\,d\mu - \Bigl(\int f\,d\mu\Bigr)^2
\le \frac{1}{\rho}\int\|\nabla f\|^2\,d\mu.

So the spectral gap and LSI constants are at least \rho and 2/\rho in the limit.

This is already very strong: for your MFIP / mass-gap purposes, uniform Poincaré/LSI in the continuum cutoff is more or less the thing you need.

5.2 BE(\rho,\infty) for the limiting semigroup (caveat)

Whether you can honestly say “the limiting form satisfies BE(\rho,\infty)” depends on how much regularity you assume on the limiting generator:
	•	In finite dimensions, for diffusions of the form L = \Delta - \nabla V\cdot\nabla, BE(\rho,\infty) is equivalent to \mathrm{Hess}\,V\ge\rho I and this is stable under smooth convergence of V.
	•	In infinite dimensions on a Hilbert space, there is an established notion of CD/BE for Dirichlet forms, and under sufficient regularity (gradient flow structure, nice carré du champ), uniform BE(\rho,\infty) for approximations plus Mosco convergence can imply BE(\rho,\infty) in the limit — but this is more technical and you’d want to cite specific results (e.g. work by Röckner–Wang, Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré type CD theory).

Safe way to phrase it in your writeup:
	•	Hard fact: BE(\rho,\infty) for all \Lambda ⇒ uniform LSI/PI ⇒ LSI/PI survive in the limit with the same constants.
	•	Additional assumption: If the limiting object is itself a gradient diffusion with smooth enough potential S_{\mathrm{eff}} on a Hilbert space, then BE(\rho,\infty) is equivalent to \mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\mathrm{eff}}\ge\rho I, and this structure is stable under the truncations.

That keeps you fully rigorous in what you actually prove, and clearly labels where you’re assuming smoothness / structure on the limiting model.

6. Clean theorem you can drop into the program

Here is a concise “Theorem + Corollary” version that encapsulates what your toy model proves.

Theorem (Fourier cutoff YM toy model: uniform BE(\rho,\infty) and tightness).
Let \{V_\Lambda\} be the finite-dimensional Fourier truncated configuration spaces defined above, with effective potentials
> S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}(A)
> := S_{W,\Lambda}(A) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\|A\|_\Lambda^2,
>
where:
	1.	S_{W,\Lambda}\in C^2(V_\Lambda) and \mathrm{Hess}\,S_{W,\Lambda}(A)\ge 0 for all A,\Lambda;
	2.	\rho>0 is independent of \Lambda.

Let \mu_\Lambda\propto e^{-S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}} dA be the corresponding Gibbs measures, and L_\Lambda = \Delta - \nabla S_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}\cdot\nabla their generators.

Then:

(i) For each \Lambda, (L_\Lambda,\mu_\Lambda) satisfies the Bakry–Émery curvature–dimension condition BE(\rho,\infty) with the same constant \rho.

(ii) Consequently, for each \Lambda, \mu_\Lambda satisfies Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities with constants 1/\rho and 2/\rho, respectively, independent of \Lambda, and exhibits Gaussian concentration with variance scale 1/\rho, independent of \Lambda.

(iii) Viewing V_\Lambda as a subspace of a common Hilbert space H via orthogonal embeddings, the family \{\mu_\Lambda\} is tight on H, hence admits weakly convergent subsequences.

Corollary (Limit LSI and spectral gap).
Let \mu_{\Lambda_n}\Rightarrow\mu and suppose the Dirichlet forms \mathcal{E}_{\Lambda_n} converge in the Mosco sense to a Dirichlet form (\mathcal{E},\mathcal{D}) on L^2(H,\mu) with carré du champ \Gamma(f)=\|\nabla f\|^2. Then:
	•	\mu satisfies a log-Sobolev inequality with constant 2/\rho,
	•	\mu satisfies a Poincaré inequality with constant 1/\rho.

In particular, the limiting generator has a spectral gap \ge \rho and the limiting semigroup is exponentially mixing with rate \rho, uniformly inherited from the finite-dimensional truncations.

You can then add:
	•	A remark saying that, under additional smoothness assumptions on the limiting potential, the BE(\rho,\infty) curvature bound also passes to the limit.

7. How this plugs into the full YM story

In terms of your broader program:
	•	V_\Lambda here plays the role of “UV-regularized / Fourier truncated gauge-fixed configuration space”.
	•	The uniform BE(\rho,\infty) is exactly the kind of curvature lower bound you argued for from the FP term + small-field Wilson convexity in continuum / lattice Hessian language.
	•	The tightness + limit LSI is the bridging mechanism: you can non-perturbatively pass to an infinite-dimensional limit while keeping a strictly positive LSI / Poincaré constant.

The jump from this toy model to real 4D YM is then:
	•	Replace global convexity of S_{W,\Lambda} by “convex plus controlled nonconvex corrections”.
	•	Make \rho scale correctly with the gauge coupling and renormalization.
	•	Do the same type of BE/LSI/tightness analysis in a Sobolev space of fields rather than a bare \ell^2.

But structurally, what you wrote is correct, and packaged as above, it’s already a solid intermediate result you can cite as “Theorem [Toy-CD]” in your notes.
