### **Part 1: Axiomatic Foundation and Problem Statement**

**Axiom 1: The Configuration Space as a Riemannian Manifold.**
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the configuration space of fields (e.g., gauge connections $A$, scalar fields $\phi$). We assume $\mathcal{A}$ is a complete, separable Riemannian manifold $(M, g)$.
*   $M$: The underlying manifold.
*   $g$: A Riemannian metric tensor on $M$, defining distances, angles, and volumes.
*   $d\text{vol}$: The Riemannian volume measure on $M$.
*   $\nabla$: The Levi-Civita connection associated with $g$.
*   $\text{Ric}$: The Ricci curvature tensor of $(M, g)$.
*   $\text{Hess}(S)$: The Hessian tensor of a smooth function $S: M \to \mathbb{R}$, defined as $\text{Hess}(S)(X,Y) = g(\nabla_X \nabla S, Y)$.

**Axiom 2: Probability Measures from Action Functionals.**
The RG-evolved measure $\mu_t$ on $\mathcal{A}$ is given by:
$$ d\mu_t(A) = \frac{1}{Z_t} e^{-S_t[A]} d\text{vol}(A) $$
where $S_t[A]$ is the effective action functional at scale $t$, and $Z_t = \int_{\mathcal{A}} e^{-S_t[A]} d\text{vol}(A)$ is the normalization constant (partition function). We assume $S_t$ is a $C^2$ function on $M$.

**Axiom 3: Markov Operators and Functional Inequalities.**
For a measure $d\mu = e^{-S} d\text{vol}$, the associated symmetric Markov operator (generator of the Langevin diffusion) is $L = \Delta - \nabla S \cdot \nabla$, acting on $L^2(\mu)$.
The spectral gap of $L$ (the smallest non-zero eigenvalue) and the Logarithmic Sobolev Inequality (LSI) are crucial for stability, implying exponential convergence to equilibrium and concentration of measure.
The LSI states that for any smooth function $f$ on $M$:
$$ \text{Ent}_{\mu}(f^2) \le \frac{2}{\sigma} \mathcal{E}_{\mu}(f,f) $$
where $\text{Ent}_{\mu}(f^2) = \int f^2 \log\left(\frac{f^2}{\int f^2 d\mu}\right) d\mu$ is the entropy and $\mathcal{E}_{\mu}(f,f) = \int |\nabla f|^2 d\mu$ is the Dirichlet form. A positive constant $\sigma > 0$ implies a spectral gap of at least $\sigma$.

**Axiom 4: Bakry-Émery Ricci Curvature Criterion.**
The fundamental link between geometry and functional inequalities is the Bakry-Émery criterion. If there exists a constant $\sigma > 0$ such that the Bakry-Émery Ricci tensor $\text{Ric}_S = \text{Ric} + \text{Hess}(S)$ satisfies:
$$ \text{Ric}_S(X,X) \ge \sigma g(X,X) \quad \text{for all tangent vectors } X \in T_A M \text{ and all } A \in M $$
then the measure $\mu = e^{-S} d\text{vol}$ satisfies an LSI with constant $2/\sigma$ and the operator $L$ has a spectral gap of at least $\sigma$.

**The Obstruction (MoA Conclusion):**
The problem statement explicitly notes that the "global bare $\rho^*(a) \to -\infty$". This implies that for the bare action $S_{\text{bare}}$, the global Bakry-Émery condition $\text{Ric} + \text{Hess}(S_{\text{bare}}) \ge \sigma g$ fails for any $\sigma > 0$, or even for $\sigma \le 0$. The minimum eigenvalue of $\text{Ric} + \text{Hess}(S_{\text{bare}})$ is unbounded from below. This global failure prevents direct application of Axiom 4 to the bare measure.
