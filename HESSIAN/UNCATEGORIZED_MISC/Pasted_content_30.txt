I’ll focus on two pieces:
	1.	A precise formulation of tightness for gauge-invariant Yang–Mills measures in a continuum limit.
	2.	How a uniform Bakry–Émery CD(\rho,\infty) (or BE(\rho,\infty)) condition might be stable under limits (lattice \to continuum) via Dirichlet form convergence.

I will treat these as “research program”–level steps: precise formulations and plausible strategies, not claims that they are already established in 4D.

1. Tightness for Gauge-Invariant Yang–Mills Measures

1.1 What you ultimately want

Fix a bounded Euclidean region \Lambda \subset \mathbb{R}^4. For each lattice spacing a with \Lambda_a\subset \Lambda a hypercubic lattice approximation, you have a gauge-invariant lattice measure
\mu_a(dU) = Z_a^{-1} e^{-S_a[U]} \prod_{\ell \subset \Lambda_a} dU_\ell,
with Wilson action and Haar measure on SU(N).

You want to view \mu_a as a probability measure on some continuum configuration space modulo gauge and show:
	1.	The family \{\mu_a\}_{a\downarrow 0} is tight.
	2.	Any weak limit \mu is a probability measure on a space of “gauge orbits of distribution-valued connections” (in the spirit of Cao–Chatterjee’s 3D construction). ￼
	3.	Limits of gauge-invariant lattice observables converge to continuum correlators.

The main technical problem: the “state space” is not a simple Banach space; it’s something like a quotient of a distribution space by an infinite-dimensional gauge group.

1.2 Step 1: Embed lattice fields into a fixed Banach space

Pick a Sobolev index s>2 so that H^{-s}(\Lambda) is large enough to contain typical Yang–Mills configurations (like a 4D GFF-style scaling heuristic). For each lattice configuration U, construct an interpolated connection 1-form A_a(U) on \Lambda (e.g. piecewise-constant or smooth interpolation on each plaquette) and regard
A_a : \text{(lattice configs)} \to H^{-s}(\Lambda;\mathfrak{su}(N)).

This gives a pushforward measure
\nu_a := (A_a)_\# \mu_a
on H^{-s}(\Lambda;\mathfrak{su}(N)). There is still gauge redundancy, but we at least have a well-defined Borel probability measure on a separable Banach space.

Goal (tightness in the raw field space):
Prove that \{\nu_a\}_{a\downarrow 0} is tight in H^{-s}, i.e. for all \varepsilon>0 there exists a compact set K_\varepsilon \subset H^{-s} such that
\nu_a(K_\varepsilon) \ge 1-\varepsilon
\quad\text{for all sufficiently small }a.

This kind of tightness is standard in constructive QFT: you show uniform moment or exponential moment bounds on norms \|A\|_{H^{-s}}, then use Prokhorov. For scalar fields, one uses correlation inequalities and uniform bounds on the covariance. For gauge fields, it’s harder, but conceptually similar.

1.3 Using functional inequalities from CD(\rho,\infty)

Assume (this is your Prong C input) that at each scale a you can construct a gauge-fixed version of \mu_a,
\mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}}(dA) = Z_a^{-1} e^{-S_{\mathrm{eff},a}(A)} dA,
on some Hilbert space H_a\subset H^{-s} (say, Coulomb gauge), such that:
	•	S_{\mathrm{eff},a} is uniformly \rho-convex in directions where you’ve integrated out gauge redundancies:
\mathrm{Hess}\, S_{\mathrm{eff},a} \;\ge\; \rho\, I
\quad\text{in form sense on }H_a,
with \rho>0 independent of a. This is the infinite-dimensional analogue of your lattice Hessian bound.
	•	The corresponding diffusion generator
L_a = \Delta - \langle \nabla S_{\mathrm{eff},a}, \nabla \cdot \rangle
satisfies a Bakry–Émery curvature–dimension condition BE(\rho,\infty) (a.k.a. CD(\rho,\infty)) with respect to \mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}}. ￼

In this setting, you know a lot: BE(\rho,\infty) implies (for symmetric diffusion operators) a logarithmic Sobolev inequality (LSI) and Poincaré inequality with constants controlled by 1/\rho. ￼ In particular:
	•	For any 1-Lipschitz functional F: H_a\to\mathbb{R} with \int F\,d\mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}}=0,
\int e^{\lambda F} d\mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}} \le \exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{2\rho}\right)
\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\mathbb{R},
i.e. Gaussian concentration with variance scale 1/\rho.

The trick is to choose Lipschitz functionals that control the Sobolev norm \|A\|_{H^{-s}}. For instance, if you pick a finite-dimensional projection \Pi_R onto modes up to some cutoff R, then
F_R(A) := \|\Pi_R A\|_{H^{-s}}
is Lipschitz in the Hilbert norm and obeys exponential integrability. Using a diagonal argument:
	1.	For each fixed cutoff R, concentration + LSI give uniform (in a) bounds on the tails of F_R.
	2.	As R\to \infty, completeness of H^{-s} + control of the tail in frequency domain can be handled by the Gaussian-like short-distance behaviour (heuristically Yang–Mills looks like a GFF at small scales ￼), or by explicit ultraviolet renormalization estimates if you had them.

The formal structure: under CD(\rho,\infty), you get uniform exponential moment bounds of the form
\int \exp\big(c\|A\|_{H^{-s}}^2\big) d\mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}}(A) \le C
\quad\text{for some }c>0, C<\infty \text{ independent of }a.
\tag{*}

If you can obtain (*), it implies tightness of \{\mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}}\} in H^{-s} (by standard arguments: exponential integrability of a proper lower semicontinuous norm implies tightness via Chebyshev and Prokhorov).

1.4 From gauge-fixed tightness to gauge-invariant tightness

Gauge invariance complicates the topology, but not dramatically:
	•	The gauge group \mathcal{G} (e.g. Sobolev H^{s'} gauge transformations) acts continuously on H^{-s}, and you can either:
	•	work with the gauge-fixed measures \mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}} on a slice; or
	•	push them forward to a quotient “orbit space” in the sense of Borel equivalence relations, like Cao–Chatterjee do in 3D by viewing the state space as “random distributional gauge orbits”. ￼

For tightness of gauge-invariant observables, it suffices to have tightness of the underlying \mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}} on a fixed field space together with:
	•	uniform LSI / Poincaré so that expectations of bounded-Lipschitz gauge-invariant functionals are uniformly controlled and equicontinuous in a.

Concretely, denote by \mathcal{O} the class of bounded, gauge-invariant, Lipschitz functionals on H^{-s} (for instance, built from smeared Wilson loops after suitable renormalization). Then:
	•	Tightness of \mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}} in H^{-s} + equicontinuity on \mathcal{O} implies tightness of the family of laws \{\mu_a\circ O^{-1}\} in \mathbb{R} for each O\in\mathcal{O}.
	•	A standard diagonal argument then yields subsequences with joint convergence for all O_1,\dots,O_k \in \mathcal{O}.

So the technical core is really: can your CD(\rho,\infty) inequality be lifted to an infinite-dimensional, gauge-fixed setup, and can you get a Gaussian-type exponential tail for a norm that controls the topology?

There are abstract frameworks where BE(K,\infty) is extended to Dirichlet forms on infinite-dimensional spaces (Gross measure, gradient flows in Wasserstein space). Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré, Ambrosio–Erbar–Savaré and others have made this precise in metric-measure spaces and for Dirichlet forms. ￼

To apply them to Yang–Mills, you’d need a careful construction of the gauge-fixed Dirichlet form and its generator; that’s nontrivial but conceptually clear.

2. Stability of CD(\rho,\infty) under Continuum Limits

Next: suppose at each scale a you have a Bakry–Émery curvature lower bound
CD(\rho,\infty)
\quad\text{for }(\mu_a,L_a),
with \rho>0 independent of a. Under what conditions can you pass this to a limit a\to 0 and conclude that the limiting Dirichlet form also satisfies CD(\rho,\infty)?

2.1 BE(K,\infty) for Dirichlet forms

Let (X_a,\mu_a) be probability spaces and (\mathcal{E}_a,\mathcal{D}_a) symmetric, strongly local Dirichlet forms on L^2(X_a,\mu_a) with generator L_a and carré du champ \Gamma_a. The BE(K,\infty) condition can be formulated as: for all nice f,
\Gamma_{2,a}(f) \;\ge\; K\,\Gamma_a(f),
where
\Gamma_{2,a}(f)
:= \frac{1}{2}\left(L_a \Gamma_a(f) - 2\Gamma_a(f,L_a f)\right).

This is the abstract version of your Bochner-type inequality:
\Gamma_2(f)
= \|\nabla^2 f\|_{HS}^2 + \mathrm{Ric}_V(\nabla f,\nabla f) \ge K \Gamma(f),
with K=\rho. ￼

The BE condition BE(K,\infty) for Dirichlet forms is known to be equivalent to a raft of functional inequalities and gradient estimates for the associated semigroup (P_t^a)_{t\ge 0}. For instance:
|\nabla P_t^a f|^2 \le e^{-2Kt} P_t^a(|\nabla f|^2)
\quad\text{(a.e.)},\ \forall t\ge 0,
and log-Sobolev, Poincaré, Harnack inequalities etc. ￼

This functional-analytic rephrasing is exactly what you need for stability: you want conditions under which convergence of Dirichlet forms / semigroups preserves these inequalities.

2.2 Convergence of Dirichlet forms: Mosco convergence

A standard notion: Mosco convergence of Dirichlet forms. Given forms (\mathcal{E}_a,\mathcal{D}_a) on Hilbert spaces L^2(X_a,\mu_a) and a candidate limit (\mathcal{E},\mathcal{D}) on L^2(X,\mu), Mosco convergence implies convergence of resolvents and semigroups in strong operator topology. ￼

For our purposes, you’d want a setting where:
	1.	All X_a embed into a common Polish space X (e.g. H^{-s} or a quotient space).
	2.	\mu_a \Rightarrow \mu weakly on X.
	3.	(\mathcal{E}_a,\mathcal{D}_a) Mosco-converges to (\mathcal{E},\mathcal{D}) on L^2(X,\mu).

Then:
	•	P_t^a \to P_t strongly on L^2(X,\mu).
	•	Spectral quantities (gap, etc.) are lower semicontinuous.

Exactly this kind of framework is used in the analysis of heat kernels and geometric limits of metric measure spaces. ￼

2.3 Known stability results for CD / BE conditions

In the metric-measure setting, the synthetic curvature-dimension conditions CD(K,N) and their strengthening RCD(K,N) are known to be stable under measured Gromov–Hausdorff (pmG) convergence and related topologies. Lott–Villani, Sturm and many others showed:
	•	If (X_n,d_n,m_n) satisfy CD(K,N) with uniform K,N, and they converge in an appropriate sense (pmG, D-convergence, concentration topology), then the limit (X,d,m) satisfies CD(K,N). ￼

For the BE(K,\infty) formulation on Dirichlet forms, Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré and coauthors have shown:
	•	The BE(K,\infty) condition is equivalent to (or implies) an evolution variational inequality (EVI) property of the heat flow as a gradient flow of entropy in the Wasserstein space.
	•	EVI/gradient-flow characterizations are stable under suitable convergence of measures and forms (Γ-convergence / Mosco convergence), hence one can deduce stability of BE(K,\infty) under limit passages. ￼

More recent work also discusses “rough” curvature-dimension and stability under weaker convergences. ￼

So at an abstract level:

If (X_a,\mu_a,\mathcal{E}_a) satisfy BE(K,\infty) with uniform K, and they converge to (X,\mu,\mathcal{E}) in a sense that preserves semigroup / Dirichlet form structure (Mosco, pmG, etc.), then (X,\mu,\mathcal{E}) also satisfies BE(K,\infty).

The exact technical hypotheses (e.g. quasi-regularity, locality, non-branching) must be checked in each application.

2.4 Translating this to the Yang–Mills / PBH context

In your program, the rough picture would be:
	•	For each lattice spacing a, construct a continuum-embedded, gauge-fixed Dirichlet form (\mathcal{E}_a,\mathcal{D}_a) on L^2(H^{-s},\mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}}), coming from the YM effective action S_{\mathrm{eff},a} and your PBH flow (essentially, \mathcal{E}_a(f,f) = \int |\nabla f|^2 d\mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}}).
	•	Prove a uniform BE(\rho,\infty) estimate with \rho>0 independent of a; this is what Prong C gives at the finite-dimensional (lattice) level, and you’d need an infinite-dimensional extension.
	•	Show that \{\mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}}\} is tight and converges weakly to some \mu on a suitable state space X of distributional connections (or gauge orbits).
	•	Show Mosco convergence of Dirichlet forms (\mathcal{E}_a,\mathcal{D}_a)\to (\mathcal{E},\mathcal{D}) on L^2(X,\mu) (this requires uniform coercivity, control on drift terms, etc.).

Under these hypotheses, you could invoke the general BE(K,\infty) stability results to conclude that the limiting Dirichlet form satisfies BE(\rho,\infty). That buys you:
	•	Uniform log-Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities in the continuum.
	•	A uniform spectral gap estimate for the generator L of the continuum heat flow in field space, with gap \ge \rho.
	•	Strong concentration, which feeds into control of continuum correlation functions.

This pushes your Prong C nonperturbative anomaly-source positivity into the continuum limit.

2.5 Where the hard work sits

The hardest steps (and those that are not currently solved in 4D YM) are:
	1.	Defining a good field-space topology and measure (X,\mu) that can be realized as a limit of (X_a,\mu_a^{\mathrm{gf}}). Recent progress in 3D YM on the torus shows one way to do this (Cao–Chatterjee’s “random gauge orbits”). ￼
	2.	Showing Mosco convergence of field-space Dirichlet forms with interacting, non-Gaussian drifts across scales, including renormalization. Techniques from SPDE on manifolds and infinite-dimensional Dirichlet forms might be adaptable. ￼
	3.	Extending BE(\rho,\infty) uniformly in a to infinite-dimensional limits. At the finite-dimensional lattice level, Prong C gave you a discrete CD(\rho,\infty) via strictly convex gauge-fixing. To pass to infinite dimensions, you need careful control of the Hessian of the effective action and its coercivity in the limit—this is essentially a uniform convexity/curvature control problem in an infinite-dimensional, gauge-invariant setting.

3. How These Pieces Fit Your Mass Gap Program

If you could actually execute the two steps we just drilled into, you’d have:
	1.	Tightness and limit: A continuum Euclidean Yang–Mills measure \mu on a distributional field space (or on gauge orbits), obtained as a limit of lattice measures.
	2.	CD(\rho,\infty) stability: The continuum Dirichlet form associated with the YM effective action satisfies BE(\rho,\infty) with the same positive \rho coming from the lattice Prong C analysis.
	3.	Uniform spectral gap: BE(\rho,\infty) implies a Poincaré/spectral gap inequality with constant \rho, transferring your anomaly-source bound to the continuum as a nonzero field-space spectral gap.
	4.	Combined with OS reflection positivity and reconstruction (still another large step), this spectral gap should be connected to a physical mass gap in the Minkowski theory.

The tightness and BE-stability machinery are the core analytic tools that could make your PBH/Bakry–Émery path to the mass gap technically viable, but both require careful, new work in the Yang–Mills setting.
