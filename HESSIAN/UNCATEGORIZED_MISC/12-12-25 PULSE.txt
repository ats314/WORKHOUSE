# Haar density, Weyl factor, and the quadratic term

Let (G) be a compact connected Lie group with maximal torus (T), Lie algebra (\mathfrak g), Cartan (\mathfrak t), and root system (R\subset \mathfrak t^*). Write elements near the identity as (g=\exp(iH)) with (H\in\mathfrak t) small. The Weyl integration formula gives, for a class function (f),
[
\int_G f(g),dg = \frac{1}{|W|}\int_{\mathfrak t} f!\big(\exp(iH)\big), j(H), dH,
]
with the **Haar Jacobian**
[
j(H)=\prod_{\alpha>0}\left(\frac{\sin\frac{\alpha(H)}{2}}{\frac{\alpha(H)}{2}}\right)^{2}
= \bigl|\Delta(e^{iH})\bigr|^2,
]
where the product is over positive roots and (\Delta) is the Weyl denominator. Define the “Haar action” (log‑Jacobian)
[
S_H(H)\coloneqq -\log j(H)= -\sum_{\alpha>0} 2\log!\left(\frac{\sin\frac{\alpha(H)}{2}}{\frac{\alpha(H)}{2}}\right).
]

## Small‑(H) expansion (quadratic term)

Using (\displaystyle \frac{\sin x}{x}=1-\frac{x^2}{6}+O(x^4)),
[
\log!\left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)= -\frac{x^2}{6}+O(x^4).
]
Set (x=\tfrac{\alpha(H)}{2}). Then
[
S_H(H)= -\sum_{\alpha>0} 2\left(-\frac{(\alpha(H))^2}{24}+O(|H|^4)\right)
= \frac{1}{12}\sum_{\alpha>0}\bigl(\alpha(H)\bigr)^2 + O(|H|^4).
]
Thus the quadratic form at the identity is
[
\boxed{\quad S_H(H)= \tfrac{1}{12}\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha(H)^2 + O(|H|^4)\quad}
]
and the Hessian at (H=0) is
[
\nabla^2 S_H(0)= \tfrac{1}{12}\sum_{\alpha>0} \alpha\otimes\alpha,
]
viewing (\alpha) as a linear form on (\mathfrak t) and (\alpha\otimes\alpha) as the rank‑one operator (v\mapsto \alpha(v),\alpha^\sharp) (with (\alpha^\sharp) the metric dual).

## Concrete check: (G=SU(n))

For (SU(n)) with (\mathfrak t={H=\mathrm{diag}(h_1,\dots,h_n),\ \sum h_i=0}) and roots (\alpha_{ij}(H)=h_i-h_j) ((i<j)),
[
j(H)=\prod_{i<j}\left(\frac{\sin\frac{h_i-h_j}{2}}{\frac{h_i-h_j}{2}}\right)^{2},\qquad
S_H(H)=\frac{1}{12}\sum_{i<j}(h_i-h_j)^2+O(|H|^4).
]
Equivalently, (\nabla^2 S_H(0)=\tfrac{1}{12}\sum_{i<j}(e_i-e_j)\otimes(e_i-e_j)) on the trace‑zero subspace.

# **RP ⇒ OS transfer operator and its gap**

**Setup.** On a reflection‑positive Euclidean lattice theory with spacing (a>0), Osterwalder–Schrader (OS) reconstruction yields a Hilbert space (\mathcal H), a self‑adjoint Hamiltonian (H\ge 0), and a **transfer operator**
[
T_a ;=; e^{-aH},\qquad |T_a|\le 1.
]
Let ({\lambda_n}_{n\ge 0}) be eigenvalues of (T_a) in non‑increasing order with eigenvectors ({\psi_n}), and (E_n) the corresponding energy levels of (H) (so (E_n\ge 0), (E_0) is the ground energy).

**Eigenvalue–energy relation.**
[
\lambda_n ;=; e^{-aE_n},\qquad E_n ;=; -\frac{1}{a}\log \lambda_n .
]

**Mass gap.** The (OS/physical) mass gap is
[
m ;=; E_1-E_0 ;=; -\frac{1}{a}\log!\left(\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_0}\right).
]
If the vacuum is normalized so that (\lambda_0=1) (i.e., (E_0=0)), then (m = -\tfrac{1}{a}\log \lambda_1).

# **Reversible diffusion ⇒ (L^2) contraction**

Let ((\mu,L)) be a reversible Markov diffusion (generator (L), invariant measure (\mu)) with **Poincaré/spectral‑gap constant** (\lambda_P>0), i.e.
[
\mathrm{Var}*\mu(f);\le;\frac{1}{\lambda_P},\int \Gamma(f),d\mu,
]
equivalently the (L^2(\mu)) semigroup (P_t=e^{tL}) contracts:
[
|P_t f-\mu f|*{L^2(\mu)} ;\le; e^{-\lambda_P t},|f-\mu f|_{L^2(\mu)}.
]

# **Lemma (Diffusion gap ↔ Transfer gap via OS time‑slice).**

# RP under Coarse‑Graining: what actually works

**Background (2 lines).**
Reflection positivity (RP) for a Euclidean lattice measure (e.g., Wilson action) means: with respect to a plane reflection (θ), the bilinear form ⟨f,θf⟩ is ≥0 for all observables supported on the “+” half. This is the gateway to OS reconstruction (Hilbert space + positive Hamiltonian). The usual failure mode under RG is that generic block maps do **not** commute with θ or they generate non‑RP cross‑terms.

**Practical route.**
Use **positivity‑preserving, reflection‑commuting** block maps—essentially **block averages** aligned to the reflection plane. These are the maps underlying **chessboard estimates**: they average/replicate local patterns over reflected tiles while keeping θ‑symmetry manifest. The general pattern:

* Choose a reflection plane that **cuts blocks cleanly** (no partial blocks crossing the mirror).
* Define the coarse variable as a **linear average with nonnegative weights** of fine variables inside each block.
* Ensure the averaging operator **commutes with θ** (block partitions are θ‑invariant).
* Pushforward of an RP measure by such a map remains RP; product structures needed for chessboard bounds are preserved.

---

# Drop‑in lemma (RP ⇒ RP under block averaging)

**Lemma (RP preserved by reflection‑commuting positive linear coarse‑graining).**
Let (Ω,𝔽,μ) be a lattice Euclidean field/gauge measure on Λ⊂ℤ^d that is reflection positive w.r.t. plane reflection θ:Λ→Λ, with θ‑induced involution on observables (Θf = f∘θ, complex conjugation suppressed).
Let P: L^2(μ)→L^2(μ) be the linear projection implementing **block averaging** on a θ‑invariant partition 𝔅 of Λ (i.e., θ(𝔅)=𝔅) with **nonnegative weights** summing to 1 in each block, and suppose P and Θ commute: PΘ=ΘP. Denote the coarse σ‑algebra by 𝔽_B generated by block variables and the **pushforward** measure μ_B := μ∘P^{-1} on coarse configurations.

Then μ_B is reflection positive w.r.t. the induced reflection θ_B on blocks: for any coarse observable F supported on the “+” half of blocks,
[
\int F,(\Theta_B F), d\mu_B ;=; \int (PF), \Theta(PF), d\mu ;\ge; 0.
]

*Proof (one line).* Commutation PΘ=ΘP and positivity of the quadratic form for μ give ⟨PF,Θ(PF)⟩_μ≥0; identification of PF with the pullback of F under P yields the claim. ∎

**Notes.**

* Nonnegative weights ensure P is a **Markov** (positivity‑preserving, norm‑nonincreasing) map.
* Θ‑invariance of the block partition is the only geometric constraint; align blocks to the mirror.

# Chessboard estimate stub (ready to paste)

**Proposition (Chessboard estimate under RP‑stable blocking).**
Assume μ is RP and let 𝔅 be a θ‑invariant tiling. For any local nonnegative observable G supported in a single block on the “+” half, let G^{#} denote its **reflected‑tiled** product (G replicated by reflections/translations across the chessboard). Then
[
\int G^{#}, d\mu ;\le; \Big(\int G, d\mu\Big)^{N},
]
where N is the number of tiles in the chessboard (precise exponent depends on the tiling group; equality cases as usual).

*Proof sketch.* Standard chessboard: apply RP iteratively with reflections across each mirror, each step using Cauchy–Schwarz on the RP inner product ⟨·,Θ·⟩. Because blocking and reflection commute, the same argument applies at the coarse level and is invariant under pushing forward by P. ∎

* **Model‑specifics:**

# **RP ⇒ Transfer matrix ⇒ Spectral form ⇒ Mass gap**

**Setup (Euclidean lattice/continuum):**
Pick a Hermitian, time‑reflection–even observable (F) (e.g., a gauge‑invariant local operator). Assume **reflection positivity** across a Euclidean time slice and finite action so that an Osterwalder–Schrader (OS) reconstruction applies. Then there is a Hilbert space (\mathcal H), a cyclic vacuum (|0\rangle), and a positive self‑adjoint Hamiltonian (H\ge 0) with **transfer matrix** (T_a = e^{-aH}) for time step (a>0).

**Two‑point function:**
Define (C_F(t)=\langle F(0)F(t)\rangle_E) for (t=na\in a\mathbb N). RP + OS ensure:
[
C_F(t)=\sum_{n\ge 0} \big|\langle 0|F|n\rangle\big|^2 e^{-E_n t},\qquad 0=E_0\le E_1\le E_2\le\cdots,
]
i.e., a **nonnegative spectral expansion** with energies ({E_n}) (transfer‑matrix eigenvalues (\lambda_n=e^{-aE_n})).

**Mass gap (definition/estimator):**
If (F) has nonzero overlap with the first excited state in the target channel, the **mass gap**
[
m ;=; E_1 - E_0 ;=; E_1
]
is read off from the large‑(t) decay:
[
m ;=; -\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t},\log!\frac{C_F(t)}{C_F(0)}
;=; -\frac{1}{a}\log!\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_0}.
]
(Here (\lambda_0=1) because (E_0=0) sets the vacuum energy.)

**Practical lattice extraction (discrete times (t=na)):**

* Effective mass: (m_{\rm eff}(t)=\frac{1}{a}\log!\frac{C_F(t)}{C_F(t+a)};\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}; m).
* Use variational/GEVP with an operator basis ({F_i}) to isolate (\lambda_1) in the desired quantum numbers.
* Ensure RP‑compatible smearings (reflection‑even) and sufficiently large (t) windows to suppress excited‑state contamination.

**Why RP matters:**
RP guarantees (i) positivity of the inner product in the OS Hilbert space, (ii) positivity of (H), (iii) complete monotonicity of (C_F(t)), hence the **Stieltjes‑type** spectral representation above. Without RP, the clean nonnegative spectral weights and the mass‑gap estimator lose rigor.

**Equivalently in transfer‑matrix language:**
Let (T_a) have eigenpairs ((\lambda_n,|n\rangle)) with (\lambda_n=e^{-aE_n}). Then
[
C_F(na)=\langle 0|F,T_a^n,F|0\rangle
=\sum_{m}\big|\langle 0|F|m\rangle\big|^2 \lambda_m^{,n},
]
and the asymptotics are controlled by the largest sub‑vacuum eigenvalue (\lambda_1<1). Thus
[
m ;=; -\frac{1}{a}\log\lambda_1 ;=; -\frac{1}{a}\log!\Big(\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_0}\Big).
]

**Minimal checklist (rigorous use):**

* RP of the measure/action and reflection‑even (F).
* Existence of (T_a=e^{-aH}) with (H\ge 0).
* Nonzero overlap (\langle 0|F|1\rangle\neq 0) in the target channel.
* Large‑(t) window where single‑exponential dominates (with error control).
