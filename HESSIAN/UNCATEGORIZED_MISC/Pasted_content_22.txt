Here is a clean version you can drop into your stack. I’ll work in the continuum orbit space with an explicit UV-regularized Sobolev model, and then show exactly where the g^2 scaling comes from. I’ll also be explicit about where “small field strength” is used.

⸻

1. Geometric setup

Let M be a fixed compact Riemannian 4-manifold, P\to M a principal SU(N)–bundle.

1.1 Space of connections and metric

Fix k>2 so that H^k embeds into C^0. Let
\mathcal{A} := A_0 + \Omega^1_k(M,\operatorname{ad}P)
be the affine Hilbert manifold of H^k connections on P (modeled on the Hilbert space H := \Omega^1_k(M,\operatorname{ad}P)).

For a gauge coupling g>0, define the (weak) Riemannian metric on \mathcal{A} by
\langle a,b\rangle_{A}^{(g)}
:= \frac{1}{g^2}\int_M \langle a(x),b(x)\rangle_{\mathfrak{su}(N)} \, d\operatorname{vol}(x),
\qquad a,b\in T_A\mathcal{A}\cong H.
For g=1 we write simply \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_A.

Because \mathcal{A} is affine and the metric has constant coefficients in these coordinates, (\mathcal{A},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle^{(g)}) is flat for every g: its Levi–Civita connection is the trivial one and its sectional curvature is identically zero.

1.2 Gauge group and orbit space

Let \mathcal{G} := H^{k+1}(M,SU(N)) be the Sobolev gauge group. It acts smoothly and isometrically on \mathcal{A} by
A^u := u^{-1}Au + u^{-1}du.

A connection A is irreducible if its stabilizer equals the center Z(SU(N)). Let \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}\subset\mathcal{A} be the open subset of irreducible connections and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}} := \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}/\mathcal{G}
its gauge orbit space. Standard slice theory (e.g. using Coulomb gauge) shows that \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}} is a smooth Hilbert manifold and that the quotient map
\pi : \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}\to \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}
is a principal \mathcal{G}/Z(SU(N))–bundle.

We endow \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}} with the unique Riemannian metric g_{\mathcal{M}}^{(g)} for which
\pi:(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle^{(g)})\to(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}},g_{\mathcal{M}}^{(g)})
is a Riemannian submersion.

⸻

2. Vertical / horizontal decomposition

At each A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}:
	•	The vertical space is the tangent to the gauge orbit:
V_A := \{ D_A\phi : \phi\in\Omega^0_{k+1}(M,\operatorname{ad}P)\} \subset H,
where D_A is the covariant derivative.
	•	We choose the horizontal space as the L^2–orthogonal complement of V_A:
H_A := \{ a\in H : D_A^* a = 0\},
with D_A^* the formal adjoint (covariant divergence).

Because D_A^*D_A is an elliptic, self-adjoint operator on \Omega^0_{k+1}(M,\operatorname{ad}P), we have the orthogonal decomposition
H = H_A \oplus V_A,
and T_A\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}} = H_A\oplus V_A. The differential d\pi_A identifies H_A isometrically with T_{[A]}\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}.

Thus \pi is a (Hilbert) Riemannian submersion with horizontal distribution H_A.

⸻

3. O’Neill formula for the orbit-space curvature

Let (E,g_E)\to(B,g_B) be a Riemannian submersion with horizontal distribution H and vertical distribution V. For horizontal vectors X,Y\in H_p with images \bar X,\bar Y\in T_{\pi(p)}B, O’Neill’s formula says the sectional curvature on the base is
K_B(\bar X,\bar Y)
= K_E(X,Y) + \frac{3}{4}\frac{\|[X,Y]^V\|^2}{\|X\wedge Y\|^2},
where [X,Y]^V is the vertical component of the Lie bracket of horizontal vector fields extending X,Y. (In our case E=\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}, B=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}.)

Here:
	•	K_E\equiv 0 because \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}} is flat.
	•	So for unit, orthonormal horizontal vectors X,Y at A,
K_{\mathcal{M}}^{(g=1)}(X,Y) = \frac{3}{4}\,\|[X,Y]^V\|^2.

Thus the entire curvature of the orbit space comes from the non-integrability of the horizontal distribution, i.e. from the curvature 2-form of the principal bundle \pi.

⸻

4. The connection 1-form and its curvature

4.1 Connection 1-form on the gauge bundle

Define a connection 1-form \omega on the principal bundle \pi:\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}} as follows. For A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}} and Z\in T_A\mathcal{A}\cong H, define \omega_A(Z)\in \Omega^0_{k+1}(M,\operatorname{ad}P) to be the unique solution of
D_A^* D_A \omega_A(Z) = D_A^* Z.
This is well-posed on the irreducible locus: D_A^*D_A has trivial kernel except for constants in the center (which vanish in \operatorname{ad}P), and standard elliptic theory gives a bounded inverse on the orthogonal complement.

Then:
	•	The vertical projection is Z^V = D_A\omega_A(Z).
	•	The horizontal projection is Z^H = Z - Z^V.
	•	The horizontal space is precisely \ker\omega_A = \{Z : D_A^*Z=0\}, as required.

4.2 Curvature of the connection on \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}

The curvature of this principal connection is the 2-form
\Omega_A(X,Y)
:= d\omega_A(X,Y) + \tfrac12[\omega_A(X),\omega_A(Y)]
\in \Omega^0_{k+1}(M,\operatorname{ad}P),
for horizontal vector fields X,Y.

Because the ambient space is affine and we’ll consider horizontal fields obtained as projections of constant vectors, this simplifies: for such basic horizontal lifts one finds
\Omega_A(X,Y) = -\,G_A\, D_A^*[X,Y]_{\text{pointwise}},
where:
	•	G_A := (D_A^*D_A)^{-1} is the Green operator on \Omega^0_{k+1}(M,\operatorname{ad}P),
	•	[X,Y]_{\text{pointwise}}(x) = [X(x),Y(x)] is the fiberwise Lie bracket in \mathfrak{su}(N).

This is the standard expression for the curvature of the Coulomb connection on the gauge bundle; it is an immediate adaptation of the finite-dimensional principal bundle case, plus elliptic inversion of D_A^*D_A.

For a principal bundle, the vertical part of the bracket of horizontal lifts is (fundamental vertical vector field associated to -\Omega):
[X,Y]^V_A = (D_A\, \Omega_A(X,Y)) \in V_A.

Thus for unit, orthonormal horizontal X,Y at A,
K_{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}(X,Y)
= \frac{3}{4}\,\|D_A \Omega_A(X,Y)\|^2.

All norms here are with respect to the g=1 metric on \mathcal{A}.

⸻

5. Bounding the vertical bracket in terms of SU(N) structure constants

Let us work with g=1 for the moment, drop the superscript, and fix A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}.

5.1 Choice of horizontal lifts

Let a,b\in H_A be an orthonormal pair in H_A = \ker D_A^* (so \|a\|=\|b\|=1, \langle a,b\rangle=0). They represent a 2-plane in T_{[A]}\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}.

Extend them to horizontal vector fields on \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}} by the rule
X(B) := \text{horizontal projection of }a\text{ at }B,\qquad
Y(B) := \text{horizontal projection of }b\text{ at }B.
At B=A we have X(A)=a, Y(A)=b.

Then, by the curvature formula just above,
[X,Y]^V_A = D_A \Omega_A(X,Y)
= -\,D_A G_A D_A^* [a,b]_{\text{pt}}.

So we need to bound \|D_A G_A D_A^*[a,b]\|.

5.2 Elliptic estimates for D_A^*D_A

Write L_A := D_A^*D_A. On the irreducible locus, standard elliptic theory gives:
	1.	Coercivity. There exists c_*(A_0,\varepsilon)>0 such that for all \phi\in\Omega^0_{k+1}(\operatorname{ad}P),
\langle L_A\phi,\phi\rangle_{L^2}
\ge c_* \|\phi\|^2_{H^1}.
For connections with uniformly small curvature, i.e. \|F_A\|_{C^0}\le\varepsilon for fixed small \varepsilon>0, c_* can be chosen uniformly in A (it depends only on M, the reference connection and \varepsilon) by viewing L_A as a small zeroth-order perturbation of a fixed Laplacian.
	2.	Bounded inverse. On the orthogonal complement of its kernel (which is trivial in \operatorname{ad}P), L_A is invertible with bounded inverse:
\|G_A\|_{L^2\to L^2} \le c_*^{-1}.
	3.	Graph norm equivalence. There is C_1 such that
\|D_A \phi\|_{L^2} \le C_1 \|L_A \phi\|_{L^2}
\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\Omega^0_{k+1}(\operatorname{ad}P)
(this is another standard elliptic estimate, again uniform for \|F_A\|_{C^0} bounded).

Combining, we obtain a uniform operator bound
\|D_A G_A\|_{L^2\to L^2} \le C_{\mathrm{ell}}
for all A in a small-curvature neighborhood \{ \|F_A\|_{C^0}\le\varepsilon\}, where C_{\mathrm{ell}} depends only on M, the background choice, and \varepsilon.

5.3 Pointwise Lie bracket estimate

At each point x\in M, the Lie bracket on \mathfrak{su}(N) satisfies
|[X,Y]|_{\mathfrak{su}(N)} \le C_N |X|\,|Y|
for a universal constant C_N>0 depending only on SU(N) (e.g. in an orthonormal basis with structure constants f^{abc}, C_N can be taken as \max_{a,b} (\sum_c (f^{abc})^2)^{1/2}).

Thus, for a,b\in \Omega^1(M,\operatorname{ad}P),
\|[a,b]\|_{L^2} \le C_N \|a\|_{L^4}\|b\|_{L^4}.

Using Sobolev embedding H^1\hookrightarrow L^4 in 4D and the fact that a,b\in H_A\subset H^1 with unit L^2 norm on a fixed compact M, we obtain
\|[a,b]\|_{L^2}
\le C'_N \|a\|_{H^1}\|b\|_{H^1}
\le C''_N
for some constant C''_N depending only on SU(N) and M. The last inequality uses that on a fixed Sobolev sphere the H^1 norm is uniformly bounded in terms of the L^2 norm plus the horizontality constraint; in particular, on the unit L^2 sphere in the horizontal space one has \|a\|_{H^1}\le C(M)\|a\|_{L^2}=C(M). (This is a standard compactness/elliptic regularity argument: horizontality D_A^*a=0 plus small \|F_A\|_{C^0} gives control of \|D_A a\|_{L^2} in terms of \|a\|_{L^2}.)

Finally,
\|D_A^*[a,b]\|_{L^2}
\le C_2 \|[a,b]\|_{H^1}
\le C_3(N,M,\varepsilon).

5.4 Putting it together

Now:
[X,Y]^V_A = -D_A G_A D_A^*[a,b],
so
\|[X,Y]^V_A\|_{L^2}
\le \|D_A G_A\|_{L^2\to L^2} \,\|D_A^*[a,b]\|_{L^2}
\le C_{\mathrm{ell}}\,C_3(N,M,\varepsilon)
=: C_*(N,M,\varepsilon).

Recall a,b were chosen orthonormal in H_A, so X,Y are orthonormal horizontal vectors at A. Then
K_{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}(X,Y)
= \frac{3}{4}\,\|[X,Y]^V_A\|^2
\le \frac{3}{4}\,C_*^2
=: C_0^{(1)}(N,M,\varepsilon).

Thus for the g=1 metric,
|K_{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}(X,Y)| \le C_0^{(1)}
for all orthonormal horizontal X,Y at all connections with \|F_A\|_{C^0}\le\varepsilon, and C_0^{(1)} depends only on SU(N), the background manifold M, and the fixed curvature bound \varepsilon.

Up to here the argument is independent of the gauge coupling.

⸻

6. Restoring the gauge coupling g

Recall that for each g>0 we defined
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_A^{(g)} = \frac{1}{g^2}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_A^{(1)}.
That is, the field-space metric is a constant rescaling of the g=1 metric by a factor c = 1/g^2.

For any Riemannian manifold (X,g), if we scale the metric by a constant factor c>0, i.e. g' = c\,g, then sectional curvatures satisfy
K_{g'} = \frac{1}{c} K_g.
(This follows directly from the definitions: the Riemann (1,3) tensor is unchanged, but the lowering of indices and the normalization by \|X\wedge Y\|^2 each introduce a factor of c.)

Apply this first on \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}: scaling the metric by 1/g^2 keeps it flat (curvature zero). On the orbit space \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}, the same scaling carries over because the metric is induced by the Riemannian submersion. Thus the sectional curvature obeys
K_{\mathcal{M}}^{(g)} = g^2\, K_{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}.

Using the bound obtained above,
|K_{\mathcal{M}}^{(g)}(X,Y)|
= g^2 |K_{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}(X,Y)|
\le g^2 C_0^{(1)}(N,M,\varepsilon).

Set
C_0 := C_0^{(1)}(N,M,\varepsilon),
and note that for any fixed manifold M and fixed “small field strength” threshold \varepsilon, this constant depends only on the Lie group SU(N) (through its structure constants) and the fixed background data; in particular, it is independent of the specific connection A as long as \|F_A\|_{C^0}\le\varepsilon.

We have proved:

⸻

Theorem (Curvature bound on the Yang–Mills gauge orbit space)

Let P\to M be a principal SU(N)–bundle over a compact Riemannian 4-manifold. Equip the space of H^k connections \mathcal{A} with the L^2 metric scaled by the gauge coupling g>0,
\langle a,b\rangle^{(g)}_A
= \frac{1}{g^2}\int_M \langle a(x),b(x)\rangle_{\mathfrak{su}(N)}\,d\operatorname{vol}(x).
Let \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}/\mathcal{G} be the orbit space of irreducible connections with the induced Riemannian metric, and let K_{\mathcal{M}}(X,Y) be the sectional curvature of the 2-plane spanned by orthonormal tangent vectors X,Y\in T_{[A]}\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}.

Fix a small curvature threshold \varepsilon>0. Then there exists a constant C_0>0, depending only on SU(N) (and the fixed background M,\varepsilon), such that for every connection A with \|F_A\|_{C^0}\le\varepsilon,
\boxed{
|K_{\mathcal{M}}(X,Y)| \;\le\; C_0\, g^2
}
for all 2-planes spanned by orthonormal X,Y\in T_{[A]}\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}.

7. Relation to your lattice / Hessian picture

On the lattice, the same mechanism appears in a fully finite-dimensional form:
	•	The configuration space is a finite product SU(N)^{|B(\Lambda)|} with a bi-invariant product metric.
	•	The gauge group acts isometrically, and the orbit space inherits its metric as a Riemannian quotient.
	•	The effective action’s Hessian on the irreducible sector has a uniform positive lower bound \lambda_{\min}\ge c_0\sim (N^2-1)/(2N), giving a curvature-type lower bound and hence a spectral gap; see your lattice Hessian derivation for details.  ￼

The continuum curvature bound proven above is the geometric analogue of that finite-cutoff statement: the factor g^2 comes purely from the choice of field-space scaling, and the constant C_0 is controlled by the structure constants of \mathfrak{su}(N) and elliptic estimates for the Coulomb connection on the gauge bundle.
