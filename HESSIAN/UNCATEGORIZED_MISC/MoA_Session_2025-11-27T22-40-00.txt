\title{Geometric--Spectral Aspects of the Lattice Yang--Mills Mass Gap:\\
Finite Cutoff Convexity and a Conjectural Stability Mechanism}

\author{(synthetic compilation of working notes)}
\date{\today}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We discuss geometric and spectral aspects of the mass gap problem for
four--dimensional $SU(N)$ Yang--Mills theory, in the framework of
lattice regularisation. The discussion splits into two stages:
\emph{finite--cutoff existence}, i.e.\ the presence of a non--zero gap
for each fixed lattice spacing $a>0$, and \emph{continuum stability},
i.e.\ the behaviour of this gap along a renormalisation trajectory as
$a\to 0$.

On the finite--cutoff side, we exploit the geometry of the compact
configuration space $SU(N)^{|\mathcal{B}|}$ and the Haar measure to
derive an explicit positive mass term in exponential coordinates, which
yields a uniform lower bound on the Hessian of the effective action for
small fields. This leads, via Bakry--\'Emery curvature and transfer
matrix techniques, to a spectral gap at fixed $a$ in appropriate
regimes.

On the speculative side, we formulate a \emph{Geometric--Spectral
Stability Mechanism} tying together: (i) Haar--induced convexity at
finite cutoff, (ii) a formal Riccati--type evolution equation for the
Hessian of an effective action under a viscous Hamilton--Jacobi flow,
and (iii) the non--perturbative, topological origin of the transfer
matrix gap via Wilson loops. We state a \emph{Geometric--Spectral
Stability Conjecture} which encodes the hoped--for survival of the mass
gap in the continuum limit through a hand--off from extrinsic lattice
stiffness to intrinsic geometric and topological effects. At present,
this is a programmatic scenario rather than a proof of the Clay
Yang--Mills mass gap problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{The mass gap problem and its lattice formulation}

\subsection{Continuum mass gap}

The Clay Millennium problem on the Yang--Mills mass gap asks, roughly
speaking, for a rigorous construction of four--dimensional $SU(N)$
Yang--Mills theory in Minkowski space with a positive spectral gap above
the vacuum in the physical Hilbert space. In Euclidean signature, one
expects the existence of a quantum field theory with Euclidean
correlation functions satisfying the Osterwalder--Schrader axioms and a
non--zero \emph{physical mass}
\[
  m_{\mathrm{phys}} > 0
\]
entering the exponential decay of two--point functions.

The mass gap can be characterised in several equivalent ways: as the
lowest non--zero eigenvalue of the Hamiltonian $H$ in the physical
Hilbert space, as the decay rate of time correlations, or as the inverse
correlation length in space. We will adopt the Hamiltonian/transfer
matrix viewpoint when working on the lattice.

\subsection{Lattice regularisation and two--step strategy}

On a hypercubic lattice with spacing $a>0$, the gauge field is
represented by link variables $U_b \in SU(N)$ attached to oriented
bonds $b\in\mathcal{B}$ and the dynamics is encoded in a Wilson action
$S_W(U)$ or, in the Hamiltonian formulation, in a Kogut--Susskind
Hamiltonian $H(a,g)$ acting on a Hilbert space
\[
  \mathcal{H}_a = L^2\!\big(SU(N)^{|\mathcal{B}_s|}, d\mu_H\big)
\]
where $\mathcal{B}_s$ are spatial bonds and $\mu_H$ is the product Haar
measure.

The corresponding transfer matrix $T_a$ is related to the Hamiltonian by
\[
  T_a = e^{-a_t H(a,g)},
\]
with $a_t$ the temporal lattice spacing (possibly equal or related to
$a$). The lattice spectral gap at spacing $a$ and bare coupling $g$ is
the difference between the two largest eigenvalues of $T_a$, re--scaled
by $a_t$:
\[
  \Delta(a,g) = -\,\frac{1}{a_t}\log\frac{\lambda_1(a,g)}{\lambda_0(a,g)}.
\]

A natural strategy for the mass gap problem splits into two steps:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Finite--cutoff existence.} For each fixed lattice spacing
  $a>0$ (and suitable bare couplings) prove that $\Delta(a,g)>0$ and
  obtain bounds that are uniform in the spatial volume.

  \item \textbf{Continuum stability.} Identify a renormalisation
  trajectory $g(a)$ such that as $a\to 0$ with $g=g(a)$, the physical
  mass scale extracted from $\Delta(a,g(a))$ approaches a finite,
  strictly positive limit.
\end{itemize}

In this note we focus mainly on the first step and on a conjectural,
geometric mechanism for the second.

\section{Configuration space, Haar measure and the Wilson action}

\subsection{Lattice configuration space}

Let $\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^4$ be a finite hypercubic lattice with
periodic boundary conditions. Let $\mathcal{B}$ denote the set of
oriented nearest--neighbour bonds $b=(x,\mu)$ with $x\in\Lambda$ and
$\mu\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, and let $N_b = |\mathcal{B}|$.

\begin{definition}
The \emph{configuration space} of $SU(N)$ lattice gauge fields on
$\Lambda$ is
\[
  \mathcal{C}_\Lambda = SU(N)^{N_b}
\]
equipped with the product topology and the product Haar probability
measure
\[
  d\mu_H(U) = \prod_{b\in\mathcal{B}} dU_b.
\]
\end{definition}

We endow $SU(N)$ with its standard bi--invariant Riemannian metric
induced by the negative Killing form on its Lie algebra
$\mathfrak{su}(N)$. The product manifold $\mathcal{C}_\Lambda$ inherits
the product Riemannian structure. Let $\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_\Lambda}$ be
the Laplace--Beltrami operator on $\mathcal{C}_\Lambda$ and
$\nabla_{\mathcal{C}_\Lambda}$ its Riemannian gradient.

\subsection{Wilson action}

The standard Wilson action on $\Lambda$ is
\[
  S_W(U) = \sum_{p} \Big(1 - \frac{1}{N}\,\Re\operatorname{Tr}U_p\Big),
\]
where the sum is over oriented plaquettes $p$ and $U_p$ is the ordered
product of link variables $U_b$ around $p$. The Euclidean lattice
Yang--Mills measure at inverse coupling $\beta$ is
\[
  d\mu_{\Lambda,\beta}(U)
   = Z_{\Lambda,\beta}^{-1}\, e^{-\beta S_W(U)}\, d\mu_H(U).
\]

The Hamiltonian and transfer matrix can be obtained by standard
Osterwalder--Seiler/L\"uscher reconstructions from this Euclidean
measure; we will not reproduce these constructions in detail and focus
instead on geometric aspects.

\section{Haar measure in exponential coordinates and a mass term}

We now derive the effective action contribution of Haar measure in
exponential coordinates and extract a lattice mass term.

\subsection{Exponential coordinates on $SU(N)$}

Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{su}(N)$ be the Lie algebra of $SU(N)$ and
let $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ be an $Ad$--invariant inner product
proportional to the Killing form. For small $A\in\mathfrak{g}$, the
exponential map $\exp\colon\mathfrak{g}\to SU(N)$ is a diffeomorphism of
a neighbourhood of $0$ onto a neighbourhood of the identity.

On the lattice, for link variables sufficiently close to the identity we
can write
\[
  U_b = \exp(iagA_b),\qquad A_b\in\mathfrak{su}(N),
\]
with $a>0$ the lattice spacing and $g$ the bare coupling. The product
map identifies a neighbourhood of $(0,\dots,0)\in\mathfrak{g}^{N_b}$
with a neighbourhood of $(I,\dots,I)\in\mathcal{C}_\Lambda$.

\subsection{Haar Jacobian and measure action}

The Haar measure transforms under this change of variables as
\[
  d\mu_H(U) = J(A)\, dA,\qquad dA = \prod_{b\in\mathcal{B}} dA_b,
\]
where $J(A)$ is the Jacobian density. A classical formula (see e.g.\ the
theory of compact Lie group integration) expresses $J$ in terms of the
adjoint representation.

For each $A\in\mathfrak{g}$, let $\mathrm{ad}_A\colon\mathfrak{g}\to
\mathfrak{g}$ be the adjoint operator
$\mathrm{ad}_A(X) = [A,X]$. Then for $X\in\mathfrak{g}$ small,
\[
  J(A) = \det_{\mathfrak{g}}\!\left(
    \frac{\sinh(\tfrac{\mathrm{ad}_{iagA}}{2})}{\tfrac{\mathrm{ad}_{iagA}}{2}}
  \right).
\]
Define the \emph{measure action}
\[
  S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A) = -\log J(A).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Small field expansion of $S_{\mathrm{Haar}}$]
\label{lem:Haar-expansion}
For $A$ in a sufficiently small neighbourhood of $0$ one has
\[
  S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
  = c_N a^2 g^2 \sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname{Tr}(A_b^2)
    + O(a^4\|A\|^4)
\]
for some constant $c_N>0$ depending only on $N$ and the choice of
$\operatorname{Tr}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $Y = \frac{\mathrm{ad}_{iagA}}{2}$. Then
\[
  \frac{\sinh Y}{Y}
  = I + \frac{Y^2}{3!} + O(\|Y\|^4).
\]
Therefore
\[
  \log\left(\frac{\sinh Y}{Y}\right)
  = \frac{Y^2}{3!} + O(\|Y\|^4),
\]
and thus
\[
  -\log J(A)
  = -\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\!\log\left(
      \frac{\sinh Y}{Y}\right)
  = -\frac{1}{3!}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(Y^2) + O(\|Y\|^4).
\]
Now $Y^2 = -\frac{a^2 g^2}{4}\,\mathrm{ad}_A^2$. For the adjoint
representation of a compact semi--simple Lie algebra,
$\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{ad}_A^2)
  = -C_2(\mathrm{ad})\,\langle A,A\rangle$, with $C_2(\mathrm{ad})>0$
the quadratic Casimir in the adjoint representation; for $SU(N)$,
$C_2(\mathrm{ad}) = 2N$. Combining these facts,
\[
  -\frac{1}{3!}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(Y^2)
  = -\frac{1}{6}\left(-\frac{a^2 g^2}{4}\right)
    \cdot C_2(\mathrm{ad}) \,\langle A,A\rangle
  = \frac{a^2 g^2 C_2(\mathrm{ad})}{24}\,\langle A,A\rangle.
\]
If we normalise $\operatorname{Tr}$ on matrices so that
$\langle A,A\rangle = -\operatorname{Tr}(A^2)$ up to a positive factor,
this can be written as
\[
  S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
  = c_N a^2 g^2 \sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname{Tr}(A_b^2)
    + O(a^4\|A\|^4)
\]
for a suitable constant $c_N>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The precise value of $c_N$ is not important for our qualitative
discussion. What matters is the sign: $S_{\mathrm{Haar}}$ contributes a
\emph{strictly positive} quadratic form in the $A_b$ near the identity.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Finite--cutoff mass term}

Combining the Wilson action with the Haar measure, the effective
Euclidean action in $A$--coordinates is, schematically,
\[
  S_{\mathrm{eff}}(A)
  = \beta\, S_W\big(\exp(iag A)\big) + S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Haar--induced mass at fixed cutoff]
\label{prop:Haar-mass}
For each fixed lattice spacing $a>0$ and couplings in a suitable regime
where $U_b$ remain close to the identity, there exists a constant
$m_H^2(a)>0$, of order $a^2 g^2$, such that the Hessian of
$S_{\mathrm{Haar}}$ satisfies
\[
  \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(A)
  \ge m_H^2(a)\, I
\]
as a quadratic form on the tangent space of $\mathfrak{g}^{N_b}$ near
$A=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating the expansion in Lemma \ref{lem:Haar-expansion} twice,
the leading quadratic term yields a constant positive multiple of the
identity on each $\mathfrak{g}$ factor; the $O(a^4\|A\|^4)$ remainder is
higher order and can be made arbitrarily small in operator norm by
restricting to a sufficiently small neighbourhood of $A=0$.
\end{proof}

The Wilson action Hessian adds a further contribution. For the small
field regime we are considering, it can be bounded in norm by a
multiple of $\beta$; with $a$ fixed one can arrange that the Haar
contribution dominates, yielding a uniform lower bound on the full
Hessian.

We emphasise that this analysis is \emph{local in configuration space}:
it concerns fields close to the identity. Extending convexity to the
global configuration space requires further work (gauge fixing, handling
reducibles, etc.) which we will not repeat in full detail here.

\section{Finite--cutoff convexity and Bakry--\'Emery curvature}

We now connect the Hessian bound to spectral properties through
Bakry--\'Emery $\Gamma$--calculus, interpreted on the finite lattice
configuration space.

\subsection{Langevin dynamics on configuration space}

Consider the overdamped Langevin dynamics on $\mathcal{C}_\Lambda$
associated with the effective action $S_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Formally,
\[
  dU_t = -\nabla_{\mathcal{C}_\Lambda} S_{\mathrm{eff}}(U_t)\,dt
          + \sqrt{2}\, dB_t,
\]
where $B_t$ is Brownian motion on $\mathcal{C}_\Lambda$ and
$\nabla_{\mathcal{C}_\Lambda}$ is the Riemannian gradient. The
infinitesimal generator $L$ acts on smooth functions $f$ by
\[
  L f
  = \Delta_{\mathcal{C}_\Lambda} f
    - \langle\nabla_{\mathcal{C}_\Lambda} S_{\mathrm{eff}},
            \nabla_{\mathcal{C}_\Lambda} f\rangle.
\]
The invariant measure for $L$ is
\[
  d\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}(U)
  = Z^{-1}\, e^{-S_{\mathrm{eff}}(U)}\, d\mathrm{vol}(U),
\]
where $d\mathrm{vol}$ is the Riemannian volume on $\mathcal{C}_\Lambda$.

Define the carré du champ operator
\[
  \Gamma(f) = \frac{1}{2}\big( L(f^2) - 2f\,Lf\big)
            = \|\nabla_{\mathcal{C}_\Lambda} f\|^2,
\]
and its iterated version
\[
  \Gamma_2(f)
  = \frac{1}{2}\big(L\Gamma(f) - 2\Gamma(f,Lf)\big).
\]

\subsection{Bakry--\'Emery curvature}

On a Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ with potential $S$, one has the
fundamental identity
\[
  \Gamma_2(f)
  = \|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
    + \langle (\mathrm{Ric} + \nabla^2 S)\nabla f, \nabla f\rangle,
\]
where $\mathrm{Ric}$ is the Ricci curvature tensor. The \emph{Bakry--\'Emery
curvature} of the triple $(M,g,S)$ is the lower bound $\rho$ such that
\[
  \mathrm{Ric} + \nabla^2 S \ge \rho g.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Bakry--\'Emery criterion on the lattice]
\label{thm:BE}
Let $(M,g,S)$ be a weighted Riemannian manifold with generator
$L = \Delta - \langle\nabla S,\nabla\cdot\rangle$ and invariant
probability measure $\mu\propto e^{-S}d\mathrm{vol}$. If there exists
$\rho>0$ such that
\[
  \mathrm{Ric} + \nabla^2 S \ge \rho g
\]
as quadratic forms, then for all smooth $f$,
\[
  \Gamma_2(f) \ge \rho\,\Gamma(f),
\]
and $\mu$ satisfies a Poincar\'e inequality
\[
  \mathrm{Var}_\mu(f) \le \frac{1}{\rho}\int \|\nabla f\|^2 d\mu,
\]
and a logarithmic Sobolev inequality
\[
  \mathrm{Ent}_\mu(f^2)
   \le \frac{2}{\rho}\int \|\nabla f\|^2 d\mu.
\]
In particular, the generator $L$ has a spectral gap at least $\rho$ on
$L^2(\mu)$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is standard and can be found in the Bakry--\'Emery literature;
we will not reproduce it here.

\subsection{Finite--cutoff convexity on $SU(N)^{N_b}$}

We now apply this to $\mathcal{C}_\Lambda = SU(N)^{N_b}$ with its
product metric and the effective action $S_{\mathrm{eff}}$. The Ricci
curvature of $SU(N)$ equipped with a bi--invariant metric is positive
and proportional to the metric; the product manifold inherits a
non--negative Ricci curvature with a strictly positive lower bound
$\mathrm{Ric}\ge \rho_0 g$ depending only on $N$ and the choice of
normalisation, not on the volume.

The Haar measure contribution $S_{\mathrm{Haar}}$ yields, in a small
field regime, a strictly positive Hessian as in Proposition
\ref{prop:Haar-mass}. The Wilson action Hessian may contribute positive
or negative directions, but for sufficiently small fields and
appropriate coupling its operator norm remains bounded by a constant
independent of the volume.

\begin{proposition}[Local Bakry--\'Emery curvature bound]
\label{prop:local-BE}
There exists a neighbourhood $\mathcal{U}$ of the identity in
$\mathcal{C}_\Lambda$ and a constant $\rho_*(a)>0$, depending on $a$ and
the coupling parameters but independent of the volume, such that
\[
  \mathrm{Ric} + \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{eff}} \ge \rho_*(a) g
\]
on $\mathcal{U}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
On $\mathcal{C}_\Lambda$,
$\mathrm{Ric} = \mathrm{Ric}_{SU(N)} \otimes I^{\otimes (N_b-1)}$ is
bounded below by a constant $\rho_0>0$ independent of the volume. Near
the identity, Proposition \ref{prop:Haar-mass} gives a strictly positive
contribution to the Hessian from $S_{\mathrm{Haar}}$ of order
$a^2 g^2$, while the Wilson Hessian is bounded in norm by a volume--independent
constant. Shrinking $\mathcal{U}$ if necessary and choosing parameters so
that the Haar contribution dominates any negative curvature from the
Wilson term yields the desired lower bound.
\end{proof}

Strictly speaking, Theorem \ref{thm:BE} is global: it requires a
curvature bound on the entire manifold. Extending the local estimate in
Proposition \ref{prop:local-BE} to a global bound involves controlling
the action outside the small--field regime and requires additional
arguments (e.g.\ a suitable gauge fixing, polynomial bounds on the
action, and a partition of unity). We will not attempt a complete
construction here and content ourselves with the observation that, at
finite cutoff, the Haar measure provides a robust source of convexity.

\section{Transfer matrix and strong--coupling mass gap}

We sketch how finite--cutoff convexity is related to a spectral gap of
the transfer matrix in the Hamiltonian formulation, at least in the
strong--coupling regime.

\subsection{Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian and transfer matrix}

On a spatial lattice $\Lambda_s\subset\mathbb{Z}^3$, the Kogut--Susskind
Hamiltonian for $SU(N)$ Yang--Mills can be written as
\[
  H = H_E + H_B,
\]
where $H_E$ is an ``electric'' term quadratic in left and right
invariant vector fields (electric field operators) and $H_B$ is a
``magnetic'' term built from plaquette operators. We will not require
the detailed expression; see Kogut--Susskind or standard treatments.

The transfer matrix $T$ over one time step is formally
\[
  T = e^{-a_t H}.
\]
Under suitable conditions, $T$ is a positive, self--adjoint, compact
operator on the physical Hilbert space, with a discrete spectrum
\[
  \lambda_0 > \lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \cdots \ge 0.
\]
The mass gap is
\[
  \Delta(a_t) = -\frac{1}{a_t} \log\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_0}.
\]

\subsection{Strong--coupling expansion and Wilson loop excitations}

In the strong--coupling regime (small plaquette coupling), a character
expansion and cluster expansion can be used to analyse the spectrum of
$T$; see e.g.\ work of Fr\"ohlich--Seiler, Osterwalder--Seiler and
others. One finds:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The vacuum eigenfunction $\Psi_0$ is strictly positive and
  unique (Perron--Frobenius).

  \item Excited states can be constructed by acting on $\Psi_0$ with
  Wilson loop operators $W_C$, giving wavefunctions
  $\Psi_C(U) = W_C(U)\Psi_0(U)$.

  \item The corresponding eigenvalues $\lambda_C$ are suppressed by
  factors depending on the length and shape of the loops, leading to a
  non--zero spectral gap.
\end{itemize}

At fixed lattice spacing, this yields a rigorous mass gap in the
strong--coupling phase. However, the strong--coupling expansion does not
directly control the continuum limit, which lies at weak bare coupling.

\begin{remark}
The strong--coupling mass gap can be viewed as arising from a sort of
``knotting energy'' associated with non--trivial Wilson loops: creating
a flux tube or string costs energy proportional to its length, encoded
in a string tension. This topological intuition plays a role in the
conjectural Geometric--Spectral Stability mechanism described later.
\end{remark}

\section{Viscous Hamilton--Jacobi flow and Hessian evolution}

We briefly recall the viscous Hamilton--Jacobi (vHJ) picture and the
associated Hessian evolution equation, which form the PDE backbone of
the conjectural hand--off mechanism.

\subsection{Densities, heat flow and vHJ equation}

Let $(M,g)$ be a complete Riemannian manifold with Laplace--Beltrami
operator $\Delta$. Let $\rho_t$ be a one--parameter family of smooth,
strictly positive probability densities on $M$ satisfying the heat
equation
\[
  \partial_t \rho_t = \Delta \rho_t.
\]
Assume that each $\rho_t$ admits a representation
\[
  \rho_t(x) = Z_t^{-1} e^{-S_t(x)}
\]
for some smooth ``effective action'' $S_t(x)$ and normalisation constant
$Z_t$.

\begin{lemma}[Viscous Hamilton--Jacobi equation]
\label{lem:vHJ}
After absorbing a time--dependent constant into $S_t$, the effective
action satisfies
\[
  \partial_t S_t = \Delta S_t - \|\nabla S_t\|^2.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating $\rho_t = Z_t^{-1} e^{-S_t}$ in time yields
\[
  \partial_t \rho_t
  = -Z_t^{-1} e^{-S_t}(\partial_t S_t + \partial_t\log Z_t).
\]
On the other hand,
\[
  \Delta \rho_t
  = Z_t^{-1} e^{-S_t}(-\Delta S_t + \|\nabla S_t\|^2).
\]
Equating $\partial_t\rho_t$ and $\Delta\rho_t$ gives
\[
  -\partial_t S_t - \partial_t\log Z_t
  = -\Delta S_t + \|\nabla S_t\|^2.
\]
Define $\tilde S_t = S_t + \log Z_t$. Then
\[
  \partial_t \tilde S_t = \Delta \tilde S_t - \|\nabla \tilde S_t\|^2.
\]
Dropping the tilde gives the claimed vHJ equation, up to an arbitrary
additive function of $t$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hessian evolution and Riccati structure}

Let $H_t = \nabla^2 S_t$ be the Hessian of $S_t$, viewed as a symmetric
$(1,1)$--tensor. Differentiating the vHJ equation twice in space and
using commutation relations for covariant derivatives yields a tensorial
evolution equation of the form
\[
  \partial_t H_t = \Delta_L H_t - 2H_t^2 + \mathcal{R}_t,
\]
where $\Delta_L$ is a Lichnerowicz Laplacian on symmetric $2$--tensors
and $\mathcal{R}_t$ is a curvature term involving the Riemann curvature
of $M$ and third derivatives of $S_t$.

The schematic structure
\[
  \partial_t H_t \approx \Delta H_t - 2H_t^2 + \text{(curvature)}
\]
suggests that $H_t$ might obey a \emph{matrix Riccati} inequality. In
particular, one expects the smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(H_t)$ to
satisfy, under suitable conditions, a differential inequality of the
form
\[
  \dot\lambda(t) \ge -2\lambda(t)^2 + \sigma(t),
\]
with $\sigma(t)$ depending on curvature bounds and derivatives of $S_t$.
If one could show that $\sigma(t)\ge\sigma_*>0$ for all $t$ and that
$\lambda(0)\ge 0$, then comparison with the scalar ODE
\[
  \dot\ell = -2\ell^2 + \sigma_*,\qquad \ell(0)=0
\]
would yield a positive lower bound on $\lambda(t)$ for $t>0$,
independent of the initial convexity.

We do not attempt to make this fully rigorous here; the goal is to
outline the structure that motivates the conjectural mechanism in the
next section.

\section{Geometric--Spectral Stability Mechanism (Conjectural)}

We now synthesise the finite--cutoff convexity, the vHJ/Riccati
structure, and the transfer matrix picture into a conjectural mechanism
for the stability of the mass gap under refinement of the lattice.

\subsection{Finite--cutoff priming by Haar mass}

Proposition \ref{prop:Haar-mass} shows that at each fixed lattice
spacing $a>0$, the Haar measure produces an explicit quadratic term in
the effective action, scaling as $a^2 g^2$, which enforces strict
convexity in a neighbourhood of the identity in configuration space. In
the Bakry--\'Emery framework this provides a positive contribution to
the curvature $\mathrm{Ric} + \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{eff}}$, leading to a
spectral gap for the associated Langevin dynamics and, through
reconstruction, for the transfer matrix in appropriate regimes.

Physically, one can think of this as a finite--cutoff ``geometric
stiffness'': the lattice geometry and Haar measure prevent wild
fluctuations of the gauge field and pin the theory in a gapped phase at
small lattice spacing.

\subsection{Conjectural Riccati hand--off}

The basic tension is that the Haar mass scale $m_H^2(a)$ vanishes as
$a^2$ in the continuum limit $a\to 0$. A purely perturbative reading of
this would suggest that the convexity provided by Haar disappears, and
with it the mass gap. The Geometric--Spectral Stability scenario posits
a different outcome:

\begin{quote}
The Haar mass term is the \emph{initiator} of convexity at finite
cutoff, not the \emph{sustainer}. Once a certain level of convexity is
present, the non--linear dynamics of the Hessian under an effective
flow and the intrinsic curvature of the configuration space can maintain
and even enhance this convexity, independently of the explicit $a^2$
prefactor.
\end{quote}

Formally, one imagines an effective action $S_t$ evolving under a vHJ
equation as in Lemma \ref{lem:vHJ}, with Hessian $H_t$ obeying a
Riccati--type evolution. On the configuration manifold
$\mathcal{C}_\Lambda = SU(N)^{N_b}$, the Ricci curvature is positive and
independent of the lattice spacing $a$. If the curvature term
$\mathcal{R}_t$ and the Laplacian $\Delta_L$ can be controlled so that
the smallest eigenvalue $\lambda(t)$ of $H_t$ satisfies
\[
  \dot\lambda(t) \ge -2\lambda(t)^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{geom}},
\]
with $\sigma_{\mathrm{geom}}>0$ depending only on the group geometry,
then the scalar ODE
\[
  \dot\ell = -2\ell^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{geom}},\qquad \ell(0)=0
\]
has solution
\[
  \ell(t) = \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{geom}}}{2}}\,
            \tanh\big(\sqrt{2\sigma_{\mathrm{geom}}}\,t\big)
\]
which increases from $0$ to
