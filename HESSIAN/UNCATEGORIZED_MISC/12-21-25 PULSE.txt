## Setup (minimal, checkable)

* Configuration space: product manifold (\mathcal X=\prod_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal X_i) (links, faces, or coarse blocks). Metric uses product norm from a fixed inner product on each (\mathcal X_i).
* Energy (S:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R) is (C^2) and (\mu\propto e^{-S},d\mathrm{vol}).
* **Finite‑range Hessian control:** there exists (R<\infty) and (\kappa>0) such that for every block (B\subset\Lambda) (chosen from a fixed R‑overlapping cover), the block‑restricted Hessian satisfies
  [
  \nabla^2_{B,B} S(x);\succeq;\kappa, I_{B}\quad\text{for all }x,
  ]
  and cross‑Hessians (\nabla^2_{B,B'} S) vanish if (\mathrm{dist}(B,B')>R) (or are bounded by a small (\varepsilon) you can track).
* Regularity: (S) has at most quadratic growth at infinity on each block coordinate (automatic on tori; otherwise impose a mild coercivity).

## What “uniform measure concentration” means here

* **Poincaré (spectral‑gap) inequality**
  [
  \mathrm{Var}_\mu(f);\le;\frac{1}{\rho}\int |\nabla f|^2,d\mu,
  ]
  with (\rho>0) **independent of (|\Lambda|)** and the arrangement of blocks.
* Optionally, **log‑Sobolev** with constant (\alpha>0) likewise uniform (gives sub‑Gaussian concentration and mixing).

## Mechanism (why this is true)

1. **Local convexity ⇒ local contraction.** For the Glauber/Langevin update that refreshes a block (B) conditioned on its (R)-neighborhood, the conditional law (\mu(\cdot\mid x_{B^c})\propto e^{-S_B(\cdot;x_{B^c})}) is (\kappa)-strongly log‑concave on (\mathcal X_B). That gives a blockwise Poincaré constant (\rho_B\ge\kappa) and (for LSI) (\alpha_B\gtrsim \kappa).
2. **Finite overlap ⇒ Dobrushin‑type propagation.** Because blocks only interact within range (R), the influence matrix of one block’s update on another decays off‑diagonal and has operator norm (<1) once you choose a partition with bounded overlap. This gives a **contractive block Gibbs sampler** in (L^2(\mu)) (or in Wasserstein for LSI).
3. **From sampler contraction to global inequalities.** A standard equivalence: an (L^2)‑contractive Markov kernel with spectral radius (<1) yields a global Poincaré constant; likewise, a uniform Wasserstein/entropy contraction yields an LSI. Constants depend on (\kappa) and on a finite combinatorial overlap factor (C_\mathrm{ov}(R,d)), **not on (|\Lambda|)**.

Here’s a clean, first‑pass map of how to handle coercivity/Poincaré‑type estimates in gauge theory without tripping over gauge redundancy.

# Two equivalent framings (pick one and stay in it)

**1) Intrinsic (quotient) picture.**
Work on the configuration space modulo gauge: (\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}). Norms, gradients, and inequalities are defined on gauge‑equivalence classes. A Poincaré (or LSI) inequality looks like
[
\mathrm{Var}*{\mu}(f);\le;\frac{1}{\rho}\int*{\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}} |\nabla f|^2,d\mu,
]
with (\nabla) the horizontal (gauge‑invariant) gradient. No gauge slice, no ghosts, no Faddeev–Popov—everything is intrinsic. Pro: fully geometric, constants are “physical”. Con: analytically heavier (you must construct the horizontal calculus and volume element on the quotient).

**2) Slice (gauge‑fixed) picture.**
Fix a gauge, usually **Coulomb** (\nabla\cdot A=0), and work on a concrete linear slice (\mathcal{S}={A:\nabla!\cdot A=0}\subset\mathcal{A}). Every (A) decomposes orthogonally (in (L^2)) as
[
A ;=; A_\perp ;+; \nabla \phi,\qquad \nabla!\cdot A_\perp=0,
]
so the **transverse projector** (P_\perp) is the (L^2)‑orthogonal projector onto divergence‑free fields. The measure picks up the **Faddeev–Popov (FP)** determinant: in non‑Abelian Coulomb gauge, the FP operator is
[
\mathcal{M}(A) ;=; -,\nabla\cdot D(A),
]
and (d\mu_{\text{g.f.}}\propto e^{-S(A_\perp)},\det\mathcal{M}(A_\perp),dA_\perp.
]
Pro: concrete Hilbert space estimates with standard elliptic tools and Fourier methods. Con: you must track (\det\mathcal{M}) (Gribov issues, positivity windows, etc.).

# Don’t mix constants across pictures

* If you prove a coercivity bound on the quotient
  [
  \langle v,,\mathcal{H}_{\text{phys}},v\rangle;\ge;\rho,|v|^2\quad (v\ \text{horizontal}),
  ]
  keep (\rho) tagged as **quotient‑level**.
* If you prove it on the Coulomb slice
  [
  \langle A_\perp,,\mathcal{H}*{\perp}(A*\perp),A_\perp\rangle;\ge;\rho_{\text{Coul}}|A_\perp|^2,
  ]
  then (\rho_{\text{Coul}}) is a **slice‑level** constant and any translation to the quotient must account for the FP weight (and its spectral control).

# Minimal, practical recipe (Coulomb slice)

1. **Project**: (A\mapsto A_\perp=P_\perp A) with (P_\perp=I-\nabla\Delta^{-1}\nabla!\cdot) (on the torus, zero‑mode handled separately).
2. **Quadratic control**: for actions with leading quadratic form ( \langle A_\perp, (m^2-\Delta)A_\perp\rangle ) (after gauge fixing), you get the base Poincaré gap (\rho_0 = m^2) (or (\sim c/L^2) if massless but on finite torus).
3. **Nonlinear terms**: treat via smallness/convexity on the slice; constants track through (P_\perp) (orthogonal) so no spurious gauge directions contaminate the gap.
4. **FP control**: establish a spectral window ( \mathcal{M}(A_\perp)\ge \lambda_{\mathrm{FP}}>0 ) on your admissible region; this keeps the weighted measure tame and lets slice‑level LSI/Poincaré lift to physical statements.

# Minimal, practical recipe (intrinsic)

1. **Horizontal calculus**: define the Levi form (or metric) on (\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}) via the (L^2) metric with vertical directions quotiented out; horizontal gradient = (P_\perp\nabla) intrinsically.
2. **Coercivity**: show the physical Hessian is positive on horizontals; this is the same inequality as Coulomb‑transverse but now no FP bookkeeping.
3. **Measure**: use the pushforward of (e^{-S(A)}) and verify log‑concavity/curvature conditions directly on the quotient.

# What to fix early in your stack

* **Choose one framework and stick to it per lemma.** If the lemma is Coulomb‑slice, write its assumptions and constants in slice language (with (\mathcal{M})). If intrinsic, never mention (\mathcal{M}) or gauges.
* **Name constants by layer**: e.g., (\rho_{\text{phys}}) (quotient), (\rho_{\text{Coul}}) (slice), (\lambda_{\mathrm{FP}}) (FP), (\rho_{\text{box}}) (finite‑volume box).
* **State projection once**: (P_\perp) is (L^2)‑orthogonal, so (|A|^2=|A_\perp|^2+|\nabla\phi|^2). This prevents “gauge energy” from faking coercivity.

# A compact lemma you can drop in
