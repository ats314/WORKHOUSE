# Haar state as a Jacobi spectral measure: a radial/spectral route to gauge-invariant gaps (toy models)

## Abstract

A recurring bottleneck in mass-gap programs is that the *gauge-invariant* sector is hard to analyze directly.
One concrete way to reduce complexity is:

1. Identify a commutative “radial” $C^\ast$-subalgebra generated by a self-adjoint observable $X$.
2. Understand the Haar state restricted to that subalgebra as an explicit probability measure $dm_X$.
3. Represent the (reduced) generator/transfer operator as a **Jacobi operator** (tridiagonal matrix) in an orthogonal-polynomial basis for $L^2(dm_X)$.
4. Read off spectral information (and possible gaps) from Jacobi coefficients and classical inequalities.

The curated Haar-measure material includes a particularly sharp instance of this plan:
for $SU_q(2)$, the Haar state restricted to the commutative subalgebra generated by
\[
X=\frac{\alpha+\alpha^\ast}{2}
\]
is the *classical arcsine law* on $[-1,1]$, and more general “spherical” elements yield the Askey--Wilson measure.

This note explains why that is exciting for lattice gauge theory:
it hands you an exactly solvable 1D spectral model that can be used as a testbed for curvature/BE and
for spectral-gap heuristics (and potentially as a block in a rigorous comparison argument).

---

## 1. The paradigm: Jacobi matrices and orthogonal polynomials

Let $J$ be a Jacobi operator on $\ell^2(\mathbb Z_+)$,
\[
J e_n = a_{n+1}e_{n+1} + b_n e_n + a_n e_{n-1},\qquad a_n>0,\ b_n\in\mathbb R,
\tag{1.1}
\]
with $e_{-1}=0$.
Under standard determinacy/self-adjointness hypotheses, the spectral theorem identifies $J$ with multiplication
by $x$ on $L^2(dm)$ for a unique probability measure $dm$ and orthonormal polynomials $\{p_n\}$ satisfying the
three-term recurrence
\[
x p_n(x)=a_{n+1}p_{n+1}(x)+b_n p_n(x)+a_n p_{n-1}(x).
\tag{1.2}
\]

**Why a physicist should care:** many reduced (radial, class-function) operators in gauge theory become either
second-order differential operators in one variable or discrete analogues (difference/Jacobi operators).
If you can identify $dm$, the spectral problem becomes a 1D classical analysis problem.

---

## 2. Haar state on $SU_q(2)$ and the arcsine law

For the compact quantum group $SU_q(2)$ ($0<q<1$), let $h$ be its Haar state, and let $\alpha,\gamma$ be standard generators.
A key curated result states:

> For the self-adjoint element $X=(\alpha+\alpha^\ast)/2$, the restriction of the Haar state to $C^\ast(X)$ is
> the classical arcsine measure on $[-1,1]$:
> \[
> h(p(X))=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-1}^1 p(x)\,\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}.
> \tag{2.1}
> \]

Two “surprising” features:

1. The measure is **classical** (not $q$-dependent).
2. The density has integrable endpoint singularities $\sim (1-x^2)^{-1/2}$, which often control low-energy spectral behavior.

From the same source, more general self-adjoint “spherical” elements $\rho_{\tau,\sigma}$ have Haar laws given by
Askey--Wilson measures. Those measures are precisely the orthogonality measures for Askey--Wilson polynomials,
whose recurrence coefficients are explicit.

---

## 3. Why this is a useful toy model for gauge-invariant gaps

Consider any gauge model where the gauge-invariant sector is (or contains) a block generated by a single
self-adjoint observable $X$.
Then:

- The Hilbert space of that sector is $L^2(dm_X)$.
- Any symmetric operator that preserves polynomials in $X$ is naturally represented in the $\{p_n\}$ basis
as a Jacobi (or banded) matrix.

If, in addition, the model’s reduced dynamics resembles a diffusion/heat semigroup, then classical results give:

- explicit eigenfunctions (orthogonal polynomials),
- explicit spectral measures,
- explicit lower bounds on the first nonzero eigenvalue (spectral gap) in many cases.

This dovetails with Bakry--Émery:

- curvature bounds yield spectral gaps,
- Jacobi models can *compute* or *sharpen* those gaps,
- and can provide exactly solvable benchmarks for conjectured inequalities.

## 4. A concrete research direction inside the project

A plausible “bridge lemma” to pursue:

1. Fix a finite lattice and gauge-fix enough to reduce a single block (e.g. a plaquette or a small cube) to a
commutative algebra generated by a radial variable $X$.
2. Identify the induced measure on $X$ under the Gibbs weight and compare it (in a controlled way) to a reference Haar law $dm_X$.
3. Show that the block generator (conditional dynamics) is a Jacobi operator with explicitly controlled coefficients $a_n,b_n$.
4. Prove a **uniform** lower bound on the block spectral gap (or log-Sobolev constant), then bootstrap via block dynamics to
the full lattice.

If successful, this would create a rare thing in nonabelian gauge theory: an explicit, orthogonal-polynomial control of the
gauge-invariant spectrum that can be blended with curvature-based estimates.

---

## 5. Caution and scope

This is a *toy-model and method* note:

- The specific observable $X=(\alpha+\alpha^\ast)/2$ in $SU_q(2)$ is not literally the Wilson loop trace in $SU(2)$,
but it is a rigorously analyzable self-adjoint generator whose Haar law is explicit.
- The value is methodological: it gives you an exactly solvable playground where the “Haar $\to$ Jacobi” pipeline is explicit.

The potential payoff is high: if a comparable radialization can be done for lattice Yang–Mills blocks,
one gets a spectral microscope powerful enough to complement the curvature engine.
