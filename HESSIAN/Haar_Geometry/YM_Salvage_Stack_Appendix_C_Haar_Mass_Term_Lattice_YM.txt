YM MASS GAP PROJECT – TECHNICAL APPENDIX
Appendix C – Haar Measure Induced Mass Term in Lattice Yang–Mills
=================================================================

C.0. Purpose
------------

This appendix derives, in detail, the effective "mass term" that arises
purely from the Haar measure on the group manifold when one writes lattice
Yang–Mills variables in exponential coordinates.  The key result is that the
Haar measure contributes an effective potential

    S_Haar(A) = − log J(A),

whose second-order expansion in the gauge potential A has a strictly positive
quadratic form, i.e., a positive mass term independent of the bare action.

This is an exact finite-dimensional, group-theoretic statement.

We will work with G = SU(N) for definiteness, but the calculations adapt to
any compact Lie group with similar properties.


C.1. Lattice Configuration Space and Exponential Coordinates
------------------------------------------------------------

On a given lattice, denote by 𝔅 the set of oriented bonds (links).  A lattice
configuration is a collection of group elements

    U = (U_b)_{b∈𝔅} ∈ G^{𝔅}.

The product Haar measure is

    dμ_0(U) = ∏_{b∈𝔅} dHaar_G(U_b).

To express U_b in terms of a "gauge potential" A_b taking values in the Lie
algebra 𝔤 = su(N), we use the exponential map.  Let a be the lattice spacing,
and g the gauge coupling.  For small fields A_b, write

    U_b = exp(i a g A_b),

where A_b ∈ su(N) is anti-Hermitian (or equivalently, i A_b is Hermitian).


C.2. Jacobian of the Exponential Map
------------------------------------

We need the Jacobian determinant of the map

    A_b  ↦  U_b = exp(i a g A_b)

from 𝔤 to G.  In terms of left-invariant or right-invariant measures, the
Haar measure is invariant under translation but not under the nonlinear change
of coordinates.  The general formula for the Jacobian density J(A) is known
from Lie group geometry:

    dHaar_G(U) = J(A) dA,     U = exp(A),

with

    J(A) = det ( (sinh(ad_A/2)) / (ad_A/2) ),

where ad_A : 𝔤 → 𝔤 is the adjoint map ad_A(X) = [A,X].  This formula arises
from the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff structure and the relation between the
left-invariant volume form and the Lebesgue measure in a linear coordinate
system on 𝔤.


C.2.1. Spectral Representation of ad_A
--------------------------------------

The operator ad_A is skew-adjoint with respect to the Killing form on 𝔤.
Let {λ_j} be the eigenvalues of ad_A (complex numbers, purely imaginary for
su(N) under the standard inner product).  Then the Jacobian can be expressed
as the product over eigenvalues:

    J(A) = ∏_{j} (sinh(λ_j/2) / (λ_j/2)).

Since λ_j = 0 occurs with multiplicity equal to the dimension of the central
subalgebra (one for SU(N)), the corresponding factor should be interpreted as
taking the limit (sinh x)/x → 1 as x → 0, so no singularity arises.

We are interested in the small-A behavior, i.e., in a Taylor expansion of
−log J(A) up to quadratic order in A.


C.3. Expansion of −log J(A)
---------------------------

Let T = ad_A.  The function

    f(T) = (sinh(T/2)) / (T/2)

is an analytic function of T.  For a scalar variable x, the power series is

    sinh(x/2) = (x/2) + (x^3/2^3 · 3!) + (x^5/2^5 · 5!) + ...

Hence

    sinh(x/2) / (x/2)
      = 1 + x^2 / (2^2 · 3!) + x^4 / (2^4 · 5!) + ...

      = 1 + x^2/24 + O(x^4).

Thus for the operator T = ad_A, the functional calculus gives

    f(T) = I + (T^2 / 24) + O(||T||^4).

Therefore,

    J(A) = det f(T)
         = det(I + T^2 / 24 + O(||T||^4)).

Taking the logarithm:

    log J(A) = Tr log(I + T^2/24 + O(T^4)).

Using log(I + X) = X − X^2/2 + ..., we get to second order in T:

    log J(A) = Tr(T^2 / 24) + O(||T||^4).

The linear term in T vanishes since f(T) is an even function and Tr(T) = 0
for ad_A in a semisimple Lie algebra.

Thus

    S_Haar(A) := − log J(A) = −(1/24) Tr(T^2) + O(||T||^4).


C.3.1. Expressing Tr(T^2) in Terms of A
---------------------------------------

Recall T = ad_A.  Acting on a basis {X_k} of 𝔤, we have

    T(X_k) = [A, X_k].

Then

    Tr(T^2) = ∑_k ⟨ T^2 X_k, X_k ⟩
            = ∑_k ⟨ [A, [A, X_k]], X_k ⟩,

where ⟨·,·⟩ is the Killing form or a suitably normalized invariant inner
product on 𝔤.  Using invariance of the inner product:

    ⟨ [A, Y], Z ⟩ = ⟨ Y, [A, Z] ⟩,

we find

    ⟨ [A, [A, X_k]], X_k ⟩
      = −⟨ [A, X_k], [A, X_k] ⟩.

Therefore,

    Tr(T^2) = − ∑_k || [A, X_k] ||^2.

In particular, for SU(N), Tr(T^2) is negative, so −(1/24) Tr(T^2) is positive.
One may also recast Tr(T^2) in terms of Casimir invariants.  Specifically,
for SU(N), we know that

    Tr( (ad_A)^2 ) = c_ad Tr(A^2),

where Tr(A^2) is the standard Hilbert–Schmidt norm on 𝔤 and c_ad is a
group-dependent positive constant equal to twice the dual Coxeter number:

    c_ad = 2N for SU(N), up to normalization conventions.

With a conventional normalization of the inner product on 𝔤, one obtains

    Tr( (ad_A)^2 ) = − 2 N Tr(A^2),

so that

    −(1/24) Tr(T^2) = (N / 12) Tr(A^2).

The precise coefficient depends on the normalization of Tr on 𝔤, but what
matters structurally is that it is strictly positive.


C.4. Effective Quadratic Term and Mass
--------------------------------------

Combining the above, for small A we obtain

    S_Haar(A) = c_0 Tr(A^2) + O(||A||^4),

with c_0 > 0 depending only on the group and normalization conventions.  On
the lattice, summing over links b ∈ 𝔅:

    S_Haar(U)
      = − ∑_{b∈𝔅} log J(A_b)
      = ∑_{b∈𝔅} (c_0 Tr(A_b^2) + O(||A_b||^4)).

Thus the total Haar-induced part of the effective action (after change of
variables to A_b) has a strictly positive quadratic part in A:

    S_Haar^{(2)}(A) = c_0 ∑_{b∈𝔅} Tr(A_b^2).

In terms of the Hessian at the origin, we find

    ∇^2 S_Haar(0) = 2 c_0 I,

in the natural basis of the link-space of su(N)-valued fields.  Equivalently,
the measure

    dμ_0(U) = exp(−S_Haar(A)) dA

has a Gaussian-like "mass term" proportional to Tr(A^2).


C.5. Combination with Wilson Action
-----------------------------------

In a standard lattice gauge theory, one typically starts with the Wilson
action in terms of plaquette loops:

    S_Wilson(U) = β ∑_{plaquettes p} (1 − (1/N) Re Tr U_p),

where U_p is the ordered product of link variables around p.  Expanding near
the identity, U_b = exp(i a g A_b), and using a continuum limit, this
produces the usual Yang–Mills action ∝ ∫ Tr(F^2).  However, at the lattice
level, the change of variables to A_b also transforms the Wilson action in a
nonlinear way and adds the Jacobian contribution S_Haar(A).

Thus, the full effective action in A-coordinates is schematically

    S_eff(A) = S_Wilson(A) + S_Haar(A).

To quadratic order,
    S_eff(A) = S_Wilson^{(2)}(A) + S_Haar^{(2)}(A) + higher-order terms.

The Wilson quadratic term, in standard normalization, produces something like

    S_Wilson^{(2)}(A) ~ β a^4 ∑_x Tr( (∇_μ A_ν − ∇_ν A_μ)^2 ),

which in momentum space contributes a kinetic term ∝ |p|^2.  The Haar term
S_Haar^{(2)}(A) adds a strictly positive *mass-like* term c_0 ∑ Tr(A_b^2).

At fixed lattice spacing a and coupling g, this implies that the Hessian of
S_eff at A = 0 has strictly positive spectrum, bounded below by c_0 > 0, even
if the Wilson term alone would give only nonnegative eigenvalues with a
zero-mode at p = 0.


C.6. Interpretation as "Mass from Geometry"
-------------------------------------------

The key point is that the Haar measure contribution S_Haar(A) is not an
arbitrary added mass term; it is forced by the geometry of the compact gauge
group and the choice of exponential coordinates on the lattice configuration
space.  Its Hessian is positive definite and independent of the Wilson action
parameters.

This means that, at finite lattice spacing, the gauge fields experience an
effective mass proportional to c_0 that originates from the measure, not from
explicit mass terms in the action.  This mass term can be viewed as a
"measure-induced gap" in the quadratic approximation at the origin.


C.7. Summary
------------

In this appendix we have:

  • Written the lattice gauge variables U_b ∈ SU(N) in exponential
    coordinates U_b = exp(i a g A_b), with A_b in the Lie algebra.

  • Recalled the Jacobian formula for the exponential map in a compact Lie
    group:

        J(A) = det( sinh(ad_A/2) / (ad_A/2) ).

  • Expanded J(A) for small A using the functional calculus for T = ad_A,
    obtaining

        − log J(A) = −(1/24) Tr( (ad_A)^2 ) + O(||A||^4)
                    = c_0 Tr(A^2) + O(||A||^4),

    with c_0 > 0 determined by the group and normalization.

  • Summed over links to obtain a lattice effective action contribution

        S_Haar(A) = c_0 ∑_{b} Tr(A_b^2) + O(∑ ||A_b||^4),

    producing a strictly positive quadratic term (a genuine mass term) in the
    gauge potential variables.

This provides a precise, finite-dimensional statement that the Haar measure
geometry generates a local mass term in lattice Yang–Mills when working in
exponential coordinates.
