I · What this session found
Three things, in descending order of how much they change the route.

Finding 1 — the decisive unknown is smaller than stated

The dossier's open theorem demands that an RG trajectory carry three uniformities to the continuum: a nonzero mass in physical units, an isolation gap, and a nonzero source residue. Using charge conjugation as a superselection label, and one elementary inequality, those collapse. The residue is not independent of the gap. The mass lower bound and the isolation are both consequences of the Yang–Mills mass gap. What is left that is genuinely new is a single statement about a single operator:

the normalized lattice correlator of a fixed-physical-radius carrier source,
at one fixed physical Euclidean separation, does not tend to zero as a → 0.
No renormalization constant appears in it — it is a ratio. No trajectory appears in it. It is not a theorem about the RG at all.

Finding 2 — the corpus already contains a source-aware RG program

corpus-import/programs/pmbsf/ — 30 files, 14 MB — is a Bałaban/Dimock constructive gauge-RG attack whose Open Theorem C is titled "source-weighted Bałaban expansion" and whose referee ledger states, verbatim: "Bałaban/Dimock provide the unmarked constructive gauge-RG backbone. The source-weighted marked upgrade remains an open analytic task." Its literature map is blunter still: "I found no primary source that already proves a Bałaban/Dimock RG with activities multiplied by arbitrary marked/source weights." That is the route's decisive unknown, already named and already reduced to four subtasks. The repository's ledger tracks none of it — a repo-wide grep for PMBSF, Lemma Q, Bałaban or Dimock across src/, lean/, tests/, ledger/, CERTIFIED.md and FRONTIER.md returns zero machine checks.

Three qualifications the first reading missed, all of which matter. PMBSF's headline Theorem FNG is for Q₈, a finite group, and its own document says it does not extend to SU(2), naming three obstructions (spin-wave dominance, no analogue of its covariance hypothesis, and a β-threshold mismatch of six decades). PMBSF explicitly places "reconstruction of the Hilbert space; identification of a physical mass gap; continuum scaling limit; uniformity of constants" outside its scope (§14.11) — it stops at finite-lattice projected coercivity. And the two programs are firewalled by design: the one-plaquette master records PMBSF as "Program B", "logically independent of every theorem in the manuscript", and its §C.7 forbids citing PMBSF at all. What transfers is mathematics, not evidence.

II · The finite-spacing half, as twelve lemmas
Stated so each rung carries its own evidence tier rather than its rhetorical confidence. Rungs L1–L6 are the electric-shell theorem; L7–L11 are the volume-uniform patch; L12 is what actually remains at fixed spacing.

Three arithmetic facts underneath everything were re-derived independently in this session, in exact rationals, and agree: the SU(3) Casimir numerators below 18 are exactly {0, 4, 9, 10, 16}; the fundamental plaquette sits at E_F = 4·(2/3) = 8/3; and the additive semigroup of one-link numerators takes the values 14, 16, 17 consecutively around 16 — 15 is not attainable, which is what makes the separation ratio come out at 1/33 rather than 1/15.

L1
Casimir ladder and shell energies
T1 · checkable
e(p,q) = (p²+q²+pq+3p+3q)/6. Smallest nonzero value 2/3, attained only by 3 and 3̄; every other nontrivial irrep costs ≥ 3/2. Hence E_F = 8/3, the 1×2 rectangle costs 6·(2/3) = 4, and the candidate gap is 4/3. Pure rational arithmetic — this is Lean-reachable.

L2
Support classification below energy 4
T1 · enumerable
Energy < 4 forces ≤ 5 occupied edges. Gauss's law forbids a degree-one vertex (Inv(V_λ) is trivial for nontrivial λ), so every component has minimum degree ≥ 2. The dossier's "theta graph needs ≥ 6 edges" argument is loose; the MASTER_PROOF_STATE's exhaustive enumeration for L = 3…7 is the honest version and returns zero branched supports at every L.

L3
Schur propagation around a cycle
T1
At a degree-two vertex Inv(V_λ ⊗ V_μ) ≅ Hom(V_λ*, V_μ), so one irrep propagates consistently. A non-fundamental irrep costs ≥ 3/2 per edge, and the shortest winding cycle then costs ≥ 9/2 > 4. Every sub-4 state is a fundamental simple cycle of length ≤ 5.

L4
Triality kills winding — unconditionally for L ≥ 6
T1
Edge trialities form a ℤ₃-valued 1-cycle whose class is the one-form center charge; a ≤5-edge path cannot achieve displacement 3L ≥ 9. The sharper form matters: a winding cycle needs ≥ L edges, so for L ≥ 6 the classification holds with no center-charge restriction at all. The ℤ₃ block buys uniformity only at L = 3,4,5 — and the chain wants L → ∞. The block is not load-bearing where the route needs it.

L5
Bipartite ⇒ the cycle is a plaquette
T1
The lifted cycle closes in ℤ³, which is bipartite, so it is even; length ≤ 5 and even and ≥ 4 means exactly 4, and every 4-cycle in the cubic lattice bounds an elementary plaquette. Its charge-odd combination is |p,−⟩ = (|p,3⟩ − |p,3̄⟩)/√2.

L6
Sharpness
T1
The 1×2 rectangle is neutral, charge-odd, outside the shell, and sits exactly at 4. So the threshold 4 and the gap 4/3 are attained, not merely bounded.

L7
Yarotsky hypotheses and the cell regrouping
T3 · imported
Yarotsky's Theorem 1 admits infinite-dimensional site spaces and unbounded non-negative onsite terms with gap ≥ 1 — the compass artifact in corpus-import/literature/ asserts the opposite ("stated for finite-dimensional sites") and dropped the route on that basis. Group the three outgoing links per vertex, rescale by 3/2, and ε(u) ≤ 27u. Still missing: the periodic-torus adaptation, which the paper notes but does not prove.

L8
The separation ratio θ_F = 1/33
T1 · re-derived here
In units of 1/6 the attainable totals around 16 are 14 and 17. So θ_F = min(2/30, 1/33) = 1/33, attained on the 17 side. Verified independently in this session over the full additive semigroup.

L9
Riesz contour and resolvent bound
T1 · re-derived here
With ρ = (1−λ)/12: the distance to the 16-patch is (1−λ)/12 − 32λ/12 and to the 17-patch is 1/6 − 17λ/6 − (1−λ)/12; both equal (1−33λ)/12, so ‖(H̃ − z)⁻¹‖ ≤ 12/(1−33λ) and the condition is exactly λ < 1/33. The 14-side distance is (3−27λ)/12, strictly larger. Arithmetic confirmed.

L10
Symmetry commutation ⇒ block restriction
T1
Gauge, charge conjugation, translations and the ℤ₃ one-form charge all commute with H(u) exactly (each V_i is a product of central single-link multiplications), so the Riesz projection restricts and its rank splits sector by sector.

L11
Rank constancy along the segment
missing paragraph
Rank 3L³ follows from L7–L10 plus one unwritten step: rank-constancy in u along t ↦ tu, together with mult_block(8/3) = 3L³ at u = 0. This is a completion, not an error — but it is not written anywhere.

L12
The domain is at the wrong end of the coupling axis
structural
u = β_N/(2N) = β₃/6 = 1/g_H⁴, so the continuum is g_H → 0, i.e. u → ∞. The necessary condition for the patch is u < 8/99 ≈ 0.081; a sufficient estimate gives u < 1/(891 c₂) ≈ 10⁻³. The near-Γ exclusion radius (ADR 0003) is non-vacuous only for u < 0.133. Every fixed-spacing statement in the program lives at small u; the continuum is at infinite u. Nothing connects them, and nothing in the finite-spacing half can.

What this half actually delivers

A complete, sharp classification of the charge-odd spectrum below 4 at zero coupling, and a conditional volume-uniform isolation of the whole 8/3 patch in a strong-coupling domain. That is real. It is also, by L12, disjoint from the continuum question rather than a step toward it. The finite-spacing half tells you which object to follow; it cannot follow it.

III · Five reductions
Each of these is elementary. That is the point: they are elementary statements that remove non-elementary hypotheses from the open theorem. Every one was checked computationally in this session; the checks are listed with each.

R1 · A finite mass follows from one correlator ratio
Let C(t) = ⟨ψ, e^{−tH}ψ⟩ = ∫ e^{−tE} dρ(E) with ρ ≥ 0 finite and M = inf supp ρ. Then C(t) ≤ C(0)e^{−Mt}, so

M  ≤  (1/t₁) · log( C(0) / C(t₁) )     for every t₁ > 0.
Because log C is convex (Hölder), the right side is non-increasing in t₁ and tends to M: larger separations give sharper bounds, and any single separation already gives a valid one. Verified on 20,000 random positive spectral measures in continuous time and 20,000 in discrete transfer-matrix time — zero violations of either monotonicity or the bound.

The consequence is the whole reduction. The ratio C(t₁)/C(0) is invariant under multiplicative renormalization of the source: Z cancels. So a uniform-in-a upper bound on the carrier mass in physical units follows from a uniform lower bound on a ratio of bare lattice numbers at one fixed physical separation. No renormalized operator, no continuum field, no trajectory.

This derives G18's own external sharpening

If the source's overlap degrades like a^p — Schierholz 1988 measured a⁵ for a local dimension-4 operator, and the corpus records it as G18's external_sharpening — then the bound reads M ≤ (p/t₁)·log(1/a) and diverges logarithmically. It never becomes false; it becomes vacuous. A source at fixed physical radius is the only kind that keeps it finite. That is exactly the gap entry's conclusion — "the smeared basis is structural, not a convenience" — now with a one-line derivation instead of a citation.

R2 · The residue is not an independent unknown
With separated-time normalized sources ψ(t₀) = e^{−t₀H/2}O†Ω, let w_I be the weight at the bottom of the source's spectral support, Δ the gap above it within that support, and r₀ = W_rest/w_I = 1/z(0) − 1 the bare weight ratio. Then

z(t₀)  ≥  1 / ( 1 + r₀ · e^{−t₀Δ} ).
Verified on 20,000 random configurations — zero violations. The matrix form is free: T₁ is absolutely irreducible with real character, so its real commutant is ℝ and ν(B) = c(B)·I₃ for every Borel set. The z_G I₃ structure the dossier derives from covariance is bookkeeping, not evidence.

So the residue hypothesis ν(I) ⪰ z_* I₃ is not a spectral statement. Given the gap, it is a statement about the bare source alone: r₀(a) bounded. And if one is willing to let t₀ grow like (p/Δ)·log(1/a), a merely polynomial degradation suffices. The exponent p enters only as a multiplicative constant on a logarithm — getting it wrong by a factor of three changes t₀ by a factor of three and changes nothing else.

The corpus's own Monte Carlo (§10.2, β = 5.8941, L = 14, N_t = 16, 2000 configurations) is the only data on r₀ anywhere in the program, and it will not carry the weight one would like:

source	z(0)	r₀ = 1/z(0) − 1	what it supports
raw plaquette (APE level 0, 1×1)	0.00724 ± 0.01647	not bounded	The measurement is 0.44σ from zero; the 2σ interval is [−2.57%, +4.02%]. It does not establish that the bare source has any overlap, or that the overlap is positive. r₀ has no finite upper bound at 2σ.
variational (GEVP, 7 of 12 retained)	0.7997	0.25	No error bar recorded, and §10.2 itself warns the cosh amplitude "is not automatically a normalized overlap probability."
Two corrections to how this number is carried. The ledger and FRONTIER.md both say the bare operator carries "<4% (2σ)"; the arithmetic is 0.007236 + 2(0.016469) = 4.0175%, which is not < 4%. And the engine gate named "raw plaquette spectral fraction" passes iff −2σ ≤ frac ≤ 1 + 2σ — a range sanity check that would pass just as happily at 0.9. Nothing in the program tests the smallness that G18's statement rests on.

The APE basis is also smeared at fixed lattice levels (0, 4, 12, 24), not at fixed physical radius, so the 0.7997 is not a measurement of the quantity R1 and R2 need — its smearing radius shrinks with a, which is precisely the Schierholz failure mode. The honest position: the a-dependence of r₀ for a fixed-physical-radius source is unmeasured, and the existing run cannot be reinterpreted to supply it. That is a run to design, not a number to look up.

R3 · Charge conjugation supplies the isolation
C commutes with H at every coupling, and the vacuum is C-even. So define

M(a)  :=  inf spec( H_a | C-odd, P = 0 )  −  E₀.
This is a sector bottom — a variational quantity — not an interior isolated eigenvalue. Four things follow at once:

Lower bound. M(a) ≥ the full mass gap, so any positive lower bound in physical units is implied by (and strictly weaker than) the Clay statement.
No decay channel. C is multiplicative on multiparticle states, so an n-glueball state has C = ∏C_i; a C-odd state needs at least one C-odd constituent. The lightest C-odd threshold is therefore M + m(0^{++}), strictly above M. Stability is automatic.
Isolation. The C-odd continuum starts at M + m(0^{++}), so the mass shell is isolated with gap m(0^{++}) — given no accumulation of discrete C-odd masses at M, which is a mild hypothesis, not a theorem.
No vacuum subtraction. A C-odd source has ⟨O⟩ = 0 identically. The disconnected piece never appears.
And the numbers are not marginal. From AT_2020_SU3 Table 17, as pinned in literature/index.yaml: m(1^{+−})/√σ = 6.065(40), m(0^{++})/√σ = 3.405(21). So the two-glueball threshold is 6.810 — the 1^{+−} sits below it — and the C-odd isolation gap is 3.405√σ ≈ 1.65 GeV, about 56% of the mass itself.

An unregistered agreement

The corpus records its own Monte Carlo as aM(T₁^{+−}) = 1.6897(1206) and a√σ = 0.26283(232) (§10.2), and separately records AT_2020's 6.065(40) as "the current best SU(3) target for the spectral bridge". It never forms the ratio. Computed here: M/√σ = 6.429 ± 0.462 against 6.065 ± 0.040 — agreement at 0.78σ. That is the program's own carrier source, measured in its own run, landing on the modern continuum spectrum. It is a one-line T2 check that does not exist.

R4 · The Γ charge is a fragile hedgehog — and the corpus already knew
This one I got wrong, and the way it went wrong is worth more than the result.

Using the governing document's own conventions (§2.5, §3.2): d_i = e^{ik_i} − 1, ψ(k) = (d̄₃, −d̄₂, d̄₁), S(k) + 4I = B(k)B(k)†. Since d_i = 2i·sin(k_i/2)·e^{ik_i/2}, the gauge V(k) = diag(e^{ik₃/2}, e^{ik₂/2}, e^{ik₁/2}) makes the carrier real:

V(k)ψ(k) = −2i·( s₃, −s₂, s₁ ),      s_i = sin(k_i/2).
So the carrier line is the complexification of a real line and its projective class lies in ℝP² ⊂ ℂP². I computed the first Chern class by Fukui–Hatsugai–Suzuki on a 200×200 mesh and it vanishes on every generator of H₂(T³ ∖ {Γ}) = ℤ³: spheres about Γ at radii 0.02 / 0.20 / 1.00 / 2.00 / 3.00, and the coordinate 2-tori k₁ = π, k₂ = π, k₃ = π — all 0.000000. (Checked first that B†ψ = 0, Sψ = −4ψ, ‖ψ‖² = q_a to residual 0 and 3.6×10⁻¹⁵ over 2,000 random momenta.)

I concluded the Γ singularity was metric rather than topological. That conclusion is wrong, and the corpus says so in a document nothing cites:

corpus-import/records/audits/FLATBAND_PAPER_EXPANSION_AUDIT_2026-07-25.md

"Do not substitute a Chern number: the singular flat line is not an isolated complex band, and its ordinary Chern number is not the relevant invariant." The audit names the right one — a π₂ charge from the flattened chiral matrix on a small S² — and records its value: "Since det R = +1, the linearized texture has degree +1."

A second document reaches the same place independently. In what it calls the centered gauge — M(k) = 2·[[s₂,−s₁,0],[s₃,0,−s₁],[0,s₃,−s₂]], real — it finds ker M = span{s} and concludes: "On a sphere around Γ, ŝ = s/|s| gives a unit embedded map S² → ℝP². Since adding one trivial real orbital changes the target to ℝP³ with π₂(ℝP³) = 0, the correct topology is a fragile unit Hodge hedgehog, not stable BDI protection."

Both statements are true and they are not in conflict: the line bundle is trivial on T³∖{Γ} (my c₁ = 0), while the direction field ŝ : S² → ℝP² has π₂ degree +1, and that degree is what obstructs continuous extension to Γ. What my computation does establish is the negative half: there is no Chern/monopole charge, so that class of obstruction is closed off. What it does not establish is what I said it did.

The corpus's own verdict is sharper than either: the charge is fragile. One extra trivial real orbital changes the target from ℝP² to ℝP³, where π₂ = 0, and it dies. So the Γ texture is not symmetry-protected, and any argument that leans on it as protection is leaning on nothing.

Two things follow for method, both uncomfortable. My "centered gauge" was the corpus's, rederived under a different name — the exact failure mode AGENTS.md names as measured ("re-deriving something that already exists under different notation"), committed while holding the file that warns against it. And the audit that holds the right invariant is cited by nothing in src/, ledger/, tests/, or any root document, so no search of the verification layer would have surfaced it. Both invariants — c₁ = 0 and the π₂ degree — are computed nowhere in the repository.

R5 · The spin split is exactly 3/5 : 2/5
The corpus gives the operator identity itself (§10.1): Im Tr e^{iX} = −P₃/6 + P₅/120 − …, so the leading continuum term of the carrier source is Tr(F_jk)³ = d^{abc}B_n^a B_n^b B_n^c — the diagonal component S_nnn of a symmetric rank-3 spatial tensor. That is dimension six, not four, and it is not an axial vector. Working out the consequences exactly:

statement	exact result
rep carried by the diagonal triple {S₁₁₁, S₂₂₂, S₃₃₃} under O	T₁ (character identical to the vector rep)
D¹ ↓ O	T₁
D³ ↓ O	A₂ ⊕ T₁ ⊕ T₂
T₁ multiplicity in D^J ↓ O, J = 0…12	0,1,0,1,1,2,1,2,2,3,2,3,3
J = 1 fraction of ‖S_iii‖²	3/5 = 0.600000
J = 3 fraction of ‖S_iii‖²	2/5 = 0.400000
Gram of the J=1 projections of the three sources	(3/5)·I₃ — full rank
The MASTER_PROOF_STATE reads this as "the carrier couples to J = 3 at leading order", which is true and is the correct correction to the dossier's axial-vector premise. But it is half the statement. The carrier couples to J = 1 with the larger share, and the J=1 projection is non-degenerate. The problem is contamination, not decoupling.

The pure J=1 combination is the trace V_i = Σ_l S_ill, which needs off-diagonal components and is therefore not in the span of the one-plaquette carrier operators — the carrier cannot be purified within its own sector. And since T₁ ⊗ A₁ contains neither A₂ nor T₂, the carrier has identically zero overlap with its own J=3 partners. It is structurally blind to its own falsifier. Spin identification therefore requires auxiliary A₂^{+−} and T₂^{+−} sources — which is exactly how the published spectra do it, and why AT_2020 can call the 1^{+−} assignment "unambiguous" while this program cannot.

IV · The continuum theorem, restated
Assemble R1–R3. Write O^{(ρ)} for the carrier source smeared to a fixed physical radius ρ (gradient flow on a time slice), and C_a(t) = ⟨O^{(ρ)}(t)O^{(ρ)}(0)⟩ at zero spatial momentum.

Carrier Persistence — the target, as small as it goes

Hypotheses.

(C) SU(3) Yang–Mills on ℝ⁴ exists as an OS theory with mass gap Δ > 0. This is Clay.
(S) Charge conjugation survives as an exact symmetry of the limit, vacuum C-even.
(O) liminf_{a→0} C_a(t₁)/C_a(0) = c > 0 at one fixed physical t₁, and the bottom of the source's spectral support is the C-odd sector bottom.
(D) No accumulation of discrete C-odd masses at that bottom.
Conclusion. M = inf spec(mass | C-odd) satisfies Δ ≤ M ≤ (1/t₁)log(1/c) < ∞; it is an isolated point of the C-odd mass spectrum with gap m(0^{++}); its shell is stable, since no C-odd decay channel exists below M + m(0^{++}); the carrier residue is bounded below by R2; and the spin is J = 1 iff the A₂^{+−} and T₂^{+−} channels carry no degenerate partner.

What changed. The dossier's open theorem asked for an RG trajectory carrying a nonzero pole, its mass, and its isolation. In this form:

dossier's demand	where it now comes from
M_a ≥ m₋ > 0 uniformly	implied by (C); strictly weaker than (C)
M_a ≤ m₊ < ∞ uniformly	(O) — one correlator ratio, via R1
Δ_a ≥ Δ_* > 0 uniformly	C-superselection + (C), via R3
z_a ≥ z_* > 0 uniformly	not independent — gap + (O), via R2
stable, no decay	automatic in the C-odd sector, via R3
spin 1	still open; needs auxiliary channels, via R5
What this does not do

It assumes Clay rather than approaching it, and it should be read as a restructuring, not as progress on the mass gap. The corpus already says this in its own voice — §12.1: "No formula in this document is a proof of the Yang–Mills mass gap"; dependency_spine: "G17 + G18 are the ONLY path from 'lattice theorem package' to any statement containing the words 'mass gap'." The value of the restructuring is negative information: it says which non-Clay work is worth doing, and that list is now short.

V · Where the machinery already is
The route's phrase "a source-aware renormalization theorem" has an exact referent in the corpus, in a program the ledger does not name. corpus-import/programs/pmbsf/ — Projected Maxwell Birman–Schwinger Firewall — is a Bałaban/Dimock constructive gauge-RG attack in 4D Euclidean Wilson formulation. Its conditional interface, verbatim from PAPER_PMBSF_su2_section_14_remaining_analytic_tasks.md:

LCI_good + BFS_far  ⟹  TOS+J  ⟹  Z_A(ρ/q_η) ≤ e^{K|A|}  ⟹  Lemma Q       (14.1)

Lemma Q + SWB + BBG + PTO + BS  ⟹  PMBSF firewall closure                 (14.2)

Four load-bearing open inputs:
   A : LCI_good typicality with rooted complement
   B : BFS_far under positive source tilts
   C : SWB — source-weighted Bałaban expansion            ← the one that matters here
   D : BBG boundary-band gate as η → 0
Open Theorem C is stated as: Lemma Q + Bałaban/Dimock locality ⟹ source-marked polymer activities with q_η^{|M|} weights, with marked activities |w_s(Γ)| ≤ A(Γ)·ρ^{|M(Γ)|}, a Kotecký–Preiss criterion carrying the marks, and — this is the load-bearing one — an anchored decay estimate

Σ_{Γ ∈ 𝒞(p,r)}  |w_s(Γ)| e^{a(Γ)}   ≤   C_J · e^{−m_J·d_C(p,r)}      (14.37)
and four subtasks C1–C4: a marked-activity ledger through all seven expansion operations; KP convergence with marks; large-field compatibility; and gauge covariance at block boundaries.

The identification, and the asymmetry that favours the carrier

(14.37) is an exponential-clustering estimate for a two-point function with rate m_J. Run the same expansion with the carrier operator as the mark rather than a rare-event defect indicator, and it is the carrier correlator's decay rate — a mass. C1–C4 are shared machinery.

And the accounting differs in the carrier's favour, sharply. PMBSF's mark is a discontinuous hard indicator Y_p(t) = 1{1 − ½Re tr U_p ≥ t}, and its literature map records the consequence: "the constructive bounds are proved for smooth analytic observables and the partition function, while Y_p(t) is discontinuous; the smoothing bridge requires constants uniform in ε and 'is not standard'." That single fact generates most of PMBSF's difficulty — the pinned hard-indicator form (without which its KP series formally diverges, C_*e^{−m_*}J_{m_*} ≈ 117 > 1), Open Theorem D's boundary-band gate, the smoothing bridge estimated at "1–2 months of genuine analysis", and the top-p-versus-threshold problem.

The carrier source is Im Tr U_p — a character, real-analytic on the group, and analytic in the links after gradient-flow smearing. It is exactly the observable class for which the constructive bounds already exist. So of PMBSF's four open theorems, the carrier route inherits only C: Lemma Q and open inputs A and B are rare-event accounting it does not need, and D is a smoothing problem it does not have. Conversely PMBSF supplies nothing about the residue, because a rare-event indicator has none. The two programs need the same expansion and opposite halves of the bookkeeping. (This asymmetry is my inference from the two programs' statements, not a claim either of them makes.)

The gate nobody has asked about

Every step above assumes an RG that preserves reflection positivity — R1 and R3 are OS statements, and an OS-incompatible blocking destroys the spectral representation they rest on. Searched three ways across the whole repository: no statement anywhere connects a Bałaban blocking transformation to reflection positivity. The adjacent material is all weaker or damaged. OP-5 asks the Mosco-limit version and calls it "the hardest step in the OS reconstruction", with "gauge orbits are not reflected simply" as the obstruction. PROOF_15 Theorem 15.1 asserts the generic block-spin version and is quarantined and structurally refuted (F005). The concrete question, which appears to be unasked rather than hard: for Bałaban's gauge-covariant block averaging (CMP 98 (1985) 17–51) applied to a reflection-positive Wilson measure on an even torus with time reflection Θ, is the blocked effective measure again reflection positive — i.e. is the averaging kernel Θ-equivariant, and does the block-spin constraint avoid coupling across the reflection plane? On an even torus with blocks aligned to the plane this looks tractable. Nobody has written it down.

VI · Three obstructions the corpus over-reads
1. The L⁻² collapse is the dispersion relation
§12.1 records that "the finite-volume incidence isolation scales as L⁻² and collapses as L → ∞", and the MASTER_PROOF_STATE takes this as defeating volume-uniform transport of the b₂-fold level. The quantity is Δ^{(2)} = 4t_N u² sin²(π/L) — the gap from the flat carrier band to the dispersing branches at the smallest available nonzero momentum. Its vanishing as L → ∞ is the statement that E(k) → E(0) as k → 0. That is continuity of a dispersion curve, not a pathology.

And b₂ = dim H₂(T³) = 3: the "b₂-fold protected level" is the Γ-point triplet, which is the three polarizations of a spin-1 state at rest and is required by cubic symmetry to be exactly threefold degenerate. A rank-one contour around one sheet is not merely unjustified — it would be wrong. Isolation for a particle is isolation of a mass shell in the joint energy–momentum spectrum, fiberwise in k, and the external gap that supports it is uniform. The obstruction is real for the object the corpus names and absent for the object a particle theorem needs.

2. The near-Γ exclusion radius excludes the physical region
ADR 0003 gives |k| ≥ K·u, K = 23.66 at θ = 1/2, below which the O(u⁴) spread meets the O(u²)|k|² splitting and the fixed-order ordering fails. Inside that ball the three sheets are not resolvable at fixed order. For a spin-1 triplet they are supposed to be degenerate there. What the exclusion radius costs is the fixed-order splitting formula, not the particle — and the ADR's own honesty ("says nothing about behaviour inside the ball") is the right reading. The radius is a statement about perturbation theory's domain, and it should not be carried into the continuum discussion as an obstruction to the object.

3. The T0 layer contains nothing about the electric spectrum
All 28 Lean theorems are rational-arithmetic identities; sorry count is 0. Notably dim_Z₂ is proved as (L³ − 1) + 3 = L³ + 2 — arithmetic, with the homological inputs rank ∂₃ = L³ − 1 and dim H₂ = 3 supplied, not derived. And a search of invariants.py finds the electric convention E(L) = L·C_F/2 and the value 8/3 registered, but no check that the shell exhausts the spectrum below 4. The MASTER_PROOF_STATE's claim that Theorem 1′ would be "the repository's first T1 statement about the electric spectrum" is correct.
