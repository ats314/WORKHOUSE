## 1. Exact physical inversion identity

Write Uj = uj I + i vj.sigma. For every pair v1,v2 in R3 there is an SO(3)
rotation sending both to their negatives: use a rotation through pi around
an axis perpendicular to their span. This includes collinear and zero cases.
Lifting that rotation to SU(2) proves that (U1^-1,U2^-1) lies on the same
simultaneous-conjugation orbit as (U1,U2). Therefore every physical scalar
function satisfies

    F(U1^-1,U2^-1) = F(U1,U2).                              (P1)

This is special to two SU(2) holonomies. A simultaneous-conjugation-invariant
function of three or more Lie-algebra vectors can contain their scalar
triple product, so this argument does not extend by adding blocks.

Let Tg be the logarithmic rescaling and Haar half-density map. The coordinate
domains for g and -g are identical; the density factors are even, and (P1)
gives T(-g) = Tg on the physical Hilbert space. The original scaled Hamiltonian
g^2 H(g^-4) is even in g. Consequently its conjugated physical operator is
also even. This reasoning uses the complete link Hamiltonian, and therefore
retains the shared-edge coupling

    3(|grad_1 F|^2+|grad_2 F|^2)
      + sum_a |(L1_a-R2_a)F|^2.

In particular its order-g coefficient vanishes on the full physical smooth
core, not just on its ground:

    H1|physical = 0.                                       (P2)

Parity alone would not exclude a nonanalytic |g| term in a spectral quantity.
Here the existing fixed-cell harmonic/quasimode expansion supplies the
ground expansion. Its first-order equation is

    (H0-e0) psi1 = -(H1-e1) Omega0,

with psi1 perpendicular to Omega0. Equation (P2), normalization, and the
positive fixed-cell oscillator gap give

    e1 = 0,        psi1 = 0,        p1 = 0.                 (P3)

The Haar half-density also has no order-g coefficient.

## 2. Literal retained source has no order-g coefficient

For logarithmic coordinates X,Y, put

    yg = g^-1 log(exp(gX) exp(gY)),       S = X+Y.

The BCH first jet is Y1 = [X,Y]/2. A retained physical scalar is a class
function f(y) = phi(|y|^2), and <S,[X,Y]> = 0. Hence

    df(S)Y1 = 0.

More strongly, exp(-gX)exp(-gY) is the inverse of the reverse product, which
is conjugate to exp(gX)exp(gY). Thus |y(-g)|=|yg| exactly wherever the
principal logarithm is defined. The true ground law is simultaneously
conjugation invariant. Its retained vector marginal is radial, so its
signed-g first marginal coefficient also vanishes.

Combining ground, Haar, observation, and true marginal factors yields

choose K1^src=0. Equations (SP16) then give the complete physical coefficient

    W1 = 0,               t1 = Q W1 Jz = 0.                (P5)

Thus this adjacent-strip realization has an identically zero W6 first-force
term. Its first nonzero local force starts at order g^2.

For completeness, the first nonzero logarithmic radial observation jet is

    |yg|^2 = |X+Y|^2 - (g^2/12)|[X,Y]|^2 + O(g^4).         (P6)

Indeed, with C=[X,Y], the g^2 coefficient is

## 4. Domain and verification scope

The physical inversion identity is global on SU(2)^2. The polynomial
coefficients use a fixed invariant weak-field cutoff and the fixed-cell
ground expansion. Signed-g logarithmic domains coincide, and all cutoffs
can be chosen even. The chord chart here is the positive-scalar hemisphere,
used for these local coefficients; it is not a global parameterization.

The logarithmic and trace source ranges at fixed g are bounded, whereas the
Gaussian reference range is unbounded. A pointwise density-ratio formula
therefore does not, by itself, supply a global isometry from all of
L2(mu0). Equations (P4), (P9), and (P10) are source jets on their admissible
smooth/finite-polynomial cores. They do not assert uniform remainder bounds
over all retained energies or all lattice volumes. A compatible full
source transport with these core jets can have its first generator chosen
zero; its existence in any specified global operator topology remains a
separate issue.
