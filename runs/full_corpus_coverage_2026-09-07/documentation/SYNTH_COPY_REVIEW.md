## Preserved positive mechanisms

**Local curvature hinge.** S01:402–525 and 801–891 provide a sound quantitative route: if the product metric has Ricci at least κ, added Hessian at least −C_add, the Wilson Hessian is nonnegative at the reference point, and its transported Hessian has local Lipschitz constant L_W, then on a sufficiently small neighborhood

`Ric + Hess(S) >= κ − C_add − L_W r`.

Choosing `L_W r <= (κ−C_add)/2` gives a positive local floor. A retained Maxwell lower form can likewise be carried along if its adverse remainder is bounded. The missing application is a useful uniform neighborhood/globalization estimate for the actual coupling and metric, followed by the correct physical-energy comparison. Intrinsic Ricci and a log-Haar coordinate term must not be counted as two independent masses.

**Exact marginal Hessian and controlled loss.** S16:2635–2678 records

`Hess S_eff = E(Hess_qq S) − Cov(grad_q S)`.

Under a globally justified fiber Brascamp–Lieb estimate, block Hessian bounds `A>=αI`, `C>=γI` and `||B||<=M` yield the sufficient floor `α−M²/γ`. The covariance subtraction is essential. If a positive recursion `ρ_(j+1)>=ρ_j−M_j²/ρ_j` is maintained with `2 sum_j M_j² < ρ_0²`, the usual square budget gives a positive bootstrap; a single inequality `ρ_0>M` does not. This is consistent with the current score-aware repair, not an alternative that omits the score.

**Repaired local-to-global Poincaré theorem.** S08:747–763 can be repaired without its claimed weighted tail. For a reversible Dirichlet form, W>=1 with justified integration by parts, `LW<=−αW+b 1_K`, μ(K)>0, and local conditional Poincaré constant C_K, set `g=f−μ_K(f)`. Then

`∫(−LW/W)g² dμ <= E(g)`,
`α∫g² dμ <= E(g)+b∫_K g²/W dμ <= (1+b C_K)E(f)`.

Thus `Var_μ(f) <= (1+b C_K)E(f)/α`. This is a valid generic configuration-space theorem. Actual volume-uniform Wilson drift and local constants remain premises, and configuration diffusion is not the OS Hamiltonian. A logarithmic Sobolev conclusion needs additional input.

**RP/OS and actual energy.** Positive-half-local, reflection-equivariant deterministic blocking preserves reflection positivity by pulling the tested positive algebra back. Dense centered positive-source decay with a common physical clock controls spectral support through positive self-correlation measures. The current `RESULT:OS_HISTORY_BLOCK_INTERTWINER` explicitly retains these hypotheses. Generic full-energy `RESULT:FORM_SCHUR_SCALE_COMPARISON` also retains the actual cross form and induced norm, rather than identifying it with a configuration Hessian.

**HS and massive resolvent structure.** The correctly signed Poisson equation `−Lu=G−μG` gives the standard covariance pairing with the inverse Witten operator, under its domain/boundary hypotheses. An operator lower bound gives inverse domination of quadratic forms. Absolute spatial or cross-observable bounds require an actual kernel estimate, not arbitrary entrywise use of Loewner order. The current `RESULT:GAUSSIAN_FULL_FAST_GREEN` and `RESULT:DYNAMIC_CONDITIONAL_CUBIC_ENERGY` preserve this distinction.

## Specific failed steps, counterexamples and repairs

1. **Reverse gradient normalization:** S15:426–533 and S11:335–359. For q=(x₁+…+x_N)/N and f=q, the coarse gradient squared is 1 and fine pullback gradient squared is 1/N. A reverse bound with constant 1/N would require `1<=1/N²`. The pullback experiment is meaningful, but proves a different direction. Use the quotient metric and centered conditional score, as already done in `RESULT:CONDITIONAL_GRADIENT_REPAIR`.

2. **False weighted drift:** S08:751–761 divides by W but retains an extra W. The actual pointwise implication is `−LW/W>=α−b 1_K/W`, not `αW−b 1_K`. The preceding Poincaré repair is the strongest direct surviving result. In the original BEST_02:47–53, `Ent_μ(f² 1_K)` is also not conditional entropy: f=1 gives μ(K)log(1/μ(K))>0 while the local gradient vanishes. Replace it with μ(K) times entropy under normalized μ_K before discussing local LSI.

3. **Uniform convexity is not Gaussianity:** S01:964–974 concludes Gaussianity from zero curvature defect and a bounded cubic remainder. The fixed non-Gaussian potential `V_ε=x²/2+ε(1−cos x)`, 0<ε<1, has Hessian at least 1−ε, bounded third derivative, zero defect at that floor, and a constant convergent covariance. The derivative of its fourth cumulant at ε=0 is `exp(−1/2)`, so it is nonzero for sufficiently small positive ε. A genuinely vanishing nonquadratic remainder or another Gaussian-identification argument is required. This tests the asserted implication, not Yang–Mills existence.

4. **Reflection commutation alone is insufficient:** S03:423–449. Take independent symmetric signs x₊,x₋, whose fine RP form is `|E f|²`. Set y₊=x₊−x₋, y₋=−y₊. The map commutes with reflection, but `E(y₊y₋)=−2`. Require positive-half locality. Current history results already do.

5. **Operator-order reversal:** S03:502–515 uses `L_conf>=cH` as a lower gap bound for H. On vacuum⊕excitation, `L_conf=diag(0,1)` and `H=diag(0,1/100)` satisfy that premise with c=1 and disprove the claimed floor. A properly oriented physical-form/transfer comparison is required.

6. **No arbitrary bilinear order:** S14:799–825 and original EXCITING_02:145–152 infer a cross-covariance inequality from inverse Loewner order. The Gaussian covariance `C=[[3/4,1/4],[1/4,3/4]]<=I`, with precision `C⁻¹>=I`, has `Cov(x₁,x₂)=1/4>e₁·I e₂=0`. Cauchy–Schwarz in the compared quadratic form is valid; an absolute spatial estimate needs a separate kernel or locality proof. Restricted Neumann Witten comparisons also need their boundary-form hypothesis.

7. **One seed is not an infinite curvature budget:** S16:2671. For M=1 the positive sequence `2,3/2,5/6,1/3,1/4,…` satisfies `ρ_(n+1)>=ρ_n−1/ρ_n` but tends to zero, despite ρ₀>M. Use a strict summable loss budget or a demonstrated positive forcing recurrence. S04:849–919 also needs a positive seed at the later time where correction domination begins; positivity at an earlier time alone does not bridge that interval.

8. **A missing determinant term matters:** S07:446–597 has the exact formula for `−(1/2)log det M`: its Hessian is `(1/2)Tr(M⁻¹M'M⁻¹M')−(1/2)Tr(M⁻¹M'')`. Keeping only the first term does not prove convexity, including near reducible configurations. Derive and control the second term and the actual quotient domain.

9. **Model and formal scope:** S06:248–318 calls a positive-spectrum conjugated Hamiltonian a Markov generator; the generator is its negative, requiring a positive ground state and nonpositive off-diagonal Hamiltonian. The located QRacahDoob.lean correctly includes those sign hypotheses but does not contain the three named gap theorems claimed in the synthesis. Its Cheeger-bound ordering and chosen scaling exponent are arithmetic definitions/lemmas, not an actual Wilson gap. SourceTermPersistence.lean proves scalar sign/equilibrium facts assuming the positive source and defines a mass number; it does not identify that number with a physical spectrum. Recover and compile the exact cited files before assigning broader formal scope.

10. **Other source-specific repairs:** S05:578 bounds the entire Wilson Hessian by `Cβ||A||²`, which vanishes at A=0 despite its nonzero Maxwell Hessian; the intended bound must concern the adverse remainder. S18's countable dense gauge-parameter set does not cover `exists ξ≠0: D_Aξ=0`, since approximation is not exact kernel membership. The full Gribov-union argument needs a valid capacity/stratification step. S10:95 uses an energy primitive whose derivative is μ(r), whereas its flux requires rμ(r); a corrected primitive is `r²/2+a₀(r+a₀)e^(−r/a₀)−a₀²`, with the displayed overall normalization treated consistently. Its own radial far-field g(r)~constant/r is not in global H1(R³) for nonzero mass, so the existence statement needs an appropriate domain/function space. These latter phenomenological claims do not supply current G19 inputs.
