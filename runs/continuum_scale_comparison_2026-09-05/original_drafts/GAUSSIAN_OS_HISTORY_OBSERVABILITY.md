# What an exact Gaussian OS history block actually removes

5 September 2026. Outputs-only analytic continuation of the physical-scale
comparison. This note concerns an exact finite-dimensional Gaussian tangent
theory. It does not identify a nonlinear Wilson block history range by a
perturbation argument.

The established
[OS history intertwiner](../../../paper/research_notes/G19_OS_BLOCKING_AND_REVERSE_MASS_MATCHING_20260905.md),
Sections 4-5, transports the spectrum on the range of the actual history
pullback. Its orthogonal complement is an operator-theoretic object. The
[actual two-square Hamiltonian](../../../paper/research_notes/G19_WILSON_TWO_SQUARE_PHYSICAL_SHELLS_20260905.md)
supplies the strip tangent matrix used below. Neither source equates a
fixed-configuration fiber with that history complement.

The conclusion is sharp. A block may remove configuration coordinates while
retaining every oscillator through time histories. The symmetric strip really
does lose modes, but its first missing physical mode is a mixed coarse/fine
singlet, below the first invariant intrinsic-fiber excitation. An arbitrarily
small inequality between the two positive face weights removes this history
complement in the full Gaussian history algebra, and also in the actual
separately invariant slice algebra for SU(2).

## 1. Exact one-particle theorem

Let E be a finite-dimensional real Euclidean space, let Omega=Omega*>0 on E,
and let B:E -> F be any linear observation. Consider the centered stationary
Gaussian process q(t), t in R, with covariance

```text
E[q(t) q(s)*] = (2 Omega)^(-1) exp(-Omega |t-s|).
```

The observed process is y(t)=Bq(t). Use reflection t -> -t and the algebra of
all polynomials in the observed coordinates at nonnegative times. At the
one-particle level, the OS inner product is

```text
<a.y(t), b.y(s)>_OS
 = <a, B(2 Omega)^(-1) exp(-Omega(t+s)) B* b>
 = <v(t,a),v(s,b)>_E,

v(t,a)=(2 Omega)^(-1/2) exp(-t Omega) B* a.               (1)
```

After quotienting null vectors, the one-particle space is therefore the
subspace K generated by these v(t,a). If Omega has distinct positive
eigenvalues omega_1,...,omega_r and orthogonal eigenprojections P_j, then

```text
K = span{exp(-t Omega) B* a : t>=0, a in F}
  = span{Omega^k B* a : 0<=k<r, a in F}
  = direct_sum_j ran(P_j B*).                            (2)
```

The first equality with (1) holds because the invertible scalar factor
(2 omega_j)^(-1/2) on each frequency eigenspace does not change the final
spectral subspace. To prove the remaining equalities, spectral decomposition
gives one inclusion. For the reverse inclusion, polynomial interpolation
expresses each P_j on E as a polynomial of degree less than r in Omega.
Equivalently, for any h>0, the r by r matrix

```text
V_kj=exp(-k h omega_j),  k=0,...,r-1,
```

is a Vandermonde matrix with distinct nodes exp(-h omega_j). Linear
combinations of the vectors exp(-k h Omega)B*a thus recover every P_j B*a.
This also proves the continuous-time span without differentiating histories.

In particular,

```text
dim K = sum_j rank(P_j B*),
K=E iff ker B intersects ran P_j only at zero for every j. (3)
```

A single scalar observation can see an arbitrarily large number of simple
frequencies. It cannot distinguish multiple directions in one exactly
degenerate frequency eigenspace without enough observation components.

The same K is obtained from all regularly sampled nonnegative Euclidean
times {0,h,2h,...}, for every h>0. In fact r consecutive times suffice for
the one-particle span. Frequencies do not alias under positive Euclidean
sampling because omega -> exp(-h omega) is injective. Finite sets with too
few times, a single time-zero marginal, and sampling of real-time phases
are different questions.

## 2. Fock density and the exact complement

Write Gamma_s(E_C) for symmetric Fock space over the complexification of E,
with vacuum 1 and oscillator Hamiltonian dGamma(Omega). The Gaussian OS
reconstruction of all fine coordinate histories is this Fock space. Here is
the density argument needed for the blocked range, rather than an appeal to
equal-time variable counting.

Wick-order a polynomial in positive-time Gaussian variables using their
stationary Gaussian covariance. Wick's pairing formula in the reflected
inner product pairs a factor on the reflected side only with a factor on
the positive side; different homogeneous Wick degrees are orthogonal. The
degree-n OS image is

```text
:prod_l (a_l.y(t_l)):
       -> sym(v(t_1,a_1) tensor ... tensor v(t_n,a_n)),   (4)
```

with the usual factor n! if symmetric tensors are given the convention
without normalized symmetrization. Thus the degree-n image spans
Sym^n(K_C). Polynomials are dense in the Gaussian L2 space of the positive
history sigma algebra. The OS seminorm is at most its ordinary L2 norm by
Cauchy-Schwarz and reflection invariance. Consequently no additional OS
vectors are left outside the closure of (4), and the complete range is

```text
ran J = Gamma_s(K_C) inside Gamma_s(E_C)
      = Gamma_s(K_C) tensor vacuum_(K_perp).             (5)
```

K is a reducing spectral subspace of Omega. Hence this is a reducing
subspace for the oscillator transfer at every positive time. Its complement
consists of sectors with at least one quantum from K_perp, rather than a
space canonically indexed by ker B at one time. If K=E, that complement is
zero even when ker B is nonzero.

The finite-dimensional coarse coordinate process can therefore reconstruct
a larger quantum configuration space. When several frequencies occur in
one observed covariance, the observed process generally has memory. It
must not be replaced by a Markov process selected only to match its
time-zero covariance.

For example, if a scalar observed covariance is

```text
C(t)=r_1 exp(-omega_1 |t|)+r_2 exp(-omega_2 |t|),
r_1,r_2>0, omega_1!=omega_2,
```

then for t,s>0,

```text
C(t+s)C(0)-C(t)C(s)
 = r_1 r_2 (exp(-omega_1 t)-exp(-omega_2 t))
             (exp(-omega_1 s)-exp(-omega_2 s)) > 0.      (6)
```

The covariance composition identity of a scalar stationary Gaussian Markov
process fails. Both frequencies survive in its exact OS reconstruction.
The companion [Gaussian memory note](GAUSSIAN_MEMORY_AND_SPECTRAL_MATCHING.md)
develops the spectral/memory comparison rather than imposing a nonexistent
equal-time elimination.

## 3. Gauge invariance: state the history algebra

Let a compact group G act orthogonally on E and F, commute with Omega, and
make B equivariant. Then K is G-invariant. For the full history algebra
invariant under one simultaneous G action on all times, averaging (4) over
G commutes with the OS map. Averaging a dense polynomial set is dense in
the invariant subspace. Therefore

```text
ran J_physical = Gamma_s(K_C)^G
 inside Gamma_s(E_C)^G.                                (7)
```

This statement permits invariant contractions between observed vectors at
different times. An algebra generated only by separately gauge-invariant
functions of each individual time slice can be smaller. Equality (7) for
that smaller algebra requires its own density proof. No claim here removes
temporal transporters or changes the gauge group of an actual history map.

In particular one-particle observability is not, by itself, a theorem of
cyclicity for an arbitrary chosen physical observable algebra. Section 6
proves that extra cyclicity for the two-mode SU(2) example. It also gives
a counterexample to an unrestricted generalization.

## 4. The actual symmetric adjacent strip

For two adjacent Wilson squares, retain all seven link contributions. In
the product logarithms x_1,x_2 in su(N), the harmonic electric cometric is

```text
C = [[4,-1],[-1,4]] tensor I_su(N).
```

The leading scaled Hamiltonian, after subtracting the vacuum energy, is
the oscillator associated with

```text
H_osc = -(1/2) grad_x* C grad_x + (1/2)(|x_1|^2+|x_2|^2).
```

Set Q=(x_1+x_2)/sqrt(2) and Z=(x_1-x_2)/sqrt(2). Its two frequencies are

```text
omega_Q=sqrt(3),  omega_Z=sqrt(5).
```

The tangent of the based product-holonomy block is the sum x_1+x_2, hence
equivalently Bx=Q. This observation kills the Z eigenspace exactly. The
residual physical action is simultaneous Ad SU(N) on Q and Z.

All histories of the coarse Q variable leave the Z oscillator in its
vacuum. Conversely, time-zero invariant functions of Q alone already
span its complete invariant L2 Gaussian space. Thus both the full jointly
invariant history algebra and the smaller separately invariant slice
algebra have exactly the physical range

```text
Gamma_s((su(N)_Q)_C)^Ad tensor vacuum_Z.                 (8)
```

The actual physical complement of (8) starts at

```text
omega_Q+omega_Z = sqrt(3)+sqrt(5),                       (9)
```

with the unique bilinear singlet Q.Z. Indeed it contains one Z quantum,
and one adjoint quantum alone has no singlet. The least possibilities are
one Q plus one Z, or two Z quanta. Since omega_Q<omega_Z, the former wins;
all higher particle numbers cost more. The nonzero invariant Q.Z realizes
the bound. The pure fine invariant |Z|^2-centered occurs at 2sqrt(5).

The constrained-fiber class gap 2sqrt(5) therefore does not even equal the
first complement energy in this exactly solvable physical tangent theory.
The coarse and fine color charges can couple to a singlet across the two
sectors. A physical fast-sector comparison must preserve that possibility.
These displayed energies are in the sqrt(u)-scaled units of the actual
large-u strip theorem.

## 5. Arbitrarily small actual face-weight asymmetry restores observability

Keep the same seven-link electric Hamiltonian and replace the magnetic
term by

```text
2u[(1+epsilon)v(U_1)+(1-epsilon)v(U_2)],  |epsilon|<1.
```

These are positive weights of the actual two Wilson faces. The coarse
map is still the product holonomy. Its harmonic potential matrix in Q,Z
coordinates is [[1,epsilon],[epsilon,1]], while the electric matrix remains
diag(3,5). Whiten the kinetic term by

```text
Q=sqrt(3) r_Q,  Z=sqrt(5) r_Z.
```

The covariance now has the form in Section 1 with

```text
Omega_epsilon^2 = A_epsilon
 = [[3,sqrt(15)epsilon],[sqrt(15)epsilon,5]],
D=(sqrt(3),0),  observed Q=D r.                         (10)
```

Its two distinct positive squared frequencies are

```text
lambda_-=4-sqrt(1+15epsilon^2),
lambda_+=4+sqrt(1+15epsilon^2).
```

For epsilon!=0,

```text
det[D*, A_epsilon D*]=3sqrt(15)epsilon !=0.              (11)
```

Since A_epsilon and its positive square root have the same eigenspaces,
(2)-(3) imply full one-particle observability, color component by color
component. Thus the exact full vector-history range and the full jointly
Ad-invariant history range are complete for every epsilon!=0. At epsilon=0
they have the nontrivial complement (8)-(9).

This discontinuity concerns exact range, not a discontinuity of the
Hamiltonian or a large residue. The high-frequency weight in the observed
scalar Q covariance is

```text
r_+(epsilon)
 = [3/(2sqrt(lambda_+))]
       [1-1/sqrt(1+15epsilon^2)]/2
 = [45/(8sqrt(5))]epsilon^2+O(epsilon^4).                (12)
```

The high mode is weakly visible but not absent. Reconstruction of it from
the observed history becomes ill-conditioned as epsilon tends to zero.
Neither exact history range dimension nor equal-time fiber dimension is
a stable proxy for an approximate low-energy subspace.

## 6. Physical completeness from separate-slice observables for SU(2)

For SU(2), the residual adjoint action is SO(3). Write the two independent
normal coordinates as r_1,r_2 in R^3, with distinct positive frequencies
omega_1,omega_2. The observed vector has nonzero coefficients in both,

```text
y=a_1 r_1+a_2 r_2,  a_1 a_2 !=0.
```

The actual coarse class algebra contains |y|^2 and every polynomial in it.
The one-particle OS vectors carry the factors (2omega_j)^(-1/2). Absorb
these into nonzero coefficients b_j=a_j/sqrt(2omega_j).
With I_11,I_12,I_22 the quadratic invariant tensors
of the two color-vector copies, the centered radial history has pure
second-chaos OS image

```text
S(t)= b_1^2 exp(-2omega_1 t) I_11
     +2b_1 b_2 exp(-(omega_1+omega_2)t) I_12
     +b_2^2 exp(-2omega_2 t) I_22.                      (13)
```

At t=0,h,2h, the three coefficient rows form an invertible Vandermonde
matrix, multiplied by nonzero column factors. Therefore all three
quadratic singlets are already in the closed OS range of separately
invariant slice histories.

For completeness at all particle numbers, take products of centered
radial variables at any finite list of positive times. Their highest Wick
degree has OS tensor S(t_1)...S(t_n), with symmetric multiplication. Since
the S(t) span the three-dimensional quadratic space, these highest-degree
tensors span every product of I_11,I_12,I_22. Lower degrees come only from
Gaussian contractions. All color contractions use Kronecker deltas, so
the remaining invariant polynomials are lower-degree polynomials in the
same three Gram entries. Induction subtracts their already accessible OS
vectors. Every Wick polynomial in those three Gram entries is consequently
accessible. This argument uses actual products of separate-slice radial
observables; it does not insert a forbidden cross-time contraction into
the history algebra.

Finally the three Gram entries

```text
|r_1|^2, r_1.r_2, |r_2|^2
```

separate SO(3) orbits of pairs of vectors. An isometry between their spans
extends to an orientation-preserving orthogonal map in R^3, using the
unused perpendicular direction to choose determinant +1. Thus every
invariant measurable function is a function of the Gram triple. Its
polynomials are dense in the invariant Gaussian L2 space: the Gram triple
has an exponential moment in a neighborhood of zero, and an L2 function
orthogonal to all its polynomials has zero analytic Laplace transform
there. Fourier uniqueness for the resulting finite signed pushforward
measure makes that function zero. This gives the required density without
assuming a general invariant-ring theorem.

We have proved, for the actual two-mode SU(2) harmonic model with unequal
positive weights, that the smaller separately invariant history algebra
already reconstructs the entire physical oscillator Hilbert space. Its
history complement is zero. In the symmetric model the same algebra has
the nonzero complement in Section 4.

One must not generalize this physical conclusion from observability alone.
With three independently observable mode vectors in R^3, each single-vector
radial slice observable and the oscillator evolution are invariant under
the larger group O(3). Their cyclic space is therefore O(3)-even. The
nonzero polynomial det(r_1,r_2,r_3) is SO(3)-invariant and O(3)-odd, so it
is a physical vector outside that cyclic space despite complete
one-particle observability. For general SU(N), the corresponding
separate-slice invariant-algebra density must likewise be proved for the
actual selected observables.

## 7. Consequence for the physical scale target

The Gaussian test settles the proposed identification, rather than leaving
its meaning ambiguous:

1. The exact history block retains the frequency components visible to
   B, including components with arbitrarily small nonzero residues. It
   need not remove any physical states just because it reduces the
   number of equal-time variables.
2. When it does remove states, global Gauss invariance permits mixed
   coarse/fine singlets. A class gap on an intrinsic fiber can overstate
   the first energy of the actual physical complement.
3. Euclidean time sampling does not by itself remove the weakly visible
   frequencies. Replacing the generated memory by a smaller Markov
   system is an approximation with an independent spectral/error budget.

The constructive next target is an actual controlled low-energy comparison
for the generated history action or transfer, retaining its memory and
source residues. An auxiliary low-energy projection may be useful, but
its complement and comparison error must be proved; its rank cannot be
inferred from the raw spatial averaging map. The existing exact OS
intertwiner remains valid. The continuum scale comparison remains open.
