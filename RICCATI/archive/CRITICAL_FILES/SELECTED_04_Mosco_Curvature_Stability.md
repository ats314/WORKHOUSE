## Abstract

The project’s continuum step relies on an analytic principle:

> **If** a sequence of lattice Dirichlet forms converges to a continuum Dirichlet form in the **Mosco** sense,  
> and **if** each lattice form satisfies a uniform curvature-dimension inequality $CD(\rho_0,\infty)$,  
> then the continuum form inherits the same bound — hence a spectral gap.

This note states a clean version of that “lifting engine” and sketches how it fits the Yang–Mills program.

---

## 1. Dirichlet forms and Mosco convergence (minimal setup)

Let $(\mathcal{H}_a,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_a)$ be Hilbert spaces (typically $L^2(\mu_a)$), and let
\[
(\mathcal{E}_a, D(\mathcal{E}_a))
\]
be closed, symmetric, Markovian forms on $\mathcal{H}_a$.

Let $(\mathcal{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ be a limiting Hilbert space (typically $L^2(\mu)$) with a closed symmetric form $(\mathcal{E},D(\mathcal{E}))$.

To compare different spaces, one needs identification maps
\[
J_a: \mathcal{H}\to \mathcal{H}_a
\quad\text{and}\quad
K_a: \mathcal{H}_a\to \mathcal{H},
\]
with $K_aJ_a\to \mathrm{id}$ in a suitable sense (e.g. isometries or approximate isometries on a dense core).

### Definition 1.1 (Mosco convergence)

We say $\mathcal{E}_a$ Mosco-converges to $\mathcal{E}$ if:

1. (**liminf**) whenever $u_a\in\mathcal{H}_a$ and $K_a u_a\rightharpoonup u$ weakly in $\mathcal{H}$,
   \[
   \mathcal{E}(u,u) \le \liminf_{a\to 0} \mathcal{E}_a(u_a,u_a).
   \]

2. (**recovery**) for every $u\in\mathcal{H}$, there exist $u_a\in\mathcal{H}_a$ with $K_a u_a\to u$ strongly in $\mathcal{H}$ such that
   \[
   \mathcal{E}(u,u) \ge \limsup_{a\to 0} \mathcal{E}_a(u_a,u_a).
   \]

Under standard hypotheses, Mosco convergence implies strong convergence of resolvents and semigroups (a Trotter–Kato type principle for forms).

## 2. Curvature-dimension via gradient contraction

On a Riemannian manifold, Bakry–Émery $CD(\rho_0,\infty)$ is equivalent to a $\Gamma_2$ inequality.
For general Dirichlet forms, a robust substitute is the **semigroup gradient contraction** estimate:

### Definition 2.1 ($CD(\rho_0,\infty)$ via contraction)

Let $(P_t)_{t\ge 0}$ be the Markov semigroup generated by $\mathcal{E}$. We say the form satisfies $CD(\rho_0,\infty)$ if for all nice $f$,
\[
\|\nabla P_t f\|^2 \le e^{-2\rho_0 t}\,\|\nabla f\|^2.
\]
(Here “$\nabla$” is the carré-du-champ operator $\Gamma$ in Dirichlet-form language.)

---

## 3. The stability statement

### Theorem 3.1 (Mosco stability of $CD(\rho_0,\infty)$)

Assume:

1. $\mathcal{E}_a\to\mathcal{E}$ in Mosco sense.
2. The associated semigroups satisfy $P_t^a \to P_t$ strongly (for each fixed $t>0$) under the identification maps.
3. Each lattice form satisfies $CD(\rho_0,\infty)$ with the **same** $\rho_0>0$.

Then the limiting form satisfies $CD(\rho_0,\infty)$.

*Proof sketch.* Start with
\[
\Gamma_a(P_t^a f_a)\le e^{-2\rho_0 t}\Gamma_a(f_a).
\]
Use strong semigroup convergence plus Mosco liminf/recovery to pass to the limit and obtain
\[
\Gamma(P_t f)\le e^{-2\rho_0 t}\Gamma(f).
\]
Differentiate at $t=0$ (in a weak form sense) to recover $\Gamma_2\ge \rho_0\Gamma$.

## 4. How this plugs into Yang–Mills

The project’s architecture is:

1. Prove a lattice $CD(\rho_0,\infty)$ bound (geometric floor from Haar + Wilson Hessian, at least locally / on horizontals).
2. Prove tightness/existence of continuum measure $\mu$.
3. Prove Mosco convergence $\mathcal{E}_a\to \mathcal{E}$.
4. Invoke Theorem 3.1 to lift the curvature bound (and hence a spectral gap) to the continuum.
5. Use OS reconstruction / reflection positivity to transfer Euclidean spectral gap to Hamiltonian mass gap.

---

## 5. Where the real difficulty lives

This stability theorem is not the bottleneck. The bottlenecks are:

- constructing the identification maps $J_a,K_a$ in a gauge-invariant (or gauge-fixed but controlled) way;
- proving uniform estimates that survive $a\to 0$ (especially beyond the small-field sector);
- handling stratification/singularities of the orbit space without losing semigroup control.

That is exactly why the project emphasizes UV log-forest control and polarity arguments: they are meant to justify the analytic assumptions needed to invoke Mosco stability.
