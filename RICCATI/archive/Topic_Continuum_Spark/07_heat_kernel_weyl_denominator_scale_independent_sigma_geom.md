> **Target claim.** If you coarse-grain lattice link variables by a *heat-kernel convolution semigroup* (the cleanest “RG smoothing” on a compact group),
> then the induced measure on **block-holonomy conjugacy classes** retains the **same Weyl-denominator Jacobian factor** at every scale.
> Consequently, the uniform convexity bound from the Weyl Jacobian (e.g. the \(N/4\) bound you extracted) can be promoted to a **scale-independent**
> \(\sigma_{\rm geom}\) candidate.

The whole point is that the Weyl denominator is not a feature of the *action*.
It is a feature of the *quotient geometry* \(G \to G/\!\operatorname{Ad}G\).
If your coarse variable is a conjugacy class, that Jacobian is “built in” and does not renormalize away.

---

## 1. Setup: \(G=SU(N)\), conjugacy classes, and the universal Jacobian

Let \(G=SU(N)\) with normalized Haar measure \(dg\).
Let \(T\subset G\) be the maximal torus (diagonal matrices with determinant \(1\)),
with eigenangles \(\theta=(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_N)\in \mathbb R^N\) satisfying \(\sum_i \theta_i = 0 \mod 2\pi\).
Write \(t(\theta)=\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\theta_1},\dots,e^{i\theta_N})\).

Let
\[
\pi:G \longrightarrow T/W \;\cong\; G/\!\operatorname{Ad}G
\]
be the quotient map to conjugacy classes (here \(W\) is the Weyl group, \(W=S_N\) for \(SU(N)\)).

Define the Weyl denominator (up to a phase)
\[
\Delta(\theta) \;=\; \prod_{1\le i<j\le N}\Big(e^{\frac{i}{2}(\theta_i-\theta_j)}-e^{-\frac{i}{2}(\theta_i-\theta_j)}\Big)
\;=\; \prod_{i<j} 2i\,\sin\!\frac{\theta_i-\theta_j}{2},
\]
so that
\[
|\Delta(\theta)|^2 \;=\; \prod_{i<j} 4\sin^2\!\frac{\theta_i-\theta_j}{2}.
\]
This factor vanishes precisely at eigenvalue collisions (the reducible / non-regular locus).

### Lemma 1 (Weyl integration formula, “pushforward Jacobian” form)
If \(f:G\to \mathbb C\) is a **class function** (i.e. \(f(hgh^{-1})=f(g)\)),
then
\[
\int_G f(g)\,dg
\;=\;
\frac{1}{|W|}\int_{T} f(t)\,|\Delta(t)|^2\,dt,
\]
where \(dt\) is Haar measure on \(T\) (equivalently Lebesgue measure on eigenangles, modulo the usual identifications).

**Interpretation.** The pushforward of Haar measure to conjugacy classes contains a universal density factor \( |\Delta|^2\).
No dynamics. No coupling. Pure geometry.

## 2. Heat-kernel convolution as a coarse-graining semigroup on links

Let \(\Delta_G\) be the (bi-invariant) Laplace--Beltrami operator on \(G\).
Let \(K_t(g)\) be the heat kernel on \(G\), i.e. the fundamental solution of
\[
\partial_t K_t \;=\;\frac12 \Delta_G K_t,\qquad K_{t\downarrow 0}\to \delta_e.
\]
Key facts (standard on compact groups):

1. **Centrality:** \(K_t(hgh^{-1})=K_t(g)\) for all \(h,g\in G\).  
2. **Convolution semigroup:** \(K_s * K_t = K_{s+t}\), where
   \[
   (f*g)(x)=\int_G f(y)\,g(y^{-1}x)\,dy.
   \]

A clean “link-smoothing” semigroup acting on densities \(\rho\) is
\[
\rho \longmapsto \rho_t := \rho * K_t .
\]
This is the Markov semigroup generated by \(\tfrac12\Delta_G\); it is gauge-equivariant because Haar and \(\Delta_G\) are.

## 3. Block-holonomy distributions under heat-kernel coarse graining

### 3.1 The blocking map is multiplication, so densities convolve

Let \(m:G^L\to G\) be multiplication along a fixed path:
\[
m(U_1,\dots,U_L) := U_1 U_2\cdots U_L.
\]
If \(U_1,\dots,U_L\) are independent \(G\)-valued random variables with densities \(\rho_1,\dots,\rho_L\) w.r.t. Haar,
then the product \(U:=U_1\cdots U_L\) has density
\[
\rho_U \;=\; \rho_1*\rho_2*\cdots *\rho_L.
\]
This is just the definition of convolution on a group.

### 3.2 Specialize to the heat kernel

If each fine link is “heat-smeared” from the identity with parameter \(t\),
i.e. \(U_\ell \sim K_t(g)\,dg\), independent,
then the block link \(U=U_1\cdots U_L\) has density
\[
\rho_U(g) \;=\; K_t^{*L}(g) \;=\; K_{Lt}(g),
\]
by the semigroup property.

**So in the heat-kernel RG ansatz, blocking simply renormalizes the time parameter \(t\mapsto Lt\).**
Crucially, each \(\rho_U\) is still a class function.

> **Reality check.** In an interacting gauge theory the link variables are not independent.
> The clean statement above is still useful because it tells you what happens to the *central smoothing part* of the measure.
> The next section shows you do **not** need independence to keep the Weyl denominator.

## 4. The main claim: the Weyl denominator survives *any* gauge-invariant pushforward

### Theorem 2 (Scale-independent Weyl Jacobian on block-holonomy conjugacy classes)
Let \(\mu\) be any probability measure on \(G\) with density \(\rho(g)\) w.r.t. Haar, where \(\rho\) is a **class function**.

Let \(\nu := \pi_*\mu\) be the pushforward measure on conjugacy classes \(T/W\).
Then, in eigenangle coordinates \(\theta\) (restricted to a Weyl alcove), \(\nu\) has density
\[
d\nu(\theta)
\;=\;
\frac{1}{Z}\,\rho\bigl(t(\theta)\bigr)\,|\Delta(\theta)|^2\,d\theta,
\]
for a normalization constant \(Z\).

In particular, the factor \(|\Delta(\theta)|^2\) is **universal** and does not depend on \(\rho\) (hence does not depend on the RG scale).

#### Proof (one line)
For any bounded test function \(\varphi\) on \(T/W\), the pullback \(\varphi\circ\pi\) is a class function on \(G\).
Therefore
\[
\int_{T/W}\varphi(\theta)\,d\nu(\theta)
= \int_G \varphi(\pi(g))\,\rho(g)\,dg
\stackrel{\text{Weyl}}{=}
\frac{1}{|W|}\int_T \varphi(\theta)\,\rho(t(\theta))\,|\Delta(\theta)|^2\,d\theta,
\]
which identifies the density.

\(\square\)

### Corollary 3 (Heat-kernel coarse graining inherits the same Jacobian)
If \(\rho=\rho_t\) evolves by heat-kernel convolution \(\rho_t=\rho_0*K_t\),
then for every \(t\ge 0\),
\[
d\nu_t(\theta)\;\propto\;\rho_t(t(\theta))\,|\Delta(\theta)|^2\,d\theta.
\]
If in addition you use the “pure heat” ansatz \(\rho_t=K_t\), then
\[
d\nu_t(\theta)\;\propto\;K_t(\theta)\,|\Delta(\theta)|^2\,d\theta,
\qquad
d\nu_{Lt}(\theta)\;\propto\;K_{Lt}(\theta)\,|\Delta(\theta)|^2\,d\theta.
\]
The **entire scale dependence lives in the radial density** \(K_t(\theta)\); the Weyl factor is frozen.

## 5. Why this upgrades the \(N/4\) convexity bound into a scale-independent \(\sigma_{\rm geom}\)

Define the **geometric potential** coming from the orbit-volume Jacobian:
\[
S_{\rm geom}(\theta) \;:=\; -\log |\Delta(\theta)|^2
\;=\; -\sum_{i<j}\log\Big(4\sin^2\!\frac{\theta_i-\theta_j}{2}\Big).
\]
This term appears in the effective action for any eigenangle density written against flat Lebesgue measure \(d\theta\):
\[
d\nu_t(\theta) \propto \exp\bigl(-S_{\rm eff,t}(\theta)\bigr)\,d\theta,
\qquad
S_{\rm eff,t}(\theta)= -\log \rho_t(t(\theta)) + S_{\rm geom}(\theta) + \text{const}.
\]
By Theorem 2, **\(S_{\rm geom}\) does not depend on the scale parameter \(t\)**.

### Lemma 4 (Hessian is a Laplacian, hence uniformly positive on the principal stratum)
On the regular set (no eigenvalue collisions), set
\[
w_{ij}(\theta):=\csc^2\!\Big(\frac{\theta_i-\theta_j}{2}\Big)\quad (i\ne j),
\]
so \(w_{ij}(\theta)\ge 1\) wherever it is finite.
Then the second variation satisfies
\[
\delta^2 S_{\rm geom}(\theta)[x,x]
=
\frac12\sum_{i<j} w_{ij}(\theta)\,(x_i-x_j)^2.
\]
Equivalently, \(\nabla^2 S_{\rm geom}(\theta)=\tfrac12 L_{w(\theta)}\) where \(L_{w(\theta)}\) is the weighted complete-graph Laplacian.

Restricting to the constraint hyperplane \(\sum_i x_i=0\) (the tangent to the \(SU(N)\) torus),
\[
\delta^2 S_{\rm geom}(\theta)[x,x]
\;\ge\;
\frac12\sum_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)^2
\;=\;
\frac{N}{2}\,\|x\|^2 .
\]

So in this normalization one can take the **scale-independent source**
\[
\sigma_{\rm geom} \;\ge\; \frac{N}{2}.
\]

> **About your \(N/4\).** If you instead define the “Weyl-denominator potential” as
> \(S_{\rm Weyl}:=-\log|\Delta|\) (one power, not squared),
> then all Hessians are halved and you get \(\sigma_{\rm Weyl}\ge N/4\).
> Either convention is fine as long as you’re consistent about what object you call the determinant factor.

## 6. What this buys you in a mass-gap program (in one paragraph)

If your macroscopic “gap parameter” (a convexity eigenvalue, a Bakry--Émery curvature lower bound, or a Dirichlet-form spectral gap surrogate)
evolves under RG/coarse-graining like a Riccati-type inequality with a **positive source** \(\sigma_{\rm geom}\),
then Theorem 2 says: *that source is geometrically protected* whenever your coarse variables are conjugacy classes of holonomies.
Heat-kernel smoothing is the cleanest way to see it, but the real protection comes from gauge invariance + Weyl integration.

The only remaining make-or-break question is whether the continuum scaling limit lets this source survive
(i.e. whether it remains macroscopic compared to the rescaling of kinetic terms).
That is exactly the “continuum handoff” problem you flagged.

## 7. Next technical targets (what would make this genuinely new)

1. **From one holonomy to a whole lattice orbit space.** Prove an analog of Theorem 2 for a *set* of block holonomies with constraints (Bianchi identities, shared links), and identify the combined Jacobian.

2. **Show stability under a realistic RG, not just pure heat flow.**  
   Replace “\(\rho_t\) is central” by “the marginal on a block holonomy is central because of gauge invariance,” and show the Weyl Jacobian cannot be cancelled by any local counterterm.

3. **Quantify the continuum scaling of the geometric term.**  
   Track how \(S_{\rm geom}\) competes with the Wilson action under \(a\to 0\), and isolate precisely the condition under which \(\sigma_{\rm geom}\) remains \(O(1)\) in physical units.

If you can do (3) cleanly, you have a serious candidate for the “physics keeps itself gapped” mechanism.
