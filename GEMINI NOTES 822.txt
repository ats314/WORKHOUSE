How do we write the specific Lean 4 proof using IsLittleO to show that the error term (2 * eps b / g b)^2 tends to 0?

To prove that the error term vanishes, you need to bridge Mathlib's asymptotic IsLittleO definition with its topological Tendsto (limit) machinery.

The heavy lifting is done by the lemma IsLittleO.tendsto_div_nhds_zero, which converts f=o(g) directly into lim(f/g)=0. From there, it is just a matter of applying continuous functions (multiplication by a constant, squaring) to the limit.

Here is the exact Lean 4 proof:

Lean
import Mathlib.Analysis.Asymptotics.Asymptotics
import Mathlib.Topology.Basic

open Filter Asymptotics Topology

-- Define the variables as functions of our scale parameter `b`
variable {eps g : ℕ → ℝ}

lemma error_term_tends_to_zero (h_atom : eps =o[atTop] g) :
    Tendsto (fun b ↦ (2 * eps b / g b) ^ 2) atTop (𝓝 0) := by
  
  -- 1. By definition of little-o, the ratio tends to 0
  have h1 : Tendsto (fun b ↦ eps b / g b) atTop (𝓝 0) :=
    h_atom.tendsto_div_nhds_zero
    
  -- 2. Multiplying a limit by a constant 2 scales the limit by 2
  have h2 : Tendsto (fun b ↦ 2 * (eps b / g b)) atTop (𝓝 (2 * 0)) :=
    Tendsto.const_mul 2 h1
    
  -- Clean up the target limit (2 * 0 = 0)
  rw [mul_zero] at h2
  
  -- 3. Rearrange the fraction algebraically to match our goal
  have h3 : (fun b ↦ 2 * (eps b / g b)) = (fun b ↦ (2 * eps b) / g b) := by
    ext b
    ring
  rw [h3] at h2
  
  -- 4. Squaring a limit squares the target
  have h4 : Tendsto (fun b ↦ ((2 * eps b) / g b) ^ 2) atTop (𝓝 (0 ^ 2)) :=
    Tendsto.pow h2 2
    
  -- Clean up the target limit (0 ^ 2 = 0)
  rw [zero_pow (by norm_num)] at h4
  
  -- The target now exactly matches our goal
  exact h4
How this works under the hood:
𝓝 0 (Neighborhood of 0): This is Mathlib's topological way of expressing the target of a limit. Tendsto f atTop (𝓝 0) means f(x)→0 as x→∞.

Tendsto.const_mul and Tendsto.pow: Mathlib handles limits of arithmetic operations by applying the operation to the known limit. You prove the base limit approaches 0, then push the operations (multiply by 2, square it) into the limit target.

ext b and ring: Limit theorems in Mathlib are very strict about syntactic equality. While 2⋅(ϵ/g) and (2ϵ)/g are mathematically identical, they are structurally different to the typechecker. The ext b tactic introduces the point-wise variable, and ring instantly proves the algebraic equivalence, allowing rw to swap them.
