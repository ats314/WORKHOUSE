# One-page derivation: Haar curvature ⇒ Wilson mass (sign check)



## Abstract



We consider $G=\mathrm{SU}(N)$ equipped with its bi-invariant Riemannian metric induced by $-\operatorname{Tr}$ on $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{su}(N)$, and the Wilson lattice gauge action on a finite lattice $\Lambda$. The configuration space $\mathscr{A}=G^E$ (with $E$ the oriented edges) is endowed with the product Riemannian metric $g$ (pulled back from the Haar metric on each edge).  



For the associated Gibbs measure

$$

d\mu_\beta(U) \;=\; Z^{-1} e^{-S_\beta(U)}\, d\mathrm{vol}_g(U),\qquad

S_\beta(U) = \beta\sum_{p\in P}\bigl(N - \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr} U_p\bigr),

$$

we show that, near the identity sector, the Bakry–Émery tensor

$$

\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu_\beta} \;:=\; \operatorname{Ric}_g + \nabla^2 S_\beta

$$

is strictly positive in horizontal (physical) directions. More precisely, the Hessian $\nabla^2 S_\beta$ is nonnegative and, modulo gauge directions, has a uniform strictly positive lower bound proportional to $\beta$ near identity. Together with the positive Ricci curvature of the Haar product metric, this yields a uniform convexity (“mass term”) of the form $\kappa + \beta c_W$ with $c_W>0$ on horizontal directions. This implies local Poincaré / log-Sobolev inequalities and a corresponding effective mass scale, and suggests RG-stable “mass-from-curvature” mechanisms under coarse-graining.

  $$

  S_\beta(U)

  $$

  d\mu_\beta(U) \;:=\; Z^{-1} e^{-S_\beta(U)}\, d\operatorname{vol}_g(U),

  $$

  where $d\operatorname{vol}_g$ is the Riemannian volume form induced by $g$, and $Z$ is the partition function.

  $$

  d\mu \;=\; Z^{-1} e^{-S}\, d\operatorname{vol}_g

  $$

  \operatorname{Ric}_\mu \;:=\; \operatorname{Ric}_g + \nabla^2 S,

  where $\operatorname{Ric}_g$ is the Ricci curvature of $g$ and $\nabla^2 S$ is the Riemannian Hessian.

  $$

  \operatorname{Ric}_{\mu_\beta}

  \operatorname{Ric}_g + \nabla^2 S_\beta,

  computed with respect to the product Haar metric $g$ on $\mathscr{A}$.



### Horizontal directions and gauge projector



- Gauge transformations act on $\mathscr{A}$ by conjugation on links. Their infinitesimal generators span a vertical subspace (pure gauge directions) in each tangent space $T_U\mathscr{A}$.

## 2. Main Results



### Theorem 2.1 (Mass from geometry via Bakry–Émery curvature)



**Statement.**  

Consider the Wilson lattice gauge measure $\mu_\beta$ on $\mathscr{A}=G^E$ with $G=\mathrm{SU}(N)$, endowed with the product Haar metric $g$. Then:



1. The Ricci tensor of $(\mathscr{A},g)$ is strictly positive and proportional to $g$:

   $$

   \operatorname{Ric}_g \;=\; \kappa\, g,\qquad \kappa>0,

   $$

   independent of $\beta$.



2. Near the identity sector (small plaquette angles), the Hessian $\nabla^2 S_\beta$ is nonnegative on $T_U\mathscr{A}$ and strictly positive on horizontal directions:

   \[

   \nabla^2 S_\beta \;\ge\; 0,\quad

   \nabla^2 S_\beta \big|_{P_0 T_U\mathscr{A}} \;\ge\; \beta c_W\, g

   \]

   for some constant $c_W>0$ uniform over small-angle configurations.



3. Consequently, the Bakry–Émery tensor satisfies, on horizontal directions,

   $$

   \operatorname{Ric}_{\mu_\beta}

   \;=\;

   \operatorname{Ric}_g+\nabla^2 S_\beta

   \;\ge\;

   (\kappa+\beta c_W)\, g

   \quad\text{on } P_0 T\mathscr{A},

   $$

   in a small neighborhood of the identity configuration.



**Significance (for Yang–Mills).**  

The lower bound

$$

\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu_\beta} \;\ge\; (\kappa+\beta c_W)\,g

$$

on physical (horizontal) directions yields:



- Uniform *local convexity* of the effective potential under the pulled-back Haar metric.

- Local Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities with constants $\gtrsim \kappa+\beta c_W$.

- An effective *mass scale* $m_{\mathrm{eff}}^2 \sim \kappa + \beta c_W$ for small fluctuations, which is the geometric origin of a positive “mass term” in the linearized dynamics.

## 3. Supporting Lemmas



We now extract the implicit supporting statements from the derivation.



### Lemma 3.1 (Ricci curvature of the configuration space)



**Statement.**  

Let $(G,g_G)$ be a compact Lie group with bi-invariant metric such that

$$

\operatorname{Ric}_G \;=\; \kappa\, g_G,\qquad \kappa>0.

$$

Then, on the product manifold

$$

\mathscr{A} = G^E

$$

with product metric $g = \bigoplus_{e\in E} g_G$, we have

$$

\operatorname{Ric}_g \;=\; \kappa\, g \quad \text{on } T\mathscr{A}.

$$



In particular, for $G=\mathrm{SU}(N)$ with the normalization induced by $-\operatorname{Tr}$, one has

$$

\operatorname{Ric}_G \;=\; \kappa g_G,\qquad

\kappa = \tfrac{1}{4} C_{\mathrm{adj}}>0,

$$

where $C_{\mathrm{adj}}$ is the quadratic Casimir in the adjoint representation (exact normalization constants are not needed for the sign).



**Proof Sketch.**  

For a compact Lie group $G$ with a bi-invariant metric, $G$ is Einstein, i.e. $\operatorname{Ric}_G = \kappa g_G$ with $\kappa>0$. The product of Einstein manifolds with the same Einstein constant $\kappa$ and the product metric is again Einstein with the same constant; $\operatorname{Ric}$ acts component-wise. Therefore, on $\mathscr{A}=G^E$ with the product metric $g$, we have $\operatorname{Ric}_g = \kappa g$.

### Lemma 3.2 (Quadratic expansion of the Wilson action near identity)



**Statement.**  

Consider a configuration $U\in\mathscr{A}$ near the identity sector such that each plaquette holonomy admits a small-angle representation

$$

U_p = \exp(\theta_p),\qquad \theta_p\in\mathfrak{g},\quad \|\theta_p\|\ll 1.

$$

Then

$$

\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\exp(\theta_p)\bigr)

\;=\;

N - \tfrac12 \|\theta_p\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + O(\|\theta_p\|^3),

$$

where $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$ is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm on $\mathfrak{su}(N)$. Consequently,

$$

S_\beta(U)

\;=\;

\frac{\beta}{2} \sum_{p\in P} \|\theta_p\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + O(\theta^3),

$$

where $O(\theta^3)$ denotes cubic (and higher) terms in the collection $(\theta_p)_{p\in P}$.



**Proof Sketch.**  

For $X\in\mathfrak{su}(N)$ small, the power series expansion gives

$$

\exp(X) = I + X + \tfrac{1}{2}X^2 + O(\|X\|^3).

$$

Since $X\in\mathfrak{su}(N)$ is traceless and skew-Hermitian, $\operatorname{Tr} X=0$ and $\operatorname{Tr} X^2$ is real and non-positive. Using the inner product $\langle A,B\rangle = -\operatorname{Tr}(AB)$,

$$

\operatorname{Tr} X^2 = -\|X\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2,

$$

so

$$

\operatorname{Tr}(\exp(X)) = N + \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}(X^2) + O(\|X\|^3)

= N - \tfrac{1}{2}\|X\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + O(\|X\|^3).

$$

Taking the real part does not change the quadratic term, so

$$

\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\exp X)

= N - \tfrac{1}{2}\|X\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + O(\|X\|^3).

$$

Applying this with $X=\theta_p$ and summing over plaquettes yields the stated expansion of $S_\beta(U)$.

### Lemma 3.3 (Structure of the Wilson Hessian and positivity on horizontals)



**Statement.**  

Fix a configuration $U\in\mathscr{A}$ near identity and consider a small right-invariant variation

$$

\delta U_e = U_e X_e,\qquad X_e \in \mathfrak{g},

$$

with all other links fixed. Let $U_p$ be the oriented plaquette product and write, near identity,

$$

U_p = \exp(\theta_p),\qquad \theta_p\in\mathfrak{g}.

$$

Then:



1. To first order in the link variations $X_e$, the plaquette angles $\theta_p$ satisfy

   $$

   \theta_p

   =

   \sum_{e'\in\partial p}

   \sigma_{p,e'}\, \operatorname{Ad}_{\text{path}(e'\to p)} \bigl(X_{e'}\bigr)

   + O(X^2),

   $$

   where $\sigma_{p,e'}\in\{\pm1\}$ are incidence signs depending on orientation, and $\operatorname{Ad}_{\text{path}(e'\to p)}$ is the adjoint action obtained by transporting $X_{e'}$ along the remaining fixed links on the boundary of $p$.



2. The second variation of $S_\beta$ in the right-invariant frame is

   $$

   \delta^2 S_\beta[X]

   =

   \beta \sum_{p\in P}

   \bigl\langle \theta_p'(X), \theta_p'(X) \bigr\rangle

   =

   \beta \,\langle X, \mathcal{H}_W X \rangle,

   $$

   where

   $$

   X = (X_e)_{e\in E}\in T_U\mathscr{A}, \qquad

   \theta_p'(X) := \sum_{e'\in\partial p}

   \sigma_{p,e'}\, \operatorname{Ad}_{\text{path}(e'\to p)}(X_{e'}),

   $$

   and $\mathcal{H}_W$ is the *Wilson Hessian*, a symmetric, gauge-covariant, sparse elliptic operator on $T_U\mathscr{A}$.



3. The operator $\mathcal{H}_W$ is nonnegative:

   $$

   \mathcal{H}_W \;\ge\; 0

   $$

   as a quadratic form.



4. Modulo gauge (i.e., restricted to horizontal directions or equivalently after applying the projector $P_0$), one has

   $$

   \mathcal{H}_W \;\simeq\; C^\ast C

   $$

   for an appropriate discrete covariant derivative operator $C$, so that

   $$

   \delta^2 S_\beta[X_{\mathrm{hor}}]

   \;\ge\;

   \beta\, \lambda_{\min}(C^\ast C)\, \|X_{\mathrm{hor}}\|^2

   $$

   for $X_{\mathrm{hor}} \in P_0 T_U\mathscr{A}$, where $\lambda_{\min}(C^\ast C)>0$ on horizontals.



**Proof Sketch.**



1. The formula for $\theta_p$ arises by linearizing the group product of links around $p$:

   $$

   U_p = \cdots U_{e'} \cdots

   $$

   and using right-invariant variations $U_{e'} \mapsto U_{e'} e^{X_{e'}}$. At first order, the plaquette variation is a sum of transported Lie algebra elements $X_{e'}$, with signs $\sigma_{p,e'}$ determined by orientations and adjoint transports determined by the other links on the boundary.



2. Insert the small-angle expansion of Lemma 3.2 into $S_\beta(U)$, keeping terms up to second order. The second variation is the sum of squared norms of the linearized plaquette angles $\theta_p'(X)$, which can be written as a quadratic form $\beta\langle X, \mathcal{H}_W X \rangle$.



3. Since $\theta_p'(X)$ depends linearly on $X$ and $S_\beta$ at quadratic order is a sum of squared norms $\|\theta_p'(X)\|^2$, $\mathcal{H}_W$ is a sum of positive semidefinite contributions, hence $\mathcal{H}_W\ge 0$.



4. Gauge directions $X=\nabla\phi$ produce no plaquette curvature at quadratic order (the corresponding $\theta_p'(X)$ cancel), which yields a kernel in $\mathcal{H}_W$ precisely along vertical directions. On horizontal directions (after projection by $P_0$ or modulo gauge), the discrete operator has no kernel and is of the form $C^\ast C$ with a strictly positive smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(C^\ast C)>0$ in a small-angle neighborhood. This yields the claimed inequality.

### Lemma 3.4 (Gauge directions and horizontal projector)



**Statement.**  

Let $X=\nabla\phi$ be a pure gauge (vertical) variation at the identity sector. Then, to quadratic order, the plaquette angle variations cancel:

$$

\theta_p'(X) = 0

$$

for all plaquettes $p$. Consequently, the second variation $\delta^2 S_\beta[X]$ vanishes along pure gauge directions. Thus:



- $\ker \mathcal{H}_W$ contains all vertical directions generated by infinitesimal gauge transformations.

- Statements of strict positivity for $\mathcal{H}_W$ (or $\nabla^2 S_\beta$) must be formulated either:

  - on the gauge quotient space, or

  - on the horizontal subspace $P_0 T_U\mathscr{A}$.



**Proof Sketch.**  

A pure gauge transformation is locally $U_e \mapsto g_{x} U_e g_{y}^{-1}$ for vertices $x,y$ at the ends of edge $e$. Infinitesimally, this corresponds to variations $X_e$ that are differences of Lie algebra elements at neighboring vertices. Around a plaquette, the product of such gauge transformations cancels, so that the plaquette holonomy remains trivial at quadratic order, i.e. $\theta_p'(X)=0$. Thus, the second variation of the Wilson action vanishes along such vertical modes.

### 4.2 Second variation of the Wilson action (pulled-back Haar metric)



Fix a link $e\in E$ and consider a small right-invariant variation of the form

$$

\delta U_e = U_e X_e,\qquad X_e\in\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{su}(N),

$$

keeping all other links fixed.  



For a plaquette $p$ containing $e$ (denoted $p\ni e$), write the plaquette holonomy in a neighborhood of identity as

$$

U_p = \cdots U_e \cdots = \exp(\theta_p),

$$

with a small Lie-algebra angle $\theta_p\in\mathfrak{g}$.



Near identity, the linearized relation for $\theta_p$ is (Lemma 3.3):

$$

U_p = \exp(\theta_p),\qquad

\theta_p =

\sum_{e'\in \partial p}

\sigma_{p,e'}\,

\operatorname{Ad}_{\text{path}(e'\to p)}(X_{e'})

+ O(X^2),

$$

where:



- $\sigma_{p,e'} \in \{\pm 1\}$ are incidence signs depending on the relative orientation of $e'$ and the plaquette $p$;

- $\operatorname{Ad}_{\text{path}(e'\to p)}$ is the adjoint transport along the part of the plaquette boundary from $e'$ to a chosen base edge (or reference point), determined by the other (fixed) links on the boundary of $p$;

- $O(X^2)$ denotes terms quadratic and higher in the link variations $(X_e)$.



Using the expansion from Lemma 3.2:

$$

\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\exp\theta_p)

=

N - \frac{1}{2}\|\theta_p\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + O(\|\theta_p\|^3),

$$

we obtain, for configurations near the identity:

$$

S_\beta(U)

=

\beta \sum_{p\in P}

\Bigl( N - \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\exp\theta_p) \Bigr)

=

\frac{\beta}{2} \sum_{p\in P} \|\theta_p\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2

+ O(\theta^3).

$$



Taking the second variation in the right-invariant frame gives the quadratic form

\begin{align*}

\delta^2 S_\beta[X]

&=

\beta \sum_{p\in P}

\bigl\langle \theta_p'(X), \theta_p'(X)\bigr\rangle \\

&=

\beta\, \langle X, \mathcal{H}_W X\rangle,

\end{align*}

where:



- $X = (X_e)_{e\in E}\in T_U\mathscr{A}$,

- $\theta_p'(X)$ is the linearization of $\theta_p$ in $X$,

- $\mathcal{H}_W$ is the Wilson Hessian on $T_U\mathscr{A}$.



The operator $\mathcal{H}_W$ is explicitly a sparse, gauge-covariant elliptic block operator built from the incidence structure of edges and plaquettes and the adjoint transports around plaquettes.



**Crucial property:**

$$

\mathcal{H}_W \;\ge\; 0,

$$

since it appears as a sum of squared norms of linear combinations of $X_e$’s.



On *horizontal directions* (modulo gauge, or after projection by the Gauss constraint projector $P_0$), one has approximately

$$

\mathcal{H}_W \;\simeq\; C^\ast C,

$$

for a discrete covariant differential $C$ (e.g. lattice curl / coboundary operator with $\operatorname{Ad}$-transport). Consequently,

$$

\delta^2 S_\beta[X_{\text{hor}}]

\;\ge\;

\beta\, \lambda_{\min}(C^\ast C)\, \|X_{\text{hor}}\|^2,

$$

for all $X_{\text{hor}} \in P_0 T_U\mathscr{A}$, where $\lambda_{\min}(C^\ast C)>0$ on horizontals near identity.



Thus, with respect to the Haar metric $g$, the Hessian $\nabla^2 S_\beta$ is:



- **nonnegative** on all directions in $T_U\mathscr{A}$;

- **strictly positive** on horizontal directions near identity, with a lower bound proportional to $\beta$.

### 4.4 Bakry–Émery tensor and emergent mass



We now combine curvature and potential.



By definition,

$$

\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu_\beta}

=

\operatorname{Ric}_g + \nabla^2 S_\beta.

$$

Using the baseline Ricci curvature from Lemma 3.1 and the positivity of the Hessian on horizontals from Lemma 3.3, we have (on $P_0 T\mathscr{A}$)

\begin{align*}

\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu_\beta}

&=

\operatorname{Ric}_g + \nabla^2 S_\beta \\

&\ge

\kappa\, g + \beta\, P_0^\top \mathcal{H}_W P_0.

\end{align*}



Near identity configurations (small plaquette angles), there exists a uniform constant $c_W>0$ such that on horizontal directions

$$

\bigl\langle X, P_0^\top \mathcal{H}_W P_0\, X \bigr\rangle

\;\ge\;

c_W \|X\|^2,\qquad X\in P_0 T_U\mathscr{A}.

$$

Therefore,

$$

\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu_\beta}

\;\ge\;

(\kappa + \beta c_W)\, g

\quad\text{on } P_0 T\mathscr{A}.

$$



This is the **mass-from-geometry** statement:



- The Haar product geometry contributes a “background” curvature term $\kappa$.

- The Wilson action contributes an *emergent mass* term $\beta c_W$ from the Hessian in the pulled-back Haar metric.

- The sign is strictly **positive** on physical (horizontal) fluctuations.



Summarizing in boxed form:



\[

=

\operatorname{Ric}_g + \nabla^2 S_\beta

\;\ge\;
