# One-step OS dissipation as a boundary Dirichlet form (and the correct scale-\(a\) comparison)

\begin{abstract}
A recurring trap: trying to compare a one-step OS dissipation form \(\langle F,(I-T_a)F\rangle\) to the raw configuration gradient energy \(\int|\nabla F|^2\) cannot hold uniformly, because the left side is bounded while the right can be arbitrarily large on a compact manifold. This note records the clean correction: the OS one-step dissipation identifies exactly with a Dirichlet form of a boundary Markov operator \(K_a\), and the natural analytic comparison is to a **scale-\(a\) regularized** Dirichlet form \(\langle f,(I-P_a)f\rangle\) built from the configuration diffusion semigroup.
\end{abstract}

## 1. The boundedness obstruction (why the naive comparison is impossible)

Let \(K\) be any Markov operator on \(L^2(\nu)\) with \(\|K\|\le 1\). Its Dirichlet form
\[
\mathcal E_K(f,f):=\langle f,(I-K)f\rangle
\]
is bounded by \(2\|f\|_2^2\). On a compact manifold (or compact group), there exist \(L^2\)-normalized high-frequency functions with arbitrarily large \(\int |\nabla f|^2\). Therefore no constant \(c>0\) can satisfy
\[
\mathcal E_K(f,f)\ \ge\ c\int |\nabla f|^2\,d\nu\qquad\text{for all }f\in H^1.
\]
So any correct “one-step OS vs gradient” bridge must compare against a **bounded** gradient surrogate.

## 2. Strip compression: the boundary kernel \(\mathcal K_a\)

Work on a finite space–time lattice \(\Lambda=\{0,1,\dots,T\}\times\Lambda_s\) and choose the OS reflection plane between time slices \(t=0\) and \(t=1\). Let \(\Omega=G^{E(\Lambda)}\) and let the Euclidean measure have the local plaquette form
\[
 d\mu(U)=\frac{1}{Z}\Big(\prod_{p\in P(\Lambda)} w(U_p(U))\Big)\Big(\prod_{e\in E(\Lambda)} dH(U_e)\Big),
\]
with reflection-positive plaquette weight \(w\) (e.g. Wilson or heat kernel).

Let \(\sigma\) denote the spatial link configuration on slice \(t=0\) and \(\sigma'\) that on slice \(t=1\). Let \(U_0\) denote the time-like links between these slices.

Define the one-step **strip kernel**
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_a(\sigma,\sigma')
:= \int W_a(\sigma,\sigma',U_0)\,\prod_{x\in\Lambda_s} dH(U_0(x)),
}
\]
where \(W_a\) is the product of plaquette weights on straddling plaquettes, with the standard half-weighting of spatial plaquettes on each time slice to enforce symmetry.

## 3. Boundary realization of the OS inner product

Let \(\mathcal A^+\) be the positive-time algebra (observables depending only on links in \(t\ge 1\)). Reflection positivity yields the OS sesquilinear form
\[
(F,G)_{\mathrm{OS}} := \int (\Theta F)\,G\,d\mu.
\]
A locality/Markov-property conditioning argument gives an isometric embedding
\[
J:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{OS}}\hookrightarrow L^2(\Sigma,d\nu)
\]
into a boundary space \(\Sigma\) of slice variables with a boundary measure \(\nu\), such that
\[
(F,G)_{\mathrm{OS}} = \int_{\Sigma} \overline{JF(\sigma)}\,JG(\sigma)\,d\nu(\sigma).
\]

Under the same compression, the OS transfer operator becomes a self-adjoint Markov operator \(K_a\) on \(L^2(\nu)\):
\[
(F,T_a G)_{\mathrm{OS}} = \int_{\Sigma} \overline{JF(\sigma)}\,(K_a JG)(\sigma)\,d\nu(\sigma).
\]

## 4. The key exact identity: OS dissipation = boundary Dirichlet form

For \(F\in\mathcal A^+\), the one-step OS dissipation is
\[
\langle F,(I-T_a)F\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{OS}}}.
\]
The compression gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\langle F,(I-T_a)F\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{OS}}}
= \mathcal E_{K_a}(JF,JF)
:= \langle JF,(I-K_a)JF\rangle_{L^2(\nu)}.
}
\]
This turns the one-step OS gap problem into a spectral-gap/Poincar\'{e} problem for a concrete boundary Markov operator.

## 5. The correct bounded comparison: a scale-\(a\) Dirichlet form

Let \((P_t)_{t\ge 0}\) be the configuration diffusion semigroup generated by the reversible diffusion operator \(L\) (with \(\Gamma(f)=|\nabla f|^2\)). Define the bounded **scale-\(a\)** Dirichlet form
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}}^{(a)}(f,f):=\langle f,(I-P_a)f\rangle_{L^2(\nu)}.
}
\]
It admits the gradient representation
\[
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}}^{(a)}(f,f)=\int_0^a \int |\nabla P_{t/2}f|^2\,d\nu\,dt,
\]
so it measures “gradient energy smoothed at scale \(a\)” and is automatically bounded by \(2\|f\|_2^2\).

## 6. A usable theorem statement (the comparison you can actually hope to prove)

\begin{theorem}[One-step OS vs scale-\(a\) Dirichlet comparison]
There exists a constant \(c>0\) (depending on \(d,G\), and the plaquette weight class, but not on volume) such that for all \(F\in\mathcal A^+\),
\[
\langle F,(I-T_a)F\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{OS}}}
\ \ge\ c\,\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}}^{(a)}(JF,JF).
\]
\end{theorem}

In the project notes, this bound is obtained by:

1. **Local coercivity on a plaquette block** (using group Poincar\'{e} on a fixed-size block),
2. **Smoothing inequality** \(\int_0^a \!|\nabla P_{t/2}f|^2\le a\int |\nabla f|^2\),
3. **Bounded-overlap summation** over straddling plaquettes.

Finally, if the diffusion has a spectral gap \(\mathrm{gap}(-L)\ge \lambda_*>0\), then semigroup calculus gives
\[
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}}^{(a)}(f,f)\ge (1-e^{-a\lambda_*})\,\|f-\nu(f)\|_2^2,
\]
which together with the theorem yields a uniform spectral gap for \(K_a\), and hence a one-step gap for \(T_a\) and a mass gap lower bound via \(-a^{-1}\log\lambda_1(T_a)\).

## 7. Why this is an actual contribution

Even if one never proves a Yang--Mills mass gap, this “scale-\(a\) correction” is a concrete, falsifiable mathematical improvement: it replaces an impossible comparison by the correct bounded object, while keeping the same physical output (a per-step contraction rate converting to a mass scale).
