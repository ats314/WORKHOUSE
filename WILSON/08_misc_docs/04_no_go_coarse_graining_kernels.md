# Two No-go Results: Cross-Scale Consistency and Gauge-Covariant Markov Coarse Graining

This note extracts two “landmine defusers.”

These are valuable because they **prevent** the project from spending months trying to prove something that is structurally false.

---

## 1. No-go: fixed-cutoff OS data cannot imply gap permanence across scales

### Informal core statement (sharp version)

Suppose that at each lattice spacing $a$ you have:

- an OS reflection-positive Euclidean measure $\mu_a$,
- an OS Hilbert space and Hamiltonian $(\mathcal H_a,H_a)$ reconstructed from $\mu_a$,
- and a fixed-cutoff spectral gap (equivalently: Euclidean exponential time decay at scale $a$).

Then **nothing in that fixed-cutoff data alone** can force the existence (or uniqueness) of a cross-scale map
\[
\Pi_{a\to a'}:\Omega_a\to\Omega_{a'}
\quad\text{with}\quad
(\Pi_{a\to a'})_\#\mu_a=\mu_{a'}
\]
satisfying the required equivariances (reflection/time-translation compatibility, etc.) uniformly over all admissible families.

### Mechanism

You can construct OS-preserving “UV twists” that keep each $(\mathcal H_a,H_a)$ unitarily equivalent (hence preserve the gap at each $a$) while **destroying** any plausible candidate $\Pi$ commuting with reflection and time translation.

### Takeaway

This cleanly separates:

- **intrascale**: fixed-cutoff clustering $\Rightarrow$ fixed-cutoff gap,
- **interscale**: cutoff-removal needs *extra architecture* (a renormalization trajectory / projective system / coarse-graining scheme).

It also explains why the continuum step must be treated as an additional hypothesis, not something that follows automatically from OS axioms at each scale.

(Compare with the “do-no-harm” lemmas in `01_reflection_positive_rg_and_gap_persistence.md`.)

## 2. No-go: (A4)+(A5) Markov coarse-graining kernel obstruction for nontrivial $G$

This is the more algebraic obstruction.

### Setup

Let $G$ be a nontrivial compact Lie group (e.g. any non-abelian compact group).
Let $\Omega_a=G^{E(\Lambda_a)}$ and $\Omega_{a'}=G^{E(\Lambda_{a'})}$, with gauge groups
\[
\mathcal G_a = G^{V(\Lambda_a)},\qquad \mathcal G_{a'} = G^{V(\Lambda_{a'})}.
\]

Let $\Pi_{a\to a'}$ be a **Markov kernel** (a conditional expectation operator).

### Assumptions that cannot both hold

- **(A5)** $\Pi$ is a conditional expectation onto a $\sigma$-algebra generated by **gauge-invariant block variables** (diameter $\asymp a'$).  
  Hence $\Pi(g\cdot U)=\Pi(U)$ $\mu_a$-a.s. for all $g\in\mathcal G_a$.

- **(A4)** $\Pi$ is gauge covariant with respect to an induced coarse gauge action whose image contains $\mathcal G_{a'}$:  
  \[
  \Pi(g\cdot U)=g'\cdot \Pi(U).
  \]

### Contradiction

Combining (A5) and (A4) forces
\[
g'\cdot\Pi(U)=\Pi(U)\quad\text{for all }g'\in\mathcal G_{a'},
\]
so $\Pi(U)$ lies in the fixed-point set of the $\mathcal G_{a'}$-action.

For nontrivial $G$, the fixed-point set is empty (because gauge acts independently at endpoints).
Contradiction.

### Takeaway

Any “reflection-equivariant Markov property” must weaken or alter (A4)/(A5).

## 3. How to escape the obstruction (design space)

Here are the “escape hatches” that keep the project honest:

1. **Drop Markovness**: use a deterministic blocking map $P:\Omega_a\to\Omega_{a'}$ (pushforward), not a Markov kernel.  
   RP permanence only needs a map (see `01`), not Markov structure.

2. **Change the coarse variable**: coarse variables can live in a **quotient** or a different object (e.g. gauge orbit space, or a connection on a coarser bundle), not in $G^{E(\Lambda_{a'})}$ with endpoint gauge action.

3. **Gauge-fix before coarse graining**: a gauge choice can eliminate the endpoint redundancy that causes the fixed-point obstruction.

4. **Weaken gauge covariance**: demand only covariance under a *restricted* subgroup (e.g. “diagonal” gauge), or demand covariance only on a subalgebra.

Each escape hatch has costs, but the no-go theorem tells you precisely which costs are unavoidable.

## 4. Link to numerics: locality really matters

The project’s numerical notes include a “locality stress test”:

- local (Laplacian-type) terms have smallest eigenvalues decreasing with $L$ as expected,
- nonlocal/mean-field modifications can pin eigenvalues and distort the infrared structure,
- and can reduce the low-energy gap even while raising overall energies.

This strongly suggests that any coarse-graining / effective description that accidentally introduces nonlocality may sabotage the very gap mechanism one is trying to preserve.

---

## 5. Why this is “new theory potential”

These no-go results point toward a larger conceptual lesson:

> **The continuum limit is not just “take $a\downarrow 0$.”  
> It is a compatibility problem between symmetry (gauge, reflection), positivity (OS), and the categorical shape of coarse-graining maps.**

That suggests a fruitful direction: formulate RG steps as morphisms in a category of reflected probability spaces (or reflected *groupoids* when gauge is treated intrinsically), where pushforward permanence is the basic functorial property, and Markov kernels are a strict sub-class that may be too rigid in non-abelian gauge theory.
