# Dirichlet-form coarse-graining via conditional expectation and $O(g(a)^2)$ energy loss

## 1. Dirichlet forms and conditional expectation

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mu)$ be a probability space and let $(\mathcal E,\mathcal D(\mathcal E))$ be a symmetric Dirichlet form on $L^2(\mu)$.

Let $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{coarse}}\subseteq\mathcal F$ be a sub-$\sigma$-algebra (the “coarse observables”), and let
\[
P := \mathbb E_\mu[\,\cdot\mid\mathcal F_{\mathrm{coarse}}\,]
\]
be conditional expectation (a Markov contraction on $L^p(\mu)$ for $1\le p\le\infty$).

For any $f\in\mathcal D(\mathcal E)$, decompose
\[
f = \bar f + \xi,
\qquad \bar f := Pf,
\qquad \xi := (I-P)f.
\]
Then $P\xi=0$ and $\bar f$ is $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{coarse}}$-measurable.

## 2. Energy monotonicity and an exact “energy loss” inequality

A general Markov/Jensen contraction property for Dirichlet forms gives
\[
\mathcal E(Pf,Pf)\le \mathcal E(f,f).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Moreover, the energy difference is controlled by the discarded component:
\[
0\le \mathcal E(f,f)-\mathcal E(Pf,Pf)\le \mathcal E(\xi,\xi).
\tag{2.2}
\]
In particular, there is no linear-in-fluctuation term in (2.2) because $P\xi=0$ centers the fine component.

## 3. Blockwise conditional Poincaré as a sufficient mechanism

Assume $\Omega$ factors into blocks $B$ (e.g. $(2a)$-blocks) and the coarse $\sigma$-algebra is generated by block observables.  Write $\mu(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_{\mathrm{coarse}})$ for the conditional law of fine variables inside a block given the coarse configuration.

A sufficient condition for an $O(g^2)$ control of the lost energy is a **conditional blockwise Poincaré inequality**:

> **(BP)** There exists $\lambda_{\mathrm{block}}>0$ such that for every block $B$ and every smooth $\phi$ depending only on fine variables in $B$ with $\mathbb E[\phi\mid\mathcal F_{\mathrm{coarse}}]=0$,
> \[
> \mathrm{Var}_{\mu(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_{\mathrm{coarse}})}(\phi)
> \le \frac{g(a)^2}{\lambda_{\mathrm{block}}}\int |\nabla_{\mathrm{fine}}\phi|^2\,d\mu(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_{\mathrm{coarse}}).
> \tag{3.1}
> \]

Heuristically, the factor $g(a)^2$ comes from the $1/g(a)^2$ scaling in the reversible fine generator
\(
\mathcal L^{\mathrm{fine}}\approx \Delta_{\mathrm{fine}}-\nabla(S/g(a)^2)\cdot\nabla
\),
so that the conditional fine spectral gap is expected to be $\asymp 1/g(a)^2$.

If (BP) holds uniformly in coarse boundary data, then one can bound the fine fluctuation component $\xi=(I-P)f$ by summing the blockwise inequalities, yielding a relative energy estimate of the form
\[
\mathcal E(\xi,\xi)\le C\,g(a)^2\,\mathcal E(\bar f,\bar f),
\tag{3.2}
\]
and consequently
\[
\mathcal E(f,f)-\mathcal E(Pf,Pf)=O(g(a)^2)\,\mathcal E(\bar f,\bar f).
\tag{3.3}
\]

## 4. A gauge-theoretic obstruction for naive Markov kernels

A separate (short) obstruction arises for gauge theories if one simultaneously demands:

- (A5) the coarse-graining kernel is conditional expectation onto a $\sigma$-algebra generated by **gauge-invariant** block variables, and
- (A4) the kernel is **gauge covariant** with respect to an induced coarse gauge action whose image contains the full coarse gauge group.

In nontrivial compact gauge groups, (A4)+(A5) forces the coarse variable to be fixed by the entire coarse gauge group, which is incompatible with independent endpoint actions.

> **Lemma 4.1 (incompatibility of (A4)+(A5)).** Let $G$ be a nontrivial compact Lie group and let $\Pi$ be a Markov kernel from fine lattice configurations to coarse configurations.  If (A5) makes $\Pi$ fine-gauge invariant almost surely and (A4) makes $\Pi$ coarse-gauge covariant with image containing the full coarse gauge group, then $\Pi(U)$ must be fixed by the full coarse gauge action.  For nontrivial $G$, this forces degeneracy/contradiction.

*Proof.* (A5) gives $\Pi(g\cdot U)=\Pi(U)$ for all fine gauge transforms $g$.  (A4) gives $\Pi(g\cdot U)=g'\cdot \Pi(U)$ where $g'$ ranges over a subgroup containing the full coarse gauge group.  Combining yields $g'\cdot\Pi(U)=\Pi(U)$ for all coarse gauge transforms $g'$, i.e. $\Pi(U)$ is a coarse-gauge fixed point.  For nontrivial $G$, the fixed-point set is incompatible with independent endpoint actions.
\hfill $\square$

This suggests that any reflection-equivariant Markov/coarse-graining architecture must weaken at least one of these requirements (e.g. gauge fixing, working on gauge quotients, or using nontrivial bundle-valued coarse variables).
