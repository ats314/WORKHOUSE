### 1. Lattice and Tangent-Space Setup

#### 1.1 Lattice and link variables

Let $\Lambda$ be a finite hypercubic lattice (e.g., with periodic or other boundary conditions), with:

- vertex set $V(\Lambda)$,
- oriented edge (bond) set $E(\Lambda)$.

For each oriented bond $b \in E(\Lambda)$ we write:

- $x(b)$ for its initial vertex,
- $y(b)$ for its terminal vertex.

Gauge group: $G = \mathrm{SU}(N)$, Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{su}(N)$.

A configuration is an assignment
$$
U = (U_b)_{b \in E(\Lambda)}, \qquad U_b \in G.
$$

We fix a **reference configuration** $U_0 = (U_{0,b})$. In the CLEAN proof P09, the main structural result is specifically stated at the **flat connection** $U_0 = I$, i.e.
$$
U_{0,b} = I \in \mathrm{SU}(N) \quad \forall\, b.
$$

Small fluctuations are parametrized by Lie algebra variables $X_b \in \mathfrak{g}$ via
$$
U_b = U_{0,b} \, e^{X_b} = e^{X_b}
\qquad\text{for $U_0 = I$.}
$$

Collectively, $X = (X_b)_{b \in E(\Lambda)}$ is a tangent vector in
$$
T_{U_0}\mathcal{A} \cong \mathfrak{g}^{|E(\Lambda)|},
$$
where $\mathcal{A}$ is the space of all lattice gauge fields.

We equip $T_{U_0}\mathcal{A}$ with the natural inner product
$$
\langle X, Y \rangle 
:= \sum_{b \in E(\Lambda)} \kappa(X_b, Y_b),
$$
where $\kappa$ is an $\mathrm{Ad}$‑invariant inner product on $\mathfrak{g}$ (e.g. minus the Killing form or $-\operatorname{Tr}(XY)$ in the fundamental representation, since $\mathfrak{su}(N)$ is anti‑Hermitian).

#### 1.2 Wilson action and its Hessian

The Wilson action on the lattice is
$$
S_W(U) 
= \beta \sum_{p} \Big( 1 - \frac{1}{N} \Re\operatorname{Tr} U_p \Big),
$$
where the sum runs over oriented plaquettes $p$, and
$$
U_p = U_{b_1} U_{b_2} U_{b_3}^{-1} U_{b_4}^{-1}
$$
is the ordered product of link variables around $p$ in a fixed orientation.

**Definition 1.1 (Wilson Hessian)** (P09)  
At a fixed background $U_0$ (here taken as $I$),
$$
H_W = \nabla^2 S_W\big|_{U_0}
$$
is the Hessian of $S_W$ at $U_0$, viewed as a quadratic form on $T_{U_0}\mathcal{A} \cong \mathfrak{g}^{|E(\Lambda)|}$.

Concretely, for tangent vectors $X, Y$,
$$
\langle X, H_W Y \rangle
:= \left.\frac{\partial^2}{\partial s \partial t}\right|_{s=t=0} 
 S_W\big( U_0 e^{sX + tY} \big).
$$

### 2. Gauge Directions and the Physical Subspace

#### 2.1 Lattice gauge transformations and their generator

A lattice gauge transformation is a collection
$$
g = (g_x)_{x \in V(\Lambda)}, \quad g_x \in G,
$$
acting on link variables by
$$
U_b \;\mapsto\; U_b^g := g_{x(b)} U_b g_{y(b)}^{-1}.
$$

Infinitesimally, write $g_x = e^{\xi_x}$ for $\xi_x \in \mathfrak{g}$ small. At the background $U_0 = I$, the induced variation on the link in direction $b$ is
$$
\delta U_b
= \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} \left(e^{t\xi_{x(b)}} U_b e^{-t\xi_{y(b)}}\right)
= \xi_{x(b)} U_b - U_b \xi_{y(b)}.
$$
At $U_0 = I$ (so $U_b = I$),
$$
\delta U_b = \xi_{x(b)} - \xi_{y(b)}.
$$

If we view $X_b$ as the log‑coordinate of $U_b$ at $U_0$, then an infinitesimal gauge transformation corresponds to the tangent vector
$$
X_b = \xi_{y(b)} - \operatorname{Ad}_{U_b^{-1}}(\xi_{x(b)}).
$$
At $U_b = I$ this reduces to
$$
X_b = \xi_{y(b)} - \xi_{x(b)}.
$$

This matches P09:

**Definition 1.2 (Gauge generator)** (P09)  
For $\xi = (\xi_x) \in \mathfrak{su}(N)^{|V(\Lambda)|}$,
$$
(G\xi)_b
= \xi_{y(b)} - \operatorname{Ad}_{U_b^{-1}}\big(\xi_{x(b)}\big).
$$

At $U_0 = I$, this simplifies to
$$
(G\xi)_b = \xi_{y(b)} - \xi_{x(b)}.
$$

Thus $G: \mathfrak{g}^{|V|} \to \mathfrak{g}^{|E|}$ is the **discrete exterior derivative** on 0‑forms (vertex fields) with values in $\mathfrak{g}$, acting as:
$$
(G\xi)_b = (d\xi)_b = \xi_{y(b)} - \xi_{x(b)}.
$$

The **range** of $G$,
$$
\operatorname{Ran} G = \{G\xi: \xi \in \mathfrak{g}^{|V|} \},
$$
is precisely the tangent space to the gauge orbit through $U_0$: infinitesimal pure gauge directions.

#### 2.2 Physical subspace

**Definition 1.3 (Physical subspace)** (P09)  
The physical subspace is defined as the orthogonal complement of the gauge directions:
$$
\mathrm{Phys} = (\mathrm{Ran} G)^\perp
\subset \mathfrak{g}^{|E(\Lambda)|}.
$$

Orthogonal complement is taken with respect to the inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ defined above.

Concretely, a tangent field $X = (X_b)$ is in $\mathrm{Phys}$ iff
$$
\langle X, G\xi \rangle = 0
\quad\text{for all}\quad \xi = (\xi_x).
$$

Define the adjoint operator
$$
G^*: \mathfrak{g}^{|E|} \to \mathfrak{g}^{|V|},
$$
by
$$
\langle X, G\xi \rangle = \langle G^*X, \xi \rangle_{\text{vertex}}
\quad\text{for all}\quad X, \xi,
$$
where
$$
\langle \eta, \zeta\rangle_{\text{vertex}} 
:= \sum_{x \in V(\Lambda)} \kappa(\eta_x, \zeta_x).
$$

Then
$$
\mathrm{Phys}
= \ker G^*.
$$

In discrete differential form language on a finite graph, $G$ is the discrete exterior derivative $d: \Omega^0 \to \Omega^1$, and $G^*$ is (up to sign) the codifferential $d^*: \Omega^1 \to \Omega^0$.

Thus:
- $\operatorname{Ran}G$ is the space of exact 1‑forms (infinitesimal pure gauges),
- $\mathrm{Phys} = \ker G^*$ is the space of **co-closed** 1‑forms (transverse fields).

This is the beginning of the standard **discrete Hodge decomposition**.

### 3. Structure of the Wilson Hessian

#### 3.1 Decomposition at the flat connection

At the flat configuration $U_0 = I$, the Wilson action is **gauge invariant**:
$$
S_W(U^g) = S_W(U),
$$
for all small gauge transformations $g$.

Therefore, along the gauge orbit,
$$
S_W(U_0 e^{G\xi}) = S_W(U_0) \quad \forall\, \xi.
$$
Differentiating twice in directions $G\xi$,
$$
\langle G\xi, H_W G\xi \rangle = 0 
\quad\text{for all }\xi.
$$
Thus
$$
H_W(G\xi) = 0, \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad \operatorname{Ran}G \subset \ker H_W.
$$

Because $H_W$ is symmetric with respect to $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, it preserves $\operatorname{Ran}G$ and its orthogonal complement. Hence we can decompose
$$
T_{U_0}\mathcal{A}
= \operatorname{Ran}G \;\oplus\; \mathrm{Phys},
$$
and write the Hessian as
$$
H_W
= H_{\text{gauge}} \oplus H_{\text{phys}},
$$
where:

- $H_{\text{gauge}}$ is $H_W$ restricted to $\operatorname{Ran}G$ (and is identically $0$ there),
- $H_{\text{phys}}$ is $H_W$ restricted to $\mathrm{Phys}$.

This matches P09:

**Theorem 2.1** (P09, restated precisely at $U_0 = I$)  
At the flat connection $U_0 = I$,
$$
H_W = H_{\text{gauge}} + H_{\text{phys}},
$$
where:
- $H_{\text{gauge}}$ is zero on physical directions and acts on gauge directions,
- $H_{\text{phys}}$ acts on the physical subspace.

In operator-theoretic terms,
$$
H_W|_{\operatorname{Ran}G} = 0, \qquad
H_W|_{\mathrm{Phys}} = H_{\text{phys}}.
$$

#### 3.2 Wilson Hessian as a discrete 1‑form Laplacian

P09 states:

**Theorem 2.2 (Wilson Hessian Formula)** (P09)  
On the physical subspace:
$$
H_W\big|_{\text{phys}} = \beta \cdot M,
$$
where $M$ is the lattice 1‑form Laplacian restricted to physical modes. Eigenvalues of $M$: $0 < \lambda_1 \leq \lambda_2 \leq \dots$.

We now make this explicit.

##### 3.2.1 Small-field expansion of the Wilson action

Near $U_0=I$, parametrize
$$
U_b = e^{X_b}, \quad X_b \in \mathfrak{g}, \quad \|X_b\| \text{ small}.
$$

For a plaquette $p$ with oriented boundary $(b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)$, the plaquette variable is
$$
U_p = U_{b_1} U_{b_2} U_{b_3}^{-1} U_{b_4}^{-1}
= e^{X_{b_1}} e^{X_{b_2}} e^{-X_{b_3}} e^{-X_{b_4}}.
$$

To second order in $X$, we can write
$$
U_p = \exp\big(F_p(X) + O(X^3)\big),
$$
where
$$
F_p(X)
= X_{b_1} + X_{b_2} - X_{b_3} - X_{b_4} + O(X^2).
$$

Thus
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(U_p)
= \operatorname{Tr}\left( I + F_p(X) + \frac{1}{2}F_p(X)^2 + O(X^3) \right)
= N + \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\big(F_p(X)^2\big) + O(X^3),
$$
since $\operatorname{Tr}(F_p(X)) = 0$ for $F_p(X)\in\mathfrak{su}(N)$.

Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
1 - \frac{1}{N} \Re \operatorname{Tr} U_p
&= 1 - \frac{1}{N} \Re \left( N + \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}(F_p(X)^2) + O(X^3) \right)\\
&= -\frac{1}{2N} \Re \operatorname{Tr}\big(F_p(X)^2\big) + O(X^3),
\end{aligned}
\]
since the constant term $1 - \frac{1}{N}\Re(N) = 0$.

Therefore, up to quadratic order,
$$
S_W(U) 
= \beta \sum_p \left( -\frac{1}{2N} \Re \operatorname{Tr}\big(F_p(X)^2\big) \right) + O(\|X\|^3).
$$

Since we are working with an inner product $\kappa(X,Y) = -\operatorname{Tr}(XY)$ (or any $\mathrm{Ad}$‑invariant equivalent), we can write
$$
S_W(U)
= \frac{\beta}{2} \sum_p \langle F_p(X), F_p(X)\rangle_p + O(\|X\|^3),
$$
where $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_p$ is the inner product on $\mathfrak{g}$ (possibly with some normalization absorbed into $\beta$; we do not track those since we only care about positivity and proportionality to a Laplacian).

Thus (to quadratic order),
$$
S_W(U)
\simeq \frac{\beta}{2} \sum_p \|F_p(X)\|^2.
$$

The Hessian $H_W$ at $X=0$ is exactly the quadratic form associated with this expression:
$$
\langle X, H_W X \rangle
= \beta \sum_p \langle F_p(X), F_p(X)\rangle_p.
$$

This is the discrete analog of the continuum magnetic energy $\int \|F\|^2$.

##### 3.2.2 From curvature to a 1‑form Laplacian

Observe that $F_p(X)$ is a discrete **curl** (or $dX$ on 1‑forms giving 2‑forms):

- Let $\Omega^1(\Lambda, \mathfrak{g}) = \mathfrak{g}^{|E(\Lambda)|}$ denote lattice 1‑forms.
- Let $\Omega^2(\Lambda, \mathfrak{g})$ denote plaquette‑based 2‑forms.

Define a discrete exterior derivative
$$
d: \Omega^1 \to \Omega^2
$$
by, for each plaquette $p$ with boundary $(b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)$,
$$
(dX)_p = X_{b_1} + X_{b_2} - X_{b_3} - X_{b_4}.
$$

Then $F_p(X) = (dX)_p + O(X^2)$, so at quadratic order,
$$
S_W(U)
= \frac{\beta}{2} \sum_p \|(dX)_p\|^2
= \frac{\beta}{2} \langle dX, dX\rangle_{2\text{-forms}}.
$$

This gives
$$
\langle X, H_W X \rangle
= \beta \langle dX, dX\rangle_{2\text{-forms}}
= \beta \langle X, d^* d X \rangle_{1\text{-forms}},
$$
where $d^*: \Omega^2 \to \Omega^1$ is the adjoint (codifferential) with respect to the chosen inner products on 1‑ and 2‑forms.

Thus, at $U_0 = I$,
$$
H_W = \beta\, d^*d \quad \text{on all of } \Omega^1.
$$

Since we already know that $H_W$ vanishes on $\operatorname{Ran}G = \operatorname{Ran}d$ due to gauge invariance, and since $d^*d$ vanishes on exact 1‑forms if and only if $d^*d(d\xi) = 0$, one sees the algebraic consistency:

- $d^*d$ is positive semi-definite.
- $\operatorname{ker}(d^*d)$ contains (and, modulo topological subtleties, coincides with) the space of discrete harmonic 1‑forms.

The **1‑form Laplacian** on the lattice is usually defined as
$$
\Delta_1 := d^* d + d d^* : \Omega^1 \to \Omega^1.
$$

On the subspace $\ker d^*$ of co-closed forms (which is precisely the physical subspace $\mathrm{Phys} = (\operatorname{Ran}G)^\perp$), we have
$$
\Delta_1|_{\ker d^*} = d^*d.
$$

Therefore,
$$
H_W\big|_{\mathrm{Phys}}
= \beta\, d^* d\big|_{\ker d^*}
= \beta\, \Delta_1\big|_{\ker d^*}.
$$

This justifies P09’s:

$$
H_W\big|_{\text{phys}} = \beta \cdot M,
$$
with
$$
M := \Delta_1\big|_{\mathrm{Phys}},
$$
the 1‑form Laplacian restricted to the co-closed, gauge-orthogonal subspace.

We stress:

- $M$ is a symmetric, nonnegative operator on $\mathrm{Phys}$.
- Its spectrum consists of eigenvalues $0 \le \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \cdots$ accumulating at some upper bound (finite-dimensional case here).

P09 writes “Eigenvalues of $M$: $0 < \lambda_1 \leq \lambda_2 \leq \dots$”, but whether $\lambda_1 > 0$ or not depends crucially on whether **harmonic 1‑forms** (torons) are included in $\mathrm{Phys}$ or projected out. This is a key subtlety we will return to.

### 4. Positivity and Discrete Hodge Theory

#### 4.1 Discrete Hodge decomposition on a finite lattice

On a finite connected graph / lattice $\Lambda$, the discrete Hodge decomposition for 1‑forms (with values in any finite-dimensional inner product space, here $\mathfrak{su}(N)$) gives:
$$
\Omega^1 = \operatorname{Ran}d \;\oplus\; \mathcal{H}^1 \;\oplus\; \operatorname{Ran}d^*,
$$
with orthogonality with respect to $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$:

- $\operatorname{Ran}d$ is the exact 1‑forms,
- $\mathcal{H}^1 := \ker d \cap \ker d^*$ is the space of **harmonic** 1‑forms,
- $\operatorname{Ran}d^*$ is the coexact 1‑forms.

The Laplacian satisfies
$$
\Delta_1 X = 0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X \in \mathcal{H}^1,
$$
so the kernel of $M = \Delta_1|_{\mathrm{Phys}}$ is precisely
$$
\ker M = \mathcal{H}^1 \cap \mathrm{Phys}.
$$

We have already
$$
\mathrm{Phys} = \ker G^* = \ker d^*.
$$
Therefore
$$
\mathrm{Phys}
= (\operatorname{Ran}d)^\perp
= \mathcal{H}^1 \oplus \operatorname{Ran}d^*.
$$

Thus, decompose any physical field $X \in \mathrm{Phys}$ uniquely as
$$
X = X_{\mathrm{harm}} + X_{\mathrm{coexact}},
\quad X_{\mathrm{harm}} \in \mathcal{H}^1,
\quad X_{\mathrm{coexact}} \in \operatorname{Ran}d^*.
$$

On $\mathcal{H}^1$, $M X_{\mathrm{harm}} = 0$.
On $\operatorname{Ran}d^*$, $M$ is positive definite.

The **toron sector** in lattice gauge theory corresponds exactly to $\mathcal{H}^1\otimes \mathfrak{su}(N)$: global, flat but non-trivial holonomies around non-contractible cycles. These are gauge-inequivalent configurations with zero field strength, hence zero Wilson action and zero Hessian.

Consequently, strictly speaking:

- On $\mathrm{Phys}$ **including torons**, $M$ is only nonnegative:
  $$
  M \ge 0,
  $$
  and
  $$
  \lambda_1 = 0
  $$
  if the first Betti number $b_1(\Lambda)$ of the underlying cell complex is nonzero (e.g. periodic boundary conditions in $d\ge 1$).

- On the **reduced physical subspace** (excluding harmonic 1‑forms), which we may denote
  $$
  \mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{red}}
  := \mathrm{Phys} \cap (\mathcal{H}^1)^\perp = \operatorname{Ran}d^*,
  $$
  the operator $M$ is strictly positive:
  $$
  \langle X, M X \rangle > 0 \quad \forall X \in \mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{red}}\setminus\{0\}.
  $$

Thus, if:

- either the lattice has trivial $H^1$ (e.g. appropriate boundary conditions / topology),
- or one **by definition** excludes the toron sector from the “physical” Hilbert space,

then the statement “$M$ is positive definite on physical modes” is correct.

Under that interpretation, P09’s line

> 3. $M$ is positive definite on physical modes (lattice Hodge theory)

is valid; but it requires the implicit convention that toron/harmonic modes are not part of the “physical subspace” for the purpose of positivity.

The numerical table in P09:

| Mode    | $H_W$ eigenvalue | $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ eigenvalue |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| Gauge   | 0                 | 0                             |
| Toron   | 0                 | $c_0 = 4/3$                  |
| Physical| $> 0$             | $> c_0$                      |

is consistent with this:

- Gauge modes: in $\operatorname{Ran} G$, kernel of both $H_W$ and $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (the Haar mass is also gauge invariant, so no curvature along pure gauge directions).
- Toron modes: in $\mathcal{H}^1$, kernel of $H_W$ but **lifted by the Haar mass** in $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
- “Physical” in that table evidently refers to $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{red}}$ (coexact, non-harmonic 1‑forms), where $H_W$ is strictly positive.

This suggests that the **scope field** `scope: lattice_physical_sector` in the CLEAN header is implicitly meant to **exclude** gauge and toron directions, i.e., to work on $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{red}}$.

With that understanding, we now turn to positivity of $H_W$ and then of $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$.

### 5. Positivity of the Wilson Hessian on the Physical Sector

P09 states:

**Theorem 3.1 (Physical Hessian Positivity)** (P09)  

For finite lattice with $\beta > 0$:
$$
H_W\big|_{\text{phys}} \;\geq\; \beta\, \lambda_{\min}(M) \;>\; 0.
$$

Proof sketch given in P09:

1. Gauge modes are in $\ker(H_W)$.
2. On physical subspace, $H_W = \beta\,M$.
3. $M$ is positive definite on physical modes (lattice Hodge theory).
4. Therefore $H_W|_{\text{phys}} > 0$.

We now present the detailed derivation, making explicit the spectral decomposition and the role of $\lambda_{\min}(M)$.

#### 5.1 Spectral decomposition of $M$ on the (reduced) physical subspace

Assume we work on
$$
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}} := \mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{red}} = \operatorname{Ran}d^*,
$$
i.e., coexact, co-closed 1‑forms with harmonic 1‑forms removed. As explained, this matches the “Physical” sector in the numerical table, with torons treated separately.

On this finite-dimensional inner product space, the symmetric operator $M$ is diagonalizable with real, strictly positive eigenvalues.

Let $\{e_i\}$ be an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ consisting of eigenvectors of $M$:
$$
M e_i = \lambda_i e_i, \quad \lambda_i > 0.
$$

Order them:
$$
0 < \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_K,
$$
where $K = \dim \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}$.

Define
$$
\lambda_{\min}(M) := \lambda_1 > 0.
$$

#### 5.2 Action of $H_W$ on eigenmodes

On $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}$, P09 establishes:
$$
H_W|_{\mathrm{phys}} = \beta M.
$$

Thus,
$$
H_W e_i = \beta M e_i = \beta \lambda_i e_i.
$$

Therefore the eigenvalues of $H_W$ on the physical sector are
$$
\mu_i = \beta \lambda_i > 0,
$$
and
$$
\mu_{\min} = \beta \lambda_{\min}(M) = \beta \lambda_1.
$$

#### 5.3 Lower bound on the quadratic form

Let $X \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ be arbitrary. Expand
$$
X = \sum_{i=1}^K x_i e_i.
$$

Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle X, H_W X \rangle
&= \left\langle \sum_i x_i e_i, H_W \sum_j x_j e_j \right\rangle\\
&= \sum_{i,j} x_i \overline{x_j} \langle e_i, H_W e_j \rangle\\
&= \sum_{i,j} x_i \overline{x_j} \beta \lambda_j \langle e_i, e_j \rangle\\
&= \beta \sum_j \lambda_j |x_j|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Using $\lambda_j \ge \lambda_{\min}(M)$ for all $j$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle X, H_W X \rangle
&\ge \beta \lambda_{\min}(M) \sum_j |x_j|^2\\
&= \beta \lambda_{\min}(M) \, \|X\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus, as a quadratic form on $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}$,
$$
H_W\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}} \;\ge\; \beta\,\lambda_{\min}(M) \, I_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}},
$$
with $I_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}$ the identity on $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}$.

Equivalently,
$$
H_W\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}} \;\ge\; \beta\,\lambda_{\min}(M) > 0.
$$

This is the precise content of Theorem 3.1 in P09, under the interpretation that “phys” excludes gauge and toron directions and coincides with $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ as defined above.

### 6. Inclusion of the Haar Mass Term

P09 references a result P04 (“haar_mass”) and states:

> Combined with the Haar mass term, this gives $H_{\mathrm{eff}} \ge c_0 > 0$, which is the key input for Bakry-Émery spectral gap.

Formally, P09 writes:

**Corollary 3.2** (P09) Effective Hessian:
$$
H_{\text{eff}}\big|_{\text{phys}}
= H_W\big|_{\text{phys}} + c_0 I
\;\geq\; c_0 > 0.
$$

This holds even at $\beta = 0$ (pure Haar measure).

We now explain in detail how a “Haar mass” term arises and why it contributes as a positive multiple of the identity on the physical sector (and in fact on all non-gauge directions), under the standard small‑field / exponential map parametrization.

#### 6.1 Haar measure in exponential coordinates

Consider one link variable $U_b \in G = \mathrm{SU}(N)$, and parameterize near the identity as
$$
U_b = \exp(X_b), \quad X_b \in \mathfrak{g}, \quad \|X_b\|\ll 1.
$$

The Haar measure $\mu_H$ on $G$ when pulled back via the exponential map has a smooth positive density $J(X)$ with respect to Lebesgue measure $dX$ on $\mathfrak{g}$:
$$
d\mu_H(U_b) = J(X_b)\, dX_b,
\quad U_b = \exp(X_b).
$$

There is a well-known formula
$$
J(X) = \det\!\left(\frac{\sinh(\operatorname{ad}_X/2)}{\operatorname{ad}_X/2}\right),
$$
where $\operatorname{ad}_X: \mathfrak{g}\to \mathfrak{g}$ is the adjoint map $\operatorname{ad}_X(Y) = [X,Y]$ and the expression in parentheses is defined by its series expansion.

Expanding near $X=0$, we have
$$
\frac{\sinh(Z/2)}{Z/2} = I + \frac{Z^2}{24} + O(\|Z\|^4).
$$
Thus
$$
\log\left(\frac{\sinh(Z/2)}{Z/2}\right)
= \frac{Z^2}{24} + O(\|Z\|^4),
$$
and
$$
\log J(X)
= \operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{(\operatorname{ad}_X)^2}{24} + O(\|\operatorname{ad}_X\|^4)\right)
= \frac{1}{24}\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{ad}_X^2) + O(\|X\|^4).
$$

For a compact semisimple Lie algebra (like $\mathfrak{su}(N)$), $\operatorname{ad}_X$ is skew-adjoint with respect to the Killing form, and
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{ad}_X^2) \le 0,
$$
with equality iff $X=0$. In fact,
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{ad}_X^2) = -c \,\kappa(X,X)
$$
for some positive constant $c>0$ depending on the normalization of $\kappa$ (this is a standard consequence of the relation between the Killing form and $\operatorname{ad}_X$).

Hence
$$
\log J(X)
= -\frac{c}{24}\,\kappa(X,X) + O(\|X\|^4),
$$
and therefore
$$
-\log J(X)
= \frac{c}{24}\,\kappa(X,X) + O(\|X\|^4).
$$

At quadratic order,
$$
-\log J(X) \approx \frac{c}{24}\,\kappa(X,X).
$$

Thus, to leading order in $X$, the **effective density** of pure Haar measure in $X$–coordinates looks like
$$
d\mu_H(U_b)
= e^{-\frac{c}{24}\kappa(X_b,X_b) + O(\|X_b\|^4)}\, dX_b.
$$

Equivalently, the **effective potential** from the Haar measure (relative to flat Lebesgue measure $dX$) is
$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(X_b)
= -\log J(X_b)
= \frac{c}{24} \kappa(X_b, X_b) + O(\|X_b\|^4).
$$

Thus the Hessian of $S_{\mathrm{Haar}}$ at $X_b = 0$ is
$$
H_{\mathrm{Haar},b}
= \frac{c}{12}\,I_{\mathfrak{g}},
$$
up to choice of normalization of $\kappa$ (the factor of 2 comes from the second derivative).

On the full lattice, Haar measure factorizes over bonds (in the absence of Wilson/interaction terms), so the total Haar contribution is
$$
S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(X)
= \sum_{b} S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(X_b)
$$
with Hessian
$$
H_{\mathrm{Haar}}
= \bigoplus_{b} H_{\mathrm{Haar},b}
= c_0 I_{\mathrm{non-gauge}} + \text{(projection issues onto gauge directions)},
$$
for some constant $c_0 > 0$ (absorbing constants into $c_0$).

The **key point** is:

- In Lie algebra coordinates near the identity, the pure Haar measure induces a **strictly convex** quadratic potential around $X=0$ in every direction of $\mathfrak{g}$ at each link.
- Gauge invariance at the level of the measure is realized by a left–right action on $U_b$, not by translation in $X$; thus **gauge directions** are **not** flat directions of $S_{\mathrm{Haar}}$ in the $X$‑coordinates. The Haar mass term is present also in gauge directions in $X$‑space.

However, when one couples this to the Wilson action and then projects to the gauge-invariant measure on the quotient, the **effective Hessian** seen in the gauge-invariant field variables projects out gauge redundancy. P04 presumably shows how this projection leads to a uniform positive lower bound $c_0$ for the Hessian on all non-gauge directions (including torons). In P09 this is imported as an input.

Thus, at the level of the **effective action in the gauge-invariant configuration space**, P04 asserts (in the notation of P09) that there exists $c_0>0$ such that the Haar term contributes at least $c_0 I$ on the physical (and toron) directions.

Formally, we can encode this as:

> **Assumption/P04 (Haar mass term)**: In the gauge-projected variables, the contribution of the Haar measure to the effective action has Hessian $H_{\mathrm{Haar}}$ satisfying
> $$
> H_{\mathrm{Haar}} \;\ge\; c_0 I
> $$
> on the **non-gauge** sector (physical + toron directions), for some constant $c_0>0$ depending only on the gauge group and local geometry, but not on the lattice size.

We now combine this with $H_W$.

#### 6.2 Effective Hessian with Wilson plus Haar

Define the total effective action (near $U_0=I$ in the local coordinates used in P09)
$$
S_{\mathrm{eff}}(X) = S_W(X) + S_{\mathrm{Haar}}(X),
$$
with Hessian
$$
H_{\mathrm{eff}} = H_W + H_{\mathrm{Haar}}.
$$

On the (reduced) physical sector $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ (coexact, non-harmonic 1‑forms), we have:

- $H_W|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}} = \beta M \ge \beta \lambda_{\min}(M) I_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}$.
- $H_{\mathrm{Haar}}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}} \ge c_0 I_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}$ by the Haar mass assumption.

Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{\mathrm{eff}}\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}
&= H_W\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}} + H_{\mathrm{Haar}}\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}\\
&\ge \beta \lambda_{\min}(M)\,I_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}} + c_0 I_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}\\
&\ge c_0 I_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}},
\end{aligned}
\]
since $\beta \lambda_{\min}(M) \ge 0$.

In particular, for $\beta = 0$ (pure Haar measure),
$$
H_{\mathrm{eff}}\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}
= H_{\mathrm{Haar}}\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}
\ge c_0 I_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}} > 0.
$$

This is the precise content of P09’s:

$$
H_{\text{eff}}\big|_{\text{phys}} 
= H_W\big|_{\text{phys}} + c_0 I \;\geq\; c_0 > 0.
$$

Combined with the discussion of torons:

- On **physical non-toron modes**: $H_W$ is already strictly positive; Haar term shifts the eigenvalues up by $c_0$.
- On **toron modes**: $H_W=0$ but $H_{\mathrm{Haar}} \ge c_0 I$, so $H_{\mathrm{eff}} \ge c_0 I$.
- On **gauge modes**: $H_W=0$, and P04 must account for how the gauge projection eliminates those directions; in the gauge-invariant quotient variables, they do not appear as physical directions at all.
