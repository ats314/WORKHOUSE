# Riccati Spine Module  
*A single “hinge” lemma + comparison ODE that turns local convexity into a uniform curvature/mixing constant.*

This note rewrites the “Riccati stabilization spine” in a way that makes the **only truly nontrivial analytic hinge** explicit: a **tensor maximum principle for the smallest eigenvalue** of a matrix parabolic inequality. Everything else is bookkeeping once that hinge is in place.

---

## 1. Two times (don’t mix them)

There are two conceptually different evolutions floating around:

- **(RG / smoothing time) \(t\)**: a coarse–graining map \(S_0\mapsto S_t\) (Wilsonian RG, gradient flow, or a *heat–kernel surrogate*).  
  This is where a **Hessian Riccati–reaction structure** appears.

- **(Sampling / Langevin time) \(s\)**: the Markov semigroup generated by  
  \[
  L_{S_t}=\Delta_g-\langle\nabla S_t,\nabla\rangle
  \]
  which mixes to the equilibrium \(\pi_t\propto e^{-S_t}d\mu_g\).  
  This is where **Bakry–Émery curvature** yields Poincaré/LSI and a spectral gap.

The “spine” is: **RG-time dynamics produces a curvature floor; that floor is the mixing constant in Langevin time.**

---

## 2. The object that wants to satisfy a Riccati inequality

Let \((\mathsf X,g)\) be a Riemannian configuration manifold (e.g. a gauge-fixed chart, or an irreducible slice), and let \(S_t\) be a *scale-dependent effective action* produced by your chosen coarse–graining surrogate.

### 2.1 Heat-kernel surrogate (cleanest PDE identity)

Assume (as a controlled surrogate) that a density \(\rho_t\propto e^{-S_t}\) evolves by the heat equation:
\[
\partial_t\rho_t=\Delta_g\rho_t.
\]
Then \(S_t\) satisfies the viscous Hamilton–Jacobi equation (up to an additive function of \(t\) only):
\[
\partial_t S_t = \Delta_g S_t - \|\nabla S_t\|^2.
\]

Let \(H_t:=\nabla^2 S_t\) (covariant Hessian, viewed as a symmetric endomorphism of \(T\mathsf X\) or of the horizontal bundle). Differentiating twice gives the schematic reaction–diffusion structure
\[
\partial_t H_t
=
\Delta_L H_t \;-\; 2H_t^2 \;+\; \mathcal R_t,
\tag{H}
\]
where \(\Delta_L\) is a Lichnerowicz/Bochner Laplacian on symmetric tensors and \(\mathcal R_t\) collects curvature commutators and other geometric source/error terms.

**Key point:** the only “bad” nonlinearity is \(-2H_t^2\), which is *Riccati*.

### 2.2 What you actually use in the pipeline

You almost never need the exact tensor formula for \(\mathcal R_t\). What you need is to dominate it in the form
\[
\mathcal R_t(x)\succeq \sigma_*(t)\,\mathrm{Id}\;-\;E_t(x),
\qquad \|E_t(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le \varepsilon(t),
\tag{Src}
\]
on the region/bundle you care about (e.g. SAFE blocks, horizontal directions, irreducible sector).

Then (H)+(Src) becomes the inequality
\[
(\partial_t-\Delta_L)H_t \;\succeq\; -2H_t^2 \;+\; \sigma_*(t)\mathrm{Id}\;-\;E_t.
\tag{Ineq}
\]

## 3. The hinge: a matrix maximum principle lemma you can actually aim to prove

This is the “upgrade tier” lemma. It is the single step that turns the matrix inequality (Ineq) into a scalar Riccati inequality for the **worst-case curvature**.

> **Lemma (Tensor maximum principle for the smallest eigenvalue — target).**  
> Let \((M,g)\) be a closed Riemannian manifold (or a compact domain with smooth boundary and reflecting/Neumann boundary conditions).  
> Let \(E\to M\) be a smooth metric vector bundle with compatible connection, and let \(\Delta_E=\nabla^{E,*}\nabla^E\) be the connection Laplacian acting componentwise on \(\mathrm{Sym}(E)\).  
> Suppose \(H_t\in C^\infty([0,T]\times M;\mathrm{Sym}(E))\) satisfies, in quadratic-form sense,
> \[
> (\partial_t-\Delta_E)H_t \;\succeq\; -\alpha H_t^2 \;+\; \sigma_*(t)\mathrm{Id}_E\;-\;E_t,
> \qquad \|E_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le \varepsilon(t),
> \]
> for some constant \(\alpha>0\).  
> Define
> \[
> \lambda(t):=\inf_{x\in M}\lambda_{\min}\big(H_t(x)\big).
> \]
> Then \(\lambda\) satisfies the scalar differential inequality (in viscosity sense; hence a.e.)
> \[
> \dot\lambda(t)\;\ge\; -\alpha\,\lambda(t)^2 \;+\; \sigma_*(t)\;-\;\varepsilon(t).
> \tag{\(\star\)}
> \]
> Equivalently, if \(\ell\) solves \(\dot\ell=-\alpha\ell^2+\sigma_*(t)-\varepsilon(t)\) with \(\ell(0)\le\lambda(0)\), then \(H_t\succeq \ell(t)\mathrm{Id}_E\) for all \(t\in[0,T]\).

**“To be verified” hypotheses in YM use.**
- \(M\) should be the **irreducible region** (or a gauge slice) so that the **horizontal bundle** \(E=\mathrm{Hor}\subset T\mathcal C_\Lambda\) is a smooth subbundle.  
- The Laplacian used in the Hessian evolution must respect the horizontal restriction, or you must add a controlled commutator error into \(E_t\).  
- Reducibles are removed by the capacity/polarity argument so they do not affect Dirichlet-form spectra.

This lemma is precisely the place where “hand‑off” can die if any regularity or bundle-compatibility fails.

## 4. Riccati stabilization (what you get once the hinge holds)

Assume for simplicity that after some transient,
\[
\sigma_*(t)\ge \sigma_*>0,\qquad \varepsilon(t)\to \varepsilon_\infty,\qquad \sigma_*>\varepsilon_\infty.
\]
Then the scalar comparison ODE
\[
\dot\ell = -\alpha \ell^2 + (\sigma_*-\varepsilon_\infty)
\]
has a stable positive fixed point
\[
\ell_*=\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_*-\varepsilon_\infty}{\alpha}}.
\]
So the worst-case eigenvalue cannot drift to \(0\); it is driven into a **positive curvature floor**:
\[
\liminf_{t\to\infty}\;\inf_{x}\lambda_{\min}(H_t(x))\;\ge\;\ell_*.
\]

This is the rigorous content of “seed → sustain”: once \(\lambda(0)\ge 0\), the long-time floor is set by \(\sigma_*\), not by the vanishing Haar scale.

## 5. Turning the floor into a mixing constant

At each fixed scale \(t\), the relevant equilibrium is \(\pi_t\propto e^{-S_t}d\mu_g\), with Langevin generator
\[
L_t=\Delta_g-\langle\nabla S_t,\nabla\rangle.
\]
Bakry–Émery gives
\[
\Gamma_2(f)=\|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 + \big\langle(\mathrm{Ric}_g+\nabla^2 S_t)\nabla f,\nabla f\big\rangle.
\]
So if you have a lower bound on the **Bakry–Émery tensor**
\[
\mathrm{Ric}_g+\nabla^2 S_t \;\succeq\; \rho(t)\,g,
\]
then \(-L_t\) has spectral gap at least \(\rho(t)\), and \(\pi_t\) satisfies Poincaré (and under standard conditions, LSI) with constants \(\lesssim 1/\rho(t)\).

Now if intrinsic geometry gives \(\mathrm{Ric}_g\succeq \rho_0 g\) and the Riccati module gives \(H_t=\nabla^2 S_t\succeq \ell_* g\), you get a uniform-in-\(t\) curvature floor
\[
\mathrm{Ric}_g+\nabla^2 S_t \;\succeq\; (\rho_0+\ell_*)\,g.
\]
So your **local mixing constant** is
\[
\alpha:=\rho_0+\ell_*,
\]
which is what you feed into your coarse-graining inequality for the global spectral gap.

*(If you prefer to work blockwise: replace \(\rho_0\) by the block Ricci lower bound and interpret everything on the SAFE block with boundary conditions.)*

## 6. Optional globalizer: Lyapunov drift when curvature is only local

If your curvature/Hessian control holds only on a SAFE set \(B_{R_0}\), you need an “escape-prevention” mechanism. The standard one is a Foster–Lyapunov drift:

Find \(\Phi\ge 1\) such that
\[
L_t\Phi \le -c\,\Phi + C\,\mathbf 1_{B_{R_0}}
\]
with constants \(c,C>0\). Combined with a local Poincaré/LSI on \(B_{R_0}\), this upgrades to global functional inequalities for \(\pi_t\) with constants depending on \(c,C\) and the local curvature constant.

(Precisely which theorem you cite here depends on whether you want Poincaré only, LSI, or \(T_2\).)

## 7. Constants ledger (so you can “compile” the module)

**Inputs you measure / bound:**
- SAFE radius \(R_0\) (block geometry).
- Local horizontal Hessian seed at \(t=0\): \(\lambda(0)\ge 0\) (Haar mass gives a tiny positive seed).
- Intrinsic Ricci floor on your configuration manifold: \(\rho_0\).
- Source floor after reorganization: \(\sigma_*:=\inf_{t\ge t_0,x}\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal R_t(x))\) (after subtracting errors).
- Error budget: \(\varepsilon_\infty\).

**Module output:**
- Riccati floor: \(\ell_*=\sqrt{(\sigma_*-\varepsilon_\infty)/\alpha}\) with \(\alpha=2\) in the vHJ Hessian equation.
- Bakry–Émery curvature floor: \(\rho_0+\ell_*\).
- Local spectral gap / Poincaré constant: \(C_{\mathrm{PI}}\lesssim 1/(\rho_0+\ell_*)\).
- (With drift/tails) global LSI: \(C_{\mathrm{LSI}}\lesssim \frac{1}{\rho_0+\ell_*}\times(\text{drift factor})\).

## 8. What’s genuinely new/important here

1. You have isolated the *single* analytic hinge lemma \((\star)\) that must be proved to make the hand‑off mechanism a theorem.  
2. Once \((\star)\) is in place, the rest is a transparent comparison argument + standard Bakry–Émery machinery.  
3. The pipeline becomes “compilable”: you can now list exactly which geometric terms must be placed into \(\sigma_*\) versus \(\varepsilon\), and your numerics/autodiff can target those pieces directly.
