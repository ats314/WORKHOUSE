# 1. Thesis

The lattice gauge configuration space $\mathcal C = SU(N)^{|B|}$ is a **compact smooth manifold**.
Inside it sits a subset $\Sigma$ of **reducible configurations** (where holonomy lives in a proper subgroup),
which is exactly where gauge-orbit geometry degenerates.

The project documents establish a very useful fact:

> $\Sigma$ has **capacity zero** for the Yang--Mills Dirichlet form; equivalently it is **polar**.

In probabilistic language: the associated diffusion (Langevin dynamics / heat flow) almost surely never hits $\Sigma$.
In analytic language: $\Sigma$ does not affect spectral gaps, Poincaré/log-Sobolev inequalities, etc.

This is a finite-dimensional lattice analogue of a conjectured statement in the continuum program.

# 2. Definitions

## 2.1 Configuration space and Dirichlet form

Let $B$ be the oriented bond set of a finite lattice $\Lambda$ and define

$$
\mathcal C := \prod_{b\in B} SU(N) \cong SU(N)^{|B|}.
$$

Let $d\mu_{\mathrm{YM}}(U)\propto e^{-S_W(U)}\prod_{b\in B} d\mu_{\mathrm{Haar}}(U_b)$.

The Yang--Mills Dirichlet form is

$$
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{YM}}(f,f) := \int_{\mathcal C} \|\nabla f(U)\|^2\, d\mu_{\mathrm{YM}}(U).
$$

Capacity of a set $A\subset \mathcal C$ (relative to a Dirichlet form $\mathcal E$) is

$$
\mathrm{Cap}(A)
:= \inf\Bigl\{
\mathcal E(f,f)+\|f\|_{L^2(\mu)}^2
:\ f\in C_c^\infty(\mathcal C),\ f\ge 1\ \text{on } A
\Bigr\}.
$$

A set is **polar** if it has capacity $0$.

## 2.2 Reducible configurations

A configuration $U\in\mathcal C$ is **reducible** if the holonomy group generated by plaquette products
is contained in a proper closed subgroup of $SU(N)$ (up to conjugacy), e.g. a block-diagonal subgroup.
Equivalently, there exists a nontrivial decomposition $\mathbb C^N=V_1\oplus V_2$ preserved by holonomy.

Infinitesimally, reducibility is equivalent to the existence of a nonzero
covariantly constant adjoint field: a collection $\xi_x\in\mathfrak{su}(N)$ such that for each bond $b:x\to y$,

$$
U_b\,\xi_x\,U_b^{-1} = \xi_y .
$$

Let $\Sigma\subset \mathcal C$ be the set of reducibles.

# 3. The codimension--capacity mechanism

Two general facts power the proof.

## 3.1 Codimension $\ge 2$ sets have zero capacity (on compact manifolds)

**Theorem (capacity of thin sets).**
Let $M$ be a compact smooth manifold and $V\subset M$ a closed set contained in a finite union of embedded submanifolds
of codimension at least $2$. Then $V$ has capacity $0$ for any uniformly elliptic strongly local Dirichlet form on $M$.

**Idea.** In Euclidean charts, codimension $\ge2$ sets have zero capacity (Sobolev embedding / cutoff functions).
Patch by partition of unity.

## 3.2 Reducibles sit inside a codimension $\ge 2$ algebraic variety

$\Sigma$ can be described by polynomial equations enforcing “all holonomies lie in some proper subgroup up to gauge,”
so it is contained in a finite union of smooth submanifolds of codimension $\ge 2$.

# 4. Main result and consequences

**Theorem (polarity of reducibles).**
$\Sigma$ has capacity $0$ for the Yang--Mills Dirichlet form; hence $\Sigma$ is polar.

**Corollary (negligibility).**
For quasi-every initial condition, the diffusion associated with the Dirichlet form almost surely never visits $\Sigma$.
Equivalently, reducibles do not affect spectral and functional-inequality statements.

# 5. Why this matters for bigger theories

This is more than a measure-zero statement. Polarity is a *potential-theoretic* “thinness” notion tuned to the dynamics.

Three payoffs:

1. **Spectral cleanliness.** If the only potential Hessian degeneracies occur on $\Sigma$, polarity lets you ignore them when proving spectral gaps.

2. **Gauge-orbit regularization.** Reducibles are the singular strata of the orbit space.
Polarity hints that, for many analytic purposes, the orbit space behaves like its irreducible (principal) stratum.

3. **Continuum strategy template.** The continuum program faces infinite-dimensional orbit spaces and Sobolev completions.
This finite-cutoff proof avoids those technicalities but suggests the right conjecture:
reducible connections should be polar for an appropriate horizontal Dirichlet form in the continuum.
