# Centered Double-Incidence Factorization Theorem

**Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Status:** exact algebraic theorem; exact \(SU(4)\) exceptional-sector corollary relative to the accepted all-zone certificate and the project's centered-gauge symmetry conventions.

## 1. Result

The previously proved Laurent–Koszul theorem showed that a finite-range
residual \(R\) satisfying

\[
R\psi=0
\]

belongs to the one-sided cage-annihilator module.

In the centered Hodge gauge there is a stronger statement.

Let

\[
s=(s_1,s_2,s_3)^T,
\qquad
s_j=\sin\frac{k_j}{2},
\]

and let

\[
K(s)=[s]_\times
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-s_3&s_2\\
s_3&0&-s_1\\
-s_2&s_1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
K(s)s=0.
\]

For a transpose-symmetric finite-range matrix \(R\), annihilation of \(s\) is
not merely equivalent to a one-sided Koszul factorization. It forces a
two-sided factorization:

\[
\boxed{
R^T=R,\quad Rs=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R=K(s)^T\,B\,K(s)
}
\]

with \(B^T=B\).

This is the centered-gauge form of boundary-ideal membership.

## 2. Algebraic setting

The theorem is most naturally stated over any commutative ring \(A\) in which

\[
s_1,s_2,s_3
\]

form a regular sequence and \(2\) is invertible.

For the centered finite-range lattice problem one may use the Laurent ring in
half-cell variables

\[
A=\mathbb Q[t_1^{\pm1},t_2^{\pm1},t_3^{\pm1}],
\qquad
t_j=e^{ik_j/2},
\]

with

\[
x_j=t_j-t_j^{-1}=2i\,s_j.
\]

Multiplication by the harmless constant \(2i\) does not change the generated
Koszul module. The sequence \(x_1,x_2,x_3\) is regular in this Laurent ring.

For the proof below write simply

\[
x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)^T
\]

and

\[
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-x_3&x_2\\
x_3&0&-x_1\\
-x_2&x_1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

## 3. Theorem CDI — symmetric cage annihilation equals double incidence

### Theorem

Let \(A\) be as above and let

\[
R\in {\rm Mat}_{3\times3}(A)
\]

satisfy

\[
R^T=R,
\qquad
Rx=0.
\]

Then there exists a symmetric matrix

\[
B=B^T\in{\rm Mat}_{3\times3}(A)
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
R=K^T B K.
}
\]

### Proof

Because \(x_1,x_2,x_3\) are a regular sequence, the Koszul complex is exact.
Hence the three rows of \(K\) generate every row syzygy of \(x\).

Since

\[
Rx=0,
\]

there is therefore a matrix \(C\) such that

\[
R=CK.
\tag{1}
\]

Using \(R^T=R\),

\[
R=R^T=K^TC^T.
\]

Set

\[
D=C^T.
\]

Then

\[
R=K^TD.
\tag{2}
\]

Again using \(Rx=0\),

\[
0=Rx=K^T(Dx).
\]

The kernel of \(K^T=-K\) is the rank-one module generated by \(x\), so

\[
Dx=a\,x
\tag{3}
\]

for some \(a\in A\).

Let

\[
I=(x_1,x_2,x_3).
\]

We claim

\[
a\in I.
\tag{4}
\]

To see this, reduce (3) modulo \(I^2\). If \(a_0\) denotes the image of \(a\)
in \(A/I\), the constant part of \(D\) acts on the degree-one vector \(x\) as

\[
D_0x=a_0x.
\]

Therefore

\[
D_0=a_0I_3.
\]

The degree-one part of (2) is then

\[
R_1=a_0K^T.
\]

But \(K^T=-K\) is skew-symmetric, whereas \(R_1\) must be symmetric. Since
\(2\) is invertible,

\[
a_0=0.
\]

Thus \(a\in I\), proving (4).

Because \(a\in I\), choose a row vector \(\ell^T\) with

\[
\ell^T x=a.
\]

Define

\[
D'=D-x\ell^T.
\]

Since \(K^Tx=0\),

\[
K^TD'=K^TD=R.
\]

Moreover,

\[
D'x
=
Dx-x(\ell^Tx)
=
ax-xa
=
0.
\]

By exactness of the same Koszul complex, every row of \(D'\) is a syzygy of
\(x\). Therefore there exists \(B_0\) with

\[
D'=B_0K.
\]

Consequently

\[
R=K^TB_0K.
\]

Finally replace \(B_0\) by its symmetric part,

\[
B=\frac12(B_0+B_0^T).
\]

Since \(R=R^T\),

\[
K^TBK
=
\frac12
\left(
K^TB_0K+
(K^TB_0K)^T
\right)
=
R.
\]

Hence \(B=B^T\) and

\[
\boxed{R=K^TBK}.
\]

\(\square\)

## 4. Why centered gauge is the right gauge

The project's exact centered-gauge identity is

\[
JM=-2[s]_\times,
\]

where \(M\) is the real face-to-edge boundary matrix and \(J\) is a constant
Hodge rotation.

Thus

\[
K=[s]_\times=-\frac12JM.
\]

A factorization

\[
R=K^TBK
\]

is therefore equivalent, after absorbing constant Hodge rotations and factors
of \(2\), to

\[
\boxed{
R=M^T\,\widetilde B\,M.
}
\]

This is precisely a two-sided boundary/incidence factorization on the
plaquette fiber in the centered convention.

## 5. Symmetry lemma for the physical residual

Consider a finite-range real-space kernel in the centered plaquette basis.

Hermiticity gives

\[
R(k)^\dagger=R(k).
\]

Reality of the exact strong-coupling coefficients gives

\[
R(-k)=\overline{R(k)}.
\]

The plaquette orientation triplet is axial/parity-even. In centered gauge,
spatial inversion therefore acts without an internal orientation sign and
gives

\[
R(-k)=R(k)
\]

for an inversion-completed correction.

Combining these identities,

\[
R(k)=\overline{R(k)}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{R(k)^T=R(k)}.
\]

Thus the physical \(P=+\) centered residual satisfies the transpose-symmetry
hypothesis of Theorem CDI.

## 6. \(SU(4)\) exceptional-sector corollary

The accepted exact \(SU(4)\) certificate gives, in the cell gauge,

\[
H^{\rm exc}_{4,4}(k)\psi(k)
=
\Delta q_4\psi(k)
\]

as an exact all-zone Laurent identity, with

\[
\Delta q_4
=
-\frac{304746539168}{160249753125}.
\]

Let

\[
R_4
=
H^{\rm exc}_{4,4}-\Delta q_4I.
\]

After the exact centered-cell gauge/Hodge transformation, the flat vector is
the transverse-Maxwell kernel vector \(s\). Therefore

\[
R_{4,c}(k)s(k)=0.
\]

The exceptional correction is symmetry-completed, Hermitian, and belongs to
the parity-even axial plaquette sector, so in centered gauge

\[
R_{4,c}^T=R_{4,c}.
\]

Theorem CDI therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
R_{4,c}
=
[s]_\times^T\,B_4\,[s]_\times
}
\]

for some symmetric finite-range Laurent matrix \(B_4\).

Equivalently, using \(JM=-2[s]_\times\),

\[
\boxed{
H^{\rm exc}_{4,4}-\Delta q_4I
=
M^\dagger\,\widehat B_4\,M
}
\]

in the centered convention, with the constant gauge/Hodge transformations
absorbed into \(\widehat B_4\).

### Interpretation

The \(SU(4)\) exceptional residual is therefore not only in the one-sided
annihilator module. **It belongs to the two-sided boundary ideal after centered
gauge.**

This conclusion does not require recovering the missing 39-entry kernel.

It follows from three already-established ingredients:

1. exact all-zone quotient scalarity;
2. inversion/parity plus Hermiticity;
3. exactness of the three-variable Koszul complex.

## 8. New microscopic target

There are now two distinct possibilities:

### Wordwise protection

Every exceptional-bearing word/orbit separately satisfies

\[
\delta R_w\,s=0.
\]

Then determinant-to-boundary protection occurs before summation.

### Collective protection

Individual words escape, but the 76-word / 156-\(C\)-orbit sum obeys

\[
\sum_w\delta R_w\,s=0.
\]

Then the theorem identifies a **representation-theoretic cancellation law**
among the exceptional word weights.

The existing certificate proves the sum is protected but does not distinguish
these mechanisms.

That wordwise-versus-collective binary test is now the sharp remaining
microscopic question.
