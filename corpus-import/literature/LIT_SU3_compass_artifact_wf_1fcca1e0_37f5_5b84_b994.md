# Rigorous Spectral-Gap Theorems for the 3+1D SU(3) Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian at Strong Coupling: Applicability, Novelty, and SOS Certificates

## TL;DR
- The planned theorem — an explicit-threshold spectral gap for the infinite-volume 3+1D SU(3) Kogut–Susskind (KS) Hamiltonian in the gauge-invariant sector, with certified rational perturbative asymptotics — appears **genuinely new**: no published rigorous theorem gives an explicit spectral gap for the canonical KS (E²/plaquette) Hamiltonian of SU(N) in 2+1 or 3+1 D via a controlled small-coupling analysis; the closest rigorous statements (Osterwalder–Seiler 1978; Borgs 1988) are Euclidean transfer-matrix / continuous-time mass-gap results that do not deliver an explicit KS-Hamiltonian gap constant.
- The **most promising rigorous route** is the Fröhlich–Pizzo / Del Vecchio–Fröhlich–Pizzo–Rossi (DFPR) local iterative Lie–Schwinger block-diagonalization, whose higher-dimensional (d≥2) and unbounded-interaction versions match your structure (gapped on-site/on-link H₀ + short-range perturbation) and produce **explicit, size-uniform thresholds**, but every published DFPR theorem assumes **finite-dimensional** local Hilbert spaces (ℂ^M) — the single hypothesis your L²(SU(3)) links violate and that must be re-proved.
- SOS/Positivstellensatz gap certificates (Ozawa; Netzer–Thom; Kaluba–Nowak–Ozawa; Kaluba–Kielak–Nowak) are a mature, rigorous, computer-assisted technology for spectral gaps of the *group-ring Laplacian*, directly relevant to your one-plaquette/SU(N) certificate program; fixed-degree limitation theorems exist in the Lasserre/Sum-of-Squares CSP literature (Grigoriev, Schoenebeck, Tulsiani) and provide templates for a "no N-uniform fixed-degree certificate" negative result, though none is yet specialized to SU(N) plaquette Hamiltonians.

## Key Findings

**1. Yarotsky's theorems are the closest existing "classical-perturbation" template, but are stated for finite-dimensional sites.** D. Yarotsky, *Ground States in Relatively Bounded Quantum Perturbations of Classical Lattice Systems*, Commun. Math. Phys. 261, 799–819 (2006), proves gap persistence, exponential decay of truncated correlations, and analyticity of the ground state for H₀ classical (diagonal) plus a perturbation that splits into a small bounded part and a part bounded *relative* to H₀. This "relatively bounded" framework is conceptually the best fit to an unbounded commuting H₀ (the Casimir sum) plus bounded plaquette perturbation, but the setting is quantum spin systems with finite-dimensional single-site spaces; the constants are existential, not explicit.

**2. Lie–Schwinger block-diagonalization gives explicit-threshold, size-uniform gaps in d≥1, but only for finite-dimensional sites.** Fröhlich–Pizzo (CMP 375, 2039–2069, 2020) established the chain case; DFPR extended it to unbounded interactions (CMP 381, 1115–1152, 2021) and to higher dimensions d≥2 (J. Math. Phys. 63, 073503, 2022). The higher-dimensional paper explicitly assumes ℋ_i ≃ ℂ^M with M finite and N-independent — exactly the assumption your link space L²(SU(3)) breaks.

**3. Bravyi–Hastings–Michalakis / Michalakis–Zwolak do NOT apply**, because your model is neither built from commuting projectors nor frustration-free at y≠0, and the local spaces are infinite-dimensional. These are the wrong tool; De Roeck–Salmhofer and Nachtergaele–Sims–Young likewise assume finite-dimensional (or quadratic-fermionic) structure.

**4. Novelty: the planned theorem is new.** No rigorous explicit-threshold spectral-gap theorem exists for the KS Hamiltonian of SU(N) at strong coupling in the gauge-invariant sector. The closest rigorous statements are Euclidean (Osterwalder–Seiler) or continuous-time/log-transfer-matrix (Borgs).

**5. SOS certificates are a real, rigorous technology** for spectral gaps (Ozawa's characterization of property (T)), with explicit constants and computer-assisted-but-rigorous proofs; fixed-degree limitation theorems exist as templates.

**6. Finite-size (Knabe-type) criteria require frustration-freeness** and therefore do not directly apply at y≠0, where your model is frustrated.

## Details

### Item 1 — Yarotsky's stability theorems

**Citations.**
- D. Yarotsky, "Ground states in relatively bounded quantum perturbations of classical lattice systems," Commun. Math. Phys. 261, 799–819 (2006), DOI 10.1007/s00220-005-1456-9.
- D. Yarotsky, "Perturbations of ground states in weakly interacting quantum spin systems," J. Math. Phys. 45(6), 2134–2152 (2004).
- D. Yarotsky, "Uniqueness of the ground state in weak perturbations of non-interacting gapped quantum lattice systems," J. Stat. Phys. 118, 119–144 (2005).
- D. Yarotsky, "Quasi-particles in weak perturbations of non-interacting quantum lattice systems," arXiv:math-ph/0411042 (2004) — relevant because it constructs the quasi-particle / dispersion picture from isolated eigenvalues of the decoupled model, the abstract analogue of your m₋(k), m₊(k) bands.

**Itemized hypotheses (2006 CMP paper).**
(i) Lattice with a finite-dimensional single-site Hilbert space; H₀ = Σ_x h_x classical, i.e., a sum of commuting operators diagonal in a product basis, with a unique product ground state and a uniform gap.
(ii) The perturbation is decomposed as a sum of a uniformly bounded, finite-range (or rapidly decaying) part with small norm, plus a part that is **relatively bounded** with respect to H₀ (relative bound < 1).
(iii) Smallness of the coupling / norm of the bounded part.

**Conclusions:** (a) persistence of the spectral gap in the thermodynamic limit; (b) exponential decay of truncated correlations; (c) analyticity of the ground state in the coupling. The AKLT model, viewed on a coarse-grained length scale, is shown to fall into this class.

**(a) Finite- vs infinite-dimensional sites:** As published, single-site spaces are finite-dimensional. The "relatively bounded" mechanism is the one feature in the entire literature specifically designed for an *unbounded* H₀, which is why it is conceptually the closest to your Casimir H₀. However, the infinite-dimensionality of L²(SU(3)) per link is not covered. **(b)** Yes — a unique product ground state and a uniform H₀ gap are required (your electric vacuum and gap 8/3 satisfy this). **(c)** Gap persistence + exponential clustering + ground-state analyticity. **(d)** Constants are existential, not explicit.

**Applicability verdict:** Yarotsky's theorem is the closest classical-perturbation template and matches your H₀ = ½Σ C₂ (commuting, nonnegative, unique product vacuum, gap) and bounded plaquette V. The **one hypothesis that fails is finite-dimensionality of the site space**; and the conclusions are non-explicit in the threshold. It is a strong *structural* precedent, not a black-box that yields your explicit y₀.

### Item 2 — Lie–Schwinger block-diagonalization

**Citations.**
- J. Fröhlich, A. Pizzo, "Lie–Schwinger block-diagonalization and gapped quantum chains," Commun. Math. Phys. 375, 2039–2069 (2020), arXiv:1812.02457.
- S. Del Vecchio, J. Fröhlich, A. Pizzo, S. Rossi, "Lie–Schwinger block-diagonalization and gapped quantum chains with unbounded interactions," Commun. Math. Phys. 381, 1115–1152 (2021), arXiv:1908.07450.
- Same authors, "…: analyticity of the ground-state energy," J. Funct. Anal. (2020), arXiv:1908.07486.
- Same authors, "Local iterative block-diagonalization of gapped Hamiltonians: a new tool in singular perturbation theory," J. Math. Phys. 63, 073503 (2022), arXiv:2007.07667.

**Itemized hypotheses (common core, with the d≥2 paper as reference).**
(i) Lattice Λ_N^d ⊂ ℤ^d; local Hilbert space ℋ_i ≃ ℂ^M with **M finite and N-independent** (verbatim in arXiv:2007.07667: "ℋ_i ≃ ℂ^M, where M is an arbitrary, but finite, N-independent integer").
(ii) Unperturbed operator K₀ = Σ_i H_i, a sum of on-site terms, each with a unique ground state Ω and gap ≥ 1 above it (H ↾ {ℂΩ}^⊥ ≥ 1).
(iii) Perturbation t·K_I with K_I a sum of short-range bounded interaction potentials (in the "unbounded interactions" paper: form-bounded w.r.t. the on-site terms).
(iv) |t| small.

**Conclusions:** the spectral gap above the ground-state energy persists, bounded below by a positive constant **uniformly in the lattice size**, for |t| below an explicit threshold; the analyticity paper adds analyticity of the ground-state energy (and, translation-invariant, analyticity of the energy per site in the thermodynamic limit). **Thresholds are explicit** (constructive multiscale scheme based on local Lie–Schwinger conjugations on connected subsets / "minimal rectangles" in d>1).

**Which versions cover d>1 and unbounded terms?** The d≥2 paper (2022) covers higher dimensions; the 2021 paper covers unbounded interactions. Both keep finite-dimensional on-site spaces. The unperturbed term is always a sum of **on-site** terms; your H₀ is a sum of **on-link** Casimirs, which is structurally the same (single-cell commuting terms). Your V is a sum of single-plaquette bounded terms coupling the 4 links of a plaquette — a bounded short-range interaction, exactly the DFPR form.

**Applicability verdict:** DFPR is the **most promising rigorous route** and the closest in both structure and output (explicit, size-uniform threshold in d≥2). The decisive obstruction is hypothesis (i): L²(SU(3)) is infinite-dimensional. Two sub-issues must be handled: (1) the large-denominator/projection estimates that control the multiscale conjugations use finite M; with the Casimir gap they would be replaced by bounds involving the unbounded H₀ (the "relatively bounded" spirit of Yarotsky) — plausible but not proven; (2) gauge invariance/Gauss law must be threaded through the local conjugations, which is not addressed in DFPR. A KS-adapted DFPR theorem would be a genuine extension, not a corollary.

### Item 3 — BHM / Michalakis–Zwolak and relatives

**Citations.**
- S. Bravyi, M. Hastings, S. Michalakis, "Topological quantum order: stability under local perturbations," J. Math. Phys. 51, 093512 (2010), arXiv:1001.0344.
- S. Michalakis, J. Zwolak, "Stability of frustration-free Hamiltonians," Commun. Math. Phys. 322, 277–302 (2013), arXiv:1109.1588.
- B. Nachtergaele, R. Sims, A. Young, "Quasi-locality bounds for quantum lattice systems. Part II. Perturbations of frustration-free spin models with gapped ground states," Ann. Henri Poincaré 23 (2022), DOI 10.1007/s00023-021-01086-5 — BHM strategy under LTQO.
- W. De Roeck, M. Salmhofer, "Persistence of exponential decay and spectral gaps for interacting fermions," Commun. Math. Phys. 365, 773–796 (2019).

**Itemized hypotheses (BHM/MZ).**
(i) H₀ a sum of geometrically local, pairwise **commuting projectors** (BHM) / gapped **frustration-free** local Hamiltonian (MZ), on a finite-dimensional-spin D-dim lattice.
(ii) Topological order conditions: TQO-1 (ground-space local indistinguishability / QEC distance) and TQO-2 (local–global ground-space consistency); MZ uses Local Topological Quantum Order (LTQO).
(iii) Perturbation a sum of short-range bounded-norm interactions, strength below a constant threshold.

**Conclusion:** spectral bands from the low-lying H₀ eigenvalues persist, separated by a constant gap; ground-space splitting is super-polynomially small.

**Which hypotheses the SU(3) KS setup satisfies/violates:**
- Commuting projectors / frustration-freeness: **VIOLATED** at y≠0. H₀ = ½ΣC₂ is a sum of commuting operators but is *not* a sum of projectors, and once V is added the total is not frustration-free (the product vacuum is no longer a simultaneous ground state of each local term). ✗
- Finite-dimensional sites: **VIOLATED** (L²(SU(3))). ✗
- Local perturbation, bounded, short range: satisfied (‖χ_f‖∞ = 3, single-plaquette). ✓
- TQO/LTQO: not applicable — your gap is a conventional (non-topological) strong-coupling gap with a unique product vacuum, not a degenerate topologically ordered ground space.

**Applicability verdict:** BHM/MZ are the **wrong tool**: they are built for frustration-free commuting-projector (topological) models with finite-dimensional spins. Your model is a conventional gapped perturbation problem with an unbounded H₀ and no frustration-freeness at y≠0. De Roeck–Salmhofer drops finite-dimensionality only in the sense of Fock space over a lattice (quadratic fermionic H₀ separated by a gap, weak rapidly-decaying interaction), not for L²(compact group) link variables, and needs a quasi-free reference; it does not apply. Nachtergaele–Sims–Young stays within finite-dimensional frustration-free spin systems (with an LTQO condition).

### Item 4 — Prior art on Hamiltonian LGT gaps (the novelty check)

**(a) Osterwalder–Seiler (Euclidean).** K. Osterwalder, E. Seiler, "Gauge field theories on a lattice," Ann. Phys. 110, 440–471 (1978), DOI 10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8. Verbatim abstract: they "verify physical positivity for the Schwinger functions… This implies the existence of a positive self-adjoint transfer matrix," then "prove existence and analyticity of the infinite volume limit of strongly coupled Yang-Mills theories on the lattice and… verify Wilson's confinement bound." Concretely: reflection (physical) positivity ⟹ a positive self-adjoint **transfer matrix**; a convergent strong-coupling cluster expansion uniform in volume; existence and analyticity of the infinite-volume limit; and Wilson's area-law/confinement bound. This yields a mass gap / exponential clustering for the **Euclidean transfer matrix** on a lattice with fixed temporal spacing. It does **not** carry out the time-continuum (a₀→0) reduction to the Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian, and no published work makes that reduction into a rigorous KS-Hamiltonian spectral-gap theorem. Modern Euclidean refinements: Shen–Zhu–Zhu, "A stochastic analysis approach to lattice Yang–Mills at strong coupling," Commun. Math. Phys. 400, 805–851 (2023); and Cao–Nissim–Sheffield, "Dynamical approach to area law for lattice Yang–Mills," arXiv:2509.04688 (2025, to appear Proc. Amer. Math. Soc.), whose Theorem 1.6 states: "Let d ≥ 2, N ≥ 2, and G ∈ {U(N), SU(N), SO(2(N−1))}. Then for β < β*_G, there are constants C and c such that… |⟨W_ℓ⟩| ≤ C exp(−c area(ℓ))," with the explicit threshold set in their Definition 1.4 as β*_{SU(N)} = β*_{U(N)} = 1/(8(d−1)); the proof verifies the mass-gap condition of Durhuus–Fröhlich [DF80] (1980), which requires the gauge group to have nontrivial center.

**(b) Continuous-time / Hamiltonian constructive work.** C. Borgs, "Confinement, deconfinement and freezing in lattice Yang-Mills theories with continuous time," Commun. Math. Phys. 116, 309–342 (1988), DOI 10.1007/BF01225259. Verbatim abstract: "In this paper I analyse lattice Yang-Mills theories with continuous time. After a short discussion of more conceptual questions, such as the existence of a Hamilton operator in the infinite volume limit, I study the phase diagram. The existence of a strong coupling/low temperature confinement phase (which was not proven up to now) is established for arbitrary compact groups, continuous or discrete… The methods… rely on cluster expansion methods, combined with renormalisation ideas." Borgs proves existence of the Hamilton operator in the infinite-volume limit (defined abstractly as −log of the transfer matrix; compactness argument in his Appendix A) and, per the paper's introduction, physical properties "like mass gap or confinement" in Section 3 via convergent cluster expansions. This is the closest bona fide mathematical-physics result, but (i) the Hamiltonian is the abstract log-transfer-matrix operator, not the explicit KS E²/plaquette operator; (ii) an explicit gap constant/coupling threshold could not be verified from the accessible text (numbered theorems paywalled). Related: Fredenhagen–Marcu, "Charged states in Z₂ gauge theories," Commun. Math. Phys. 92, 81–119 (1983) — a rigorous confinement *order parameter* (charged-state overlap ratio), not a KS gap theorem.

**(c) Modern era (2015–2026).** For **finite-group / Z_N (Kitaev-type) gauge theories**, spectral gaps and their stability are rigorously established because those are commuting-projector, frustration-free, finite-dimensional models covered by BHM (Kitaev 2003; BHM 2010; e.g., "Kitaev's quantum double model as an error correcting code," arXiv:1908.02829, proving TQO for arbitrary finite groups). These do **not** cover SU(3) with a continuous group and unbounded electric term. Recent quantum-simulation literature (e.g., minimally truncated qutrit SU(3) KS Hamiltonians, arXiv:2601.10065; "Quantum gate sets for lattice QCD in the strong-coupling limit," EPJ Quantum Technology 2024) computes spectra numerically but proves no infinite-volume gap. A very recent claim — Faizal et al., "Reflection-Positive Construction of a Four-Dimensional SU(N) Yang-Mills Theory with Mass Gap and Confinement," arXiv:2606.19362 / Fortschr. Phys. (Wiley, 2026), DOI 10.1002/prop.70097 — asserts a strictly positive, volume-uniform spectral gap for the transfer operator H_σ = −log T_σ in a strong-coupling window via Kotecký–Preiss cluster expansion. This is an extraordinary, very recent, not-community-verified claim, and it concerns the log-transfer-matrix Hamiltonian, not the time-continuum KS operator.

**VERDICT (item 4):** The planned theorem — explicit y₀, SU(3), 3+1D KS (E²/plaquette) Hamiltonian, gauge-invariant sector, with certified rational small-y asymptotics of the dispersion — **would be new** and is **not** a corollary of existing results. The single closest existing rigorous statement is **Borgs (1988)**: a mass-gap/confinement result for the continuous-time lattice Yang–Mills Hamiltonian (as log-transfer-matrix) at strong coupling for arbitrary compact groups. It differs from the target in three respects: it works with the abstract transfer-matrix Hamiltonian rather than the canonical KS operator; it provides no explicit coupling threshold that can be read off; and it produces no certified perturbative asymptotics of the excitation dispersion. On the Euclidean side, Osterwalder–Seiler (1978) is the foundational strong-coupling clustering/mass-gap result but does not deliver a KS-Hamiltonian gap.

### Item 5 — SOS / Positivstellensatz spectral-gap certificates

**Citations.**
- N. Ozawa, "Noncommutative real algebraic geometry of Kazhdan's property (T)," J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 15, 85–90 (2016). Characterization: Γ has property (T) iff Δ² − εΔ = Σ_i b_i* b_i (a sum of hermitian squares) in ℝ[Γ] for some ε>0 — i.e., an algebraic spectral gap of the group Laplacian Δ = |S| − Σ_{s∈S} s.
- T. Netzer, A. Thom, "Kazhdan's Property (T) via semidefinite optimization," Exp. Math. 24, 371–374 (2015), arXiv:1411.2488. Abstract: for SL(3,ℤ), "Δ² − (1/6)Δ is a hermitian sum of squares in the group algebra… This corresponds to a spectral gap of 1/72 ∼ 0.014 for the associated random walk operator," a result they note improves prior spectral-gap bounds by a factor of about 2500; the SOS representation was found by SDP and then made exact symbolically.
- M. Kaluba, P. Nowak, N. Ozawa, "Aut(F₅) has property (T)," Math. Ann. 375, 1169–1191 (2019), arXiv:1712.07167. Their Theorem 1 gives SAut(F₅) property (T) with Kazhdan constant κ(SAut(F₅),S) > 0.18 (solver-certified value ≥ 0.18027, from an SOS decomposition of Δ² − λΔ with λ = 1.3).
- M. Kaluba, D. Kielak, P. Nowak, "On property (T) for Aut(Fₙ) and SLₙ(ℤ)," Ann. of Math. 193, 539–562 (2021), arXiv:1812.03456: "Aut(Fₙ) has Kazhdan's property (T) for every n ≥ 6," with an explicit Kazhdan constant at Kazhdan radius 2; combined with Kaluba–Nowak–Ozawa this yields property (T) for all n ≥ 5.
- M. Kaluba, P. Nowak, "Certifying numerical estimates of spectral gaps," arXiv:1703.09680; M. Nitsche, "Computer proofs for Property (T), and SDP duality," arXiv:2009.05134, and "A computer-free property (T) proof for high-rank Aut(Fₙ)," Bull. LMS (2026); Fujiwara–Kabaya improvements for SLₙ(ℤ).
- Netzer–Thom real closed separation theorems; Ozawa, "A substitute for Kazhdan's property (T) for universal non-lattices," Anal. PDE 17, 2541–2560 (2024).

**Mechanism and relevance.** These prove a spectral gap of a self-adjoint element of a group *-algebra by exhibiting a sum-of-hermitian-squares certificate; the numerics are done by SDP and then *rigorously* certified (interval arithmetic / exact symbolic rounding). This is exactly the paradigm for a one-plaquette SU(N) certificate: the single-plaquette Hamiltonian lives in (a representation of) a group *-algebra, and a hermitian-SOS certificate would give a rigorous lower bound on its gap with an explicit constant. Adjacent to this, SDP/noncommutative-SOS lower bounds on ground-state energies and gaps of local Hamiltonians are an active area (e.g., the frustration-free SDP hierarchy of arXiv:2411.03680, Quantum 2026), reinforcing that SOS lower bounds are a legitimate, rigorous gap technology.

**Limitation theorems (templates for a negative result).** For fixed-degree SOS/LP relaxations, the Lasserre/Sum-of-Squares CSP literature proves that fixed (even linear) numbers of rounds cannot certify certain facts: Grigoriev (2001) and Schoenebeck (2008) — Ω(n) Lasserre rounds fail for random 3-XOR/3-SAT; Tulsiani (2009) — extensions to equations over abelian groups; Ghosh–Tulsiani and the VCSP dichotomy (arXiv:1602.05409; "The limits of SDP relaxations for general-valued CSPs," arXiv:1612.01147) — sub-linear Lasserre levels do not beat the basic LP relaxation. Also degree-4 SoS planted-clique lower bounds (Meka–Potechin–Wigderson). No such limitation theorem is yet specialized to SU(N) one-plaquette Hamiltonians under 't Hooft scaling.

**Applicability verdict:** SOS certification is a *rigorous, explicit-constant* route for the **one-plaquette / SU(N) gap** sub-problem and for the group-theoretic core of your program; it is the natural companion to a "certified spectral program." For the planned **limitation theorem** (fixed-degree certificates cannot give N-uniform gap constants under 't Hooft scaling), the CSP/Lasserre negative-result templates (Grigoriev–Schoenebeck–Tulsiani; VCSP dichotomy) are the closest existing analogues, but adapting them from Boolean/abelian CSPs to the noncommutative SU(N) group-ring setting is unaddressed and would itself be new.

### Item 6 — Finite-size criteria

**Citations.**
- S. Knabe, "Energy gaps and elementary excitations for certain VBS-quantum antiferromagnets," J. Stat. Phys. 52, 627 (1988).
- D. Gosset, E. Mozgunov, "Local gap threshold for frustration-free spin systems," arXiv:1512.00088 (J. Math. Phys. 2016): improves Knabe from Θ(n⁻¹) to Θ(n⁻²), for periodic, translation-invariant chains.
- M. Lemm, E. Mozgunov, "Spectral gaps of frustration-free spin systems with boundary," J. Math. Phys. 60, 051901 (2019), arXiv:1801.08915: threshold ~n^{−3/2}, robust in 1D/2D, arbitrary lattices/finite-range.
- M. Lemm, "Knabe's spectral gap method for open boundary conditions," arXiv:1709.02756.
- The martingale method: B. Nachtergaele, "The spectral gap for some spin chains with discrete symmetry breaking," Commun. Math. Phys. 175, 565–606 (1996).
- SDP-hierarchy generalization: "A Hierarchy of Spectral Gap Certificates for Frustration-Free Spin Systems," Quantum (2026), arXiv:2411.03680.

**Itemized hypotheses.** (i) **Frustration-freeness** (the local terms are projectors/positive with a common zero-energy ground state) — essential to all Knabe descendants; (ii) finite-dimensional spins; (iii) finite-range/translation-invariance (for the Θ(n⁻²) version, which requires strict translation invariance per Gosset–Mozgunov); (iv) a computed finite-size gap exceeding an explicit n-dependent threshold. Nachtergaele's martingale method requires a frustration-free (or nearly so) sequence of ground-state projections satisfying a bound on overlaps of neighboring local projections.

**Applicability verdict:** These criteria are **not directly applicable**: your model is frustration-free only at y=0 (where the gap is trivially the H₀ gap 8/3), but at y≠0 the perturbed Hamiltonian is *frustrated* (the product vacuum is no longer a simultaneous eigenstate of each local term), and the local spaces are infinite-dimensional. A finite-size criterion for frustrated systems would require a Knabe-type theorem *without* frustration-freeness, which does not exist in the literature; the SDP-hierarchy certificate program (arXiv:2411.03680) also assumes frustration-freeness. These tools could at most control the y=0 endpoint, not the gap for |y|<y₀.

## Recommendations

1. **Primary route — adapt DFPR local Lie–Schwinger to L²(SU(3)) links (staged).** (a) First re-prove the DFPR d≥2 theorem replacing ℂ^M by L²(SU(3)) with H₀ = ½C₂: verify that the large-denominator/projection estimates survive when the on-link gap is provided by the unbounded Casimir rather than a finite matrix gap (use the relatively-bounded structure à la Yarotsky). (b) Thread the Gauss-law constraint through the local conjugations so the block-diagonalization stays in the gauge-invariant sector. (c) Extract the explicit threshold y₀. **Benchmark that would change the plan:** if the projection estimates cannot be made gap-uniform because the number of intermediate SU(3) representations contributing at each scale proliferates (destroying N-independence of constants), fall back to route 2.

2. **Fallback route — Yarotsky-style relatively-bounded cluster expansion / Kirkwood–Thomas.** Datta–Kennedy, "Expansions for one quasiparticle states in spin 1/2 systems," J. Stat. Phys. 108, 373–399 (2002), proved *convergent* one-quasiparticle dispersion expansions for the transverse-field Ising model via the Kirkwood–Thomas Schrödinger-equation ansatz — the exact analogue of your flat-band m₋(k) and gapped m₊(k) expansions. Adapting that machinery (together with Yarotsky's quasi-particle construction, arXiv:math-ph/0411042) to the gauge-invariant single-plaquette-flux sector would both (i) discharge "Assumption 6.5" (infinite-volume linked-cluster validity) rigorously and (ii) certify the rational coefficients as genuine asymptotics. This directly targets your stated goal and is the most surgical way to convert your certified rational data into a theorem.

3. **Independent corroboration via the Euclidean bridge.** Because Osterwalder–Seiler (1978) and Borgs (1988) already give a strong-coupling mass gap on the transfer-matrix / continuous-time side, a rigorous time-continuum-limit argument connecting the anisotropic Wilson transfer matrix to H_KS would provide a second, independent proof of a gap (though likely without your explicit y₀). Publishing that reduction explicitly would itself be a contribution, since it appears to be missing from the rigorous literature.

4. **SOS sub-program.** Pursue the one-plaquette SU(N) hermitian-SOS certificate (Ozawa / Netzer–Thom / Kaluba–Nowak toolchain, with interval-arithmetic certification) as a rigorous, explicit-constant lower bound on the single-cell gap; and develop the limitation theorem by porting Grigoriev–Schoenebeck–Tulsiani fixed-degree lower-bound techniques to the SU(N) group ring. **Threshold:** if a low-degree certificate already yields an N-uniform constant numerically, the limitation theorem is moot and effort should shift to certifying the constant rather than proving impossibility.

5. **Novelty claim in the paper.** State explicitly (per this review) that no prior rigorous explicit-threshold KS-Hamiltonian gap theorem exists; cite Borgs (1988) and Osterwalder–Seiler (1978) as the closest precedents and delineate the three differences (canonical KS operator vs log-transfer-matrix; explicit y₀; certified dispersion asymptotics). Position the result as the first explicit-threshold gap for the canonical SU(N) KS Hamiltonian in the gauge-invariant sector.

## Caveats

- **Borgs (1988) theorem details unverified at the level of numbered theorems/thresholds.** The abstract and introduction confirm existence of the infinite-volume Hamiltonian and a strong-coupling confinement phase with mass gap via cluster expansions, but the full text was paywalled; the exact hypotheses, theorem numbers, and any explicit gap constant could not be checked. Treat the "closest existing statement" designation accordingly.
- **Faizal et al. (2026) is an unverified, extraordinary claim** (a route to the Clay problem). It is cited here only for completeness; it should not be relied upon as established literature, and it addresses H_σ = −log T_σ, not the KS operator in the time-continuum limit.
- The "general theorem" that all lattice gauge models have a finite strong-coupling radius of convergence is folklore-cited (e.g., in coupled-cluster reviews such as arXiv:cond-mat/9411012) and could not be attached to a single citable theorem with an explicit SU(N) 3+1D KS gap bound.
- Whether the DFPR estimates truly extend to infinite-dimensional link spaces with unbounded H₀ is **plausible but unproven**; no paper was found that carries out a Lie–Schwinger block-diagonalization for infinite-dimensional local Hilbert spaces.
- All perturbative rational coefficients in the task statement (the m₋(k), m₊(k) expansions) and "Assumption 6.5" are the researcher's own inputs and were not independently verifiable from public literature; the recommendations treat discharging Assumption 6.5 as an open task, not a solved one.
- No literature specializes fixed-degree SOS limitation theorems to SU(N) one-plaquette Hamiltonians under 't Hooft scaling; the cited CSP/Lasserre negative results are analogues, not direct precedents.
- Cao–Nissim–Sheffield (arXiv:2509.04688) and the frustration-free SDP hierarchy (arXiv:2411.03680, Quantum 2026) are recent and, while peer-reviewed/accepted, are cited here for the Euclidean-side and SOS-hierarchy state of the art respectively; neither addresses the KS Hamiltonian gap directly.
