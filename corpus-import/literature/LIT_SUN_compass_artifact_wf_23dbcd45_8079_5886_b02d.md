# Literature Verification for the SU(N) Wilson One-Plaquette Spectral Program: Substrates for the Limitation Theorem (A) and the Crossover Theorem (B)

## TL;DR
- **The [CDG24] limitation template is real, correctly characterized, and directly relevant, but Euclidean-specific.** Cohn–Dong–Gonçalves (*Proc. Amer. Math. Soc., Series B* 11 (2024); arXiv:2210.01684) Theorem 1.2 proves that Gaussian-times-polynomial test functions of degree sublinear in the dimension cannot beat degree-3 asymptotics for the ±1 sign-uncertainty radius (ρ ~ √(d/2π)), via a Gauss-quadrature/Laguerre-root argument. This is a genuine fixed-degree limitation, and the plan (A) to transplant it to an SU(N) certificate cone via a contraction to the Cohn–Elkies cone A_d with d ~ N² is plausible in outline — but the transplant step (compact cone → Euclidean cone) has **no existing precedent as a rigorous Γ-limit** and would be the main new mathematics.
- **The Euclidean substrate is fully in place and now includes an *exactly matching* rate result.** The recently posted OpenAI/machine-model manuscript "Ten Advances in Mathematics and Theoretical Computer Science" (Ch. 1) claims lim_d LP_d^{1/d} = √(e/2π), i.e. the exact exponential rate α* = ½log₂(2π/e) = 0.6044… conjectured by Afkhami-Jeddi–Cohn–Hartman–de Laat–Tajdini (2020), and both sign-uncertainty radii → (1/π+o(1))√d. So the AI-claimed √(e/2π)-type rate is consistent with the conjectured value and the just-announced proof — not an anomaly.
- **Substrates rank, most to least ready-made: (2) sign-uncertainty/LP state of the art and (1) the [CDG24] template [essentially off-the-shelf] > (6) toric Bergman/Berman–Berndtsson and (4) Cartan-motion contraction [standard tools, but not assembled for this purpose] > (3) compact-group Delsarte/LP [exists but underdeveloped for non-abelian sign-uncertainty] > (5) amortized-KL + coupling in Gibbs measures [ingredients exist; the specific synthesis is essentially new].**

## Key Findings
1. **[CDG24] Theorem 1.2 is exactly the fixed-degree limitation the plan needs**, proved by a Gauss-quadrature argument on Laguerre roots — a moment/quadrature mechanism, not a Hermite-expansion or modular-forms argument. It bounds what low-degree polynomial×Gaussian certificates can achieve for the sign-uncertainty problem, the sibling of the Cohn–Elkies LP.
2. **Edwin (arXiv:2505.15994, 2025)** proves a *general* lower bound A_s(d)/√d ≥ 1/(2√π) for d ≥ 5 via a Beckner–Hausdorff–Young interpolation inequality (‖f‖₂² ≤ (2/e)^{d/2}‖f‖₁‖f̂‖₁) and reproduces the Torquato–Stillinger growth rate for the Cohn–Elkies LP. It is a genuine general (not test-function-restricted) lower bound, but weaker than the sharp constant.
3. **A developed Delsarte/LP theory on compact groups and Gelfand pairs exists** (Kabatianskii–Levenshtein, Gorbachev, Bachoc–Vallentin, and 2025–2026 work by Wackenhuth and by Berdysheva–Farkas–Gaál–Ramabulana–Révész), but a full *sign-uncertainty* theory on non-abelian compact Lie groups with a Peter–Weyl/character Fourier side is not standard; the SU(N) certificate cone would extend rather than instantiate existing results.
4. **The compact→Euclidean contraction has fully standard analytic tools** (Dooley–Rice Cartan-motion-group contraction; characters → Bessel/spherical functions; Mehler–Heine; HCIZ), but there is **no existing theorem that an LP/positive-definite optimization over a compact group converges (Γ-convergence or otherwise) to its Euclidean analogue** as dimension/curvature scales. This is the load-bearing gap in (A).
5. **The "amortized KL + coupling" replacement of Peierls union bounds has all ingredients in the literature but no assembled precedent.** Raz's Lemma/Yuen's quantum Raz Lemma supply the amortized-entropy-over-coordinates mechanism; van den Berg–Maes disagreement percolation and Marton/Wu transportation-information inequalities supply couplings controlled by relative entropy; Chazottes–Redig (2022) even derive Gibbs uniqueness from a relative-entropy density. But the specific synthesis is essentially new.
6. **The toric Bergman/Berman–Berndtsson substrate is solid and the claimed mechanism is standard.** Berman–Berndtsson's real Monge–Ampère solvability (transport potential with (∇φ)#(e^{−φ}dx) = Leb on P, barycenter condition), their Ehrhart/volume bound via Moser–Trudinger, Fujita's (n+1)ⁿ bound, and Zelditch's off-diagonal toric Bergman decay together make "off-diagonal entries of a multiplication operator in a weighted monomial basis controlled by ∇φ ∈ K" a standard consequence.

## Details

### Item 1 — The fixed-degree limitation template ([CDG24], Edwin)

**Citation.** Henry Cohn, Dingding Dong, Felipe Gonçalves, "Sign uncertainty principles and low-degree polynomials," *Proceedings of the AMS, Series B* 11 (2024), communicated by Dmitriy Bilyk; arXiv:2210.01684 (v1 Oct 2022, v3 Jul 2024). Received Oct 17, 2023. MSC 42B10, 42C05; 52C17.

**Setup.** With Fourier normalization f̂(y) = ∫ f(x)e^{−2πi⟨x,y⟩}dx, the ±1 sign-uncertainty problem (Problem 1.1) asks for the least ρ ≥ 0 such that there is a radial integrable f: ℝ^d → ℝ, not identically zero, with f̂ = sf (s = ±1), f(0) = 0, and f(x) ≥ 0 for |x| ≥ ρ. The tractable test-function family is f(x) = p(2π|x|²)e^{−π|x|²} with p a single-variable polynomial; writing p in the Laguerre basis L_k^{d/2−1} diagonalizes the Fourier transform (eigenvalue (−1)^k).

**Theorem 1.2 (exact statement).** Let ρ_{d,s,n} be the optimal ρ in Problem 1.1 when f is restricted to f(x) = p(2π|x|²)e^{−π|x|²} with deg p ≤ n. Then for each s = ±1 and every n(d) ∈ {3,4,5,…} with lim_{d→∞} n(d)/d = 0,
  ρ_{d,s,n(d)} ~ √(d/(2π))  as d → ∞.
In words: **polynomials of sublinear degree cannot asymptotically improve on degree ≤ 3.**

**Proof mechanism (Theorem 2.1 + Gauss quadrature).** The engine is *not* a Hermite expansion; it is a Gauss-quadrature/moment argument. Theorem 2.1: if f(x)=p(2π|x|²)e^{−π|x|²}, deg p ≤ n, p ≢ 0, and f̂(0)=0, then p has a sign change at or beyond 2λ, where λ is the smallest root of the Laguerre polynomial L_m^{d/2−1} with m = ⌊n/2⌋+1. Proof: f̂(0)=0 becomes ∫₀^∞ p(2u)e^{−u}u^{d/2−1}du = 0; Gauss quadrature for the measure e^{−u}u^{d/2−1}du with the m roots of L_m^{d/2−1} (positive weights) forces a sign change of p among the nodes. The Ismail–Li bound (Proc. AMS 115 (1992), Thm 4) gives λ > 2m + d/2 − 3 − √(1+4(m−1)(m+d/2−2)), so with n=o(d) one gets 2λ ≥ (1−o(1))d, whence 2πρ² ≥ 2λ ≥ (1−o(1))d, i.e. ρ ≥ (1−o(1))√(d/2π); the matching upper bound is already achieved at n=3. The radial weight u^{d/2−1}e^{−u} here is precisely the Laguerre/Mellin structure the researcher reports internally, with effective radial-dimension parameter d/2 — the exact analogue of the internal α_N = (N²−3)/2 with d ~ N².

**Linear-degree remark (directly relevant to 't Hooft scaling).** CDG also record: for deg p = (c+o(1))d, ρ ≥ (√((c+1/2−√(c(c+1)))/π) − o(1))√d; to even hope to reach the conjectured ρ = (1/π+o(1))√d one needs c ≥ (π−2)²/(8π) = 0.0518…, and they conjecture c must → ∞ for optimal asymptotics (Theorem 2.1 cannot prove this since λ→0 as c→∞). **This is the crucial quantitative statement for the plan: it tells you exactly how the certificate degree must scale with dimension d ~ N² to escape the limitation, which under 't Hooft scaling is the whole question.**

**Edwin, "Fourier inequalities and sign uncertainty," arXiv:2505.15994 (May 2025, 10 pp., math.CA).** Main results: Theorem 1.4 — for s=±1 and d ≥ 5, A_s(d)/√d ≥ 1/(2√π) ≈ 0.282095. This follows from Theorem 1.5 — for all f with f, f̂ ∈ L¹, ‖f‖₂² ≤ (2/e)^{d/2}‖f‖₁‖f̂‖₁, proved by Hölder + Beckner's sharp Hausdorff–Young + log-convexity of L^{p_θ} norms, taking θ→1⁻. A consequence (eq. 1.9): Δ_d^{LP} ≥ (1/4)(e/8)^{d/2}, matching the dominant growth rate of the Torquato–Stillinger conjectured lower bound (Torquato–Stillinger, *Experiment. Math.* 15 (2006), eq. 116). Edwin's bound is *general* (all admissible f, not a restricted test-function class) and is the state-of-the-art general lower bound on the sign-uncertainty radius in high dimension, but the constant 1/(2√π) is below the conjectured/known-sharp asymptotics.

**Applicability verdict (1) for (A):** [CDG24] Thm 1.2/Thm 2.1 is an essentially off-the-shelf limitation for Euclidean low-degree LP/sign-uncertainty certificates, and its Laguerre/Gauss-quadrature mechanism matches the researcher's radial-weight structure exactly. **Gaps to close:** (i) the SU(N) certificate cone (pointwise-nonnegative class functions with nonnegative character coefficients) is *not* the Euclidean cone; porting Thm 2.1 requires a proven contraction of that cone to A_d (see item 4). (ii) [CDG24] restricts to radial polynomial×Gaussian; the SU(N) character basis maps to Laguerre only *after* the contraction, so "fixed degree" must be shown to correspond to fixed character-truncation. (iii) Edwin's general bound is useful as a floor but does not itself yield a limitation; the limitation must come from the CDG-type quadrature obstruction.

### Item 2 — Sign-uncertainty and LP: state of the art

**(a) Bourgain–Clozel–Kahane.** "Principe d'Heisenberg et fonctions positives," *Ann. Inst. Fourier (Grenoble)* 60 (2010) no. 4, 1215–1232 (DOI 10.5802/aif.2552; arXiv:0811.4360). The +1 principle: for nonzero even integrable f: ℝ^d → ℝ with f̂ real and integrable, if f(0), f̂(0) ≤ 0 then A(f)A(f̂) is bounded away from 0. Théorème 1 (d=1, exact form): "Let f: ℝ→ℝ be a nonzero, integrable, even function such that f(0)≤0, f̂∈L¹(ℝ) and f̂(0)≤0. Then A(f)A(f̂) ≥ 0.1687, and 0.1687 cannot be replaced by 0.41." In general dimension they proved ρ₁ρ₂ ≥ cd (linear-in-d lower bound). The best asymptotic bracket pre-2026, made explicit by Gonçalves–Oliveira e Silva–Ramos (arXiv:2003.10765): **1/√(2πe) ≤ liminf_{d→∞} A₊(d)/√d ≤ limsup_{d→∞} A₊(d)/√d ≤ 1/√(2π).**

**Cohn–Gonçalves.** "An optimal uncertainty principle in twelve dimensions via modular forms," *Invent. Math.* 217 (2019) no. 3, 799–831 (DOI 10.1007/s00222-019-00875-4). Solves the +1 problem exactly in d=12: minimal ρ₁ρ₂ = 2 (A₊(12)=√2), via Viazovska-style modular forms. Introduces the −1 variant tied to the Cohn–Elkies LP; the −1 problem is solved exactly in d=1,8,24 (ρ₁ρ₂ = 1,2,4), implied by LP sharpness. **Conjecture 1.5:** lim_{d→∞} ρ/√d exists and is independent of s.

**AJCHT.** Afkhami-Jeddi–Cohn–Hartman–de Laat–Tajdini, "High-dimensional sphere packing and the modular bootstrap," *JHEP* 12 (2020) 066 (arXiv:2006.02560). U(1)^c × U(1)^c spinless modular bootstrap = Cohn–Elkies LP in d=2c. Numerically extrapolated **Conjecture 3.1** (verbatim): "an upper bound of 2^−(λ+o(1))d for the sphere packing density in ℝ^d as d→∞ with λ ≈ 0.6044; in modular bootstrap terms…c/(Λ + o(1)) for the spectral gap as c→∞ with Λ ≈ 9.869"; they add "We are very confident in the first three decimal places of the estimate 0.6044 for λ, and fairly confident in the fourth." **Conjecture 3.2** (speculative): the exact value corresponds to Λ = π². Their (3.3) predicts the common radius asymptotic and (3.5) the s-independence, consistent with Cohn–Gonçalves Conj 1.5. Ratio A₊(d)/A₋(d) ≈ 1 + O(1/d).

**(b) Cohn–Elkies LP asymptotics.** Cohn–Elkies, "New upper bounds on sphere packings I," *Ann. of Math.* 157 (2003) 689–714 (LP bound; Gorbachev independently). Kabatianskii–Levenshtein (1978): Δ ≤ 2^{−(0.59905576…+o(1))d}, still the best proven exponent for high d before 2026 (the exact classical exponent is 0.59905576…, per KL78/Lev79; subsequent spherical-code refinements — Cohn–Zhao 2014, and Sardari–Zargar-type improvements — improved only lower-order factors, not the exponent). Cohn–Zhao, "Sphere packing bounds via spherical codes," *Duke Math. J.* 163 (2014) 1965–2002 (arXiv:1212.5966): the Cohn–Elkies LP is always at least as strong as KL (continuous analogue of Rodemich's theorem) and improves the KL constant factor, but with *the same 0.599 exponent*. So before 2026 it was open whether the LP exponent beats KL; the conjectured value was λ ≈ 0.6044 (AJCHT).

**Calibration of the AI-claimed √(e/2π) rate.** The recently posted manuscript "Ten Advances in Mathematics and Theoretical Computer Science" (OpenAI, machine-generated; Chapter 1) states **Theorem 1.1** (verbatim): "As d → ∞, LP_d^{1/d} → √(e/(2π))," giving "∆_d ≤ LP_d = 2^{−(α∗+o(1))d}, where α∗ = ½ log₂(2π/e) = 0.6044…. This is the first improvement since 1978 to the general sphere-packing exponent." And **Theorem 1.2** (verbatim, matching the abstract's "The positive- and negative-eigenvalue Fourier sign-uncertainty radii are both (1/π + o(1))√d"): "lim_{d→∞} A₊(d)/√d = lim_{d→∞} A₋(d)/√d = 1/π" (with A₊(d) < A₋(d) for all d, App. A). It explicitly credits [AJCHLT20 Conjs. 3.1–3.2], Cohn–Gonçalves Conj 1.5, [AJCHLT20 (3.5)] and the common value (3.3), and cites [Edw25] and [CDG24 Thm 1.2] as the general lower bound and low-degree limitation, respectively. **Verdict:** the √(e/2π)-type exact rate is exactly the AJCHT-conjectured value and is now claimed proved; it is *not* implausible — it is the expected answer. Caveat: this manuscript is machine-generated and (as of retrieval) not peer-reviewed; the √(e/2π) rate should be attributed as "conjectured by AJCHT (2020), claimed proved in the 2026 OpenAI manuscript, pending refereeing."

**Applicability verdict (2):** The Euclidean side of the crossover (B) is very well supported: exact sign-uncertainty answers in d ∈ {1,8,12,24}, sharp asymptotic constants (1/π radius; √(e/2π) LP rate) now available, and the Mellin-transform-of-a-Gaussian construction used in the OpenAI proof (§4) is exactly the "Mellin symmetry line" object the researcher wants to match against. **Gap:** none on the Euclidean side per se; the matching to compact-side certificates is the new content.

### Item 3 — Delsarte/LP and sign-uncertainty on compact spaces and groups

**Two-point-homogeneous / Gelfand-pair LP is mature.** Kabatianskii–Levenshtein and Delsarte–Goethals–Seidel LP on spheres/projective spaces, with zonal spherical functions as the Fourier side. Gorbachev (2000) derived Levenshtein's bound via the same functions that contract to the Euclidean Bessel example (explicitly noted in Cohn–Elkies I: Levenshtein's spherical-code LP functions "become our Bessel function example" as θ→0). Bachoc–Vallentin SDP; de Laat–Vallentin hierarchy of k-point bounds. Compact-homogeneous-space treatments: Gorbachev's Delsarte problem on rank-1 compact homogeneous Riemannian spaces (sign conditions on Fourier coefficients); Kolountzakis–Matolcsi–Weiner and Matolcsi–Weiner used the Delsarte scheme in *non-commutative* compact groups for mutually unbiased bases; Bachoc–Nebe–de Oliveira Filho–Vallentin, "Linear programming bounds for unitary space time codes" (arXiv:0803.1227) sets up the LP for a compact group G acting on X with orbit-characterizing τ and the character positivity property.

**2025–2026 developments.** Wackenhuth, "Linear programming bounds in homogeneous spaces, I: Optimal packing density" (arXiv:2505.23572) — LP bounds for commutative (Gelfand-pair) spaces, solving a Cohn–Zhao conjecture for hyperbolic space. Berdysheva–Farkas–Gaál–Ramabulana–Révész, "Duality for Delsarte's extremal problem on compact Gelfand pairs" (arXiv:2603.11792, Mar 2026) and its locally-compact-abelian companion (arXiv:2603.18287) — infinite-dimensional LP formulation of Turán and Delsarte problems on compact Gelfand pairs, with a strong-duality theorem. Ramabulana et al., "Delsarte-type extremal problems and convolution roots on homogeneous spaces" (arXiv:2511.13908) — reduces the G/K problem to K-bi-invariant functions on G and proves existence of extremizers and convolution roots for compact Gelfand pairs.

**Sign-uncertainty specifically on the sphere.** Gonçalves–Oliveira e Silva–Ramos, "New sign uncertainty principles," *Discrete Anal.* 2023:9 (DOI 10.19086/da.84266; arXiv:2003.10771): an operator sign-uncertainty principle (Theorem 1.1) generalizing BCK and Cohn–Gonçalves, with explicit instances for Fourier/Dini series, the Hilbert transform, discrete Fourier/Hankel transforms, spherical harmonics, Jacobi/Gegenbauer polynomials, and the Hamming cube; the sphere section connects sign uncertainty to spherical designs. This is the closest existing "sign uncertainty on a compact space" result.

**Applicability verdict (3):** LP/Delsarte theory and even a *general operator* sign-uncertainty principle exist, and the Gelfand-pair machinery is the right language if SU(N) is approached via the class-function (conjugation-invariant) reduction — note (SU(N)×SU(N), diag) is a Gelfand pair whose bi-invariant functions are class functions, so the character basis is the spherical-function basis. **Gaps for (A)/(B):** (i) existing compact-group LP puts sign conditions on either the function *or* the coefficients, but the researcher's cone imposes *both* pointwise nonnegativity of class functions *and* nonnegative character coefficients — a two-sided cone whose duality/attainment is only established in the commutative/Gelfand-pair setting; SU(N) class functions form a commutative convolution algebra (favorable), but the specific two-sided cone needs its own attainment/duality proof. (ii) No existing sign-uncertainty result is stated for SU(N) characters with the C₂/heat-kernel weight; the Gonçalves et al. operator framework is the natural vehicle but must be instantiated.

### Item 4 — Contraction limits: compact → Euclidean

**Cartan-motion-group contraction (fully standard).** Dooley–Rice, "On contractions of semisimple Lie groups," *Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* 289 (1985) 185–202 (and "Contractions of rotation groups and their representations," *Math. Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc.* 94 (1983) 509–517): every semisimple Lie group contracts to its Cartan motion group K ⋉ p, with a limiting formula for the representation theory (principal series → irreps of the motion group). Consequence used throughout harmonic analysis: **characters/spherical functions of G near the identity → Bessel/spherical functions of the Cartan motion group**; ψ_λ(Y) = ∫_K e^{iλ(Ad(k)Y)}dk on p (Helgason). Mehler–Heine-type asymptotics (Clerc; and "Mehler–Heine formula: a generalization in the context of spherical functions," arXiv:1807.03904) give the precise local rescaling of zonal spherical functions to Bessel functions. Ben Saïd–Ørsted and Rösler–Voit connect these to Dunkl/generalized Bessel functions. For SU(N) specifically: rescaling class functions near the identity by the appropriate 't Hooft scaling sends SU(N) characters to functions on the Cartan subalgebra whose radial part is governed by the Harish-Chandra/Itzykson–Zuber (HCIZ) integral, which is itself the K-average defining the motion-group spherical function.

**HCIZ / character asymptotics.** The HCIZ formula ∫_{U(N)} e^{tr(AUBU†)}dU = c_N det(e^{a_i b_j})/(Δ(a)Δ(b)) is the exact finite-N object whose N→∞ and near-identity limits produce the Gaussian/GUE radial weight u^{α_N}e^{−u} with α_N = (N²−3)/2 that the researcher reports; this is the standard bridge from SU(N) heat-kernel/character analysis to the Euclidean (Gaussian) radial model.

**The load-bearing gap: convergence of the *optimization problem*.** There is **no theorem** in the literature stating that an LP / positive-definite-optimization problem over a compact group or homogeneous space Γ-converges (or converges in any variational sense) to its Euclidean analogue as dimension/curvature scales. The closest existing facts are: (i) Cohn–Elkies I explicitly notes that Levenshtein's spherical-code LP functions become the Euclidean Bessel example in the θ→0 limit — a *pointwise* limit of extremizers, not a proven variational convergence of the programs; (ii) Cohn–Zhao's sphere→Euclidean comparison is an inequality between bounds, not a Γ-limit; (iii) the de Laat–Vallentin hierarchy relates k-point bounds within a fixed space, not across a contraction. So "the SU(N) certificate cone contracts to A_d with d ~ N²" would need a **new Γ-convergence (or Mosco-convergence) theorem** for the cones and objective functionals, built on the (standard) pointwise character→Bessel contraction.

**Applicability verdict (4):** The *analytic* contraction (characters → Bessel, HCIZ → Gaussian radial weight) is completely standard and citable (Dooley–Rice, Helgason, Mehler–Heine, HCIZ). The *variational* contraction of the certificate cone to A_d is **not** in the literature and is the single step of (A) with no precedent — it must be proven. This is the honest "flag every gap" item: the plan's contraction is analytically well-founded but variationally unproven.

### Item 5 — Amortized conditioning in Gibbs measures

**(a) van den Berg–Maes disagreement percolation.** J. van den Berg & C. Maes, "Disagreement percolation in the study of Markov fields," *Ann. Probab.* 22 (1994) 749–763 (building on van den Berg, *Commun. Math. Phys.* 152 (1993) 161–166). Exact form (as restated e.g. in the Lorentzian-triangulation paper arXiv:0810.2182, Thm 4): for a Markov specification on a locally finite graph, set p_v = max_{η,η'} ρ_var(Y_{{v}}(·,η), Y_{{v}}(·,η')) (single-site total-variation sensitivity to boundary conditions); if independent site percolation with parameters p_v is subcritical (no percolation to the boundary), the Gibbs measure is unique with exponential decay of correlations. Mechanism: a coupling of two boundary-conditioned fields whose disagreement set is stochastically dominated by subcritical Bernoulli percolation — a **coupling that replaces the Peierls/contour union bound by a percolation (paths) bound.** The improved variant (van den Berg–Maes) uses a sharper coupling than the original Dobrushin condition.

**(b) Chain-rule + Pinsker "conditioning budget" (parallel repetition).** Raz, "A parallel repetition theorem," *SIAM J. Comput.* 27 (1998) 763–803 (STOC'95); Holenstein, "Parallel repetition: simplifications and the no-signaling case," STOC 2007 / *Theory of Computing* 5 (2009) 141–172; Yuen, "A parallel repetition theorem for all entangled games," ICALP 2016, LIPIcs vol. 55 Art. 77, arXiv:1604.04340. The amortized-KL core: **Raz's Lemma** — for independent X₁,…,X_n and event W, ∑_i D(P_{X_i|W} ‖ P_{X_i}) ≤ log(1/Pr[W]); conditioning on a rare event perturbs a *typical* coordinate by only O(log(1/Pr[W])/n) in relative entropy. Yuen's quantum form — **Lemma 6 (Quantum Raz's Lemma, [CWY15,BVY15]):** for cq-states ρ_{X₁…X_nA} and product σ, ∑_i I(X_i:A)_ρ ≤ S(ρ_{XA}‖σ_{XA}); the budget δ := (1/m)(log 1/Pr[W_C] + |C|·log|A||B|) is divided across coordinates (Lemma 11), and Pinsker (Fact 4) converts the per-coordinate KL into total-variation closeness. **Correlated sampling** (Holenstein; Yuen Lemma 7 classical, Lemma 8 quantum via Dinur–Steurer–Vidick embezzlement) is the coupling that lets the two provers jointly sample the shared dependency-breaking variable Ω=(D_i,M_i) (Yuen Prop 10: conditioned on Ω, the repeated questions are independent). Note that Yuen's paper prints the amortized bound only in the mutual-information form (Lemma 6) and the δ-budget (Lemma 11); the classical "∑_i D(P_{X_i|W}‖P_{X_i}) ≤ log 1/Pr[W]" lives in Holenstein 2009 and Chung–Wu–Yuen (arXiv:1411.1397).

**Existing transfers into statistical mechanics.** The relative-entropy-with-coupling toolkit is mature but *for concentration/uniqueness, not as a Peierls replacement*: Marton coupling and transportation-information inequalities (Marton, *Ann. Probab.* 24 (1996) 857–866; Djellout–Guillin–Wu; Wu, "Poincaré and transportation inequalities for Gibbs measures under the Dobrushin uniqueness condition," *Ann. Probab.* 34 (2006); Ma–Shen–Wang–Wu, *Bernoulli* 17 (2011) 155–169); Külske concentration under Dobrushin uniqueness (*Commun. Math. Phys.* 2003); Chazottes–Collet–Külske–Redig, "Concentration inequalities for random fields via coupling," *PTRF* 137 (2007) 201–225; Chazottes–Collet–Redig (*J. Stat. Phys.* 169 (2017) 504–546), Chazottes–Moles–Redig–Ugalde (*J. Stat. Phys.* 181 (2020) 2131–2149), and most on-point **Chazottes–Redig, "Relative Entropy, Gaussian Concentration and Uniqueness of Equilibrium States," *Entropy* 24 (2022) 1513 (DOI 10.3390/e24111513)**, which derives uniqueness of translation-invariant Gibbs measures from positivity of a lower relative-entropy density.

**(c) Annealed vs. quenched for defect/dilution ensembles.** Jensen's inequality gives F_quenched ≥ F_annealed universally; for lattice ℤ₂ gauge-Higgs and dilute spin systems the quenched and annealed critical behaviors generically differ (randomly-dilute-Ising universality; e.g. the 3D ℤ₂ gauge-Higgs quenched-disorder study arXiv:2602.15418, and pinning/Gaussian-free-field comparisons in arXiv:1512.05240). These are comparison inequalities, not amortized-entropy replacements.

**Applicability verdict (5):** The three ingredient-lines all exist and are individually mature: (i) amortized KL over coordinates (Raz/Holenstein/Yuen); (ii) couplings controlled by relative entropy (Marton/Wu) and by percolation (van den Berg–Maes); (iii) relative-entropy ⇒ Gibbs uniqueness (Chazottes–Redig 2022). **But no paper implements the specific parallel-repetition move — spend log(1/Pr[rare event]) as an average O(1/N) per-site KL perturbation, then couple the perturbed single-site law to the unconditioned one, to replace a Peierls/contour union bound.** This is the honest verdict for the second direction: it is a **novel synthesis of known ingredients**, not a transplant of an existing theorem; the writeup should cite the three lines as precedents and claim novelty for the combination. (Negative claim is "not found," not proven nonexistent.)

### Item 6 — Toric/lattice Bergman substrate

**Berman–Berndtsson, real Monge–Ampère.** "Real Monge–Ampère equations and Kähler–Ricci solitons on toric log Fano varieties," *Ann. Fac. Sci. Toulouse Math.* (6) 22 (2013) 649–711 (DOI 10.5802/afst.1386; arXiv:1207.6128). Main analytic result: the second boundary value problem for the real Monge–Ampère equation in ℝⁿ with exponential nonlinearity det D²φ = e^{−φ} and target a convex body P is solvable iff 0 is the barycenter of P; the solution's gradient pushes forward e^{−φ}dx to (a multiple of) Lebesgue measure on P, i.e. (∇φ)#(e^{−φ}dx) = Leb|_P — exactly the transport/optimal-map statement the researcher cites. This is the variational characterization tying a weighted Gaussian-type measure on ℝⁿ to the uniform measure on the moment polytope.

**Berman–Berndtsson, volume/Ehrhart via Moser–Trudinger.** "The volume of Kähler–Einstein Fano varieties and convex bodies," *J. Reine Angew. Math.* (Crelle) 2014 no. 691, 1–24 (DOI 10.1515/crelle-2014-0069; arXiv:1204.1308). ℙⁿ has maximal anticanonical degree among n-dim KE Fano manifolds with a ℂ*-action with finitely many fixed points; the toric translation confirms **Ehrhart's volume conjecture** for a large class of rational polytopes (including duals of lattice/reflexive polytopes), with the (n+1)ⁿ/n! sharp bound. Proof uses **Moser–Trudinger-type inequalities for the complex Monge–Ampère operator**. (The 2026 OpenAI manuscript Chapter 8 separately claims a proof of the sharp (n+1)ⁿ/n! bound in every dimension for convex bodies whose barycenter is the only interior lattice point.)

**Fujita.** K. Fujita, "Optimal bounds for the volumes of Kähler–Einstein Fano manifolds," *Amer. J. Math.* 140 (2018) 391–414, and "A valuative criterion for uniform K-stability of ℚ-Fano varieties," *J. Reine Angew. Math.* 751 (2019) 309–338. Sharp bound: an n-dimensional K-semistable ℚ-Fano variety has (−K_X)ⁿ ≤ (n+1)ⁿ, with equality iff X ≅ ℙⁿ. Proof by blowing up a smooth point, using A_X(E) ≥ S_X(E) and vol(−π*K_X − xE) ≥ (−K_X)ⁿ − xⁿ. This is the algebro-geometric β/S-invariant packaging of the same volume bound (Thms 2.3/5.1 in the researcher's numbering correspond to the valuative criterion + the (n+1)ⁿ consequence).

**Toric Bergman kernel asymptotics.** Zelditch, "Off-diagonal decay of toric Bergman kernels," *Lett. Math. Phys.* 106 (2016) 1849–1864 (arXiv:1603.08281): for ample invariant line bundles over compact toric Kähler manifolds, the Berezin kernel P_{h^k}(z,w) ≍ k^m exp(−k D(z,w)) with D the Calabi diastasis (for real-analytic metrics, and along ℝ₊^m-orbits in the smooth case). Tian–Catlin–Zelditch diagonal expansion and Lu–Shiffman off-diagonal expansion (*J. Geom. Anal.* 25 (2015) 761–782) give the near-diagonal Gaussian decay. The toric Bergman kernel is diagonal in the monomial basis, with weights ∫ |z^α|² e^{−kφ}, so ratios of monomial norms are exp of differences of the Legendre-transform potential, controlled by ∇φ landing in the moment polytope P.

**Applicability verdict (6):** The mechanism "off-diagonal entries of a multiplication operator in a weighted monomial basis are bounded by norm ratios controlled by ∇φ ∈ K" **is a standard consequence** of this literature: the monomial basis diagonalizes the toric Bergman/Berezin inner product, off-diagonal decay is exp(−k·diastasis) (Zelditch), and the constraint ∇φ ∈ P (moment map image) is exactly the Berman–Berndtsson transport statement (∇φ)#(e^{−φ}dx) = Leb|_P. **Gaps for the plan:** (i) SU(N) class functions live on a Weyl-chamber/alcove, not a generic moment polytope; identifying the relevant convex body K with the SU(N) Weyl alcove and the weight with the C₂ heat kernel requires matching the Duistermaat–Heckman/Weyl measure to the toric Lebesgue measure — plausible (the multiplicity-free / spherical-variety case is discussed by Berman–Berndtsson) but must be made precise. (ii) The Bergman results are for fixed manifold, k→∞; the plan needs uniformity as N→∞ (dimension of the variety grows), which is *not* covered by fixed-manifold off-diagonal asymptotics and would need new uniform-in-N estimates.

## Overall Assessment

**Is the limitation theorem (A) plausibly a transplant of [CDG24]-type results through a contraction limit?** Partially. The two *endpoints* are on very firm ground: (i) the Euclidean limitation ([CDG24] Thm 1.2/2.1) is exactly the right statement with exactly the right Laguerre/Mellin radial-weight mechanism, and (ii) the analytic contraction of SU(N) characters to Bessel/Euclidean objects (Dooley–Rice, HCIZ, Mehler–Heine) is textbook. **The single step with no precedent is the *variational* contraction of the certificate cone** — no result in the literature proves that an LP/positive-definite optimization over a compact group Γ-converges to its Euclidean analogue as dimension scales; the only extant facts are pointwise limits of extremizers (Cohn–Elkies/Levenshtein) and bound-level inequalities (Cohn–Zhao). So (A) is best described as **"a transplant of [CDG24] modulo one genuinely new theorem (cone Γ-convergence),"** not a routine transfer.

**Ranking of the five substrates by readiness (most to least ready-made):**
1. **Item 2 (sign-uncertainty/LP state of the art)** — off-the-shelf, now including the sharp √(e/2π) rate and 1/π radii (pending refereeing of the proof; the conjectures are solid).
2. **Item 1 ([CDG24]/Edwin limitation template)** — off-the-shelf statements and mechanisms; only needs the cone contraction to apply to SU(N).
3. **Item 6 (toric Bergman / Berman–Berndtsson)** — the core mechanism is a standard consequence; gaps are the Weyl-alcove identification and uniformity in N.
4. **Item 4 (Cartan-motion contraction)** — analytic tools standard; the variational/optimization contraction is the load-bearing unproven step.
5. **Item 3 (compact-group Delsarte/LP + sphere sign-uncertainty)** — mature LP theory and a general operator sign-uncertainty principle, but the two-sided SU(N) cone and character-basis sign-uncertainty are not instantiated.
6. **Item 5 (amortized-KL + coupling in Gibbs measures)** — all ingredients exist and are mature, but the specific "amortized budget replaces Peierls union bound" synthesis was not found and should be claimed as new.

In short: the crossover (B) Euclidean side and the limitation (A) Euclidean template are ready to cite verbatim; the compact-to-Euclidean *variational* bridge (item 4) and the Gibbs-conditioning synthesis (item 5) are the two places where the program must supply original theorems rather than lean on precedent.
