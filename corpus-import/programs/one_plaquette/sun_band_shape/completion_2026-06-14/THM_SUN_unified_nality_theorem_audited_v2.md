# Audited finite-degree \(N\)-ality-complete fourth-order theorem

## 1. Unified local invariant rule

For a fourth-order one-flux matrix element, each link carries at most six
fundamental/antifundamental factors:

\[
r_\ell+s_\ell\le 6.
\]

The local Haar invariant space in
\(V^{\otimes r_\ell}\otimes\bar V^{\otimes s_\ell}\)
is nonzero exactly when

\[
r_\ell-s_\ell\equiv0\pmod N.
\]

The Haar projector is the projector onto the complete finite-\(N\) invariant
space. Its balanced component is represented by walled-Brauer pairings;
nonzero-\(N\)-ality components are generated by epsilon tensors and pairings,
with finite-rank dependencies removed by the exact Gram quotient.

Equivalently,

\[
H_{4,N}
=
\sum_{w,\sigma}
\left[
\prod_\ell \mathbf 1_{N\mid\nu_\ell(w,\sigma)}
\right]
\mathcal W_N(w,\sigma),
\qquad
\nu_\ell=r_\ell-s_\ell,
\]

where every \(\mathcal W_N\) is evaluated with the complete local Haar
projector. The roots-of-unity identity

\[
\mathbf 1_{N\mid\nu}
=
\frac1N\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}e^{2\pi i a\nu/N}
\]

is the exact admissibility selector; it is not itself the Haar projector.

## 2. Degree-six rank classification

- \(N\ge7\): only balanced sectors occur.
- \(N=6\): one final charge-conjugation orbit with local charge \(\pm6\).
- \(N=5\): the complete fourth-order corpus contains no exceptional assignment.
- \(N=4\): charge-\(\pm4\) sectors occur, including intermediate determinant channels.
- \(N=3\): charge-\(\pm3,\pm6\) sectors occur and modify the band shape.
- \(N=2\): charge conjugation is gauge-equivalent to the identity, so the
  \(C=-\) one-flux sector is zero.

\[

## 6. Global band theorem

For every \(N\ge3\),

\[
A_N>0,\qquad B_N>0.
\]

Therefore \(\Gamma\) is the unique global minimum, \(R=(\pi,\pi,\pi)\)
is the unique global maximum, and

\[
\boxed{
\Delta c_{4,N}=A_N+B_N>0.
}
\]

The rank-unified structural statement is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&A_N=A_N^{\rm bal} && (N\ge3),\\
&B_N=B_N^{\rm bal} && (N\ge4),\\
&B_3=B_3^{\rm bal}-\frac{25}{64},\\
&q_N=q_N^{\rm bal}+\Delta q_N^{\rm exc},
\end{aligned}

\]

where the last line uses direct finite-rank balanced contractions at exceptional
ranks.
