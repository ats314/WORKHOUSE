# 4. Lemma Q: local SU(2) rare-source factorization

## 4.1 Role of Lemma Q

The deterministic spine of Section 3 reduces sparse-defect coercivity to projected-capacity control. The missing probabilistic input is a statement that high-plaquette sources generated by the SU(2) Wilson measure behave, inside a shaved Bałaban block core, as genuinely rare sources even after conditioning on the exterior.

The required theorem is not pointwise independence. It is not a product-measure approximation. It is a conditional rare-source factorization estimate: every additional marked plaquette in the block core must contribute one factor of the global source intensity \(q_\eta\), up to a uniform constant.

This section states that input as **Lemma Q**.

For notational brevity, write

\[
X_p=X_{p,\eta}.
\]

Recall that

\[
0\le X_p\le1,
\qquad
q_\eta=\mathbb E_{\mu_{L,\beta}}X_p,
\]

and

\[
\mathbf1_{\{\phi_p\ge t\}}
\le
X_p
\le
\mathbf1_{\{\phi_p\ge t-\eta\}}.
\]

Let \(C\) be a Bałaban block, \(C^\circ\) its shaved core, and \(\mathcal F_{C^c}\) the exterior link sigma-field. For an exterior boundary condition \(\xi\), write \(\mu_C^\xi\) for the corresponding block Gibbs measure.

The target estimate is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\prod_{p\in B}X_p
\le
(C_Qq_\eta)^{|B|},
\qquad
B\subset\mathcal P(C^\circ).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The constant \(C_Q\) may depend on the structural parameters of the theorem, such as \(\beta\), \(\eta\), the block-shaving radius, and the chosen source threshold, but it must not depend on

\[
L,\qquad
C,\qquad
B,
\]

or on the number of marked plaquettes.

Equation (4.1) is the \(q_\eta\)-per-source law.

## 4.2 Formal statement of Lemma Q

### Lemma Q — local rare-source factorization

Fix \(\eta>0\), a source threshold \(t\in(0,2)\), and an upper-envelope source \(X_{p,\eta}\) satisfying

\[
\mathbf1_{\{\phi_p\ge t\}}
\le
X_{p,\eta}
\le
\mathbf1_{\{\phi_p\ge t-\eta\}}.
\]

There exist constants

\[
\beta_0<\infty,
\qquad
M_0<\infty,
\qquad
r_*<\infty,
\qquad
C_Q<\infty,
\tag{4.2}
\]

such that for every

\[
\beta\ge\beta_0,
\]

every finite periodic lattice \(T_L^4\), every admissible Bałaban block \(C\) of side length at least \(M_0\), every shaved core

\[
C^\circ=C^{\ominus r_*},
\]

and every finite plaquette set

\[
B\subset\mathcal P(C^\circ),
\]

one has, for every tempered exterior boundary condition \(\xi\in\mathcal T_C\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\left[
\prod_{p\in B}X_{p,\eta}
\right]
\le
(C_Qq_\eta)^{|B|}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equivalently, in conditional expectation notation,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mu_{L,\beta}}
\left[
\prod_{p\in B}X_{p,\eta}
\;\middle|\;
\mathcal F_{C^c}
\right]
\le
(C_Qq_\eta)^{|B|}
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

on the tempered-boundary event \(\mathcal T_C\).

The theorem is local in the block core. It is not an assertion that arbitrary adversarial boundary data preserve source rarity. Non-tempered boundary configurations are assigned to rooted or large-field budgets.

## 4.3 Interpretation

The estimate (4.3) says that the block conditioning may change constants but may not change the source scaling. In particular, the conditional law may create local correlations among nearby high-plaquette sources, but it cannot turn a \(k\)-source event into an event with probability larger than a constant times \(q_\eta^k\).

For \(|B|=1\), Lemma Q gives

\[
\mathbb E_C^\xi X_p
\le
C_Qq_\eta.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For \(|B|=2\), it gives

\[
\mathbb E_C^\xi X_pX_r
\le
(C_Qq_\eta)^2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For general \(B\), it gives the multiplicative source estimate needed by the polymer expansion:

\[
\mathbb E_C^\xi X_B
=
O(q_\eta^{|B|}).
\tag{4.7}
\]

The theorem is deliberately stronger than a pair-correlation bound. Pair estimates do not by themselves control source-marked polymers of arbitrary size. The constructive expansion needs uniform \(k\)-source accounting.

## 4.4 Why pair bounds are insufficient

A pair-correlation bound of the form

\[
\left|
\mathbb E[X_pX_q]-\mathbb E[X_p]\mathbb E[X_q]
\right|
\le
Cq_\eta^2e^{-md(p,q)}
\tag{4.8}
\]

would imply a version of Lemma Q only for \(|B|=2\). It does not imply

\[
\mathbb E\prod_{p\in B}X_p=O(q_\eta^{|B|})
\tag{4.9}
\]

for arbitrary \(B\).

A simple schematic counterexample explains the gap. Let \(R\) be a rare common ancestor event with

\[
\mathbb P(R)=q_\eta.
\]

Conditional on \(R\), let three source variables \(X_1,X_2,X_3\) occur with probabilities of order one. Outside \(R\), tune their small background probabilities so that each marginal remains \(q_\eta\). Pair correlations can be kept of order \(q_\eta^2\), but

\[
\mathbb E[X_1X_2X_3]
\asymp q_\eta,
\tag{4.10}
\]

not \(q_\eta^3\). Thus low-order pair control does not prevent hidden common-source clustering at higher order.

A source-weighted polymer expansion sums over marked sets of arbitrary cardinality. Its activity bookkeeping requires

\[
\mathbb E X_B
\le
(C_Qq_\eta)^{|B|}
\tag{4.11}
\]

uniformly in \(|B|\), not merely pairwise covariance decay.

This is the precise reason Lemma Q is formulated as a \(k\)-source theorem.

## 4.5 Rooted Lemma Q

The proof architecture must allow local bad geometry. The local cap-intersection theorem developed later holds on an LCI-good event. Its complement need not be harmless pointwise and need not be unconditionally rare under all boundary conditions. The correct way to handle this is by rooted estimates.

Let \(p_0\in\mathcal P(C^\circ)\). A rooted source is an observable

\[
Y_{p_0}
\]

supported in a fixed local neighborhood of \(p_0\), satisfying

\[
\boxed{
0\le Y_{p_0}\le X_{p_0,\eta}.
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

Typical rooted sources have the form

\[
Y_{p_0}
=
X_{p_0,\eta}\mathbf1_{\mathcal B_{p_0}},
\tag{4.13}
\]

where \(\mathcal B_{p_0}\) is a bad local event, such as bad staple geometry or failure of the LCI-good condition.

### Rooted Lemma Q

Under the same assumptions as Lemma Q, for every rooted source \(Y_{p_0}\) satisfying (4.12), and every finite set

\[
B\subset\mathcal P(C^\circ)\setminus\{p_0\},
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\left[
Y_{p_0}
\prod_{p\in B}X_{p,\eta}
\right]
\le
(C_{Q,\rm root}q_\eta)^{|B|}
\mathbb E_C^\xi Y_{p_0}.
}
\tag{4.14}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mu_{L,\beta}}
\left[
Y_{p_0}
\prod_{p\in B}X_{p,\eta}
\;\middle|\;
\mathcal F_{C^c}
\right]
\le
(C_{Q,\rm root}q_\eta)^{|B|}
\mathbb E_{\mu_{L,\beta}}
\left[
Y_{p_0}
\;\middle|\;
\mathcal F_{C^c}
\right].
}
\tag{4.15}
\]

The rooted theorem says that once a bad local event is paid for as the root, additional sources still cost one factor \(q_\eta\) each.

## 4.6 Why the rooted formulation is necessary

A statement of the form

\[
\mathbb P_C^\xi(\mathcal B_{p_0})\le Cq_\eta
\tag{4.16}
\]

for every exterior boundary \(\xi\) is too strong for the intended theorem. Exterior data can force local configurations in a way that makes some bad local geometry common. What the firewall mechanism needs is weaker and more stable:

\[
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\left[
X_B
\mid
Y_{p_0}\ \text{has occurred}
\right]
\le
(Cq_\eta)^{|B|}.
\tag{4.17}
\]

That is exactly (4.14). The root carries the local irregularity. The rest of the marked sources must still factor.

This distinction is important for the LCI reduction. The local cap-intersection estimate is expected to hold on a good heat-bath geometry event. The complement is absorbed into

\[
Y_{p_0}^{\rm LCI}
=
X_{p_0,\eta}\mathbf1_{(\mathcal G_{e,p_0}^{\rm LCI})^c},
\tag{4.18}
\]

not discarded.

## 4.7 Cavity-intensity formulation

Lemma Q can be obtained from a stronger one-source cavity bound. For a finite source set

\[
S\subset\mathcal P(C^\circ)\setminus\{p\},
\]

define the cavity intensity

\[
\lambda_p(S;\xi)
=
\frac{
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\left[
X_p\prod_{r\in S}X_r
\right]
}{
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\left[
\prod_{r\in S}X_r
\right]
},
\tag{4.19}
\]

whenever the denominator is nonzero.

If the denominator is zero, then

\[
0\le
X_p\prod_{r\in S}X_r
\le
\prod_{r\in S}X_r
\]

implies

\[
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\left[
X_p\prod_{r\in S}X_r
\right]=0.
\]

Thus the desired upper bound is automatically true in the zero-denominator case. The cavity intensity is needed only when the denominator is positive.

The distance-sensitive cavity target is

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_p(S;\xi)
\le
C_{\rm cav}q_\eta
\exp\left(
\sum_{r\in S}J(p,r)
\right),
}
\tag{4.20}
\]

where

\[
J(p,r)\le C_Je^{-m_Jd_C(p,r)}
\tag{4.21}
\]

and

\[
J_*=
\sup_p
\sum_{r\ne p}J(p,r)
<\infty.
\tag{4.22}
\]

The factor

\[
\exp\left(\sum_{r\in S}J(p,r)\right)
\]

allows nearby sources to increase the conditional rate of \(X_p\), but only through an exponentially summable influence kernel.

## 4.8 Cavity-intensity bound implies Lemma Q

The implication from the cavity bound to Lemma Q is a chain-rule argument.

Let

\[
B=\{p_1,\ldots,p_n\}.
\]

Define

\[
B_j=\{p_1,\ldots,p_j\},
\qquad
B_0=\varnothing.
\]

Let

\[
E(A)=\mathbb E_C^\xi\prod_{p\in A}X_p,
\qquad
E(\varnothing)=1.
\tag{4.23}
\]

If \(E(B_{j-1})>0\), then

\[
E(B_j)=E(B_{j-1})\lambda_{p_j}(B_{j-1};\xi).
\tag{4.24}
\]

If \(E(B_{j-1})=0\), then

\[
E(B_j)=0
\]

because \(X_{B_j}\le X_{B_{j-1}}\). Hence \(E(B)=0\), and Lemma Q is trivial.

Assume therefore that all intermediate denominators are positive. Iterating (4.24),

\[
E(B)
=
\prod_{j=1}^n
\lambda_{p_j}(B_{j-1};\xi).
\tag{4.25}
\]

Using (4.20),

\[
E(B)
\le
(C_{\rm cav}q_\eta)^n
\exp\left(
\sum_{j=1}^n
\sum_{i<j}J(p_j,p_i)
\right).
\tag{4.26}
\]

For each \(j\),

\[
\sum_{i<j}J(p_j,p_i)\le J_*.
\]

Therefore

\[
E(B)
\le
(C_{\rm cav}q_\eta)^n e^{nJ_*}
=
(C_{\rm cav}e^{J_*}q_\eta)^{|B|}.
\tag{4.27}
\]

Thus Lemma Q follows with

\[
\boxed{
C_Q=C_{\rm cav}e^{J_*}.
}
\tag{4.28}
\]

This proves

\[
\boxed{
\text{cavity-intensity bound}
\Longrightarrow
\text{Lemma Q}.
}
\tag{4.29}
\]

## 4.9 Rooted cavity-intensity formulation

For a rooted source \(Y_{p_0}\), define

\[
\lambda_p^Y(S;\xi)
=
\frac{
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\left[
Y_{p_0}X_p\prod_{r\in S}X_r
\right]
}{
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\left[
Y_{p_0}\prod_{r\in S}X_r
\right]
},
\tag{4.30}
\]

whenever the denominator is positive. If the denominator is zero, the numerator is also zero and the rooted estimate is trivial.

The rooted cavity target is

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_p^Y(S;\xi)
\le
C_{\rm cav}q_\eta
\exp\left(
J(p,p_0)+
\sum_{r\in S}J(p,r)
\right).
}
\tag{4.31}
\]

The additional term \(J(p,p_0)\) accounts for the interaction between the added source \(X_p\) and the rooted local event \(Y_{p_0}\).

## 4.10 Rooted cavity bound implies rooted Lemma Q

Let

\[
B=\{p_1,\ldots,p_n\}
\subset\mathcal P(C^\circ)\setminus\{p_0\}.
\]

Define rooted expectations

\[
E^Y(A)=
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\left[
Y_{p_0}\prod_{p\in A}X_p
\right],
\qquad
E^Y(\varnothing)=\mathbb E_C^\xi Y_{p_0}.
\tag{4.32}
\]

If an intermediate denominator vanishes, the final rooted expectation vanishes and the desired bound is trivial. Otherwise,

\[
E^Y(B)
=
E^Y(\varnothing)
\prod_{j=1}^n
\lambda_{p_j}^Y(B_{j-1};\xi).
\tag{4.33}
\]

Using (4.31),

\[
E^Y(B)
\le
E^Y(\varnothing)
(C_{\rm cav}q_\eta)^n
\exp\left(
\sum_{j=1}^nJ(p_j,p_0)
+
\sum_{j=1}^n\sum_{i<j}J(p_j,p_i)
\right).
\tag{4.34}
\]

The triangular source-source sum is bounded by

\[
\sum_{j=1}^n\sum_{i<j}J(p_j,p_i)
\le nJ_*.
\tag{4.35}
\]

For the root-interaction sum, using the symmetric or symmetrized kernel convention,

\[
\sum_{j=1}^nJ(p_j,p_0)\le J_*.
\tag{4.36}
\]

Therefore

\[
E^Y(B)
\le
E^Y(\varnothing)
(C_{\rm cav}q_\eta)^n
e^{(n+1)J_*}.
\tag{4.37}
\]

For \(n\ge1\), absorb the one-time \(e^{J_*}\) root factor into the per-source constant by taking

\[
\boxed{
C_{Q,\rm root}=C_{\rm cav}e^{2J_*}.
}
\tag{4.38}
\]

Indeed,

\[
e^{(n+1)J_*}\le e^{2nJ_*}
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]

For \(n=0\), the rooted inequality is the identity

\[
E^Y(\varnothing)=E^Y(\varnothing).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_C^\xi
\left[
Y_{p_0}\prod_{p\in B}X_p
\right]
\le
(C_{\rm cav}e^{2J_*}q_\eta)^{|B|}
\mathbb E_C^\xi Y_{p_0}.
}
\tag{4.39}
\]

This proves the rooted Lemma Q from the rooted cavity-intensity bound.

## 4.11 Cavity intensity versus TOS+J

The cavity-intensity formulation and TOS+J are closely related but not identical in presentation.

The cavity-intensity bound controls hard source insertions:

\[
\frac{
\mathbb E_C^\xi[X_p\prod_{r\in S}X_r]
}{
\mathbb E_C^\xi[\prod_{r\in S}X_r]
}
\le
Cq_\eta
\exp\left(\sum_{r\in S}J(p,r)\right).
\tag{4.40}
\]

TOS+J controls soft positive tilts:

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu^{S,s}}X_p
\le
Cq_\eta
\exp\left(\sum_{r\in S}J(p,r)\right),
\qquad
0\le s\le\rho/q_\eta.
\tag{4.41}
\]

The soft tilt expands as

\[
\prod_{r\in S}(1+sX_r)
=
\sum_{S'\subset S}s^{|S'|}\prod_{r\in S'}X_r.
\tag{4.42}
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu^{S,s}}X_p
=
\frac{
\sum_{S'\subset S}s^{|S'|}
\mathbb E_C^\xi[X_p\prod_{r\in S'}X_r]
}{
\sum_{S'\subset S}s^{|S'|}
\mathbb E_C^\xi[\prod_{r\in S'}X_r]
}.
\tag{4.43}
\]

If the cavity-intensity bound holds uniformly for every subset \(S'\subset S\), then

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu^{S,s}}X_p
\le
Cq_\eta
\max_{S'\subset S}
\exp\left(\sum_{r\in S'}J(p,r)\right)
\le
Cq_\eta
\exp\left(\sum_{r\in S}J(p,r)\right).
\tag{4.44}
\]

Thus a uniform cavity-intensity bound implies TOS+J.

The paper uses TOS+J as the load-bearing statement because it is exactly what appears in the source-partition telescoping identity:

\[
\frac{Z_{A_j}(s)}{Z_{A_{j-1}}(s)}
=
1+s\mathbb E_{\mu^{A_{j-1},s}}X_{p_j}.
\tag{4.45}
\]

Cavity intensity is useful conceptually, but TOS+J is structurally better matched to the positive source-radius proof.

## 4.12 Constants ledger

The constants in Lemma Q are composites of more primitive inputs.

The dependency chain is:

\[
\boxed{
(C_{\rm LCI},C_{\rm BFS},C_J,m_J)
\Longrightarrow
(C_{\rm TOS},J_*)
\Longrightarrow
K=\rho C_{\rm TOS}e^{J_*}
\Longrightarrow
C_Q=\rho^{-1}e^K.
}
\tag{4.46}
\]

Here:

- \(C_{\rm LCI}\) is the local cap-intersection constant;
- \(C_{\rm BFS}\) is the far-source stability constant;
- \(C_J,m_J\) define the influence kernel;
- \(J_*=\sup_p\sum_{r\ne p}J(p,r)\);
- \(C_{\rm TOS}\) is the assembled tilted one-source constant;
- \(\rho\) is the source-radius parameter;
- \(K\) is the source-radius exponent;
- \(C_Q\) is the final Lemma Q constant.

From Sections 5 and 6,

\[
K=\rho C_{\rm TOS}e^{J_*},
\tag{4.47}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
C_Q(\rho)
=
\rho^{-1}\exp(\rho C_{\rm TOS}e^{J_*}).
}
\tag{4.48}
\]

If \(C_{\rm TOS}\) and \(J_*\) are fixed, the formal optimizer is

\[
\rho=(C_{\rm TOS}e^{J_*})^{-1},
\tag{4.49}
\]

giving

\[
C_Q^{\rm opt}=e\,C_{\rm TOS}e^{J_*}.
\tag{4.50}
\]

This optimization is not used in the proof. It only clarifies that \(C_Q\) is finite once the TOS+J constants are finite.

The structural parameters

\[
\beta_0,\qquad
M_0,\qquad
r_*
\]

enter earlier:

- \(\beta_0\) sets the concentration regime where the heat-bath LCI theorem is expected to hold;
- \(M_0\) sets the minimum block side for the source-weighted Bałaban/far-source expansion;
- \(r_*\) sets the shaving margin separating the core from boundary effects.

## 4.13 How Lemma Q feeds the projected-capacity firewall

Lemma Q is not itself a coercivity theorem. Its role is to supply the rare-source probability half of the PMBSF mechanism.

In schematic form, the downstream chain is

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{Lemma\ Q}
+
\mathrm{source\mbox{-}weighted\ Ba\l aban}
\Longrightarrow
\mathrm{source\ cumulant/polymer\ control}.
}
\tag{4.51}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{source\ cumulant/polymer\ control}
+
\mathrm{PTO\ trace\mbox{-}overlap\ summability}
\Longrightarrow
\mathrm{projected\ capacity\ control}.
}
\tag{4.52}
\]

Then Section 3 gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{projected\ capacity\ control}
\Longrightarrow
\Theta_D<1
\Longrightarrow
M-PV_DP\succeq(1-\Theta_D)M.
}
\tag{4.53}
\]

Thus Lemma Q supplies the missing \(q_\eta\)-per-source factor. PTO summability supplies the deterministic trace-overlap summation. The Birman--Schwinger criterion supplies the coercivity conclusion.

## 4.14 Empirical signatures and limits

The project's exact heat-bath block diagnostics test the \(|B|=1\) and \(|B|=2\) signatures of Lemma Q at the working point

\[
\beta=3.5,\qquad
q_\eta=0.003,\qquad
\eta=0.005.
\]

The exact-HB side-8 and side-10/core-margin-3 diagnostics show conditional single-source and pair cavity ratios of order one in median and moderate in maxima. This is consistent with Lemma-Q-type behavior at low order.

However, these diagnostics do not prove Lemma Q. They do not test arbitrary \(k\)-source sets. At \(q_\eta=0.003\), fixed-position triple events require very large sample sizes to estimate directly. Therefore the higher-order content of Lemma Q is structural and analytic, not empirical.

The numerical evidence should be read as support for the target mechanism, not as proof of any open theorem.

## 4.15 What Lemma Q does and does not assert

Lemma Q is:

1. finite-volume;
2. block-conditional;
3. local to shaved Bałaban cores;
4. an upper bound on conditional moments of the smoothed source field;
5. uniform in lattice size, block, and source set;
6. restricted to tempered exterior boundaries, with rooted treatment of bad complements.

Lemma Q is not:

1. an assertion of independence of \(X_p\);
2. a statement about arbitrary observables;
3. a pair-correlation theorem;
4. a statement uniform over arbitrary adversarial boundary configurations;
5. a hard-source theorem before the boundary-band gate;
6. a multi-block clustering theorem before the source-weighted Bałaban expansion;
7. a continuum statement.

Lemma Q enables the source-weighted polymer expansion by supplying the multiplicative factor

\[
(C_Qq_\eta)^{|B|}
\]

for a marked source set \(B\).

## 4.16 Current status

In this manuscript, Lemma Q is an open analytic theorem, but it is no longer a primitive black box.

The proved reductions are

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{TOS+J}
\Longrightarrow
Z_A(\rho/q_\eta)\le e^{K|A|}
}
\tag{4.54}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
Z_A(\rho/q_\eta)\le e^{K|A|}
\Longrightarrow
\mathrm{Lemma\ Q}.
}
\tag{4.55}
\]

The proposed route to \(\mathrm{TOS+J}\) is

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{LCI}_{\rm good}
+
\mathrm{BFS}_{\rm far}
\Longrightarrow
\mathrm{TOS+J}.
}
\tag{4.56}
\]

Here:

- \(\mathrm{LCI}_{\rm good}\) is the local cap-intersection theorem for incident plaquettes under the exact SU(2) one-link heat-bath law on \(S^3\);
- \(\mathrm{BFS}_{\rm far}\) is the Bałaban far-source stability theorem controlling how non-incident source tilts distort local heat-bath parameters.

The remaining analytic problem is therefore sharper than “prove Lemma Q.” It is

\[
\boxed{
\text{prove LCI-good typicality and Bałaban far-source stability strongly enough to imply TOS+J.}
}
\tag{4.57}
\]
