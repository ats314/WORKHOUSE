[

This is the point that unifies the cube theorem, the pentagonal (O(u^4)) result, the fifth-order determinant correction, the mixed-cell test, and the Hodge flat-band results without contradiction.

Start with

The important object is not merely the final chain (q_r-q_0), but the full path

[
\boxed{q_0\rightarrow q_1\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow q_r.}

Its physical weight is obtained only after exact Haar/Gram reduction and electric propagation:

[

======

\langle q_{\rm out}|

[

The effective operator is therefore

[
\boxed{

where (F_r) denotes the energy-dependent folded terms and (\mathscr H_r^{\rm phys}) is obtained only after quotienting Gram-null directions, disconnected pieces, intermediate (P)-returns, symmetry redundancies, and rooted linked-cluster subtractions. This is the underlying structure already present in the Nested-Quotient synthesis; the newer calculations show that the history must be retained as an ordered object rather than replaced too early by its static cellular boundary.

# 10. Novelty relative to published theory

Strong-coupling effective Hamiltonians themselves are not novel. Münster constructed effective transfer matrices inside degenerate glueball eigenspaces and extracted dispersion relations in 1985; Hamer and collaborators subsequently computed high-order Hamiltonian strong-coupling glueball series. Linked-cluster methods are also established. ([ScienceDirect][1])

What I did **not** find in the targeted literature search is the particular combination established here: a Hodge/boundary-ideal flat-band quotient, exact localization of a mobility coefficient onto an elementary 3-cell boundary, the symbolic all-(N) law (640/[N(N^2-1)^3]), and the distinction between static cellular circuits and resolvent-weighted temporal circuits.

The recent Illa–Savage–Yao hyperhoneycomb work constructs improved Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonians on noncubic tessellations, but its stated goal is regulator improvement/quantum simulation rather than this spectral escape classification. ([arXiv][2])

Likewise, Bao's August 2026 work connects higher-form codes, topological sectors, strong-coupling center sheets and transfer-matrix masses, which is conceptually nearby, but it does not state the temporal-circuit/Hodge/Feshbach mobility theorem found here. ([arXiv][3])

So the strongest reasonable novelty claim is:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{The novel content is the quotient-and-escape mechanism and its exact}
\atop
\textbf{cellular/operator realizations—not strong-coupling perturbation theory itself.}
}
]

A publication should still include a dedicated historical literature review before claiming absolute priority.
