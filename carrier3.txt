I · The scorecard
Five candidate reductions went to five independent hostile referees, each instructed to default to "refuted" and to check the mathematics themselves. Here is what came back.

reduction	verdict	what is left
R1 mass bound from one correlator ratio	sound, corrected	True, but false as written on a periodic time lattice — which is every relevant Monte Carlo. Corrected form survives. Not new: it is the standard imaginary-time interpolation between the Rayleigh quotient and the gap.
R2 the residue is not independent	refuted	The inequality holds with two corrections. The headline is false: the compression does not occur. One real gain fell out — the overlap exponent.
R3 C-superselection supplies isolation	refuted	One trivial line survives. The Clay direction was reversed, the isolation gap wrong by 2.7–2.8×, and the below-threshold argument circular.
R4 the Γ singularity is metric	refuted	The computation was forced to return zero a priori. True, trivial, already in the corpus, and the corpus's version is strictly stronger.
R5 the 3/5 : 2/5 spin split	sound, corrected	All four claims check out exactly. Three corrections, one strengthening. Mostly not novel — the subduction table is Morningstar–Peardon 1999 Table VI.
The retraction

My headline was that the decisive unknown compresses from three uniformities to one estimate on one operator, conditional on Clay. That is wrong. R2's hypothesis (iii) — a polynomially bounded bare overlap — is itself a residue hypothesis: its whole content is a finite constant in Z₀ ~ c₁a^p, equivalent to ⟨G|d^{abc}F^aF^bF^c|0⟩ ≠ 0, which is precisely the unknown FRONTIER.md's own headline names — "the corpus proves protection and computes coefficients; it assumes, and nowhere proves, that the protected object is the glueball." Nothing compresses. The count goes 3 → 4, with one hypothesis strengthened.

And R3's use of Clay was backwards. M_a is a lattice quantity at spacing a; Jaffe–Witten asks for a continuum theory on ℝ⁴. A lattice gap uniform in a is a route to Clay, not a consequence of it.

What the session did produce is in Parts V–VII: an audit of the corpus that found a 14 MB Bałaban program the ledger does not name, a load-bearing gap whose second half has never been written as mathematics, a statistic that does not support the claim resting on it, and one question that gates the whole construction and has never been asked.

The one piece of new mathematics, and it came out of a refutation

Refuting R2 produced the overlap law the program needs and did not have. For a local lattice source whose lowest-dimension continuum operator has dimension d, the bare zero-momentum spectral fraction is

Z₀(a) ≈ |f_d|² · a^{2d−3} / (2M).
d = 4 gives a⁵ — reproducing, from scratch, the Schierholz 1988 exponent the ledger records as G18's external sharpening. The C-odd carrier's leading continuum operator is d^{abc}F^aF^bF^c at dimension six, so p = 2·6 − 3 = 9. Not 5. Extrapolating the corpus's own measured raw fraction gives Z₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻⁶ at a√σ = 0.10 and 2.4×10⁻⁹ at 0.05. A power, so formally survivable — and brutal. For a source smeared to a fixed physical radius R, Z₀ = |f_R|²R^{2d−3}/(2M) is a-independent. That is the sharpest statement yet of why the smeared basis is structural.

II · The finite-spacing half, as twelve lemmas
Stated so each rung carries its own evidence tier rather than its rhetorical confidence. Rungs L1–L6 are the electric-shell theorem; L7–L11 are the volume-uniform patch; L12 is what actually remains at fixed spacing.

Three arithmetic facts underneath everything were re-derived independently in this session, in exact rationals, and agree: the SU(3) Casimir numerators below 18 are exactly {0, 4, 9, 10, 16}; the fundamental plaquette sits at E_F = 4·(2/3) = 8/3; and the additive semigroup of one-link numerators takes the values 14, 16, 17 consecutively around 16 — 15 is not attainable, which is what makes the separation ratio come out at 1/33 rather than 1/15.

L1
Casimir ladder and shell energies
T1 · checkable
e(p,q) = (p²+q²+pq+3p+3q)/6. Smallest nonzero value 2/3, attained only by 3 and 3̄; every other nontrivial irrep costs ≥ 3/2. Hence E_F = 8/3, the 1×2 rectangle costs 6·(2/3) = 4, and the candidate gap is 4/3. Pure rational arithmetic — this is Lean-reachable.

L2
Support classification below energy 4
T1 · enumerable
Energy < 4 forces ≤ 5 occupied edges. Gauss's law forbids a degree-one vertex (Inv(V_λ) is trivial for nontrivial λ), so every component has minimum degree ≥ 2. The dossier's "theta graph needs ≥ 6 edges" argument is loose; the MASTER_PROOF_STATE's exhaustive enumeration for L = 3…7 is the honest version and returns zero branched supports at every L.

L3
Schur propagation around a cycle
T1
At a degree-two vertex Inv(V_λ ⊗ V_μ) ≅ Hom(V_λ*, V_μ), so one irrep propagates consistently. A non-fundamental irrep costs ≥ 3/2 per edge, and the shortest winding cycle then costs ≥ 9/2 > 4. Every sub-4 state is a fundamental simple cycle of length ≤ 5.

L4
Triality kills winding — unconditionally for L ≥ 6
T1
Edge trialities form a ℤ₃-valued 1-cycle whose class is the one-form center charge; a ≤5-edge path cannot achieve displacement 3L ≥ 9. The sharper form matters: a winding cycle needs ≥ L edges, so for L ≥ 6 the classification holds with no center-charge restriction at all. The ℤ₃ block buys uniformity only at L = 3,4,5 — and the chain wants L → ∞. The block is not load-bearing where the route needs it.

L5
Bipartite ⇒ the cycle is a plaquette
T1
The lifted cycle closes in ℤ³, which is bipartite, so it is even; length ≤ 5 and even and ≥ 4 means exactly 4, and every 4-cycle in the cubic lattice bounds an elementary plaquette. Its charge-odd combination is |p,−⟩ = (|p,3⟩ − |p,3̄⟩)/√2.

L6
Sharpness
T1
The 1×2 rectangle is neutral, charge-odd, outside the shell, and sits exactly at 4. So the threshold 4 and the gap 4/3 are attained, not merely bounded.

L7
Yarotsky hypotheses and the cell regrouping
T3 · imported
Yarotsky's Theorem 1 admits infinite-dimensional site spaces and unbounded non-negative onsite terms with gap ≥ 1 — the compass artifact in corpus-import/literature/ asserts the opposite ("stated for finite-dimensional sites") and dropped the route on that basis. Group the three outgoing links per vertex, rescale by 3/2, and ε(u) ≤ 27u. Still missing: the periodic-torus adaptation, which the paper notes but does not prove.

L8
The separation ratio θ_F = 1/33
T1 · re-derived here
In units of 1/6 the attainable totals around 16 are 14 and 17. So θ_F = min(2/30, 1/33) = 1/33, attained on the 17 side. Verified independently in this session over the full additive semigroup.

L9
Riesz contour and resolvent bound
T1 · re-derived here
With ρ = (1−λ)/12: the distance to the 16-patch is (1−λ)/12 − 32λ/12 and to the 17-patch is 1/6 − 17λ/6 − (1−λ)/12; both equal (1−33λ)/12, so ‖(H̃ − z)⁻¹‖ ≤ 12/(1−33λ) and the condition is exactly λ < 1/33. The 14-side distance is (3−27λ)/12, strictly larger. Arithmetic confirmed.

L10
Symmetry commutation ⇒ block restriction
T1
Gauge, charge conjugation, translations and the ℤ₃ one-form charge all commute with H(u) exactly (each V_i is a product of central single-link multiplications), so the Riesz projection restricts and its rank splits sector by sector.

L11
Rank constancy along the segment
missing paragraph
Rank 3L³ follows from L7–L10 plus one unwritten step: rank-constancy in u along t ↦ tu, together with mult_block(8/3) = 3L³ at u = 0. This is a completion, not an error — but it is not written anywhere.

L12
The domain is at the wrong end of the coupling axis
structural
u = β_N/(2N) = β₃/6 = 1/g_H⁴, so the continuum is g_H → 0, i.e. u → ∞. The necessary condition for the patch is u < 8/99 ≈ 0.081; a sufficient estimate gives u < 1/(891 c₂) ≈ 10⁻³. The near-Γ exclusion radius (ADR 0003) is non-vacuous only for u < 0.133. Every fixed-spacing statement in the program lives at small u; the continuum is at infinite u. Nothing connects them, and nothing in the finite-spacing half can.

What this half actually delivers

A complete, sharp classification of the charge-odd spectrum below 4 at zero coupling, and a conditional volume-uniform isolation of the whole 8/3 patch in a strong-coupling domain. That is real. It is also, by L12, disjoint from the continuum question rather than a step toward it. The finite-spacing half tells you which object to follow; it cannot follow it.

III · Five reductions, and what is left of them
R1 · Mass bound from one correlator ratio sound, corrected
Let C(t) = ⟨ψ, e^{−tH}ψ⟩ = ∫e^{−tE}dρ(E) with ρ a nonzero finite positive measure on [M,∞), M finite. Then M ≤ (1/t₁)·log(C(0)/C(t₁)) for every t₁ > 0.

Three corrections. The bound needs no log-convexity — one line from E ≥ M ρ-a.e. gives C(t) ≤ C(0)e^{−Mt}; convexity gives only the monotonicity in t₁, so my "therefore" over-attributed. Three hypotheses were missing (ρ ≠ 0, ρ finite, M finite). And it is not new: g(0⁺) is the Rayleigh quotient and g(∞) = M, so the family is exactly the standard imaginary-time interpolation between the variational bound and the exact gap.

False on a periodic time lattice

Every Monte Carlo of this kind uses periodic Euclidean time, where C(t) = ∫(e^{−tE} + e^{−(T−t)E})dρ and the bound fails for every t > 0, not merely near T/2. Counterexample: one state at E = 1, T = 8 gives g(0.5) = 0.9988, g(2) = 0.9911, g(7) = 0.1426 — all below M = 1. Log-convexity and monotonicity survive the torus; the identification of the limit with M dies. Corrected forms, machine-checked over 20,000 random measures with T ≥ 2t (0 violations, against 3,816/20,000 for the naive form):

M ≤ g(t) + (1/t)·log(1 + e^{−(T−2t)M})     [self-consistent, exact]
M ≤ (1/t₁)·log( 2·C(0)/C(t₁) )           [clean, sufficient]
The extra (log 2)/t₁ is uniform in a, so the reduction survives — but the inequality I wrote is not the one that holds.

A second load-bearing hypothesis I left implicit: positivity of the transfer matrix. It holds for the Wilson plaquette action (Lüscher 1977) and fails for Symanzik / dimension-6 improved actions (Lüscher–Weisz 1984: complex transfer-matrix eigenvalues, negative-weight contributions). Without positivity there is no ρ ≥ 0 and the whole argument is void. Any run designed to test this must use an unimproved action.

R2 · The residue is not an independent unknown refuted
What survives. The inequality, with two corrections. The gap must be the island-to-rest gap Δ′ = inf R − sup I, not measured from M_a — with an island of width 0.30 and Δ = 0.50, r₀ = 100, t₀ = 5, my bound asserts z ≥ 0.1086 where the truth is 0.0265: false by 4.1×. And r₀ must be the Loewner ratio λ_max(μ(I)^{−1/2}μ(R)μ(I)^{−1/2}), not a trace ratio — the trace bound failed in 4 of 4 random trials. The sharp form is an identity:

λ_min(ν(I)) = 1 / ( 1 + λ_max( W(I,t₀)^{−1/2} W(R,t₀) W(I,t₀)^{−1/2} ) ).
The matrix reduction to a scalar is right but for a better reason than I gave: the vector rep T₁ of O has Σ|χ|²/|G| = 1, i.e. it is absolutely irreducible, so complex Schur gives μ(B) = c(B)I₃ for every Borel set. Real character alone would not suffice. With n independent T₁ triplets — a variational basis — the average is C(B) ⊗ I₃ with C a nontrivial n×n matrix, and the Loewner correction bites.

What fails. The headline. Hypothesis (iii) is a third hypothesis and it is a residue hypothesis. The accounting also runs the wrong way: the original three constrain ν_a, the source's measure, where a gap can come from vanishing overlaps; my replacements constrain spec(H_a) in the sector, which is strictly stronger — and uniform in a that is essentially the lattice mass-gap statement itself.

And the cost was overstated in the direction that matters. t₀ need not diverge at all: at fixed physical t₀ the residue is exactly a-independent for a local operator, because the a^{2d−3} suppression is common to the island weight and to the whole fixed-physical-time correlator, and the a^{−p} in r₀ comes entirely from λ ≳ 1/a, which any fixed t₀ > 0 annihilates. My bound throws that away by piling all rest-weight at M + Δ_*. Calibrated on the corpus's own MC point, at a√σ = 0.10 the bound gives z ≥ 4.0×10⁻⁶ against a true z = 0.177 — a factor 2.3×10⁻⁵, with the ratio going to zero like a⁹. The log(1/a) device is neither necessary nor sufficient.

R3 · Charge conjugation supplies the isolation refuted
What survives is one line and it carries no existence content: Ω is C-even (uniqueness of the vacuum forces CΩ = +Ω), so H_{C=−} ⊥ Ω and M_a ≥ gap(H_a). The referee's check on this is the good one: for SU(2) the corpus itself proves P_{C=−} = 0, so the statement holds vacuously over an empty sector.

C-multiplicativity also survives, with a correct proof: on the asymptotic Fock space C = Γ(c) is a second quantization, so an n-particle state has C = ∏η_i independent of the orbital wavefunction — unlike qq̄, where C exchanges constituents. Two identical 0^{++} require even L, giving 0^{++}, 2^{++}, 4^{++}, …, never C = −1. So a C-odd state needs an odd number of C-odd constituents. The threshold arithmetic checks against three digest-pinned primaries.

What fails, and it is most of it:

The Clay direction is reversed. M_a is a lattice quantity; Jaffe–Witten is about a continuum theory on ℝ⁴ and says nothing about spec(H_a). A uniform lattice gap plus spectral convergence is a route to Clay. At strong coupling the lattice gap is an independent Osterwalder–Seiler-type theorem needing no Clay input; at weak coupling it is open.
The isolation gap is wrong by 2.7–2.8× in all three independent lattice determinations. Five to six discrete C-odd shells sit strictly between M and M + m(0^{++}). The gap of the lightest C-odd shell is set by the 3^{+−}, not by the threshold.
"M is below threshold in its own sector" is circular — the threshold is defined as M + m(0^{++}), so the statement uses only m(0^{++}) > 0. Non-circularly one needs the bottom of the essential spectrum of the C-odd fiber to lie strictly above the infimum: an HVZ-type theorem. C-multiplicativity cannot supply it, because multiplicativity is a statement about asymptotic particle content — it presupposes the scattering structure being derived.
A variational infimum always exists; a mass shell need not. The C-odd fiber at P = 0 may be purely absolutely continuous from M up — resonance, no stable carrier — and nothing in the argument excludes it. Haag–Ruelle also needs isolation over an open set of momenta; on a lattice, with no Lorentz invariance, p = 0 does not propagate to p ≠ 0.
Bachmann–Dybalski–Naaijkens, whom I cited for the conclusion, say the opposite of what I used them for: "There is no general criterion that ensures the validity of the assumptions in a given quantum lattice system. In fact, proving the very existence of an isolated mass shell is in general a difficult problem."

R4 · The Γ singularity is metric, not topological refuted
I published one correction to this already, on finding the corpus's own audit. The referee's is sharper, and worse for me.

The result is true and trivial. The carrier line bundle over T³∖{Γ} is trivial, in one line with no ℝP² and no computation: |ψ(k)|² = q_a(k) > 0 for k ≠ Γ and ψ is a Laurent polynomial in e^{ik_i}, so ψ/|ψ| is a global real-analytic frame; the Berry connection is a globally defined 1-form, F = dA is exact, and every flux vanishes by Stokes.

The computation was forced to return zero before I ran it. [S²_Γ] = 0 in H₂(T³∖{Γ}) because the sphere bounds T³ minus the ball — a 3-chain lying wholly in the punctured torus. And q_a vanishes only at Γ, so there is no partner node and Nielsen–Ninomiya forces the charge to zero for any continuous bundle on the punctured torus. The Fukui–Hatsugai–Suzuki number could not have come out nonzero.

And c₁ is blind to the invariant by construction. π₂(ℝP²) = ℤ → π₂(ℂP²) = ℤ is the zero map, so a nontrivial real class dies in ℂP² and no Chern number can see it. Computing c₁ and concluding "no topological obstruction" is the precise error the corpus prohibits at FLATBAND_PAPER_EXPANSION_AUDIT_2026-07-25.md:427.

The one thing the referee corrected in my favour: in the centered gauge the carrier is exactly real for every k in the zone, not merely asymptotically, with Berry curvature identically zero pointwise. My half-link gauge was right — and it is the corpus's own gauge, rederived under a different name. In the UNIFIED §3.2 gauge, by contrast, the projective class is nowhere in ℝP²: the reality defect is ½|k|² + O(|k|⁴) near Γ and reaches 0.999992 out of a maximum of 1 elsewhere in the zone.

The corpus's version — a fragile unit Hodge hedgehog, since adding one trivial real orbital moves the target to ℝP³ where π₂ = 0 — is strictly stronger than anything I computed: it kills the class in the real category where it actually lives, which c₁ cannot reach.

R5 · The spin split is exactly 3/5 : 2/5 sound, corrected
All four claims check out exactly; the referee could not refute any of them. Three corrections and one strengthening.

Normalization. Tr X³ = ¼ d^{abc}X^aX^bX^c for su(3) with T^a = λ^a/2, so the carrier is O_n = −(a⁶/24)S_nnn + O(a⁷), not a⁶/6.
My justification was wrong. "Tr X² is C-even" is irrelevant: for Hermitian X every Tr X^n is real, so Im Tr e^{iX} contains only odd powers and Tr X² never appears. The n=1 term dies by tracelessness, leaving n=3 first.
Claim 3 was understated. Not merely that V_i is outside the span — every element of the 3-dimensional carrier span has the same split: ‖P_{J=1}T‖² = (3/5)|c|² identically in c. No linear combination purifies.
Strengthening. Under the cubic group Sym³(ℝ³) = A₂ ⊕ 2T₁ ⊕ T₂ — T₁ has multiplicity two, and the carrier's copy is at cos² = 3/5 from the J=1 isotypic copy: it is neither isotypic T₁. And the positive test is sharper than "measure A₂ and T₂": a single T₁ with no partner in any other irrep forces 2J+1 = 3, hence J = 1 uniquely.
Novelty: mostly none. The Im Tr e^{iX} expansion is boxed twice in the corpus (§10.1 and the detailed formula), the subduction table is literally Morningstar–Peardon 1999 Table VI — all 65 entries match — and the partner-degeneracy method is their §V.B and Athenodorou–Teper 2020 §6. What I contributed is the exact 3/5 : 2/5 weight and the multiplicity-2 placement, and the observation that the carrier is structurally blind to its own falsifier.

IV · What the continuum theorem still requires
With the reductions gone, the honest statement is close to where the dossier left it, with three things sharpened and one hypothesis added that I had tried to remove.

requirement	standing after the audit
M_a ≥ m₋ > 0 uniformly, physical units	Open. Not implied by Clay — it is a lattice statement and part of a route to Clay. Independently provable at strong coupling by Osterwalder–Seiler-type expansion; open in the scaling window.
M_a ≤ m₊ < ∞ uniformly	Reduces to one correlator ratio, via R1's corrected torus form M ≤ (1/t₁)log(2C(0)/C(t₁)) — requires an unimproved action for transfer-matrix positivity. This part of the reduction does hold.
isolation Δ_a ≥ Δ_* > 0	Open, and set by the 3+−, not by the two-glueball threshold. Needs an HVZ-type theorem on the essential spectrum of the C-odd fiber, over an open set of momenta, not just P = 0.
residue z_a ≥ z_* > 0	Open and independent. Equivalent to ⟨G|d^{abc}F^aF^bF^c|0⟩ ≠ 0 with a uniform constant — the corpus's own headline unknown. For a local source Z₀ ~ a⁹; for a fixed-physical-radius source, a-independent.
spin 1	Open, method known. Needs auxiliary channels; the carrier has identically zero overlap with its own J = 3 partners.
an RG that preserves reflection positivity	Never asked. See Part V.
The one structural gain that survives everything: the exponent. Because Z₀ ~ |f_d|²a^{2d−3} and the carrier's leading operator is dimension six, a bare carrier source loses overlap like a⁹ — while a source smeared to fixed physical radius does not lose it at all. The smeared basis is not a numerical convenience and not merely "structural" by citation; it is the difference between an exponent of 9 and an exponent of 0.

V · The CMP chain, and where the stopping point actually is
External work (2026-08-22) closes the fixed-spacing half — including the literal Wilson source. It also, independently, refutes the reduction in Part III. Both of those are load-bearing here.

What CMP(1)–(4) buys, stated against this repository's register
The chain proves: at sufficiently small u, in the infinite-volume charge-odd GNS sector, the isolated Riesz island is exactly ℓ²(ℤ³) ⊗ ℂ³, carrying an exponentially local Hermitian 3×3 convolution Hamiltonian whose Bloch matrix is analytic in a complex momentum strip, with a true external gap — and CMP(4) transfers this to multiplication by Im Tr U_p with

T_W = T_c(I + G⁻¹C),    G_W(k) ⪰ (1 − ‖G−I‖)(1 − ‖E‖)² I > 0  for every real k.
Three spot-checks of the load-bearing steps hold: c_W = i√2 is forced by character orthonormality (‖Im χ₃‖² = ¼(1+1) = ½); ‖D_s‖ ≤ 3√2 + 1; and the mechanism of (2.2) is exact — D_sΩ₀ = 0 gives D_s^*D_s = (1−Q_s)D_s^*D_s(1−Q_s) ≤ M_D²(1−Q_s), so ω_u(D_s^*D_s) ≤ 6gM_D². The documents are also honest about their own edges: the constants are existential, and the periodic finite-quotient step is a derived transport rather than a numbered theorem in Yarotsky.

This discharges G18's first half, and corrects its statement

G18 asks for "volume-uniform overlap of a smeared/dressed T₁^{+−} operator … with the transfer-matrix spectrum." CMP delivers something stronger on three axes and weaker on one. Stronger: infinite volume rather than volume-uniform; the bare literal Wilson operator rather than a smeared one; positive-definite at every momentum rather than a single overlap number. Weaker: only in the small-u domain.

So the gap's own sentence — "the theorem must live in the smeared basis" — is not right as a general claim. At strong coupling the bare source is fine. Smearing is forced only in the continuum direction, and Part I's exponent says exactly how much: the bare source's weight falls like a^{2d−3} = a⁹ while a fixed-physical-radius source's does not fall at all. Bare source: sufficient at fixed spacing, hopeless toward the continuum. The two results are complementary, not in tension.

The reduction in Part III fails for a reason worth keeping
The external audit gives two counterexamples, both correct, and they are the same defect my own referees found independently. Counterexample A: dμ = 1_{[M,M+1]}dE has a positive fixed-time ratio, a bottom at M, exact charge conjugation and no accumulation of discrete masses — because it has no discrete masses at all. No atom, no shell, no particle. Counterexample B: μ_a = ε_aδ_M + (1−ε_a)δ_{M+Δ} with ε_a → 0 keeps C_a(t)/C_a(0) → e^{−(M+Δ)t} > 0 while the residue at M vanishes.

Two independent adversarial passes converging on the same refutation is not the historical record speaking. It is the mathematics. Accepting it is not pessimism — but stopping there would be.

The repaired criterion
The fixed-time ratio was the wrong functional. The right one is the same correlator read at the rate rather than the value. For any positive finite μ on [M,∞),

e^{Mt}·C(t) = ∫ e^{−t(E−M)} dμ(E)  ⟶  μ({M})   as t → ∞,
by dominated convergence — always, with no hypotheses beyond finiteness. So liminf_{t→∞} e^{Mt}C(t) ≥ c > 0 is equivalent to an atom at M of weight at least c. Verified: 20,000 random measures agree with their own atom to 1.4×10⁻⁹ at t = 400.

And it defeats both counterexamples, which is the test that matters:

measure	e^{Mt}C(t)	verdict under the repaired criterion
A — absolutely continuous on [M,M+w]	(1−e^{−wt})/(wt) → 0	excluded: 5.0×10⁻² at t=100, 5.0×10⁻³ at t=1000
B — ε_aδ_M + (1−ε_a)δ_{M+Δ}	→ ε_a	excluded by a uniform-in-a bound: the liminf is exactly the vanishing residue
It is not circular: M_a := −lim_t (1/t)log C_a(t) exists by log-convexity and is bounded above by R1's corrected torus form and below by the sector gap, so M_a is defined before the criterion is applied.

What this does to the five-part stopping point

The external chain lists five uniform requirements. The repaired criterion does not prove any of them — but it changes which ones are needed for which conclusion, and that is the part worth arguing about:

(1) finite physical mass — the upper half is R1 (a variational bound, not Clay); the lower half is the C-odd sector gap, which is Clay-hard.
(3) nonzero source weight — collapses into the repaired criterion. "There is an atom of weight ≥ z" is one statement, not two. This is not the failed compression: nothing is being derived from nothing, the residue simply is the limit of e^{M_at}C_a(t).
(2) isolation including dark states — not needed for the atom. The limit above is a statement about the source's own measure and produces a nonzero mass-M component with no isolation hypothesis whatsoever. Isolation is needed for Riesz transport and for Haag–Ruelle stability — i.e. for calling it a stable particle — and can be deferred.
(4) OS convergence and (5) shell multiplicity — unchanged, and rightly firewalled.
So there is a genuine intermediate theorem between "fixed-spacing band" and "stable relativistic particle": the continuum theory contains a state of mass M with nonzero carrier overlap. It needs a uniform two-sided exponential bound on the flowed carrier correlator and OS convergence. It does not need isolation, dark-state control, or spin. It is the first item on the list that is not Clay-hard by construction.

Why the coupling axis does not connect — the mechanism, not the slogan
"Strong coupling and the continuum sit at opposite ends of the axis" is true and unhelpful, because it suggests the missing object is a continuation. It is not. The precise obstruction is:

CMP works because the unperturbed operator has a gapped product ground state — the electric term H₀ = ½Σ(−Δ_e) has onsite gap one after rescaling, and every estimate in CMP(1)–(4) descends from that, including local product-vacuum continuity 1 − ω_u(Q_T) ≤ 2|T|g. In the weak-coupling direction the roles swap: the plaquette term dominates, and its minima are the moduli space of flat SU(3) connections on T³ — a positive-dimensional continuum of vacua, not a gapped product state. There is no reference operator with a gap to perturb around.

That is why no weak-coupling analogue of CMP exists, and it says precisely what would be needed: a gapped reference Hamiltonian at weak coupling. Producing one is the mass gap problem. The obstruction is structural rather than technical — which is worse news than "the expansion diverges", but it is at least the right news, and it rules out an entire class of attempts (better constants, resummation, more orders) that the slogan leaves open.

One thing the analyticity buys that has not been claimed
A Riesz projection is analytic in u for as long as its contour stays in the resolvent set. So the island's rank — three per momentum fiber — persists automatically along any path in u on which the external gap stays open. Nothing needs to be continued by hand; the continuation is free while the gap is open.

What does not come free is the rest of CMP: the Gram positivity, the exponential locality and the frame all rest on small-u clustering and on product-vacuum continuity, which has no large-u analogue. So the honest reformulation of "continue the band" is two separate targets, and only one of them is a continuation:

a lower bound on the external gap of the C-odd island, at each u along the scaling trajectory — after which the rank structure is automatic;
a re-proof of the source-residue statement by non-perturbative means, since its current proof is irreducibly strong-coupling.
Target 2 is where the repaired criterion lands: it is a statement about one correlator's exponential rate, and it does not care how the gap was obtained.

The identification, and the asymmetry that favours the carrier

(14.37) is an exponential-clustering estimate for a two-point function with rate m_J. Run the same expansion with the carrier operator as the mark rather than a rare-event defect indicator, and it is the carrier correlator's decay rate — a mass. C1–C4 are shared machinery.

And the accounting differs in the carrier's favour, sharply. PMBSF's mark is a discontinuous hard indicator Y_p(t) = 1{1 − ½Re tr U_p ≥ t}, and its literature map records the consequence: "the constructive bounds are proved for smooth analytic observables and the partition function, while Y_p(t) is discontinuous; the smoothing bridge requires constants uniform in ε and 'is not standard'." That single fact generates most of PMBSF's difficulty — the pinned hard-indicator form (without which its KP series formally diverges, C_*e^{−m_*}J_{m_*} ≈ 117 > 1), Open Theorem D's boundary-band gate, the smoothing bridge estimated at "1–2 months of genuine analysis", and the top-p-versus-threshold problem.

The carrier source is Im Tr U_p — a character, real-analytic on the group, and analytic in the links after gradient-flow smearing. It is exactly the observable class for which the constructive bounds already exist. So of PMBSF's four open theorems, the carrier route inherits only C: Lemma Q and open inputs A and B are rare-event accounting it does not need, and D is a smoothing problem it does not have. Conversely PMBSF supplies nothing about the residue, because a rare-event indicator has none. The two programs need the same expansion and opposite halves of the bookkeeping. VII · Three obstructions the corpus may over-read
These three readings were not sent to the adversarial pass. Given its hit rate on the five that were, treat them as conjectures with an argument attached, not results.

1. The L⁻² collapse is the dispersion relation
§12.1 records that "the finite-volume incidence isolation scales as L⁻² and collapses as L → ∞", and the MASTER_PROOF_STATE takes this as defeating volume-uniform transport of the b₂-fold level. The quantity is Δ^{(2)} = 4t_N u² sin²(π/L) — the gap from the flat carrier band to the dispersing branches at the smallest available nonzero momentum. Its vanishing as L → ∞ is the statement that E(k) → E(0) as k → 0. That is continuity of a dispersion curve, not a pathology.

And b₂ = dim H₂(T³) = 3: the "b₂-fold protected level" is the Γ-point triplet, which is the three polarizations of a spin-1 state at rest and is required by cubic symmetry to be exactly threefold degenerate. A rank-one contour around one sheet is not merely unjustified — it would be wrong. Isolation for a particle is isolation of a mass shell in the joint energy–momentum spectrum, fiberwise in k, and the external gap that supports it is uniform. The obstruction is real for the object the corpus names and absent for the object a particle theorem needs.

2. The near-Γ exclusion radius excludes the physical region
ADR 0003 gives |k| ≥ K·u, K = 23.66 at θ = 1/2, below which the O(u⁴) spread meets the O(u²)|k|² splitting and the fixed-order ordering fails. Inside that ball the three sheets are not resolvable at fixed order. For a spin-1 triplet they are supposed to be degenerate there. What the exclusion radius costs is the fixed-order splitting formula, not the particle — and the ADR's own honesty ("says nothing about behaviour inside the ball") is the right reading. The radius is a statement about perturbation theory's domain, and it should not be carried into the continuum discussion as an obstruction to the object.

3. The T0 layer contains nothing about the electric spectrum
All 28 Lean theorems are rational-arithmetic identities; sorry count is 0. Notably dim_Z₂ is proved as (L³ − 1) + 3 = L³ + 2 — arithmetic, with the homological inputs rank ∂₃ = L³ − 1 and dim H₂ = 3 supplied, not derived. And a search of invariants.py finds the electric convention E(L) = L·C_F/2 and the value 8/3 registered, but no check that the shell exhausts the spectrum below 4. The MASTER_PROOF_STATE's claim that Theorem 1′ would be "the repository's first T1 statement about the electric spectrum" is correct. L1–L6 above are the cheapest available promotion in the whole program: exact, enumerable, and load-bearing for §11.4's retained-space truncation as a by-product.
