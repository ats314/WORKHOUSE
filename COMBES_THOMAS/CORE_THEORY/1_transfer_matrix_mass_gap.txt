Title: Transfer Matrix, Spectral Gap, and Mass Gap (Summary from Chat)

This document summarizes the structurally correct derivations and identities related to
the transfer matrix, spectral gap, and the Yang–Mills mass gap that appeared in our chat.
Nothing here is a new result; it is a cleaned-up restatement of standard structure.


1. Transfer Matrix on the Lattice

In Euclidean lattice Yang–Mills (pure gauge SU(3) for concreteness), with temporal lattice
spacing a, one constructs a transfer matrix T(a) acting on a Hilbert space of gauge-invariant
states on a fixed time slice.

Formally,
    T(a) = e^{-a H(a)}

where:
    - H(a) is the (lattice) Hamiltonian;
    - a > 0 is the lattice spacing in the time direction.


2. Eigenvalues and Energy Levels

Assume:
    T(a) |ψ_n(a)⟩ = λ_n(a) |ψ_n(a)⟩,

with eigenvalues λ_n(a) ≥ 0 ordered as:
    λ_0(a) > λ_1(a) ≥ λ_2(a) ≥ …

Then, by definition of the transfer matrix,
    λ_n(a) = e^{-a E_n(a)},

where E_n(a) are the energy eigenvalues of H(a). In particular:
    E_n(a) = - (1/a) log λ_n(a).


3. Definition of the Mass Gap

Let E_0(a) be the vacuum energy (lowest eigenvalue), and E_1(a) the energy of the first
nontrivial excitation (e.g. the lightest glueball in pure YM). Then the mass gap at
lattice spacing a is:
    m(a) = E_1(a) - E_0(a).

In terms of λ_0(a), λ_1(a):

    λ_0(a) = e^{-a E_0(a)},
    λ_1(a) = e^{-a E_1(a)}.

So:

    m(a) = E_1(a) - E_0(a)
         = - (1/a) [log λ_1(a) - log λ_0(a)]
         = - (1/a) log (λ_1(a) / λ_0(a)).

Thus, the mass gap is directly tied to the ratio of the two largest eigenvalues of T(a).


4. Correlation Functions and Exponential Decay

Consider a gauge-invariant operator O(t) defined on time slices, and the Euclidean
two-point function:

    C(t) = ⟨ O(t) O(0) ⟩.

Inserting a complete set of energy eigenstates:

    C(t) = Σ_n |⟨0|O|n⟩|^2 e^{-E_n t}.

As t → ∞, the dominant term is the lowest excitation that couples to O, typically
n = 1 for a suitable glueball operator. Then:

    C(t) ~ |⟨0|O|1⟩|^2 e^{-m t},
    m = E_1 - E_0.

In transfer-matrix language:

    C(t) = Σ_n |⟨0|O|n⟩|^2
           (λ_n / λ_0)^t,

and asymptotically:

    C(t) ~ const · (λ_1 / λ_0)^t = const · e^{-m t}.


5. Mass Gap in the Continuum Limit (Conceptual)

In the continuum limit a → 0, a rigorous mass gap statement would assert that:

    lim_{a→0} m(a) = m_phys > 0,

which in transfer-matrix language is equivalent to:

    lim_{a→0} [ - (1/a) log (λ_1(a) / λ_0(a)) ] = m_phys > 0.

A Clay-level proof would need to show:
    - the existence of the continuum Hamiltonian H with well-defined spectrum;
    - that the spectral gap E_1 - E_0 remains strictly positive in that limit.

This document only encodes the algebraic relationships; it is not a proof that the gap exists.
