## 1. Deterministic target: decay of \((M^{\mathrm{hinge}})^{-1}\) between distant links

After Extract 02, conditional covariances are controlled by the inverse of the hinge operator
\[
M_{\Lambda_L}^{\mathrm{hinge}}
=
m_H^2\,\mathrm{Id}+\frac12\,\alpha_W\,d_1^*d_1,
\]
a positive definite finite-range operator on link 1-cochains.

To obtain exponential clustering, one needs an estimate of the type
\[
\bigl\|\bigl(M_{\Lambda_L}^{\mathrm{hinge}}\bigr)^{-1}_{b,b'}\bigr\|
\ \le\
C\,e^{-\eta\,\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal E}(b,b')},
\]
where \(\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal E}\) is the link-graph distance.

The project provides two complementary routes:

- **Combes–Thomas (Appendix G):** robust, quick, explicit (but often non-sharp) constants.
- **Davies method (Appendix H):** semigroup/Laplace-transform approach; can yield different constants and is conceptually close to heat-kernel bounds.

## 2. Combes–Thomas inverse decay for finite-range operators (Appendix G)

### 2.1 Abstract setting

Let \(I\) be a finite index set with graph distance \(\mathrm{dist}\), and let \(M\) be a self-adjoint operator on \(\ell^2(I)\) of finite range \(R\), meaning \(M_{ij}=0\) if \(\mathrm{dist}(i,j)>R\).

Assume a **spectral gap**
\[
M\succeq m\,\mathrm{Id},\qquad m>0.
\]

Combes–Thomas estimates give exponential off-diagonal decay of \(M^{-1}\) in terms of:

- the range \(R\),
- the gap \(m\),
- a local “strength” parameter \(\nu\) controlling the size of the off-diagonal couplings.

A typical form is:
\[
\|M^{-1}_{ij}\|\ \le\ \frac{2}{m}\,\exp\!\bigl(-\eta_{\mathrm{CT}}\,\mathrm{dist}(i,j)\bigr),
\qquad
\eta_{\mathrm{CT}}=\frac1R\log\!\Bigl(1+\frac{m}{\nu}\Bigr).
\]

### 2.2 Application to the massive Maxwell operator

For \(M_{\Lambda_L}^{\mathrm{hinge}}\), finite range follows from locality of \(d_1^*d_1\): it couples only links that share a plaquette (up to fixed bounded range).

The gap is immediate:
\[
M_{\Lambda_L}^{\mathrm{hinge}}\succeq m_H^2\,\mathrm{Id},
\]
since \(d_1^*d_1\succeq 0\).

Core 7 then packages this into a decay constant \(\eta_1>0\) (explicit in terms of \(m_H^2,\alpha_W\) and lattice geometry constants) giving:

\[
\boxed{
\bigl\|\bigl(M_{\Lambda_L}^{\mathrm{hinge}}\bigr)^{-1}_{b,b'}\bigr\|
\ \le\
\frac{2}{m_H^2}\,\exp\!\bigl(-\eta_1\,\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal E}(b,b')\bigr).
}
\]

This deterministic estimate is the “Green kernel decay” input for exponential clustering on \(\mathcal K_{\Lambda_L,\beta}\).

## 3. Davies method: semigroup conjugation and Laplace transform (Appendix H)

The Davies-type approach starts from the identity
\[
M^{-1}=\int_0^\infty e^{-tM}\,\mathrm dt
\qquad\text{(Laplace transform)}.
\]

### 3.1 Conjugation inequality

For a weight \(\psi:I\to\mathbb R\), consider the conjugated semigroup
\[
e^\psi e^{-tM}e^{-\psi}.
\]
Davies’ method bounds its operator norm by analyzing commutators generated by the finite-range structure of \(M\). One obtains an inequality of the form
\[
\bigl\|e^\psi e^{-tM}e^{-\psi}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\ \le\
\exp\!\bigl(t\,\mathcal E(\psi)\bigr),
\]
where \(\mathcal E(\psi)\) depends on discrete gradients of \(\psi\) and the coupling strengths of \(M\).

Choosing \(\psi\) proportional to the distance to a fixed site gives exponential decay of the kernel of \(e^{-tM}\) in space, uniformly in \(t\).

### 3.2 Integrating in time

Integrating the heat-kernel decay in \(t\) yields an exponential decay bound for \(M^{-1}\).
The resulting exponent can be optimized over the conjugation parameter \(\lambda\) (the coefficient in \(\psi=\lambda\,\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\cdot_0)\)).

## 4. Why keep both tools?

- **Combes–Thomas** is extremely general and easy to apply once you have a gap and finite range.
- **Davies** is often closer to continuum intuition and can interface naturally with other semigroup estimates (e.g., functional inequalities).

In this project, either route supplies the crucial deterministic ingredient:
\[
\text{covariance control} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{Green-kernel decay} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{exponential clustering}.
\]
