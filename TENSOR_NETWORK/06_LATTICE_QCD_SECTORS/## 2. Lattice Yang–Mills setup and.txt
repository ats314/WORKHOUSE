**Lemma 2.1 (bounded overlap on the 4D hypercubic lattice).**
For every b=(x,\mu)\in E(\Lambda), the number of oriented plaquettes p\in P(\Lambda) whose boundary contains b (with either sign) is at most 6. More precisely, for each \nu\in\{0,1,2,3\}\setminus\{\mu\} there are at most two plaquettes in the (\mu,\nu)-plane whose boundary contains b, and no other plaquettes contain b.

*Proof.* Fix b=(x,\mu). A plaquette can contain b only if it lies in a coordinate plane spanned by \hat e_\mu and some \hat e_\nu with \nu\neq \mu.

1. **Restriction to coordinate planes:** The edge b connects x and x+\hat e_\mu. Any unit square containing this edge must lie in the plane spanned by \hat e_\mu and some \hat e_\nu (\nu \neq \mu). In 4 dimensions, there are exactly 3 such orthogonal directions \nu.
2. **Restriction within a plane:** For a fixed \nu, there are exactly two unit squares in the (\mu,\nu)-plane sharing the edge (x, x+\hat e_\mu).
* One is the square with vertices \{x, x+\hat e_\mu, x+\hat e_\mu+\hat e_\nu, x+\hat e_\nu\}, corresponding to the plaquette p_1 = (x;\mu,\nu). In this case, b is the first edge of \partial p_1, so \sigma_{p_1, b} = +1.
* The other is the square with vertices \{x, x+\hat e_\mu, x+\hat e_\mu-\hat e_\nu, x-\hat e_\nu\}, corresponding to the shifted plaquette p_2 = (x-\hat e_\nu; \mu, \nu). In this case, b is the third edge of \partial p_2 (the edge opposite the base), so it appears with a minus sign: \sigma_{p_2, b} = -1.


3. **Conclusion:** Since there are 3 choices for \nu and 2 plaquettes per choice, the total number of incident plaquettes is 3 \times 2 = 6. \square

This bounded overlap is the key combinatorial input that prevents operator norms of first and second derivatives of local actions (such as the Wilson action) from carrying an extra factor proportional to |P(\Lambda)|: every link participates in only O(1) plaquette terms, uniformly in the volume.

### 2.5 Local observables, supports, covariances, and time translations

Although the finite-volume configuration space \Omega_\Lambda=G^{E(\Lambda)} is itself finite-dimensional, virtually all statements of interest (clustering, stability in L, OS reconstruction, and ultimately the continuum discussion) are formulated for observables that are uniformly local, i.e. that depend only on a bounded region of the lattice independent of the volume parameters (L_0,L_1,L_2,L_3). This subsection fixes a precise notion of locality, defines canonical supports and distances between supports, and introduces translation operators—especially Euclidean time translations—together with the basic covariance identities they imply.

Throughout, \langle \cdot \rangle_{\Lambda,\beta} denotes expectation with respect to the Gibbs measure \mu_{\Lambda,\beta} from Definition 2.9.

*Proof.* If b\notin A, then changing U_b while holding all U_{b'} for b'\in A fixed does not change \pi_A(U), hence does not change F. This proves \mathrm{supp}(F)\subset A. Conversely, define A_0:=\mathrm{supp}(F). If U,U' agree on all links in A_0, then one can pass from U to U' by changing one link at a time outside A_0, and each such single-link modification leaves F unchanged by definition of A_0. Hence F(U)=F(U'), so F depends only on (U_b)_{b\in A_0}, i.e. F is A_0-local. \square

**Gauge-invariant local observables.**
If F is gauge invariant in the sense of Definition 2.3, then its locality can be expressed intrinsically via holonomies. Typical examples include:

* single-plaquette observables U\mapsto \Phi_\beta(U_p(U)) (support is \partial p, four links),
* Wilson loop observables U\mapsto \mathrm{Tr}\big(U(\gamma)\big) for a fixed closed loop \gamma,
* finite products and linear combinations of such objects.

The proof strategy ultimately concerns decay of connected correlations of such gauge-invariant local observables.

**Remark (time periodicity versus large-L_0 limits).**
Because \Lambda is a time circle, the map t\mapsto C_{F,G}^\circ(t) is periodic. When deriving mass-gap lower bounds from Euclidean decay, one either works with a reflection plane compatible with the torus (typically L_0 even) and confines to t\le L_0/2, or passes to infinite volume first in the time direction so that t\in\mathbb Z is unbounded. The geometric support-distance \mathrm{dist}_\Lambda(\mathrm{supp}(F),\mathrm{supp}(\tau_tG)) correctly captures time separation in the regime where wrap-around does not occur (i.e. where the relevant supports have diameter much smaller than L_0); this stabilization is treated explicitly in the thermodynamic-limit section.
