Part 16 — Lattice Osterwalder–Schrader Axioms for Wilson Yang–Mills

In Parts 1–15 we lived entirely in **configuration space**:

* Finite-dimensional Riemannian manifolds (M_\Lambda = G^{E(\Lambda)}) with product bi-invariant metrics and product Haar volume;
* Gibbs measures (d\mu_\Lambda \propto e^{-S_\Lambda} d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda});
* Diffusion generators (L_\Lambda) and Bakry–Émery curvature leading to functional inequalities and spectral gaps in (L^2(\mu_\Lambda)).

From the standpoint of quantum field theory, this is only half the story. To talk about **mass gaps** we need a Hamiltonian acting on a Hilbert space of states and a notion of Euclidean-time evolution. This is where the **Osterwalder–Schrader (OS)** framework enters.

This part has three goals:

1. Adapt the OS axioms (reflection positivity, Euclidean invariance, etc.) to the lattice Yang–Mills setting;
2. Explain how the standard Wilson lattice gauge theory satisfies these axioms at finite lattice spacing and volume;
3. Make explicit which objects (fields, observables, Schwinger functions) will later feed into OS reconstruction of a finite-volume Hamiltonian (H_\Lambda).

We work throughout at **fixed lattice spacing (a>0)** and **finite spacetime volume**; no continuum or infinite-volume limit appears yet. All configuration spaces are finite-dimensional, so there are no measure-theoretic monsters hiding under the bed. 

---

## 16.1. Spacetime lattice and gauge fields

We now upgrade our spatial configuration manifold to a **(d+1)-dimensional Euclidean spacetime lattice** with one distinguished “time” direction.

### 16.1.1. Spacetime geometry

Fix:

* A Euclidean time circle (or segment) with (T) time-slices:
  [
  \mathbb{Z}_T := {0,1,\dots,T-1}
  ]
  with periodic time understood unless otherwise stated;
* A finite spatial lattice (\Lambda_S \subset \mathbb{Z}^d) (e.g. an (L^d) box with periodic or fixed boundary conditions).

The full spacetime lattice is
[
\Lambda := \mathbb{Z}_T \times \Lambda_S.
]

We write lattice points as ((t,\mathbf{x})) with (t \in \mathbb{Z}_T), (\mathbf{x}\in\Lambda_S). The Euclidean time direction will be labeled by (\mu=0), spatial directions by (\mu=1,\dots,d).

Let (E(\Lambda)) denote the set of oriented edges of this spacetime lattice (time-directed and space-directed), and (P(\Lambda)) the set of oriented elementary plaquettes as usual.

### 16.1.2. Lattice gauge fields

On each oriented spacetime edge ((t,\mathbf{x};\mu)\in E(\Lambda)) we place a link variable
[
U_{t,\mathbf{x},\mu} \in G,
]
where (G) is a compact, connected Lie group (e.g. (SU(N))).

The **full configuration manifold** is
[
M_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)},
]
with points denoted (U = (U_{t,\mathbf{x},\mu})_{(t,\mathbf{x};\mu)\in E(\Lambda)}).

We equip (M_\Lambda) with the product bi-invariant metric (g_\Lambda) and product Haar volume (\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}) exactly as in Part 2, just with a bigger edge index set. Thus
[
d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U) = \prod_{(t,\mathbf{x};\mu)\in E(\Lambda)} dU_{t,\mathbf{x},\mu},
]
with each factor (dU_{t,\mathbf{x},\mu}) the normalized Haar measure on (G).

### 16.1.3. Wilson action on spacetime

The **Wilson plaquette action** on spacetime is defined in the usual way: for each oriented plaquette (p\in P(\Lambda)) we form its plaquette holonomy
[
U_p(U) = \prod_{\ell\in \partial p} U_\ell,
]
in some fixed oriented order, and define the plaquette contribution
[
S_p(U) := \beta\left(1 - \frac{1}{N}\Re\operatorname{Tr} U_p(U)\right),
]
for (G=SU(N)). The total Wilson action on the spacetime lattice is
[
S_W(U) := \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} S_p(U).
]

As before:

* (S_W) is smooth, local, and nonnegative;
* (S_W) is gauge-invariant under the usual site-based conjugation action of the **spacetime** gauge group
  [
  \mathcal{G}*\Lambda := G^{V(\Lambda)},\quad
  (g\cdot U)*{t,\mathbf{x},\mu} = g_{t,\mathbf{x}} U_{t,\mathbf{x},\mu} g_{t+\hat\mu,\mathbf{x}+\hat\mu}^{-1}.
  ]

All the gauge geometry statements from Part 2 apply verbatim in spacetime.

## 16.2. Gauge-invariant local observables

To state OS axioms, we need the notion of **local fields** and their expectations under the finite-volume Yang–Mills measure.

### 16.2.1. Local gauge-invariant functions

A (spacetime) **local observable** is a complex-valued function of finitely many link variables. Think:

* single plaquette observables (\operatorname{Tr} U_p),
* Wilson loops along finite closed loops (C) in spacetime,
* finite products and polynomial combinations thereof.

We restrict to those observables that are **gauge-invariant** under the spacetime gauge group action—this is the physical sector. Let (\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) be the *-algebra generated by such gauge-invariant local functions:
[
\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}
:= \text{*-algebra generated by gauge-invariant local observables on } M_\Lambda.
]

By gauge invariance, every (\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) satisfies
[
\mathcal{O}(g\cdot U) = \mathcal{O}(U)
\quad \forall g\in\mathcal{G}*\Lambda.
]

### 16.2.2. Euclidean measure and Schwinger functions

The **finite-volume Euclidean Yang–Mills measure** (Wilson-only, to keep life simple) is
[
d\mu_\Lambda(U)
:= Z_\Lambda^{-1} e^{-S_W(U)} , d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U),
]
where
[
Z_\Lambda = \int_{M_\Lambda} e^{-S_W(U)},d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U).
]

For gauge-invariant local observables (\mathcal{O}_1,\dots,\mathcal{O}*n\in\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) supported near spacetime points (x_1,\dots,x_n\in\Lambda), we define the **Schwinger functions**
[
S^{(\Lambda)}*n(x_1,\dots,x_n)
:= \big\langle \mathcal{O}*1(x_1)\cdots \mathcal{O}*n(x_n)\big\rangle*\Lambda
:= \int*{M*\Lambda} \mathcal{O}_1(x_1)\cdots \mathcal{O}*n(x_n),d\mu*\Lambda(U).
]

These are the Euclidean correlation functions to which the OS axioms apply.

Everything here is absolutely finite and smooth: (M_\Lambda) is compact, (\mu_\Lambda) has a smooth strictly positive density, and all observables are bounded and smooth. No integrability drama.

## 16.3. Time reflection and the positive-time algebra

The OS axioms require a **time-reflection** operation and a distinguished algebra of **positive-time observables**.

### 16.3.1. Time reflection (\theta) on spacetime

We choose the time reflection (\theta) about the time slice (t=0) (or equivalently about (t=T/2) with periodic time) by
[
\theta(t,\mathbf{x}) := (-t,\mathbf{x})\ (\mathrm{mod}\ T).
]

This acts on:

* **Sites**: ((t,\mathbf{x})\mapsto \theta(t,\mathbf{x})).
* **Temporal edges**: a link from ((t,\mathbf{x})) to ((t+1,\mathbf{x})) is mapped to the reversed temporal link from (\theta(t+1,\mathbf{x})) to (\theta(t,\mathbf{x})). On the gauge field we take
  [
  \theta(U)*{t,\mathbf{x},0} := U*{\theta(t+1,\mathbf{x}),0}^{-1}.
  ]
* **Spatial edges**: links in purely spatial directions are mapped without spatial reflection:
  [
  \theta(U)*{t,\mathbf{x},i} := U*{\theta(t,\mathbf{x}),i},\quad i=1,\dots,d.
  ]

This can be written more invariantly: (\theta) pulls back the configuration by time reversal and flips orientations in the time direction. The exact lattice indexing is slightly fussy but standard. 

We extend (\theta) to observables by:
[
(\theta\mathcal{O})(U) := \overline{\mathcal{O}(\theta U)}.
]
So (\theta) acts as a conjugate-linear involution on (\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}).

### 16.3.2. Positive-time region and algebra

Define the **strictly positive-time region**
[
\Lambda^+ := {(t,\mathbf{x})\in\Lambda : t>0}.
]

Let (\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^+\subset \mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) be the *-subalgebra generated by gauge-invariant local observables supported in (\Lambda^+). That is, each factor in the product depends only on link variables whose spacetime support lies entirely in (\Lambda^+).

Elements (F\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+) are (complex linear combinations of) finite products of such positive-time local observables. These are the “test functions at positive times” in the OS formalism.

## 16.4. Lattice OS axioms for Yang–Mills

We now write the OS axioms (in a finite-volume lattice version) and indicate their status for Wilson Yang–Mills.

Given (\mu_\Lambda), define the expectation functional
[
\langle F\rangle_\Lambda := \int_{M_\Lambda} F(U),d\mu_\Lambda(U),
\quad F\in L^1(\mu_\Lambda).
]

We implicitly restrict to (F\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}) (gauge-invariant sector).

### 16.4.1. OS0 — Regularity and symmetry

**Axiom OS0 (regularity & permutation symmetry)** (lattice version):

* For any gauge-invariant local observables (\mathcal{O}_1,\dots,\mathcal{O}_n) and spacetime points (x_1,\dots,x_n\in\Lambda), the Schwinger function
  [
  S_n^{(\Lambda)}(x_1,\dots,x_n) = \big\langle \mathcal{O}_1(x_1)\cdots \mathcal{O}*n(x_n)\big\rangle*\Lambda
  ]
  is a well-defined finite number.
* (S_n^{(\Lambda)}) is symmetric under permutations of the pairs ((x_j,\mathcal{O}_j)).

On a finite lattice with compact configuration space and smooth, bounded action, this is automatic: all relevant integrals converge and we can permute integrands freely.

### 16.4.2. OS1 — Euclidean invariance

Let (\tau) be a lattice Euclidean symmetry of (\Lambda): e.g. a translation, or a discrete rotation/reflection that preserves the lattice and the Wilson action. The measure (\mu_\Lambda) is built from a sum over plaquettes and product Haar measure, so for periodic boundary conditions it is invariant under the full lattice Euclidean group (and under discrete rotations/reflections preserving orientation, and under time translations).

**Axiom OS1 (Euclidean invariance)** (lattice version):
For such a symmetry (\tau),
[
S_n^{(\Lambda)}(x_1,\dots,x_n)
= S_n^{(\Lambda)}(\tau x_1,\dots,\tau x_n)
]
for all gauge-invariant local observables and points.

For the Wilson action, this is straightforward: (S_W(U)) and (d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U)) are invariant under relabeling links and plaquettes by lattice Euclidean symmetries, so the measure is invariant, and therefore so are the Schwinger functions.

### 16.4.3. OS2 — Reflection positivity

This is the main nontrivial OS axiom, and the heart of the connection to a Hilbert space and Hamiltonian.

**Axiom OS2 (reflection positivity)** (lattice version):
For all finite collections (F_1,\dots,F_n\in \mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{+}) and coefficients (c_1,\dots,c_n\in\mathbb{C}),
[
\sum*{i,j=1}^n \overline{c_i} c_j, \big\langle (\theta F_i), F_j \big\rangle_\Lambda ;\ge; 0.
]
Equivalently, the quadratic form
[
Q(F) := \langle (\theta F) F\rangle_\Lambda
]
is nonnegative for all (F\in \mathcal{A}*\Lambda^+):
[
\langle (\theta F) F \rangle*\Lambda ;\ge; 0.
]

Intuitively: the map
[
(F,G) \mapsto \langle (\theta F) G\rangle_\Lambda
]
is a positive semidefinite sesquilinear form on the positive-time algebra. This is what will become the inner product on the pre-Hilbert space of “positive-time wavefunctions”.

#### 16.4.3.1. Why Wilson’s action is reflection positive

One does not want to reprove the entire reflection positivity of Wilson gauge theory here; it’s a well-known and nontrivial result. But the structural reason is:

* The Wilson action can be decomposed as a sum of terms that either live entirely in positive time, entirely in negative time, or straddle the reflection plane in a way that can be written as a sum of squares;
* The product Haar measure is invariant under time reflection (\theta);
* When you integrate (e^{-S_W(U)}) against products of ((\theta F_i) F_j), the structure of the action and measure allows you to rewrite the integral as a sum of norms squared of certain functions of the positive-time configuration.

Put differently: the Boltzmann weight (e^{-S_W(U)} d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U)) can be represented (schematically) as
[
\int e^{-S_\Lambda(U)},d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U)
= \int \overline{\Phi(U^-)} \Phi(U^+),dU^-,dU^+,
]
where (U^\pm) are configurations on positive/negative time halves, and (\Phi) is a certain functional built from plaquettes across the reflection plane. This type of representation directly implies reflection positivity.

In the constructive QFT literature, this is done in detail for lattice gauge theories; we take it as known for Wilson pure-gauge action. Your own OS notes and Part IV source text record this as a standard fact in precisely this notation. 

**Important nuance:** if we add extra terms (S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}) (e.g. gauge-fixing) to the action, they must be chosen to **preserve** reflection positivity. Not all gauge-fixing terms do. For the purposes of this part, we stick with:

* Either the pure Wilson action (S_\Lambda = S_W),
* Or an effective action (S_\Lambda = S_W + S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}) with the explicit assumption that (e^{-S_{\mathrm{add},\Lambda}}) is reflection positive as a multiplication operator (e.g. local in time, symmetric under (\theta), and positive in the appropriate sense).

## 16.5. Finite-volume OS framework: summary of what we have

Collecting the above:

* We have a finite-dimensional compact configuration manifold (M_\Lambda = G^{E(\Lambda)}) with gauge fields on a discrete spacetime lattice;
* The Wilson action (S_W) is local, gauge-invariant, and Euclidean-invariant;
* The corresponding Gibbs measure (\mu_\Lambda) defines well-behaved Schwinger functions (S_n^{(\Lambda)}) on gauge-invariant local fields;
* Under time reflection (\theta) and positive-time algebra (\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+), the Wilson measure satisfies **reflection positivity** OS2;
* Euclidean invariance OS1 and regularity OS0 are automatic.

Thus:

> **Proposition 16.1 (Lattice OS axioms for Wilson Yang–Mills).**
> For each finite spacetime lattice (\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}*T\times\Lambda_S) and compact gauge group (G), the Wilson lattice gauge theory with measure
> [
> d\mu*\Lambda(U) = Z_\Lambda^{-1} e^{-S_W(U)} d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U)
> ]
> satisfies:
>
> * OS0 (regularity and permutation symmetry) for all gauge-invariant local observables,
> * OS1 (Euclidean invariance) under lattice translations and discrete rotations/reflections,
> * OS2 (reflection positivity) with respect to the time reflection (\theta) and positive-time algebra (\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^+),
>   when restricted to gauge-invariant local observables in (\mathcal{A}*\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}).

This is essentially the content of your original Part IV Section 2, wrapped into the new 20-part structure.

## 16.6. Why this matters: bridge to a finite-volume Hamiltonian

Given OS0–OS2, the standard OS reconstruction theorem (appropriately adapted to the lattice) allows us to build:

* A Hilbert space (\mathcal{H}_\Lambda) of states;
* A cyclic “vacuum” vector (\Omega_\Lambda);
* A self-adjoint Hamiltonian (H_\Lambda\ge 0);
* A representation of translations in Euclidean time:
  [
  U_\Lambda(t) = e^{-t H_\Lambda}, \quad t\in \mathbb{Z}_T,
  ]
* A correspondence between Schwinger functions and vacuum expectation values:
  [
  \big\langle \mathcal{O}*1(t_1)\cdots \mathcal{O}*n(t_n)\big\rangle*\Lambda
  = \langle \Omega*\Lambda,, \mathcal{O}*1(t_1)\cdots\mathcal{O}*n(t_n),\Omega*\Lambda\rangle*{\mathcal{H}_\Lambda},
  ]
  where (\mathcal{O}*i(t)) are operators in the Heisenberg picture generated by (H*\Lambda).

The spectral gap of (H_\Lambda),
[
\Delta_\Lambda := \inf\big( \sigma(H_\Lambda)\setminus{0}\big),
]
is the **finite-volume mass gap**. Exponential decay in Euclidean time of certain Schwinger functions is equivalent to (\Delta_\Lambda>0).

All of that is the content of **Part 17/20**, where the OS reconstruction is spelled out and the link between time-correlator decay and (\Delta_\Lambda) is made precise. For now, Part 16’s job is to guarantee the OS axioms are available so that the entire OS machinery can be invoked.

## 16.7. Relation to the curvature/functional-inequality framework

It’s worth emphasizing how this OS layer sits relative to the curvature-controlled Gibbs machinery of Parts 1–15:

* The Bakry–Émery analysis and functional inequalities live in **configuration space** ((M_\Lambda,\mu_\Lambda,L_\Lambda)). There, the key players are the Riemannian metric, the effective action (S_\Lambda), and curvature bounds (\operatorname{Ric}*{\mu*\Lambda}\ge \rho g_\Lambda) (locally and in physical directions).
* The OS framework lives in **spacetime** and constructs a **Hilbert space of states** and a Hamiltonian (H_\Lambda) starting from Schwinger functions of gauge-invariant observables.
* The two worlds are connected but not trivially identical: (L_\Lambda) and (H_\Lambda) are different operators with different spectra, and comparing them requires additional structure (that’s Part 18).

Part 16 establishes that, at least at fixed lattice spacing and finite volume, the Wilson lattice gauge theory is **OS-compatible**: it admits a Hamiltonian (H_\Lambda) whose spectral properties encode physical masses. Parts 17 and 18 then discuss how the curvature/functional-inequality control over (\mu_\Lambda) might constrain those spectral properties.

So from here on, you have two analytic “engines” running in parallel:

1. A **curvature–BE–FI engine** in configuration space ((L_\Lambda)),
2. An **OS–Schwinger–Hamiltonian engine** in spacetime ((H_\Lambda)).

The rest of Part IV is about engineering an interface between them sharp enough to say meaningful things about the Yang–Mills mass gap.
