# Part IV — Osterwalder–Schrader Analysis and Continuum Limit  
## Section 2 — Lattice OS Axioms for Wilson Yang–Mills

In this section we restate the Osterwalder–Schrader (OS) axioms in a form adapted to **lattice Yang–Mills** and recall the standard fact that the Wilson lattice gauge theory satisfies these axioms at **finite lattice spacing and finite volume**.

We work at a fixed lattice spacing \(a>0\) and on a finite Euclidean spacetime lattice with one distinguished (discrete) time direction. All fields and measures are purely finite-dimensional; the continuum limit is not considered here.

Our aim is not to reprove OS for Wilson’s action from scratch — that is a substantial body of known work — but to:

1. Make precise what the OS axioms look like on a lattice,
2. Identify the relevant objects (fields, Schwinger functions) in the Yang–Mills setting,
3. Record which axioms are known to hold for the Wilson action and why.

These facts will be used in later sections to define a finite-volume Hamiltonian \(H_\Lambda\) and to formulate spectral gap/mass gap questions.

---

## 2.1. Spacetime lattice and fields

### 2.1.1. Spacetime decomposition

Fix:

- A discrete time direction with sites \(t \in \mathbb{Z}_T := \{0,1,\dots,T-1\}\),
- A spatial lattice \(\Lambda_S \subset \mathbb{Z}^d\) (e.g. a finite box with periodic boundary conditions).

The full spacetime lattice is the Cartesian product
\[
\Lambda := \mathbb{Z}_T \times \Lambda_S.
\]

We will use indices \((t,\mathbf{x}) \in \Lambda\), where \(t\) is Euclidean time and \(\mathbf{x}\) is a spatial site.

### 2.1.2. Gauge fields on the spacetime lattice

On each oriented edge \((t,\mathbf{x};\mu)\) of \(\Lambda\) (with \(\mu = 0\) the time-direction, \(\mu = 1,\dots,d\) spatial directions), we place a link variable \(U_{t,\mathbf{x},\mu} \in G\), where \(G\) is a compact gauge group (e.g. \(SU(N)\)).

Let:

- \(E(\Lambda)\) be the set of oriented edges of \(\Lambda\),
- \(M_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)}\) be the configuration manifold of all link variables.

We equip \(M_\Lambda\) with the product Haar measure \(\prod dU_{t,\mathbf{x},\mu}\), and denote by \(S_\Lambda(U)\) the standard Wilson action (possibly supplemented by gauge-fixing terms, which must preserve reflection positivity if they are used; see below).

### 2.1.3. Gauge-invariant local observables

A **local observable** \(\mathcal{O}\) is a complex-valued function of a finite set of link variables. For example:

- Plaquette observables \(\operatorname{Tr} U_p\) associated with a plaquette \(p\),
- Wilson loops \(\operatorname{Tr} U_C\) for closed loops \(C\),
- Finite products and polynomial combinations of such.

We restrict attention to **gauge-invariant local observables**, i.e. those invariant under local gauge transformations \(g_{t,\mathbf{x}}\in G\) acting as
\[
U_{t,\mathbf{x},\mu} \mapsto g_{t,\mathbf{x}} U_{t,\mathbf{x},\mu} g_{t+\hat\mu,\mathbf{x}+\hat\mu}^{-1}.
\]

We denote by \(\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}\) the *-algebra generated by such gauge-invariant local observables.

## 2.2. Reflection and time ordering

To state the OS axioms, we need:

1. A notion of **time reflection** \(\theta\) acting on the spacetime lattice and on fields,
2. A notion of **positive-time algebra** \(\mathcal{A}^+_\Lambda\).

### 2.2.1. Time reflection \(\theta\)

Define the reflection about the time slice \(t = 0\) (or equivalently \(t = T/2\) with periodic time) by
\[
\theta(t,\mathbf{x}) := (-t, \mathbf{x}) \mod T.
\]

On link variables, \(\theta\) acts by pulling back under this map and inverting orientation appropriately. For example:

- For a temporal link from \((t,\mathbf{x})\) to \((t+1,\mathbf{x})\):
  \[
  \theta\big(U_{t,\mathbf{x},0}\big) = U_{\theta(t+1,\mathbf{x}),0}^{-1}.
  \]
- For a spatial link from \((t,\mathbf{x})\) to \((t,\mathbf{x}+\hat{i})\):
  \[
  \theta\big(U_{t,\mathbf{x},i}\big) = U_{\theta(t,\mathbf{x}),i}.
  \]

The precise definition depends on the orientation conventions, but the key is that \(\theta\) reverses the Euclidean time direction while acting as identity on spatial coordinates.

We extend \(\theta\) to observables by composition:
\[
(\theta\mathcal{O})(U) := \overline{\mathcal{O}(\theta U)},
\]
where complex conjugation enters to match the usual OS formulation (so that reflection acts as a conjugate-linear involution on the algebra of observables).

### 2.2.2. Positive-time subalgebra \(\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+\)

Define:

- The positive-time region
  \[
  \Lambda^+ := \{ (t,\mathbf{x}) \in \Lambda : t > 0 \},
  \]
- The algebra \(\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+\) generated by gauge-invariant local observables supported in \(\Lambda^+\).

Thus any \(F \in \mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+\) is a finite linear combination of products of local gauge-invariant observables each depending only on link variables with time coordinates \(t>0\).

## 2.3. Lattice OS axioms specialized to gauge-invariant observables

We now state the OS axioms in this lattice Yang–Mills context. For each finite \(\Lambda\), consider the expectation functional
\[
\langle \cdot \rangle_\Lambda := \int_{M_\Lambda} (\cdot)\, d\mu_\Lambda(U),
\]
where
\[
d\mu_\Lambda(U) = Z_\Lambda^{-1} e^{-S_\Lambda(U)} \prod_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)} dU_\ell.
\]

We will implicitly restrict attention to gauge-invariant observables in \(\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{inv}}\).

### 2.3.1. OS0 — Regularity & symmetry

The Schwinger functions
\[
S_n(x_1,\dots,x_n)
:= \langle \mathcal{O}_1(x_1)\cdots \mathcal{O}_n(x_n)\rangle_\Lambda
\]
are:

- Well-defined (finite) for all choices of gauge-invariant local observables \(\mathcal{O}_j\) and spacetime points \(x_j\),
- Symmetric under permutations of the pairs \((x_j,\mathcal{O}_j)\),
- Invariant under lattice Euclidean symmetries (translations and rotations that preserve the lattice), provided the action and measure are themselves invariant under these symmetries (true for Wilson’s action on periodic lattices).

These properties are straightforward consequences of the finite-dimensional, compact nature of \(M_\Lambda\), the smoothness of the action, and the symmetries of the Wilson action.

### 2.3.2. OS1 — Euclidean invariance

Let \(\tau\) be a lattice Euclidean symmetry (translation or discrete rotation/reflection that preserves the lattice and the action). Then
\[
\langle \mathcal{O}_1(x_1)\cdots \mathcal{O}_n(x_n)\rangle_\Lambda
= \langle \mathcal{O}_1(\tau x_1)\cdots \mathcal{O}_n(\tau x_n)\rangle_\Lambda.
\]

For Wilson’s action on a periodic lattice, Euclidean invariance holds because:

- The sum over plaquettes is invariant under permutations of lattice sites and directions,
- The Haar measure is invariant under re-labeling of edges.

We will not dwell on these symmetries, as they are standard; the key nontrivial OS axiom is **reflection positivity**.

### 2.3.3. OS2 — Reflection positivity

Reflection positivity is the core OS condition. In the lattice Yang–Mills setting, it takes the form:

> For any finite collection \(F_1,\dots,F_k \in \mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+\),
> \[
> \sum_{i,j=1}^k \overline{c_i} c_j \langle (\theta F_i) F_j \rangle_\Lambda \;\ge\; 0
> \quad \forall c_1,\dots,c_k \in \mathbb{C}.
> \]
> Equivalently,
> \[
> \langle (\theta F) F \rangle_\Lambda \ge 0 \quad\forall F\in\mathcal{A}_\Lambda^+.
> \]

Intuitively, this says the quadratic form defined by \((F,G) \mapsto \langle (\theta F) G \rangle_\Lambda\) is positive semidefinite on the positive-time algebra.

For Wilson’s lattice gauge theory, reflection positivity can be justified in two complementary ways:

- **Externally**, via established results in the constructive field theory literature (e.g. Osterwalder–Seiler and related work), which prove reflection positivity for the Wilson pure-gauge action using locality and gauge invariance;
- **Internally, in this project**, via the dedicated OS/Reflection-positivity proof documents (e.g. your `PROOF_12_OS_Reconstruction.md` and related OS review notes), which reconstruct the standard argument in the notation and conventions of this stack.

In both perspectives the key inputs are:

- The locality and gauge-invariant form of the action,
- The fact that plaquette terms can be grouped into contributions that are “entirely in \(\Lambda^+\)”, “entirely in \(\Lambda^-\)”, or “straddling the reflection plane” in a way that preserves positivity.

At a heuristic level, one can see this by writing the action as a sum of terms that are symmetric under \(\theta\) and checking that the Boltzmann factor \(e^{-S_\Lambda}\) is a sum/integral of squared moduli of holomorphic functionals; the proof documents in your stack make this precise in the lattice Yang–Mills setting.

### 2.3.4. OS3 — Cluster property (finite volume version)

In the infinite-volume continuum theory, the cluster property says that widely separated observables become uncorrelated. On a finite lattice, this must be interpreted in a controlled way.

At finite \(\Lambda\), one can formulate a **finite-volume cluster property** along the time direction:

> For local gauge-invariant observables \(A,B\) supported in disjoint time slabs, the truncated correlator
> \[
> \langle A(t) B(0) \rangle_\Lambda - \langle A(t)\rangle_\Lambda \langle B(0)\rangle_\Lambda
> \]
> decays as \(t\to\infty\) at a rate controlled by the spectral gap of the OS Hamiltonian \(H_\Lambda\).

This property is a consequence, rather than part, of the OS reconstruction once a spectral gap for \(H_\Lambda\) is known. For the purposes of this section, we treat cluster properties as a **goal** for later spectral analysis rather than as an input axiom; the key OS inputs are reflection positivity and Euclidean invariance.

## 2.4. Summary: OS axioms for lattice Yang–Mills

Collecting the above:

1. The finite-dimensional nature of \(M_\Lambda\), smoothness and positivity of \(e^{-S_\Lambda}\), and gauge invariance yield well-defined, symmetric, Euclidean invariant Schwinger functions for gauge-invariant local observables.

2. The Wilson action is known to be **reflection positive** with respect to time reflection \(\theta\), and the product Haar measure is invariant under \(\theta\). Thus OS2 holds for the finite-volume lattice Yang–Mills measure.

3. The combination of OS0–OS2 is sufficient to feed into the **OS reconstruction theorem**, which constructs a finite-volume Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}_\Lambda\), a vacuum state \(\Omega_\Lambda\), and a positive self-adjoint Hamiltonian \(H_\Lambda\), together with a transfer matrix interpretation.

4. Cluster properties (OS3 in the continuum) will be addressed in terms of spectral gaps for \(H_\Lambda\) in later sections; they are not additional axioms in the finite-volume lattice setting.

From the perspective of the mass-gap program, this section establishes that:

- At fixed lattice spacing and finite volume, **Wilson lattice Yang–Mills has the correct OS structure** to define a finite-volume Hamiltonian \(H_\Lambda\),
- The analytic work of Parts I–III can be viewed as providing **configuration-space controls** (via curvature and functional inequalities) that may influence, but do not yet determine, the spectral properties of this OS Hamiltonian.

In the next section we recall the OS reconstruction theorem in the lattice context and relate exponential decay of Euclidean-time Schwinger functions to the spectral gap of \(H_\Lambda\).
