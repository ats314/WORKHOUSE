Title: Uniform Convexity and Spectral Gap for a Lattice \(\phi^4\) Scalar Field via Bakry–Émery Theory

**Abstract**

We consider a real scalar \(\phi^4\) field on a finite four‑dimensional periodic lattice with strictly positive quadratic mass term. We prove that the lattice Euclidean action is uniformly strictly convex with a configuration‑independent Hessian lower bound \(\rho_* = m_0^2\). For the associated Langevin generator \(L_\Lambda = \Delta - \nabla S_\Lambda\cdot\nabla\), Bochner’s identity yields a Bakry–Émery curvature‑dimension condition \(CD(\rho_*,\infty)\). Standard Bakry–Émery theory then implies sharp Poincaré and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities for the Gibbs measure, with constants \(1/\rho_*\) and \(2/\rho_*\), respectively. As a consequence, the self‑adjoint operator \(H_\Lambda = -L_\Lambda\) on \(L^2(\mu_\Lambda)\) has a nonzero spectral gap bounded below by \(\rho_* = m_0^2\), uniformly in the lattice volume. This establishes a dynamic mass gap for the scalar model and provides the prototype scheme to be reused for lattice Yang–Mills.

---

## 1. Lattice scalar field and Gibbs measure

Let \(\Lambda_L \subset \mathbb{Z}^4\) be a finite hypercubic lattice with periodic boundary conditions. A real scalar field configuration is
\[
\phi = (\phi_x)_{x\in\Lambda_L} \in \mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}.
\]

The Euclidean action is
\[
S_\Lambda(\phi) \;=\; \sum_{x\in \Lambda_L} \left(
  \frac{m_0^2}{2}\,\phi_x^2 + \frac{\lambda}{4}\,\phi_x^4
\right)
+ \frac{\kappa}{2} \sum_{\langle x,y\rangle} (\phi_x - \phi_y)^2,
\]
with parameters
\[
m_0^2>0,\qquad \lambda>0,\qquad \kappa\ge 0.
\]

The associated Gibbs measure on \(\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\) is
\[
d\mu_\Lambda(\phi) = Z_\Lambda^{-1} e^{-S_\Lambda(\phi)} \prod_{x\in\Lambda_L} d\phi_x,
\]
where \(Z_\Lambda\) is the normalizing partition function. Since \(S_\Lambda(\phi)\to +\infty\) as \(\|\phi\|\to\infty\) and \(S_\Lambda\) is continuous, \(Z_\Lambda<\infty\) and \(\mu_\Lambda\) is a well‑defined probability measure.

We denote by \(\nabla S_\Lambda\) and \(\nabla^2 S_\Lambda\) the gradient and Hessian with respect to the standard Euclidean structure on \(\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\), and by \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\) and \(\|\cdot\|\) the corresponding inner product and norm.

---

## 2. Uniform convexity: Hessian lower bound

We decompose the action into on‑site and interaction parts:
\[
S_\Lambda(\phi) = \sum_{x\in\Lambda_L} V(\phi_x) + K(\phi),
\]
where
\[
V(\phi_x) = \frac{m_0^2}{2}\phi_x^2 + \frac{\lambda}{4}\phi_x^4,
\qquad
K(\phi) = \frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle} (\phi_x - \phi_y)^2.
\]

### Lemma 1 (Uniform Hessian lower bound)

For all \(\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\) and all \(v\in\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\),
\[
\langle v, \nabla^2 S_\Lambda(\phi)\, v\rangle \;\ge\; \rho_* \|v\|^2,
\qquad \rho_* = m_0^2.
\]

**Proof.**

1. **On‑site potential.**  
   For each site \(x\),
   \[
   V'(\phi_x) = m_0^2 \phi_x + \lambda \phi_x^3,\qquad
   V''(\phi_x) = m_0^2 + 3\lambda \phi_x^2.
   \]
   Since \(\lambda>0\) and \(\phi_x^2\ge 0\),
   \[
   V''(\phi_x) \;\ge\; m_0^2
   \quad\text{for all } \phi_x\in\mathbb{R}.
   \]

2. **Interaction term.**  
   The interaction is quadratic in \(\phi\):
   \[
   K(\phi) = \frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle} (\phi_x - \phi_y)^2.
   \]
   Its Hessian is independent of \(\phi\) and coincides (up to \(\kappa\)) with the graph Laplacian on \(\Lambda_L\). For any \(v\),
   \[
   \langle v, \nabla^2 K(\phi)\, v\rangle
   = \kappa \sum_{\langle x,y\rangle} (v_x - v_y)^2 \;\ge\; 0,
   \]
   since each term \((v_x - v_y)^2\) is non‑negative and \(\kappa\ge 0\).

3. **Total Hessian.**  
   The Hessian of \(S_\Lambda\) is the sum of the Hessians of its components. Therefore,
   \[
   \begin{aligned}
   \langle v, \nabla^2 S_\Lambda(\phi)\, v\rangle
   &= \sum_{x\in\Lambda_L} V''(\phi_x)\, v_x^2
      + \langle v, \nabla^2 K(\phi)\, v\rangle \\[0.3em]
   &\ge \sum_{x\in\Lambda_L} m_0^2 v_x^2 \\[0.3em]
   &= m_0^2 \sum_{x\in\Lambda_L} v_x^2
    = m_0^2 \|v\|^2.
   \end{aligned}
   \]
   This bound is uniform in \(\phi\) and in the lattice size. ∎

Thus \(S_\Lambda\) is uniformly strictly convex with modulus \(\rho_* = m_0^2\).

## 3. Bakry–Émery curvature and functional inequalities

### 3.1 Langevin generator and carré du champ

Consider the Langevin generator \(L_\Lambda\) acting on smooth \(f:\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\to\mathbb{R}\):
\[
L_\Lambda f = \Delta f - \nabla S_\Lambda \cdot \nabla f,
\]
where \(\Delta\) is the standard Laplacian on \(\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\).

The carré du champ operator \(\Gamma\) and its iterated version \(\Gamma_2\) are defined by
\[
\Gamma(f) = \frac12\big(L_\Lambda(f^2) - 2 f L_\Lambda f\big) = |\nabla f|^2,
\]
\[
\Gamma_2(f) = \frac12 L_\Lambda \Gamma(f) - \Gamma(f,L_\Lambda f),
\]
where \(\Gamma(f,g) = \tfrac12(L_\Lambda(fg) - f L_\Lambda g - g L_\Lambda f)\).

For generators of the form \(L = \Delta - \nabla S\cdot\nabla\) on Euclidean space, Bochner’s identity yields
\[
\Gamma_2(f)
 = \|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
   + \langle \nabla^2 S\, \nabla f, \nabla f\rangle,
\]
where \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\) denotes the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the Hessian \(\nabla^2 f\).

### 3.2 Curvature–dimension condition

**Proposition 2 (Bakry–Émery \(CD(\rho_*,\infty)\)).**  
With \(\rho_* = m_0^2\) as in Lemma 1, for all smooth \(f\),
\[
\Gamma_2(f) \;\ge\; \rho_*\, \Gamma(f).
\]

**Proof.**

By Bochner’s identity applied to \(S_\Lambda\),
\[
\Gamma_2(f)
 = \|\nabla^2 f\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
   + \langle \nabla^2 S_\Lambda\, \nabla f, \nabla f\rangle.
\]
The first term is non‑negative. Applying Lemma 1 with \(v = \nabla f\) gives
\[
\langle \nabla^2 S_\Lambda\, \nabla f, \nabla f\rangle
\;\ge\; m_0^2 \|\nabla f\|^2
\;=\; \rho_* \Gamma(f).
\]
Hence
\[
\Gamma_2(f) \;\ge\; \rho_* \Gamma(f).
\]
Thus the curvature–dimension condition \(CD(\rho_*,\infty)\) holds. ∎

### 3.3 Poincaré and log‑Sobolev inequalities

Let \(P_t = e^{tL_\Lambda}\) be the Markov semigroup generated by \(L_\Lambda\). The measure \(\mu_\Lambda\) is invariant and reversible for \(P_t\), and the \(CD(\rho_*,\infty)\) condition controls the decay of \(\Gamma(P_t f)\) in time.

**Theorem 3 (Poincaré and log‑Sobolev inequalities).**  
The Gibbs measure \(\mu_\Lambda\) satisfies, for all smooth \(f\):

1. **Poincaré inequality**
   \[
   \mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)
   \;\le\; \frac{1}{\rho_*} \int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda.
   \]

2. **Log‑Sobolev inequality**
   \[
   \mathrm{Ent}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f^2)
   \;\le\; \frac{2}{\rho_*} \int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda,
   \]
   where \(\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu_\Lambda}(g) = \int g\log g\, d\mu_\Lambda - \big(\int g\, d\mu_\Lambda\big)\log\big(\int g\, d\mu_\Lambda\big)\).

**Proof (standard Bakry–Émery argument, sketched).**

Under \(CD(\rho_*,\infty)\), one has for all smooth \(f\),
\[
\frac{d}{dt} \int \Gamma(P_t f)\, d\mu_\Lambda
= -2 \int \Gamma_2(P_t f)\, d\mu_\Lambda
\le -2\rho_* \int \Gamma(P_t f)\, d\mu_\Lambda.
\]
Gronwall’s lemma yields
\[
\int \Gamma(P_t f)\, d\mu_\Lambda
\le e^{-2\rho_* t} \int \Gamma(f)\, d\mu_\Lambda.
\]

1. **Poincaré.**  
   The variance along the semigroup satisfies
   \[
   \frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(P_t f)
   = -2 \int \Gamma(P_t f)\, d\mu_\Lambda.
   \]
   Integrating from \(t=0\) to \(t=\infty\), using \(\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(P_t f)\to 0\) as \(t\to\infty\) and the bound on \(\Gamma(P_t f)\), gives
   \[
   \mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)
   = 2\int_0^\infty \int \Gamma(P_t f)\, d\mu_\Lambda\, dt
   \le 2\int_0^\infty e^{-2\rho_* t}\, dt \int \Gamma(f)\, d\mu_\Lambda
   = \frac{1}{\rho_*} \int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda.
   \]

2. **Log‑Sobolev.**  
   Similarly, for \(g_t = P_t(f^2)\), the entropy satisfies
   \[
   \frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu_\Lambda}(g_t)
   = -4\int \Gamma(\sqrt{g_t})\, d\mu_\Lambda.
   \]
   Using \(CD(\rho_*,\infty)\) and the same decay estimate for \(\Gamma(P_t \sqrt{g})\) yields
   \[
   \mathrm{Ent}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f^2)
   \le \frac{2}{\rho_*} \int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda.
   \]
   The details are standard in Bakry–Émery theory and use only the \(CD(\rho_*,\infty)\) condition and reversibility. ∎

## 4. Dynamic mass gap: spectral gap of the Langevin generator

Define the operator
\[
H_\Lambda = -L_\Lambda
\]
on \(L^2(\mu_\Lambda)\), with domain containing smooth, compactly supported functions (which are dense).

### 4.1 Self‑adjointness and spectrum

For smooth \(f,g\),
\[
\int g\, L_\Lambda f\, d\mu_\Lambda
= \int g(\Delta f - \nabla S_\Lambda\cdot\nabla f)\, e^{-S_\Lambda} d\phi.
\]
Integrating by parts in \(\mathbb{R}^{|\Lambda_L|}\) and using the weight \(e^{-S_\Lambda}\) shows
\[
\int g\, L_\Lambda f\, d\mu_\Lambda
= -\int \nabla g\cdot\nabla f\, d\mu_\Lambda
= \int f\, L_\Lambda g\, d\mu_\Lambda,
\]
so \(L_\Lambda\) is symmetric in \(L^2(\mu_\Lambda)\), and \(H_\Lambda = -L_\Lambda\) is symmetric and non‑negative:
\[
\int f H_\Lambda f\, d\mu_\Lambda
= \int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda \;\ge\; 0.
\]

Since the underlying space is finite‑dimensional and \(\mu_\Lambda\) has a smooth, strictly positive density with respect to Lebesgue measure, \(H_\Lambda\) has compact resolvent; hence its spectrum is discrete, with eigenvalues
\[
0 = \lambda_0 < \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \uparrow \infty.
\]
The eigenvalue \(\lambda_0 = 0\) corresponds to constant functions, which are invariant under \(P_t\).

### 4.2 Spectral gap from the Poincaré inequality

**Corollary 4 (Spectral gap).**  
The first non‑zero eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\) of \(H_\Lambda\) satisfies
\[
\lambda_1 \;\ge\; \rho_* = m_0^2.
\]

**Proof.**

The Rayleigh–Ritz variational characterization of \(\lambda_1\) is
\[
\lambda_1
= \inf_{\substack{f\in L^2(\mu_\Lambda)\\ f\not\equiv \text{const}}}
\frac{\int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda}{\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)}.
\]
By the Poincaré inequality from Theorem 3, for all \(f\),
\[
\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)
\le \frac{1}{\rho_*} \int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda,
\]
or equivalently,
\[
\frac{\int |\nabla f|^2\, d\mu_\Lambda}{\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_\Lambda}(f)}
\;\ge\; \rho_*.
\]
Taking the infimum over non‑constant \(f\) yields
\[
\lambda_1 \;\ge\; \rho_* = m_0^2.
\]
Thus \(H_\Lambda\) has a non‑zero spectral gap bounded below by \(m_0^2\), uniformly in the lattice volume. ∎

## 5. Summary of the logical pattern

The derivation follows the chain:
\[
\text{Uniform Hessian lower bound } (\nabla^2 S_\Lambda \ge \rho_* I)
\;\Longrightarrow\; CD(\rho_*,\infty)
\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{Poincaré/Log‑Sobolev}
\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{Spectral gap } \lambda_1 \ge \rho_*.
\]

In the present scalar \(\phi^4\) lattice model, the uniform convexity constant is \(\rho_* = m_0^2\), yielding a dynamic mass gap of at least \(m_0^2\) for the Langevin dynamics. This scheme is fully rigorous in the finite‑volume setting and is designed to be reused, mutatis mutandis, for the analysis of lattice Yang–Mills configuration spaces.
