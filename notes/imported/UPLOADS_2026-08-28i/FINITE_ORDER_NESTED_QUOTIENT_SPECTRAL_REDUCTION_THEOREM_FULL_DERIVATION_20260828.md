## Abstract

Several results that initially appear to be separate—the finite temporal-history construction, the rooted scalar transform, traceless shape compression, the primitive \(N^{-(2r-1)}\) coefficient staircase, the all-rank shared-link hopping law, and the cubic \(X/M/P/R\) reconstruction—are parts of one hierarchy.

The common theorem is a fixed-order reduction statement. Under locality, finite-dimensional physical retained space, finite reachable Haar/Gram quotient, nonresonant reduced resolvents, and complete support bookkeeping, every connected order-\(r\) effective Hamiltonian is determined by a finite graph of energy-decorated temporal histories. The assembled history data then admit two independent, non-equivalent readout maps:

\[
\mathscr D_r
\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal R_r\ }
\text{rooted connected scalar},
\qquad
\mathscr D_r
\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal S_r^{\mathrm{spec}}\ }
\text{scalar-free spectral/operator shape}.
\]

The primitive staircase and the all-rank shared-link law are not the same coefficient formula. They are different evaluations on different subgraphs of the common temporal-history construction. Their common large-\(N\) scaling arises from the same attachment-plus-resolvent mechanism. The cubic invariant formula is a further symmetry reduction of the shape branch, while rooted Möbius inversion is the canonical connected transform of the scalar branch. The \(SU(3)\) shared-link corollary now has two concrete cube benchmarks: an author-data-derived \(B=4=T_1\) full-Hamiltonian reconstruction exhibits the negative-hopping \(A_1^{--}\oplus T_1^{+-}\oplus E^{--}\) shell and reproduces the published exact ground-state curve, while a direct \(B=6\) reachable-state reconstruction yields \((39/68)I+(5/612)G\) and observes the predicted ordering reversal after the \(\mathbf6\) and \(\mathbf8\) channels are restored.

This document proves the umbrella theorem and each corollary, states the extra hypotheses required by each, and preserves the distinction between analytic derivation, exact current-repository verification, output-certified evidence, disputed identification, and T3 imported assertions.

# I. Setting and hypotheses

## 1. Physical retained space

Let

\[
H(u)=H_0+uV,
\qquad
V=\sum_f V_f,
\]

where \(H_0\) is the electric Hamiltonian and each \(V_f\) is supported on a local face or bounded elementary cell.

Choose the actual gauge-invariant electric eigenspace

\[
H_0P=E_0P,
\qquad
Q=I-P,
\qquad
d=\dim P<\infty.
\]

The space \(P\mathcal H\) is selected before cellular compression. If two face types have different electric energies, they do not belong to the same physical retained band merely because they occur in one cellular complex.

The canonical magnetic convention in the associated master is

\[
H=H_0-uW,
\qquad
W=\sum_f
\left(
\operatorname{Tr}U_f+\operatorname{Tr}U_f^\dagger
\right).
\]

Writing \(V=-W\) converts this to \(H_0+uV\). Odd-order coefficients reverse if a source instead uses \(H_0+uW\); even-order coefficients do not.

## 2. Cellular and support structures

Let

\[
C_3\xrightarrow{\partial_3}C_2\xrightarrow{\partial_2}C_1,
\qquad
\partial_2\partial_3=0,
\]

be the relevant oriented cellular chain complex.

For the retained face coordinates, write

\[
Z_2=\ker\partial_2,
\qquad
\mathcal B_2=\operatorname{im}\partial_3,
\qquad
\mathcal H_2=Z_2\cap\ker\partial_3^\dagger.
\]

Finite-dimensional Hodge decomposition gives

\[
Z_2=\mathcal B_2\oplus\mathcal H_2.
\]

Separately, equip the set of local magnetic supports with the adjacency relation generated by overlap on an elementary gauge degree of freedom. For a finite root support \(R_0\), let

\[
\operatorname{Star}_r(R_0)
\]

be its radius-\(r\) support neighborhood.

## 3. Exact local physical quotient

On a bounded support, choose reachable Wilson-network vectors
\(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) and define the synthesis map

\[
J:\mathbb C^m\longrightarrow\mathcal H,
\qquad
Je_i=w_i.
\]

Its exact Haar Gram matrix is

\[
G=J^\dagger J,
\qquad
G_{ij}=\langle w_i,w_j\rangle.
\]

The kernel of \(G\) is a subspace of coefficient space, not a subspace of
the already realized Hilbert span. Since
\(\|Jc\|^2=c^\dagger Gc\), one has
\(\ker J=\ker G\). The physical local coordinate space is therefore

\[
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{phys}}
{}={}
\mathbb C^m/\ker G
\cong
\operatorname{ran}J.
\]

Every reduced resolvent and transition operator below acts on this quotient,
not on a syntactically distinct but Gram-null Wilson coordinate family.

## 4. Reduced resolvent and proper returns

Assume that every energy block reached in \(Q\mathcal H\) has a well-defined reduced resolvent at \(E_0\). Equivalently, if a state is degenerate with \(P\), it must be incorporated into \(P\) rather than propagated through \(Q\).

Define

\[
R=Q(E_0-H_0)^{-1}Q.
\]

A direct history has no proper intermediate return to \(P\). A history that returns to \(P\) before its final step is not propagated through \(R\); it generates an energy derivative, a higher resolvent power, or another Feshbach fold term.

## 5. Complete fixed-order hypotheses

The umbrella theorem uses the following assumptions:

1. \(V\) is local and the support graph is locally finite.
2. The root support \(R_0\) is finite.
3. The perturbative order \(r\) is fixed.
4. \(P\) is finite-dimensional.
5. Every reachable bounded-support Wilson span is finite after the exact Gram quotient.
6. Reduced resolvents are nonresonant on the declared \(Q\) blocks.
7. Proper returns to \(P\) are represented by the complete fixed-order Feshbach fold labels.
8. Vacuum and disconnected factors are removed using support-resolved linked bookkeeping.
9. Products of support kernels are combined by ordered support-union convolution before rooted subtraction.
10. For the scalar branch, the inclusive coefficient is known on the complete finite poset of rooted connected supports.
11. An energy-independent effective-Hamiltonian convention and retained-space
    normalization are fixed. Throughout this document,
    \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) means the canonical Hermitian/des-Cloizeaux
    representative. A different retained-space similarity gauge may change
    matrix coefficients while preserving the perturbative spectrum.

No coefficient corollary follows from these hypotheses alone. The theorem guarantees a finite exact construction and a canonical output decomposition; each explicit coefficient requires additional group-theoretic, geometric, or channel assumptions.

# II. The finite temporal-history construction

## 6. Ordered histories

An order-\(r\) history is an ordered word

\[
h=(f_1,\sigma_1;\ldots;f_r,\sigma_r)
\]

together with all representation, multiplicity, intertwiner, support, and fold data required to identify every intermediate physical quotient state.

It produces a path

\[
q_0\longrightarrow q_1\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow q_r.
\]

For a direct history,

\[
\mathcal A_N[h]
{}={}
\langle q_r|
V R_{r-1}V\cdots V R_1V
|q_0\rangle,
\]

where each \(R_j\) is the exact reduced resolvent on the physical quotient block reached after step \(j\).

Two histories with the same final signed face chain need not have the same amplitude:

\[
\mathcal A_N[h]\neq \mathcal A_N[\pi(h)].
\]

The time order can change the intermediate representation and therefore the Casimir denominator. Static cellular support filters the candidates; it does not replace the temporal calculation.

## 7. Lemma: bounded-support completeness

**Lemma 1.** Every rooted connected order-\(r\) contribution is contained in

\[
\operatorname{Star}_r(R_0).
\]

**Proof.** After disconnected vacuum factors are removed, the union of the root and all inserted supports is connected. A support at graph distance \(m\) from \(R_0\) requires at least \(m\) inserted supports along a connecting path. An order-\(r\) history has only \(r\) insertions, so \(m\le r\). Therefore no connected order-\(r\) history can leave \(\operatorname{Star}_r(R_0)\). \(\square\)

Because the support graph is locally finite and \(R_0\) is finite, \(\operatorname{Star}_r(R_0)\) is finite.

## 8. Lemma: Gram-null decoupling

For an electric block \(\lambda\), let
\(J_\lambda:\mathbb C^{m_\lambda}\to\mathcal H_\lambda\) be its synthesis
map and \(G_\lambda=J_\lambda^\dagger J_\lambda\). Let
\(L_\lambda:P\mathcal H\to\mathbb C^{m_\lambda}\) contain synthesis
coefficients for the retained source, so the physical source map is

\[
S_\lambda=J_\lambda L_\lambda.
\]

Define \(C_\lambda=L_\lambda^\dagger\). Then the induced retained-space
source kernel is

\[
K_\lambda
{}={}
S_\lambda^\dagger S_\lambda
{}={}
C_\lambda G_\lambda C_\lambda^\dagger.
\]

**Lemma 2.** \(K_\lambda\) is positive semidefinite, Gram-null directions contribute zero, and \(K_\lambda\) is invariant under changes of raw Wilson spanning family that represent the same physical quotient.

**Proof.** Since \(G_\lambda\ge0\), choose \(G_\lambda^{1/2}\) and write

\[
K_\lambda
{}={}
\left(C_\lambda G_\lambda^{1/2}\right)
\left(C_\lambda G_\lambda^{1/2}\right)^\dagger
\ge0.
\]

If \(v\in\ker G_\lambda\), then \(J_\lambda v=0\) and
\(G_\lambda^{1/2}v=0\), so every source contraction through \(v\) vanishes.
A change of raw spanning family changes \(J_\lambda\) and \(L_\lambda\)
contragrediently while leaving
\(S_\lambda=J_\lambda L_\lambda\) fixed; hence it leaves
\(K_\lambda=S_\lambda^\dagger S_\lambda\) fixed. This formula assumes
\(C_\lambda\) stores synthesis coefficients. If a ledger instead stores
overlaps \(S_\lambda^\dagger J_\lambda\), the coordinate contraction must use
the Moore--Penrose inverse \(G_\lambda^+\). \(\square\)

## 9. Lemma: exact history merging

Augment a partial history state by:

- its physical Gram-quotient vector;
- its current rooted support;
- its insertion depth;
- its proper-return and fold multi-index;
- any unresolved representation or multiplicity label required by future transitions.

Equivalently, a graph vertex may be written

\[
\nu=(j,\mathcal C,a,\kappa),
\]

where \(j\) is the insertion depth, \(\mathcal C\) the accumulated labeled
support, \(a\) a basis label in the exact physical Gram quotient, and
\(\kappa\) the finite fold label. A local insertion supplies a weighted edge

\[
(j,\mathcal C,a,\kappa)
\longrightarrow
(j+1,\mathcal C\cup\operatorname{supp}f,b,\kappa').
\]

Its weight is the exact transition matrix element, followed at a nonfinal
\(Q\)-space cut by the appropriate reduced-resolvent block.

**Lemma 3.** If two partial histories have identical augmented physical state data, their future continuations act identically apart from the amplitude already accumulated. Their amplitudes may therefore be added exactly.

**Proof.** Every continuation is a composition of the same local transition maps, reduced resolvents, support-union maps, and fold updates. These maps depend on the current physical quotient vector and retained metadata, not on the syntactic word that reached it. Linearity permits exact addition. \(\square\)

## 10. Feshbach construction

For an eigenvalue parameter \(E\), block elimination gives

\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}(E,u)
{}={}
PHP
+
PHQ(E-QHQ)^{-1}QHP.
\]

Indeed, if \(\psi=\psi_P+\psi_Q\), the \(Q\) equation yields

\[
\psi_Q=(E-QHQ)^{-1}QHP\,\psi_P.
\]

Substitution into the \(P\) equation gives the displayed effective operator.

Write

\[
R_E=(E-QH_0Q)^{-1}Q,
\qquad
\delta E=E-E_0.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
(E-QHQ)^{-1}Q
&=
\left(E-QH_0Q-uQVQ\right)^{-1}Q\\
&=
R_E
\sum_{n\ge0}
\left(uQVQ\,R_E\right)^n,
\end{aligned}
\]

and

\[
R_E
{}={}
\sum_{m\ge0}
(-\delta E)^mR^{m+1}.
\]

In the first display, the \(n=0\) term is \(R_E\); equivalently one may
write \(R_E\sum_{n\ge0}(uQVQ\,R_E)^n\). Because the two external
\(P\)-to-\(Q\) vertices already contribute \(u^2\), the coefficient of
\(u^r\) uses only finitely many \(n\), finitely many \(m\), and finitely many
compositions from

\[
\delta E=\sum_{\ell\ge1}u^\ell E_\ell.
\]

Thus expanding the resolvent in \(u\) and \(E-E_0\) produces:

- direct \(Q\)-space histories;
- derivatives with respect to \(E\);
- higher powers of reduced resolvents;
- products of lower-order kernels;
- Hermitian normalization terms.

For example, in the canonical Hermitian/des-Cloizeaux representative, if
\(PVP=aP\), the fourth-order scalar-band coefficient can be written

\[
H_4
{}={}
D_4-a(E_L+E_R)+a^2F-\frac12\{B,D_2\},
\]

where

\[
D_4=PVRVRVRVP,
\]

\[
E_L=PVR^2VRVP,
\qquad
E_R=PVRVR^2VP,
\]

\[
F=PVR^3VP,
\qquad
B=PVRVP,
\qquad
D_2=PVR^2VP.
\]

The terms containing \(R^2\), \(R^3\), and products of lower kernels are not optional corrections. They are the representation of proper retained-space returns in the energy-independent effective operator.

## 11. Support-union convolution

Let \(K_A\) and \(K_B\) be support-resolved kernels. Their product is assigned to the actual support union:

\[
(K_A\star K_B)(C)
{}={}
\sum_{A\cup B=C}^{\mathrm{ordered}}
K_A(A)K_B(B).
\]

The convolution is ordered because operator products need not commute. Rooted subtraction is performed only after every product has been assigned to its full support union. Subtracting first can misclassify a connected folded term as a product of smaller clusters.

# III. Umbrella theorem

## 12. Finite-Order Nested-Quotient Spectral Reduction Theorem

**Theorem.** Under hypotheses 1–11 of Section 5, the connected order-\(r\)
canonical Hermitian/des-Cloizeaux effective Hamiltonian is determined exactly
by a finite energy-decorated quotient graph. It has the form

\[
\boxed{
H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(r)}
{}={}
\sum_{[h]\in\mathscr H_r^{\mathrm{phys}}}
\mathcal A_N[h]\,T_h
+
F_r,
}
\]

where:

- \(\mathscr H_r^{\mathrm{phys}}\) is a finite set of merged physical history classes;
- \(\mathcal A_N[h]\) is the exact Haar/Gram/resolvent amplitude;
- \(T_h\) is the retained-space endpoint operator;
- \(F_r\) is the complete order-\(r\) fold contribution assembled by ordered support-union convolution.

For every complete support-resolved coefficient, the assembled history data \(\mathscr D_r\) admit two independent canonical readouts:

\[
\mathcal R_r(\mathscr D_r)
{}={}
\mu_{\mathrm{root}}
\left[
\frac1d\operatorname{tr}_P A_r(C)
\right],
\]

\[
\mathcal S_r^{\mathrm{spec}}(\mathscr D_r)
{}={}
\left[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(r)}
\right]_{\mathcal A_r/\mathbb C I}.
\]

Here \(\mathcal A_r\) is the finite retained-operator space, or the
finite-range periodic Bloch-operator space, appropriate to the problem. The
quotient removes one \(k\)-independent scalar identity. In a finite
non-Bloch retained space its canonical representative is the traceless
compression \(M_P(H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(r)})\). In a periodic problem it also
retains scalar momentum dependence. The scalar readout is obtained by rooted
Möbius inversion of the inclusive scalar support data. No direct-sum identity
between these two different codomains is asserted.

### Proof

By Lemma 1, every connected history lies in the finite support neighborhood \(\operatorname{Star}_r(R_0)\). By hypothesis, each reachable Wilson span on this neighborhood becomes finite-dimensional after quotienting by its exact Gram kernel.

At fixed \(r\), only finitely many insertion depths, support unions, representation labels, and Feshbach derivative or resolvent-power labels can occur. Lemma 3 merges syntactically different partial words that reach the same augmented physical state. The resulting energy-decorated quotient graph therefore has finitely many vertices and edges.

The Feshbach identity in Section 10 expands the exact effective operator into direct graph paths plus the finite family of proper-return folds. Lemma 2 removes every Gram-null contribution and makes the amplitudes independent of the chosen raw Wilson spanning family. Ordered support-union convolution assigns each product term to its true support before connected subtraction. This proves the finite formula for \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(r)}\).

For a support-resolved assembled operator \(A_r(C)\) on \(P\mathcal H\), write

\[
s_r(C)
{}={}
\frac{1}{d}\operatorname{tr}_P A_r(C),
\]

\[
\Phi_r(C)
{}={}
A_r(C)-s_r(C)P.
\]

Then

\[
A_r(C)=s_r(C)P+\Phi_r(C),
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}_P\Phi_r(C)=0.
\]

This is the unique decomposition

\[
\operatorname{End}(P\mathcal H)
{}={}
\mathbb C P
\oplus
\mathfrak{sl}(P\mathcal H).
\]

The inclusive scalar family \(s_r(C)\), after vacuum subtraction and complete
fold assembly, is a function on the finite rooted-support poset. The zeta
transform on that poset is invertible, so rooted Möbius inversion produces a
unique connected scalar family. Quotienting by \(\mathbb C I\) annihilates
one global scalar shift, whereas the rooted trace also receives information
from scalar support terms that have no scalar-free image. Conversely,
nonconstant or traceless operator shape can survive with zero rooted scalar.
Hence the scalar and spectral-shape outputs are independent and canonical.
\(\square\)

## 13. The two non-equivalent readout maps

Rooted subtraction and scalar-free spectral projection act on different
coordinates of the assembled support data:

\[
\mathcal R_r(\mathscr D_r)
{}={}
\mu_{\mathrm{root}}
\left[
\frac{1}{d}\operatorname{tr}_P A_r(C)
\right],
\]

\[
\mathcal S_r^{\mathrm{spec}}(\mathscr D_r)
{}={}
[A_r]_{\mathcal A_r/\mathbb C I}.
\]

For a finite non-Bloch retained space, the quotient has the unique traceless
representative

\[
M_P(A_r)
{}={}
PA_rP
{}-{}
\frac{\operatorname{tr}_P(PA_rP)}{d}P.
\]

A global scalar re-anchoring

\[
A_r\mapsto A_r+\delta P
\]

changes the rooted scalar coordinate but leaves
\(\mathcal S_r^{\mathrm{spec}}\) unchanged. A traceless or momentum-dependent
perturbation can change spectral shape while contributing zero to a declared
rooted rest scalar. Therefore neither output determines the other.

## 14. Shape is not yet transport

If

\[
M_{P(k)}\!\left(H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(r)}(k)\right)\neq0,
\]

the order-\(r\) operator is non-scalar in the retained fiber. This proves shape splitting. It does not by itself prove transport: a momentum-independent onsite multiplet splitting can be traceless.

The first dispersive order is

\[
r_{\mathrm{disp}}
{}={}
\min\left\{
r:
\operatorname{Spec}
\left[
P(k)H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(r)}(k)P(k)
\right]
\text{ depends on }k
\right\}.
\]

On a periodic system with \(d\) retained orbitals per cell, the real-space
finite-range kernel has the Bloch decomposition

\[
H_r(k)=m_r I_d+\eta_r(k)I_d+K_r(k),
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}K_r(k)=0,
\]

where

\[
\eta_r(k)=\frac1d\operatorname{tr}H_r(k)-m_r.
\]

Fiberwise traceless compression detects \(K_r(k)\), but it deliberately
erases the scalar dispersion \(\eta_r(k)I_d\). In particular, for a one-band
fiber \(d=1\), the traceless fiber is identically zero even when the band
disperses. Choose a reference momentum \(k_0\). The periodic representative
of the scalar-free spectral-shape class is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S_r^{\mathrm{spec}}
{}={}
\left(
\eta_r(k)-\eta_r(k_0),\qquad
K_r(k)
\right).
}
\]

This pair removes one global scalar anchor but retains scalar dispersion.
The cubic carrier coefficient in Section 18 is a contraction of this
spectral-shape class, not of the fiberwise traceless component alone.
Therefore both scalar and traceless momentum dependence return to the
spectral test:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite history reduction}
\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{rooted scalar/rest readout},\\
\text{scalar and traceless Bloch-shape readout}
\end{cases}
\longrightarrow
\text{Bloch spectrum test}.
}
\]

The theorem proves finite exact computability at fixed order. It does not
produce an efficient closed graph invariant for \(r_{\mathrm{disp}}\), and it
does not eliminate the need to construct the finite physical quotient graph.

# IV. Coefficient corollaries

## 15. Primitive-history corollary

The umbrella theorem guarantees a finite exact history construction. The following coefficient formula requires a narrower subgraph.

Assume:

1. the initial, intermediate, and final Wilson states remain normalized simple fundamental loops;
2. every inserted plaquette attaches along a connected oppositely oriented path;
3. each normalized attachment matrix element is \(1/N\);
4. the history has no proper return to \(P\);
5. the \(j\)-th intermediate gap is
   \[
   E_0-E_j=-d_j C_F,
   \qquad
   C_F=\frac{N^2-1}{2N},
   \]
   with \(d_j>0\);
6. no additional representation branching, multiplicity, or finite-rank determinant channel enters this subgraph.

For one temporal ordering \(\pi\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_\pi
&=
\left(\frac1N\right)^r
\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}
\frac{1}{-d_j(\pi)C_F}\\
&=
\frac{1}{N^rC_F^{r-1}}
\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}
\frac{-1}{d_j(\pi)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Define the signed temporal sum

\[
S_r
{}={}
\sum_{\pi}
\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}
\frac{-1}{d_j(\pi)}.
\]

The sign is already contained in \(S_r\); no additional factor \((-1)^{r+1}\) is to be inserted.

Summing the exact path amplitudes gives:

**Corollary 1.**

\[
\boxed{
c_{r,\mathrm{prim}}(N)
{}={}
\frac{S_r}{N^rC_F^{r-1}}
{}={}
\frac{2^{r-1}S_r}
{N(N^2-1)^{r-1}}.
}
\]

**Proof.** Substitute

\[
C_F^{-(r-1)}
{}={}
\left(
\frac{2N}{N^2-1}
\right)^{r-1}
\]

into the first fraction:

\[
\frac{1}{N^r}
\left(
\frac{2N}{N^2-1}
\right)^{r-1}
{}={}
\frac{2^{r-1}}
{N(N^2-1)^{r-1}}.
\]

Multiplication by the signed ordering sum \(S_r\) proves the formula. \(\square\)

For fixed nonzero \(S_r\),

\[
c_{r,\mathrm{prim}}(N)
\sim
2^{r-1}S_r\,N^{-(2r-1)}.
\]

The exponent follows directly: every additional primitive insertion supplies one extra \(1/N\) attachment and one extra \(1/C_F\sim1/N\) resolvent.

The certified signed temporal sums are:

| Cell channel | \(r\) | \(S_r\) | \(c_{r,\mathrm{prim}}(N)\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| tetrahedral | 2 | \(-4\) | \(-8/[N(N^2-1)]\) |
| triangular-prism square sector | 3 | \(16\) | \(64/[N(N^2-1)^2]\) |
| cube | 4 | \(-20\) | \(-160/[N(N^2-1)^3]\) |
| pentagonal-prism cap sector | 5 | \(70\) | \(1120/[N(N^2-1)^4]\) |

These geometry values are separate exact inputs to the factorization, so
their temporal census is displayed rather than hidden:

| Channel | Intermediate loop lengths | Multiplicity | Signed resolvent product | Total |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| tetrahedral | \((4)\) | 2 | \(2/(3-4)=-2\) | \(-4\) |
| triangular-prism square | \((5,6)\) or \((6,5)\) | 4 | \([2/(4-5)][2/(4-6)]=2\) | \(8\) |
| triangular-prism square | \((5,5)\) | 2 | \([2/(4-5)]^2=4\) | \(8\) |
| cubic cap | \((6,6,6)\) | 16 | \([2/(4-6)]^3=-1\) | \(-16\) |
| cubic cap | \((6,8,6)\) | 8 | \([2/(4-6)][2/(4-8)][2/(4-6)]=-1/2\) | \(-4\) |

For the \(n\)-gonal cap family, let \(T\subset\mathbb Z_n\) be the set of
side faces already inserted and let \(b(T)\) be the number of cyclic runs in
\(T\). The intermediate simple-loop length is

\[
\ell(T)=n+2b(T),
\]

so one reduced-resolvent factor is

\[
\frac{2}{n-\ell(T)}
{}={}
-\frac1{b(T)}.
\]

For a proper subset whose run lengths are
\(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_c\), define the positive ordering sum

\[
F(T)
{}={}
\sum_{\text{orderings of }T}
\prod_{k=1}^{|T|}
\frac1{b(T_k)}.
\]

Conditioning on the final inserted site gives the triangular recurrence

\[
F(T)
{}={}
\frac1{b(T)}
\sum_{x\in T}F(T\setminus\{x\}).
\]

Induction on \(|T|\), using the Catalan convolution separately on each run,
gives

\[
F(T)
{}={}
\prod_{j=1}^{c}\operatorname{Cat}(\lambda_j).
\]

In a full cap-to-cap history, choosing the final side face leaves one run of
length \(n-1\). There are \(n\) choices for that final face, hence

\[
\sum_\pi

## 20. Rooted scalar Möbius inversion

Let \(\mathcal P_r\) be the downward closure, under rooted connected
inclusion, of every support union reachable through order \(r\). Thus every
rooted connected sub-support needed by the subtraction recursion belongs to
\(\mathcal P_r\), even if its inclusive order-\(r\) coefficient vanishes.
Its finiteness follows from the bounded-support part of the umbrella theorem.
Set \(\delta_r(\mathcal S)=0\) whenever
\(\mathcal S\in\mathcal P_r\) has no inclusive order-\(r\) contribution.

For \(C\in\mathcal P_r\), let \(\delta_r(C)\) be the inclusive scalar
coefficient after all of the following operations:

1. direct histories and every order-\(r\) Feshbach fold have been assembled;
2. operator products have been assigned to their full support unions;
3. the vacuum scalar has been subtracted;
4. the retained-space scalar convention has been fixed.

Define

\[
\boxed{
\omega_r(C)
{}={}
\delta_r(C)
{}-{}
\sum_{\substack{
S\subsetneq C\\
S\ \mathrm{rooted,\ connected}
}}
\omega_r(S).
}
\]

**Corollary 4.** The recursion defines a unique connected scalar weight on
every rooted support, and its inverse is

\[
\boxed{
\delta_r(C)
{}={}
\sum_{\substack{
S\subseteq C\\
S\ \mathrm{rooted,\ connected}
}}
\omega_r(S).
}
\]

**Proof.** Order \(\mathcal P_r\) by support cardinality. For a minimal
support, the proper-subset sum is empty, so
\(\omega_r(C)=\delta_r(C)\). Assume all weights on smaller supports are
known. The triangular recursion then defines \(\omega_r(C)\) uniquely.
Rearranging that same recursion gives the inverse relation at \(C\).
Induction proves existence, uniqueness, and inversion on the finite poset.
\(\square\)

Equivalently, if \(\mu\) is the Möbius function of the rooted-support poset,

\[
\omega_r(C)
{}={}
\sum_{S\subseteq C}
\mu(S,C)\,\delta_r(S).
\]

Under the labeled-subset, per-root convention, the rooted scalar is

\[
\boxed{
m_r=\sum_C\omega_r(C).
}
\]

If the ledger stores only support isomorphism classes, the embedding
multiplicities must be shown explicitly:

\[
\omega_r([C])
{}={}
\delta_r([C])
{}-{}
\sum_{[S]\subsetneq[C]}
n_{[C]}([S])\,\omega_r([S]),
\]

\[
\boxed{
m_r
{}={}
\sum_{[C]}e([C])\,\omega_r([C]).
}
\]

Here \(n_{[C]}([S])\) counts rooted embeddings of the smaller class into the
larger class, and \(e([C])\) is the declared per-root or per-volume
occurrence weight. Labeled subsets and isomorphism classes are alternative
conventions; mixing them silently loses or duplicates multiplicities.

The inversion is exact but not self-repairing. It cannot compensate for:

- a missing direct history;
- an omitted proper-return fold;
- a product assigned to the wrong support union;
- an incorrect vacuum subtraction;
- an incomplete rooted-support ledger.

Thus the formula proves how to extract the exact rooted scalar from a
complete inclusive corpus. It does not, by itself, manufacture the currently
missing exact full-corpus cubic fourth-order scalar.
