# DOC 01 — From LSI to OS Reconstruction and a Hamiltonian Mass Gap

## 0. Purpose

This note isolates the cleanest *conceptual chain* in the project:

\[
\text{(uniform LSI)} \Longrightarrow \text{(spectral gap for a diffusion generator)}
\Longrightarrow \text{(exponential decay of Euclidean correlations)}
\Longrightarrow \text{(OS reconstruction)}
\Longrightarrow \text{(mass gap for the Hamiltonian)}.
\]

The novelty here is not “LSI implies a gap” (classical), but the attempt to **route the entire Yang–Mills mass-gap problem through a single functional inequality** while keeping **reflection positivity** by restricting to the **gauge-invariant physical algebra**.

## 1. Framework

### 1.1 Configuration space and measure

Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a (Sobolev-distributional) space of connections on \(M=\mathbb{T}^4\) and let \(\mu\) be a continuum probability measure obtained as a weak limit of gauge-fixed lattice measures \(\mu_a\).

We consider an associated (symmetric) Dirichlet form
\[
\mathcal{E}(F,F) = \int_{\mathcal{A}} \|\nabla F\|^2\, d\mu
\]
with generator \(L\) (formally \(L=\Delta - \nabla S_{\rm eff}\cdot \nabla\) for an effective action \(S_{\rm eff}\)).

### 1.2 The physical algebra and reflection positivity

Let \(\mathcal{A}_{\rm phys}\) be the algebra of **gauge-invariant cylindrical observables**, typically generated by Wilson loop functionals (or their lattice approximants).

Reflection positivity is assumed/derived *only on \(\mathcal{A}_{\rm phys}\)*:
for \(F\) supported in the positive time half-space,
\[
\langle \Theta F, F\rangle_\mu := \int (\Theta F)\,\overline{F}\, d\mu \ge 0.
\]

This restriction is essential: gauge-fixing is notorious for breaking reflection positivity on non-gauge-invariant fields.

## 2. Hypothesis H1 and its analytic consequences

### 2.1 Hypothesis H1 (Uniform log-Sobolev inequality)

Assume the continuum measure \(\mu\) satisfies an LSI with constant \(\rho_0>0\):
\[
\operatorname{Ent}_\mu(f^2) \le \frac{2}{\rho_0}\, \mathcal{E}(f,f)
\qquad \forall f \in D(\mathcal{E}).
\tag{H1}
\]

(Within the project, \(\rho_0\) is intended to be the continuum limit of a uniform lattice LSI constant.)

### 2.2 LSI ⇒ Poincaré (spectral gap)

Classically, LSI implies the Poincaré inequality with constant \(1/\rho_0\):
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(f) \le \frac{1}{\rho_0}\, \mathcal{E}(f,f).
\tag{PI}
\]

Equivalently, the generator \(L\) has a spectral gap:
\[
\| e^{tL}f - \mathbb{E}_\mu[f]\|_{L^2(\mu)} \le e^{-\rho_0 t} \|f-\mathbb{E}_\mu[f]\|_{L^2(\mu)}.
\tag{SG}
\]

## 3. Exponential decay of Euclidean correlations

Let \(O \in L^2(\mu)\) be a mean-zero local gauge-invariant observable (built from loops supported in a bounded spacetime region). Define a diffusion-time correlation:
\[
C_{\rm diff}(t) := \langle O, e^{tL} O\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}.
\]

From the spectral gap estimate (SG):
\[
|C_{\rm diff}(t)| \le e^{-\rho_0 t}\, \|O\|_{L^2(\mu)}^2.
\tag{ED}
\]

### Comment on “diffusion time” versus “Euclidean time”

A key interpretive step is to relate the semigroup \(e^{tL}\) to Euclidean time translations of Schwinger functions. In constructive QFT, this is typically mediated by a Feynman–Kac representation plus OS positivity.

In the project, this link is taken schematically as:
\[
\langle \Omega, \mathcal{O}\, e^{-tH}\, \mathcal{O}\Omega\rangle
\ \leftrightarrow\
\int \overline{F}\,(e^{tL}F)\, d\mu.
\]
A rigorous version should be spelled out in terms of the reconstructed Hilbert space and transfer matrix.

## 4. OS reconstruction and the Hamiltonian

### 4.1 OS reconstruction on the physical sector

Assuming reflection positivity on \(\mathcal{A}_{\rm phys}\), define the semi-inner product
\[
(F,G) := \langle \Theta F, G\rangle_\mu
\]
and quotient by the null space
\(\mathcal{N}=\{F:(F,F)=0\}\). The completion yields a physical Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}_{\rm phys}\).

Time translations act as a contraction semigroup \(P_t\) on \(\mathcal{H}_{\rm phys}\), and by standard semigroup theory,
\[
P_t = e^{-tH}
\]
for a self-adjoint Hamiltonian \(H\ge 0\).

### 4.2 Mass gap identification

Define the (physical) two-point function in reconstructed QFT by
\[
C_{\rm OS}(t)=\langle \Omega, \mathcal{O}\, e^{-tH}\, \mathcal{O}\Omega\rangle.
\]

If one can identify \(C_{\rm OS}(t)\) with a correlation controlled by (ED), then exponential decay implies a spectral gap:
\[
\inf(\sigma(H)\setminus\{0\}) \ge m,\qquad m \gtrsim \rho_0.
\]

**Interpretation:** the LSI constant \(\rho_0\) plays the role of the physical mass scale.

## 5. What is genuinely “new” here?

1. **Single-axiom reduction.** The program aims to reduce the Clay problem to proving *one* uniform-in-\(a\) LSI (or curvature-dimension) bound, rather than a large suite of renormalization estimates.

2. **Physical-sector OS repair.** The proof strategy explicitly keeps reflection positivity by reconstructing only on gauge-invariant observables, while using convexity/LSI to justify gauge fixing where needed.

3. **“Mass = curvature/convexity”.** The mass gap is identified with a geometric convexity parameter rather than a perturbatively generated scale.

## 6. Further work that would upgrade this from “pipeline” to “proof”

1. **Make the \(L\)–to–\(H\) link precise.** Prove a clean theorem: spectral gap of the Dirichlet generator \(L\) implies a spectral gap of the OS Hamiltonian \(H\) for the reconstructed theory on \(\mathcal{H}_{\rm phys}\).

2. **Spell out the locality/axioms.** OS reconstruction needs the full set of OS axioms (invariance, symmetry, regularity). The project focuses mostly on reflection positivity; the other axioms should be checked explicitly for the chosen core algebra.

3. **Pin down \(\rho_0\) and scaling.** To show this is *the* Yang–Mills theory, not merely a gapped Euclidean measure, one must connect \(\rho_0\) to asymptotic freedom scaling and show universality.
