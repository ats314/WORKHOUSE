#### 1. Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction and the mass gap

**Theorem 1 (Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction; schematic form)**  

Let $\{S_n(x_1,\dots,x_n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be Euclidean $n$‑point functions on $\mathbb{R}^d$ satisfying:

1. **Euclidean invariance** under the Euclidean group $E(d)$.
2. **Reflection positivity** with respect to a time reflection $\theta:\,(x_0,\mathbf{x})\mapsto(-x_0,\mathbf{x})$.
3. **Symmetry** under permutations of arguments.
4. **Cluster decomposition** (suitable falloff as arguments are separated).
5. **Regularity/temperedness** conditions (Schwartz bounds, etc.).

Then there exists a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$, a cyclic vacuum vector $\Omega\in\mathcal{H}$, a representation $U(a,\Lambda)$ of the Poincaré group with generators $P^\mu$ ($\mu=0,\dots,d-1$), and local Wightman fields $\phi_i(x)$, such that:

- The Wightman functions
  $$
  W_n(x_1,\dots,x_n)=\langle \Omega,\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)\Omega\rangle
  $$
  analytically continue the Euclidean $S_n$.
- The **spectrum condition** holds: $\sigma(P)\subset\overline{V_+}$ (closed forward light cone).
- $\Omega$ is $U$‑invariant and cyclic.
- Locality, covariance, etc., i.e. the Wightman axioms, are satisfied.

Moreover, the Hamiltonian $H=P^0$ is obtained from the Euclidean time-translation semigroup reconstructed via reflection positivity.

**Application to Conjecture D.**  

This theorem is the **bridge** your CLEAN file already names: “Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction.” Once we know that the Euclidean correlation functions of local gauge-invariant observables in Yang–Mills satisfy the OS axioms, there is a well‑defined Hamiltonian $H$ and a Wightman theory. Conjecture D is about identifying a *mass gap* for this $H$ from a Euclidean functional inequality.

**Theorem 2 (Euclidean exponential decay ⇔ mass gap in the reconstructed theory; schematic)**  

Let $(\mathcal{H},\Omega,H,\mathbf{P})$ and Euclidean correlators be obtained from OS reconstruction as above, and consider a *local* observable $O$ (e.g. a smeared field or local gauge‑invariant polynomial of fields) with vacuum expectation $\langle O\rangle := \langle\Omega,O\Omega\rangle$.

Define the Euclidean two‑point Schwinger function
$$
S_O(x)\;:=\;\langle\,O(x)O(0)\rangle - \langle O\rangle^2.
$$
Then:

1. If $H$ has a **mass gap** $\Delta>0$, i.e.
   $$
   \sigma(H)=\{0\}\cup[\Delta,\infty),
   $$
   then for large Euclidean time separation $t$ (fixed spatial separation, or in any fixed space‑like direction)
   $$
   |S_O(t,\mathbf{0})|\;\le C_O\,e^{-\Delta\,|t|}.
   $$
   More generally, in any direction $x$ with $|x|$ large,
   $$
   |S_O(x)|\;\le C_O' \,e^{-m|x|}
   $$
   for some $m\le\Delta$ depending on direction, typically $m=\Delta$ for time-like and space-like directions by Euclidean invariance.

2. Conversely, if for some $m>0$
   $$
   |S_O(x)|\;\leq C_O\,e^{-m |x|}
   $$
   for all large $|x|$ and for all local $O$ in a generating set of the local operator algebra (or, equivalently, if the two‑point functions admit a **Källén–Lehmann representation** with spectral measure supported in $[m,\infty)$ apart from a delta at zero corresponding to the vacuum), then:
   $$
   \sigma(H)=\{0\}\cup[\Delta,\infty),\quad \Delta\ge m.
   $$

**Sketch of proof (rigorous in constructive models).**

- For (1): By OS reconstruction, in the “energy representation” one has
  $$
  S_O(t,\mathbf{0}) = \langle\Omega, O(t)O(0)\Omega\rangle - \langle O\rangle^2
  = \langle \widetilde O\Omega, e^{-tH}\,\widetilde O\Omega\rangle,
  $$
  with $\widetilde O := O - \langle O\rangle\mathbf{1}$.
  Writing $\psi_O := \widetilde O\Omega$ and diagonalizing $H$,
  $$
  H = \int_{\sigma(H)} E\,dP_E,
  $$
  yields
  $$
  S_O(t,\mathbf{0}) = \int_{\sigma(H)\setminus\{0\}} e^{-Et}\,d\mu_O(E),
  $$
  where $d\mu_O(E)=\langle\psi_O, dP_E\psi_O\rangle$ is a positive measure supported on $\sigma(H)\setminus\{0\} \subset [\Delta,\infty)$.
  Hence for $t\ge0$,
  $$
  |S_O(t,\mathbf{0})|
  \le\int_\Delta^\infty e^{-Et}\,d\mu_O(E)
  \le e^{-\Delta t} \mu_O([\Delta,\infty))
  = C_O e^{-\Delta t}.
  $$

- For (2): Conversely, the two‑point functions admit a Källén–Lehmann representation:
  $$
  S_O(x) = \int_0^\infty d\rho_O(m^2)\,\Delta_E(x;m^2),
  $$
  where $\Delta_E(\cdot;m^2)$ is the free Euclidean 2‑pt function for mass $m$, and $d\rho_O$ is a positive measure. In Euclidean time direction,
  $$
  \Delta_E(t,\mathbf{0};m^2)\sim \frac{1}{2m}\,e^{-m|t|}\quad(|t|\to\infty).
  $$
  If $d\rho_O$ has support that accumulates at $0$ (i.e. no mass gap), then the integral has contributions decaying as $e^{-\epsilon |t|}$ with arbitrarily small $\epsilon>0$, contradicting a strictly positive lower bound $m$ on the decay rate:
  $$
  |S_O(t,\mathbf{0})|\le C\,e^{-m|t|}.
  $$
  Therefore the support of $\rho_O$ is contained in $\{0\}\cup[\Delta,\infty)$ with $\Delta\ge m$; the zero‑mass point, if present, corresponds to $\langle O\rangle\neq 0$ and has been subtracted. Since local fields generate the Hilbert space, this implies
  $$
  \sigma(H)\subset\{0\}\cup[\Delta,\infty),
  $$
  hence a mass gap $\Delta\ge m$.

**Application.**  

This theorem gives a precise realization of the line in your CLEAN file:

For any OS‑reconstructible theory, *once* we have exponential decay of Euclidean two‑point functions for all local generators, **we automatically get a mass gap** and vice versa. #### 2. Spectral gap / Poincaré inequality ⇒ exponential decay

There are two intertwined issues here:

**Theorem 3 (Poincaré inequality ⇔ $L^2$ spectral gap of a symmetric Markov generator).**

Let $(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)$ be a probability space and $L$ a selfadjoint, negative semidefinite operator on $L^2(\mu)$ generating a Markov semigroup $(P_t)_{t\ge0}$:

- $P_t = e^{tL}$ is positivity preserving and $\int P_t f\,d\mu=\int f\,d\mu$,
- $P_t 1 = 1$,
- $P_t$ is a contraction on $L^2(\mu)$.

Define the Dirichlet form
$$
\mathcal{E}(f,f)\;:=\;-\langle f,Lf\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}.
$$

Then the following are equivalent:

1. (**Poincaré inequality**)
   $$
   \operatorname{Var}_\mu(f)
   :=\int\bigl(f-\mu(f)\bigr)^2\,d\mu
   \;\le\;\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\mathcal{E}(f,f)
   \quad\forall f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}),
   $$
   for some $\lambda>0$.

2. (**$L^2$ spectral gap** for $L$)
   $$
   \sigma(-L) \subset \{0\}\cup [\lambda,\infty),
   $$
   i.e. the spectrum of $-L$ has a gap of size $\lambda$ above $0$, corresponding to constants.

Moreover, either condition implies the **exponential decay of the semigroup**:
$$
\|P_t f - \mu(f)\|_{L^2(\mu)}\;\le\;e^{-\lambda t}\,\|f - \mu(f)\|_{L^2(\mu)}
\quad\forall t\ge 0,\ \forall f\in L^2(\mu).
$$

*Proof sketch.*

- (1) ⇒ (2): For any $f$ with $\mu(f)=0$, Poincaré inequality gives
  $$
  \|f\|_2^2 \le \frac{1}{\lambda}\langle f,-Lf\rangle.
  $$
  Thus
  $$
  \frac{\langle f,-Lf\rangle}{\|f\|_2^2}\ge\lambda
  $$
  for all $f\perp 1$. By the variational characterization of the bottom of the spectrum of $-L$ on $1^\perp$,
  $$
  \inf_{\substack{f\perp 1\\f\neq0}}\frac{\langle f,-Lf\rangle}{\|f\|_2^2}
  =\inf\sigma(-L|_{1^\perp})\ge\lambda.
  $$
- (2) ⇒ (1): Conversely, if $\inf\sigma(-L|_{1^\perp})\ge\lambda$ then for any $f\perp1$,
  $$
  \langle f,-Lf\rangle \ge \lambda \|f\|_2^2,
  $$
  which is equivalent to the Poincaré inequality.

- The exponential decay of $P_t$ follows from the spectral theorem for $L$: for $f\perp1$,
  $$
  \|P_t f\|_2^2
  =\int e^{2t\lambda'}\,d\langle E_\lambda'f,E_\lambda'f\rangle
  \le e^{-2\lambda t}\int d\langle E_\lambda'f,E_\lambda'f\rangle
  =e^{-2\lambda t}\|f\|_2^2.
  $$

**Application.**  

Conjecture D assumes exactly such a Poincaré inequality with constant $\lambda$ (or $\lambda_{\text{loc}}$) for a certain generator (called “Langevin generator” in the CLEAN file) on a *local-observable sector*. The theorem shows rigorously that this is equivalent to a *spectral gap* for that generator.

**Theorem 4 (Spectral gap of a selfadjoint generator ⇒ exponential decay of stationary time correlations).**

Let $(X_t)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}$ be a stationary Markov process with invariant measure $\mu$ and generator $L$ as above. For $f,g\in L^2(\mu)$, define
$$
C_{f,g}(t)\;:=\;\operatorname{Cov}_\mu\bigl(f(X_0),g(X_t)\bigr)
=\int\!\left(f-\mu(f)\right)\cdot\left(P_t g-\mu(g)\right)\,d\mu.
$$
If $L$ has spectral gap $\lambda>0$ (equivalently, the Poincaré inequality holds), then
$$
|C_{f,g}(t)|\;\le\;e^{-\lambda |t|}\,\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_\mu(f)}\,\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_\mu(g)}.
$$

*Proof.*

Using stationarity and selfadjointness,
$$
C_{f,g}(t)=\langle f-\mu(f),P_t g-\mu(g)\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}.
$$
Then
$$
|C_{f,g}(t)|
\le\|f-\mu(f)\|_2\,\|P_t g-\mu(g)\|_2
\le e^{-\lambda|t|}\,\|f-\mu(f)\|_2\,\|g-\mu(g)\|_2,
$$
by the $L^2$ semigroup bound from Theorem 3.

**Application.**  

This is the rigorous analog of the “spectral gap → correlator decay” line in your status table, but in *Langevin time* (the semigroup parameter of $P_t$). It directly gives exponential decay in the *auxiliary time* of the stochastic dynamics generated by $L$.

However, *this is not yet* decay in **Euclidean spacetime separation** $|x|$ of the Schwinger functions. To use this for Conjecture D, one must carefully identify:

- Which generator $L$ is meant by the “Langevin generator” in the CLEAN file;
- How (if at all) its semigroup parameter relates to Euclidean time or space translations used in OS reconstruction.

This is one of the conceptual gaps that must be addressed, and I will return to it in the “Remaining Gaps” section.

#### 3. Transfer matrix, OS Hilbert space, and local-sector spectral gaps

In the OS construction for lattice Euclidean field theories, there is a canonical “transfer matrix” in (Euclidean) time, often denoted $T = e^{-aH}$ for lattice spacing $a$ and Hamiltonian $H$.

**Theorem 5 (Transfer matrix, reflection positivity, and Hamiltonian).**

Let $Z=\int e^{-S(\phi)}\mathcal{D}\phi$ be a Euclidean lattice field theory with reflection-positive measure and time-slice decomposition. Then:

1. There is a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_{\text{OS}}$ and a positive selfadjoint operator $H$ (Hamiltonian) such that the transfer matrix $T$ in Euclidean time is
   $$
   T = e^{-aH}.
   $$
2. Euclidean time translation by $n$ steps corresponds to applying $T^n$:
   $$
   \langle O_n O_0\rangle = \langle\Omega,O\,T^n\,O\,\Omega\rangle
   $$
   for appropriately localized operators $O$.
3. A **spectral gap** for $H$ of size $\Delta>0$ is equivalent to a **spectral gap** for $T$ at $e^{-a\Delta}<1$, i.e.
   $$
   \sigma(T) = \{1\}\cup [0,e^{-a\Delta}].
   $$

Furthermore, **locality** of the action ensures that the OS Hilbert space is generated by local field operators; one can sensibly talk about the *local sector* of $\mathcal{H}_{\text{OS}}$.

**Application.**

This theorem provides a way to rephrase Conjecture D *within* the OS/Hamiltonian framework:

- On $\mathcal{H}_{\text{OS}}$, the Poincaré inequality
  $$
  \|\psi\|^2 \le \frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{loc}}}\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle
  $$
  for $\psi$ in a dense *local sector* is precisely the statement that $H$ has a spectral gap $\lambda_{\text{loc}}$ *on that sector*. This is equivalent to an exponential decay of $\langle\psi,T^n\psi\rangle$ with rate $\lambda_{\text{loc}}$.
- Since time translations in Euclidean space are directly tied to $T=e^{-aH}$, this gives exponential decay of **Euclidean** correlators, not just of some auxiliary “Langevin time” process.

In constructive scalar field theories, this picture is fully rigorous; for lattice Yang–Mills theories, reflection positivity and OS reconstruction have been established (with gauge-invariant observables and care about gauge fixing), so this transfer-matrix picture is also rigorous at the lattice level.

#### 2. From Poincaré inequality to exponential decay and to mass gap

##### 2.1. Poincaré inequality on the OS Hilbert space

The **cleanest** way to make Conjecture D rigorous is to understand $\lambda_{\text{loc}}$ not as the gap of an *auxiliary Langevin generator*, but as the **spectral gap of $H$ on the local OS sector**.

Let $\mathcal{H}_{\text{loc}}\subset\mathcal{H}$ denote the closure of the subspace spanned by vectors of the form
$$
\psi_O := \bigl(O - \langle O\rangle\mathbf{1}\bigr)\Omega,
$$
where $O$ ranges over all local observables. Then the Poincaré inequality
$$
\|\psi\|^2 \;\le\; \frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{loc}}}\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle
\qquad \forall \psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\text{loc}},\ \langle\Omega,\psi\rangle=0
\tag{PI-H}
$$
is *exactly equivalent* to the statement that the spectrum of $H$ on $\mathcal{H}_{\text{loc}}$ is contained in $\{0\}\cup [\lambda_{\text{loc}},\infty)$, i.e. that $H$ has a **local-sector spectral gap** at least $\lambda_{\text{loc}}$.

This is a straightforward Hilbert-space analog of Theorem 3: $H$ is positive selfadjoint; constants correspond to the vacuum vector; (PI‑H) is the Rayleigh-quotient bound on the infimum of the spectrum on $\Omega^\perp$.

Given (PI‑H), we have for $t\ge0$
$$
\|e^{-tH} \psi\|\;\le\;e^{-\lambda_{\text{loc}} t}\,\|\psi\|
\quad\text{for all }\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\text{loc}}\cap\Omega^\perp.
\tag{1}
$$

Now take $\psi_O = \widetilde{O}\Omega$. The connected Euclidean correlator
$$
S_O(t)=\langle O(t)O(0)\rangle - \langle O\rangle^2
=\langle\psi_O,e^{-tH}\psi_O\rangle
$$
satisfies
$$
|S_O(t)|
\le \|\psi_O\|\;\|e^{-tH}\psi_O\|
\le \|\psi_O\|^2 e^{-\lambda_{\text{loc}} t}.
\tag{2}
$$

By Euclidean rotation invariance (or at least invariance under translations in any fixed space-like/timelike direction), this extends to
$$
|S_O(x)| \;\le\; C_O e^{-\lambda_{\text{loc}} |x|}
\quad\text{for large }|x|,
\tag{3}
$$
where the precise form of the norm $|x|$ depends on the Euclidean metric.

By Theorem 2, such exponential decay implies a **mass gap** $\Delta\ge\lambda_{\text{loc}}$ in the reconstructed Wightman theory. In particular, $\Delta>0$.

Thus, **if** $\lambda_{\text{loc}}$ is interpreted as the spectral gap of $H$ on the local sector (via a Poincaré inequality (PI‑H)), Conjecture D is *already a theorem* at the OS/Hilbert-space level:

- Poincaré inequality on the OS Hilbert space local sector ⇔ spectral gap of $H$ on that sector ⇒ exponential decay ⇒ mass gap.

The only remaining work is (i) existence of the OS reconstruction (which is known for lattice YM, conjectural in continuum), and (ii) verifying such a functional inequality for Yang–Mills.

##### 2.2. From Euclidean probability measure and Langevin generator to OS Poincaré inequality

Your CLEAN formulation, however, phrases the Poincaré inequality at the level of an **Euclidean measure** $\mu$ and a **Langevin generator** $L$:

- $\mu$ is the Euclidean Yang–Mills (or general QFT) measure on fields $\phi$.
- $L$ generates a Markov process in an *auxiliary Langevin time* $\tau$, often of form
  $$
  \partial_\tau \phi = -\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi} + \xi(\tau),
  $$
  with invariant measure $\mu$.
- $\mathcal{E}(f,f) = -\langle f,Lf\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}$ is the corresponding Dirichlet form.

You then postulate a Poincaré (or LSI) inequality:
$$
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(f) \le \frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{E}(f,f),
\tag{PI-L}
$$
for some $\lambda>0$, and want to conclude exponential **spacetime** decay of Schwinger 2‑pt functions.

The difficulty is that (PI‑L) is about decay in *Langevin time* (Theorem 4), while Conjecture D needs decay in **Euclidean spacetime separation** $x$, which is tied to the **Hamiltonian $H$** generated by Euclidean time translations in OS reconstruction.

Thus, to use (PI‑L) in proving Conjecture D, one needs an extra structural result:

> A relation between the spectral properties of the **Langevin generator $L$** (or its Dirichlet form) and the spectral properties of the **Hamiltonian $H$** (Euclidean time generator) or of the transfer matrix.

This relation is **not automatic**. In free theories and some convex-interaction models, one can often show that the Dirichlet form is comparable to $\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle$ on an appropriate domain, leading to inequalities like
$$
\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle \ge c\,\mathcal{E}(f,f)
$$
for $\psi$ corresponding to $f$ via OS reconstruction. In such cases, combining (PI‑L) with this comparability gives
$$
\|\psi\|^2 \le \frac{1}{\lambda} \mathcal{E}(f,f)
\le \frac{1}{c\lambda} \langle\psi,H\psi\rangle,
$$
i.e. (PI‑H) with $\lambda_{\text{loc}} = c\lambda$.

In more heuristic language, the conjectured scaling $m\ge c\sqrt{\lambda}$ likely encodes the **dimensionful mismatch** between the Dirichlet form (which often has the scaling of a gradient-square operator, i.e. like mass$^2$) and the Hamiltonian (which has the scaling of an energy, i.e. mass); thus $\sqrt{\lambda}$ is expected to behave like a mass parameter.

However, a general rigorous theorem connecting the spectral gap of a **Langevin generator** for the Euclidean YM measure to the mass gap of the reconstructed QFT is **not presently known** in the literature, especially in four-dimensional non‑Abelian gauge theories. That is precisely why your Conjecture D is nontrivial.

#### 3. Local vs global sectors and gauge theory

Your CLEAN file mentions:

> 2. Control local vs global topological modes  
> 3. Handle gauge fixing carefully  

and notes that:

> Local sector isolation | REQUIRES Conj IR-1/2

In a gauge theory:

- The Euclidean measure $\mu$ lives on equivalence classes of gauge connections (modulo gauge transformations) and may decompose into **topological sectors** (e.g. instanton sectors). Global modes (constant gauge transformations, global Wilson loops, etc.) can produce very slow decay for certain observables.
- A **local observable sector** means restricting attention to gauge‑invariant observables supported in bounded regions, typically insensitive to global topological data. One expects these to see only the *local* structure of the theory and to have better spectral properties.

A **local-sector Poincaré/LSI inequality** asserts that for observables $f$ built from local fields, there is a uniform lower bound $\lambda_{\text{loc}}>0$ on the Dirichlet form, even if the full measure $\mu$ has very slow modes in nonlocal directions (e.g. topological degrees of freedom whose dynamics is arbitrarily slow in volume).

This idea is standard in statistical mechanics: e.g. in spin systems with conserved quantities, one often proves LSIs or spectral gaps only on subspaces orthogonal to the conserved quantities.

In Yang–Mills, an analogous isolation of local sectors (excluding gauge and topological zero modes) is highly nontrivial and is, as you correctly note, itself currently conjectural (your Conj IR‑1/2).

**Proposition 6 (Local-sector spectral gap ⇒ Euclidean exponential decay).**

Under Assumptions A and B, for any local observable $O$ with $\langle O\rangle$ finite, there exists $C_O>0$ such that
$$
|\langle O(x)O(0)\rangle - \langle O\rangle^2|
\;\le\; C_O e^{-\lambda_{\text{loc}} |x|}
\qquad\text{for all large }|x|.
\tag{4}
$$

*Proof.*

Take $\psi_O = \widetilde{O}\Omega$ with $\widetilde{O}=O-\langle O\rangle$. Then $\psi_O\in\mathcal{H}_{\text{loc}}\cap\Omega^\perp$. Using (PI-H-loc), for all $t\ge0$,
$$
\|e^{-tH}\psi_O\|
\le e^{-\lambda_{\text{loc}} t}\,\|\psi_O\|
\tag{5}
$$
by the spectral theorem (the same reasoning as Theorem 3). Then the Euclidean time-separated correlator is
$$
S_O(t) := \langle O(t)O(0)\rangle - \langle O\rangle^2
= \langle\psi_O,e^{-tH}\psi_O\rangle,
$$
and hence
$$
|S_O(t)|
\le \|\psi_O\|\,\|e^{-tH}\psi_O\|
\le \|\psi_O\|^2 e^{-\lambda_{\text{loc}} t}.
$$
Using Euclidean invariance, analogous bounds hold for large spatial separations $|\mathbf{x}|$ and thus for $|x|$ large in any direction; in particular we obtain (4) with $C_O$ proportional to $\|\psi_O\|^2$.

This rigorously realizes the “spectral gap ⇒ correlator decay” step but now in **Euclidean spacetime**, not just in an auxiliary Langevin time.

**Proposition 7 (Euclidean exponential decay ⇒ mass gap).**

$$

\qquad\text{for all large }|x|,
\tag{6}

then the Hamiltonian $H$ has a mass gap $\Delta\ge m$:

\sigma(H)=\{0\}\cup[\Delta,\infty),\quad\Delta\ge m>0.

*Sketch of proof.*

As in Theorem 2, $S_O(t)$ is the Laplace transform of a positive spectral measure $d\mu_O(E)$ supported on $\sigma(H)\setminus\{0\}$. Because local fields generate a dense subspace, there can be no spectral weight for $H$ below $m$, aside from the vacuum eigenvalue at $0$. Thus $\Delta\ge m$.

#### 3. Where the Langevin generator and $\sqrt{\lambda}$ enter

The CLEAN formulation explicitly mentions:

$$
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(f)\le\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{E}(f,f),
$$

with $\mathcal{E}$ the Dirichlet form of a **Langevin generator** and then claims
$$
\langle O(x) O(0) \rangle \leq C e^{-m|x|},\quad m\ge c\sqrt{\lambda}.
$$

The constant $\sqrt{\lambda}$ rather than $\lambda$ is suggestive:

- In many continuum models, the Dirichlet form $\mathcal{E}$ naturally has **mass dimension 2**: e.g. for scalar field with action $S[\phi]=\int d^dx\,\bigl(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^2+\frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2+\cdots\bigr)$, one expects a generator $L$ whose spectrum has scaling like $|\mathbf{p}|^2 + m^2$.
- The Poincaré constant $\lambda$ in (PI‑L) often reflects the **minimal eigenvalue** of such a second-order operator, which in relativistic normalization behaves like $m^2$.
- The physical **mass** (energy) is $\sim m$, so $\sqrt{\lambda}$ is the natural candidate for a lower bound on the mass gap.

To turn this heuristic into a rigorous chain of inequalities, one needs a nontrivial operator comparison:

- A positive selfadjoint operator $K$ on $L^2(\mu)$ associated with the Dirichlet form $\mathcal{E}$ (e.g. $K\sim -L$),
- The OS Hamiltonian $H$ on $\mathcal{H}$,
- A map $U:L^2(\mu)\to\mathcal{H}$ intertwining $K$ and some function of $H$, such as
  $$
  UKU^{-1} \ge c\,H^2
  $$
  on an appropriate local domain.

If such a comparison holds, then the spectral gap $\lambda$ of $K$ implies
$$
\lambda\;\ge\;c\Delta^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \Delta \;\le\; \sqrt{\lambda/c},
$$
or conversely, $\Delta\ge c\sqrt{\lambda}$ for some constant $c>0$.

This is **exactly** the type of relationship your CLEAN file encodes in the inequality $m\ge c\sqrt{\lambda}$. However:

- For **free fields**, these relationships can be computed explicitly and one indeed finds that the Poincaré/LSI constant is proportional to $m^2$, giving $m\sim\sqrt{\lambda}$.
- For **interacting scalar models** in low dimensions, one can sometimes bound the LSI constant in terms of the physical mass, but typically this is technically demanding and often model-specific.
- For **Yang–Mills in 4D**, such operator comparisons between a Langevin generator and the OS Hamiltonian are **not established**.

Therefore, while the **pattern** $\Delta\sim\sqrt{\lambda}$ is strongly *motivated* and holds in simpler models, it remains conjectural in the setting of non‑Abelian gauge theories in four dimensions.

### Remaining Gaps

Even though the abstract OS/Hilbert-space implication

> local-sector spectral gap in $H$ ⇒ mass gap

is rigorously justified, several important gaps remain relative to the full Conjecture D as stated in the CLEAN document:

1. **Identification of the relevant generator.**

   - The CLEAN text calls $L$ a *Langevin generator* and writes the Poincaré inequality at the level of $(L,\mu)$.  
   - For Conjecture D to be rigorous, one needs that the *same constant* (or a controlled function of it) appears in a Poincaré inequality on the OS Hilbert space for the Hamiltonian $H$, or at least that the generator governing Euclidean time translations has a gap $\lambda_{\text{loc}}>0$ related to the $\lambda$ of the Langevin process.
   - This **link between the Langevin semigroup and the Euclidean-time translation semigroup** is not standardly available and is likely nontrivial in gauge theories.

2. **Local-sector isolation and gauge/topological modes.**

   - The local sector excludes global gauge rotations and topological modes (e.g. instanton sectors), which can have arbitrarily slow dynamics or even zero modes.
   - Your CLEAN file correctly notes that *local sector isolation* requires additional conjectures (Conj IR‑1/2). Without such isolation, a functional inequality for the full measure may fail, or its constant may degrade with volume.
   - A rigorous proof of a **volume-independent local-sector Poincaré/LSI inequality** for 4D Yang–Mills is completely open.

3. **Continuum limit.**

   - For lattice Yang–Mills, the OS axioms and reflection positivity are known, and one can define a transfer matrix with good properties.
   - However, the **continuum limit** (removing the lattice spacing $a\to0$ while maintaining a nontrivial interacting theory) is itself a major open problem.
   - Any claim about a mass gap in the full continuum theory necessarily presupposes that this constructive continuum limit exists and that the functional inequalities are uniform in $a$.

4. **Quantitative control $m\ge c\sqrt{\lambda}$.**

   - While the qualitative statement “local-sector spectral gap ⇒ positive mass gap” is conceptually and mathematically well supported, the **quantitative bound** $m\ge c\sqrt{\lambda}$ requires detailed information about the relationship between the Dirichlet form $\mathcal{E}$ and the Hamiltonian $H$.
   - Outside of special solvable or perturbative regimes, such quantitative comparisons are not generally available in gauge theories.

5. **Assumptions hidden in “Poincaré/LSI inequality”.**

   - In many rigorous results, Poincaré or LSI inequalities are established under **strong convexity** of the potential or high-temperature/small-coupling assumptions.
   - Yang–Mills in 4D is **non-perturbative** and strongly interacting in the infrared. Extending known functional inequalities to this regime, especially in the presence of gauge symmetry, is a major challenge.
