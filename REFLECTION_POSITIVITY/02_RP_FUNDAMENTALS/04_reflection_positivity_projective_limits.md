# Reflection Positivity Under RG Coarse-Graining and Projective Limits (Cylinder Level)

## Abstract
This note extracts the “architecture lemma” that is easy to say and easy to get wrong in detail:

> If your lattice measures are reflection positive at each cutoff, and your coarse-graining maps respect reflection, then the pushed-forward (blocked) measures remain reflection positive.  
> If, moreover, you have a consistent projective family of reflection-positive measures on cylinder algebras, the projective limit measure is reflection positive (on cylinders).

This is not the mass-gap estimate itself, but it is the part that lets you **transport OS structure** along an RG / continuum limiting procedure without re-proving reflection positivity from scratch at every step.

The statements below are written at the level of **cylinder observables**, because that is where RG constructions and projective limits are most natural.

---

## 1. OS reflection data on a lattice

Let \(M\) be the configuration space (e.g. \(G^{E(\Lambda)}\)) and let \(\theta:M\to M\) be the reflection involution induced by reflecting the lattice through a time plane.

Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be an algebra of bounded measurable observables on \(M\), and let \(\mathcal{A}_+\subset \mathcal{A}\) be the **positive-time** subalgebra (generated by cylinder observables supported in nonnegative Euclidean time).

A probability measure \(\mu\) on \(M\) is **reflection positive** if
\[
\langle F, F\rangle_{\theta,\mu} := \int \overline{F(\theta U)}\,F(U)\,d\mu(U) \;\ge\; 0
\qquad \forall F\in \mathcal{A}_+.
\]

---

## 2. Reflection positivity survives reflection-equivariant pushforward

### Lemma (reflection positivity under reflection-equivariant coarse-graining)
Let \((M,\theta,\mu)\) be as above, and let \(\pi:M\to M'\) be a measurable map into another configuration space \(M'\) with reflection involution \(\theta'\) such that
\[
\pi\circ \theta = \theta' \circ \pi
\qquad \text{(reflection equivariance).}
\]
Let \(\nu := \pi_\#\mu\) be the pushforward measure on \(M'\).

Let \(\mathcal{A}'_+\) be a positive-time algebra on \(M'\) and define \(\mathcal{A}_+ := \{F'\circ \pi : F'\in\mathcal{A}'_+\}\).

If \(\mu\) is reflection positive on \(\mathcal{A}_+\), then \(\nu\) is reflection positive on \(\mathcal{A}'_+\).

#### Proof
Take \(F'\in\mathcal{A}'_+\) and set \(F=F'\circ \pi\in\mathcal{A}_+\). Then
\[
\int \overline{F'(\theta' V)}\,F'(V)\,d\nu(V)
=
\int \overline{F'(\theta' \pi(U))}\,F'(\pi(U))\,d\mu(U).
\]
Using reflection equivariance \(\theta'\pi = \pi\theta\), the right-hand side becomes
\[
\int \overline{F'(\pi(\theta U))}\,F'(\pi(U))\,d\mu(U)
=
\int \overline{F(\theta U)}\,F(U)\,d\mu(U)\;\ge\;0.
\]
\(\square\)

**Takeaway:** any RG block map that commutes with reflection preserves reflection positivity at the cylinder level.

---

## 3. Reflection positivity survives projective limits (cylinder level)

Consider a directed set of scales \(i\in I\) with configuration spaces \(M_i\) and bonding maps \(\pi_{ij}:M_j\to M_i\) for \(i\prec j\), satisfying
\[
\pi_{ii}=\mathrm{id},\qquad \pi_{ik}=\pi_{ij}\circ \pi_{jk}.
\]
Assume each \((M_i,\theta_i)\) carries a reflection involution and each bonding map is reflection-equivariant:
\[
\pi_{ij}\circ \theta_j = \theta_i\circ \pi_{ij}.
\]

Let \(\mu_i\) be probability measures on \(M_i\) that form a **projectively consistent family**:
\[
(\pi_{ij})_\# \mu_j = \mu_i.
\]

### Lemma (projective limit reflection positivity on cylinders)
Assume each \(\mu_i\) is reflection positive on a positive-time cylinder algebra \(\mathcal{A}_{i,+}\).
Define the cylinder algebra on the projective limit \(M=\varprojlim M_i\) by pullback along coordinate projections \(p_i:M\to M_i\):
\[
\mathcal{A}_+ := \bigcup_{i\in I} \{F_i\circ p_i : F_i\in \mathcal{A}_{i,+}\}.
\]
Let \(\mu\) be the projective limit measure (Kolmogorov extension / Daniell–Stone).

Then \(\mu\) is reflection positive on \(\mathcal{A}_+\).

#### Proof sketch
Take a cylinder observable \(F = F_i\circ p_i\). By definition of \(\mu\), the joint law of \((p_i(U),p_i(\theta U))\) under \(\mu\) matches that under \(\mu_i\), because the \(\mu_i\) are consistent and the bonding maps commute with reflection.

Thus
\[
\int \overline{F(\theta U)}\,F(U)\,d\mu(U)
=
\int \overline{F_i(\theta_i u_i)}\,F_i(u_i)\,d\mu_i(u_i)\;\ge\;0.
\]
\(\square\)

---

## 4. What this gives you in the mass-gap program

If your lattice regulator is reflection positive (the Wilson action is), then:

1. **RG preservation:** Any reflection-equivariant blocking step keeps reflection positivity at the cylinder level (Lemma 2).

2. **Continuum architecture:** If you can arrange a projectively consistent family of blocked measures along a scaling trajectory \(a_n\downarrow 0\), the limiting (cylinder) measure is reflection positive (Lemma 3).

3. **OS reconstruction interface:** Once you also have the other OS axioms on cylinders (symmetry, regularity, etc.), you can invoke OS reconstruction to define the continuum Hilbert space and Hamiltonian.

This note does **not** solve existence of the continuum limit by itself; it only prevents you from losing reflection positivity while taking it.

---

## 5. Practical checklist for using this lemma honestly

To deploy this in a paper without inviting (correct) referee complaints, you must explicitly provide:

- A clear definition of the positive-time cylinder algebra \(\mathcal{A}_+\) at each scale.
- A proof that your coarse-graining map \(\pi\) commutes with reflection (often a one-line check once the map is defined).
- A proof of projective consistency \((\pi_{ij})_\#\mu_j=\mu_i\) **or** an explicit tightness/compactness argument that produces a subsequential projective limit.

If you write these down, this “architecture lemma” becomes a genuine theorem rather than a slogan.
