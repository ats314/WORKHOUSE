# Weak–Strong Field Decoupling in Structure Formation

## Purpose and scope

This module establishes a rigorous separation principle between linear, scale-dependent growth of density perturbations and nonlinear gravitational collapse in the Vacuum Stiffness Unification framework. The result ensures that modifications to linear growth do not back-react on nonlinear halo formation, collapse thresholds, or screening behavior.

The analysis closes the logical interface between the linear perturbation sector and the nonlinear structure-formation sector, showing that the two communicate only parametrically and never dynamically.

---

## Regime decomposition and matching scale

Let \(\Phi\) be the gravitational potential sourced by a density field \(\rho\). Define the screening radius associated with a mass \(M\) by

\[
r_s(M) := \sqrt{\frac{G M}{a_0}}.
\]

This scale partitions space into two regimes:

- **Weak-field (outer) regime:** \(r \gg r_s\), where \(|\nabla\Phi| \ll a_0\) and linear perturbation theory applies.
- **Strong-field (inner) regime:** \(r \ll r_s\), where \(|\nabla\Phi| \gg a_0\) and screening enforces Newtonian dynamics.

The transition between regimes occurs smoothly across a layer of width \(O(r_s)\).

## Linear perturbations as boundary data

Let \(\delta_L(\mathbf{x},t)\) denote the linear density contrast evolving according to

\[
\ddot\delta_L + 2H \dot\delta_L
=
4\pi G_{\mathrm{eff}}(k,t)\, \bar\rho_m\, \delta_L,
\]

with

\[
G_{\mathrm{eff}}(k,t) = G\,[1 + \alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}(k,t)].
\]

Linear growth modifies amplitudes but preserves locality and phase structure prior to shell crossing.

## Nonlinear collapse with external forcing

Consider a spherically symmetric overdensity of total mass \(M\) and physical radius \(r(t)\). Its equation of motion may be written as

\[
\ddot r = - g_{\mathrm{int}}(r) - g_{\mathrm{ext}}(t),
\]

where:

- \(g_{\mathrm{int}}(r)\) is the gravitational field generated by the enclosed mass,
- \(g_{\mathrm{ext}}(t)\) represents long-wavelength forcing induced by linear modes.

---

## Screening dominance in the collapse region

### Interior Newtonian dominance

For \(r \le r_s(M)\), the internal field satisfies

\[
g_{\mathrm{int}}(r) \sim \frac{G M}{r^2} \gg a_0.
\]

By contrast, the external field arises from weak-field linear modes and satisfies

\[
|g_{\mathrm{ext}}(t)| = O(a_0).
\]

Therefore,

\[
\frac{|g_{\mathrm{ext}}|}{|g_{\mathrm{int}}|} \ll 1
\quad \text{throughout the collapse region}.
\]

## Stability of collapse dynamics

### Collapse-time robustness

Let \(t_{\mathrm{coll}}\) denote the nonlinear collapse time. Then

\[
t_{\mathrm{coll}}
=
 t_{\mathrm{coll}}^{\mathrm{VSU}}
\left[1 + O\!\left(\frac{g_{\mathrm{ext}}}{g_{\mathrm{int}}}\right)\right].
\]

Corrections induced by linear forcing are parametrically suppressed once the collapse enters the screened regime.

### Mechanism

Screening forces the collapse dynamics to follow the Newtonian equation of motion in the interior region, while linear modes enter only through boundary conditions on the outer solution. Standard perturbation theory for ordinary differential equations yields the stated stability.

## Robustness of the collapse threshold

Define the critical overdensity

\[
\delta_c(M,z) := \delta_L(t_{\mathrm{coll}}).
\]

The modified collapse threshold derived from the unscreened-to-screened transition,

\[
\delta_c^{\mathrm{VSU}}(M,z)
=
\delta_c^{\mathrm{GR}}
\left(\frac{g_N(M,z)}{a_0}\right)^{1/4},
\]

is stable under scale-dependent linear growth. Linear modifications affect only the evaluation time of \(\delta_L\), not the structure of the collapse dynamics.

## Consequences for halo bias and clustering

Because halo bias depends on the collapse threshold via

\[
b(M,z) = 1 + \frac{\nu^2 - 1}{\delta_c(M,z)},
\]

robustness of \(\delta_c\) implies:

- no additional scale dependence enters halo bias from linear growth,
- environmental dependence arises solely through screening,
- linear and nonlinear modifications are not double-counted.
