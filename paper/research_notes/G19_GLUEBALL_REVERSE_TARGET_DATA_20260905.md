# Glueball information that constrains the reverse target

5 September 2026. External numerical evidence and its operational consequence.
This note does not use a measured mass as a mathematical hypothesis.

Athenodorou and Teper, *SU(N) gauge theories in 3+1 dimensions: glueball
spectrum, string tensions and topology*, arXiv:2106.00364v3, report continuum
extrapolations of Wilson-action simulations. Their Table 34 gives the SU(3)
ratios M/sqrt(sigma): 0++ ground 3.405(21), 2++ ground 4.894(22), and 0-+
ground 5.276(45). Their Section 5.7 identifies the scalar 0++ as the lightest
glueball in the computed spectra. These are numerical extrapolations with
quoted errors, not rigorous spectral enclosures or existence theorems.
[Primary paper, Sections 5.5-5.7 and Table 34](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.00364v3).

The useful deduction for WORKHOUSE is a choice of observables and comparisons:

- Carry a centered charge-even scalar source alongside the established odd
  plaquette band. Odd-source completeness alone does not identify the overall
  lightest mass. The finite two-square physical ground excitation derived in
  the new block calculation has an invariant quadratic oscillator source;
  its relation to a continuum scalar field must still be constructed.
- Compare dimensionless ratios in a common scheme. A pure-gauge mass in GeV
  requires a choice of physical scale; it is not an additional universal
  constant supplied by the mathematical problem.
- Demand that a candidate finite physical mass survive with nonzero
  renormalized source weight, while the eliminated local modes move to
  energies of order 1/a. This is the forward/backward meeting condition in
  [reverse-matching proof](G19_OS_BLOCKING_AND_REVERSE_MASS_MATCHING_20260905.md),
  not a fit of the small-u polynomial to the table.

No value above is used to derive or tune the block oscillator constants,
Born-Oppenheimer coefficients, or exact SU(2) rotor certificates. The
two-square constants refer to a finite weak-field block and are not proposed
as the displayed continuum mass ratios. The full-spectrum lower bound and
the limiting field theory remain separate required conclusions.
