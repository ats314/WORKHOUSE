1 Introduction
Hamiltonian strong-coupling expansions organize lattice
gauge dynamics around the electric vacuum and its flux
excitations [1, 2, 3]. In three spatial dimensions, a funda-
mental plaquette excitation carries one of three face orien-
tations. At second order, neighboring faces communicate
through a shared link, so the projected problem resembles
a three-orbital tight-binding model. The signs of those
matrix elements, however, are fixed by the orientation of
the plaquette boundary and by charge conjugation. The
resulting interference is the central object of this paper.
Flat bands generated by incidence or Gram matrices
have a long history. Local topology, line-graph construc-
tions, compact localized states, and the need for noncon-
tractible states on periodic systems are well established
[4, 5, 6, 7, 8]. Rectangular factorizations and index argu-
ments provide a general language for such zero modes [ 9].
Closely related curl-free or closed-surface structures occur
in two-form spin liquids [ 10], and a recent independent
construction treats face-graph and higher-cell flat bands
directly [12]. We therefore do not claim that incidence
flatness, cellular compact localized states, or homological
counting are new in isolation.
The result here is instead dynamical. Starting from
non-AbelianSU (N) Hamiltonian lattice gauge theory, ex-
act strong-coupling matrix elements generate an oriented
edge–face Gram operator in the charge-odd one-plaquette
sector. Group representation theory fixes its coefficient,
while the cubical chain complex fixes its kernel and spec-
trum. The separation is exact:
color dynamics−→tN, cellular geometry−→BB†.

Proposition 1 (Fixed-order local support) . Give local
face operators the adjacency relation induced by overlap-
ping on an elementary link, and let R0 be the finite root
support. On a locally finite support graph, every con-
nected rooted contribution with at most r insertions lies
in the radius-r neighborhood Starr(R0). If every bounded
support has a finite dimensional reachable quotient Equa-
tion (11), then, after choosing a basis in each quotient, the
candidate quotient-basis labels and fixed-order Feshbach
monomials form a finite universe.
Proof. After linked-cluster reduction, the union of the
root and inserted supports is connected. Reaching a face
at graph distance d requires at least d inserted supports
along a connecting path, so d≤r. Local finiteness makes
this neighborhood finite. The assumed finite dimensional
reachable quotients then admit finite bases, while a for-
mal Feshbach expansion through fixed order contains
only finitely many powers of the perturbation, energy
derivative, and reduced resolvent.
Partial histories that arrive at the same physical quo-
tient vector with the same rooted support and fold meta-
data can be merged by linearity. The proposition is a
completeness statement at each fixed order, not a closed
formula for the first dispersive order and not a guarantee
that an order-by-order search terminates if a band is flat
to all orders.

10 Relation to prior flat-band con-
structions
It is useful to state precisely what is shared with, and
what differs from, nearby constructions.
Line-graph and molecular-orbital models engineer a
rectangular matrix D and obtain zero modes from D†D
or DD† [5, 8, 9]. Here the same algebraic mechanism
appears, but D =∂2 is selected by the orientation signs
of charge-odd non-Abelian flux, and its prefactor is the
exact representation-theoretic number tN. The hopping
ratio is therefore derived from the gauge Hamiltonian
rather than tuned as a lattice design.
The two-form spin construction of Chung and Gingras
[10] also features plaquette variables, curl constraints,
flat interaction bands, and winding sectors. Its micro-
scopic degrees of freedom and emergent U(1) description
differ from the projected SU (N) flux operator studied
here. Hazra’s contemporary face-graph construction [ 12]
gives compact states and higher-cell generalizations using
a designed face-incidence Hamiltonian. Our work was
developed independently, and our new content relative
to that framework is the non-Abelian strong-coupling
derivation, the all-rank color coefficient, and the ledger-
dependent SU (3) third-order analysis.
The torus count likewise has a standard topological
interpretation. Compact cube boundaries provide L3− 1
states, while singularity of the Bloch eigenvector forces
noncontractible supplements [6, 7]. In the cubical gauge
setting those supplements are exactly the three generators
of H2(T 3).

13 Conclusions
Strong-coupling SU (N) dynamics and cubical topology
separate self-containedly through second order in the
charge-odd one-plaquette sector. The non-Abelian shared-
link calculation supplies the positive all-rank coefficient
tN, while the oriented boundary map supplies the Gram
operator BB†. Its kernel is a homological flat carrier of
boundary-free two-chains, with cube-boundary compact
states and three wrapping-sheet generators on the torus.
The master framework explains how to continue: se-
lect the complete electric eigenspace, retain temporal
order, quotient Gram-null networks, apply representation-
resolved resolvents, assemble Feshbach folds, perform
rooted linked subtraction, and only then distinguish scalar
position, internal shape, and momentum dispersion. At
every fixed order this reduces to finite algebra under
the stated locality and finite-reach assumptions. The
restricted circuit and primitive-channel formulas identify
useful cellular subchannels but are not universal transfer
laws.
Beyond second order, this document records rather
than independently reconstructs the controlling finite com-
putations. Supplied ledgers report the complete SU (3)
third-order entries and a zero census for 32 candidate
lifter classes. Conditional on that ledger, the incidence
algebra survives through order u3. Supplied ledgers also
report αN = 640/[N(N2− 1)3] and its complete axial
identification for N = 3,..., 12. Conditional on that mi-
croscopic closure, α3 = 5/12 yields axial lifting at order
u4.
Independent regeneration of the omitted third- and
fourth-order payloads is a reproducibility task; the off-
axis mixed-gradient contraction is a separate unresolved
physics calculation. No complete fourth-order bandwidth,
continuum extrapolation, glueball identification, or mass-
gap claim follows here. The unconditional result is the
dynamically generated all-rank homological flat band
through second order, with all higher-order claims explic-
itly graded.
