# Appendix G — Lyapunov drift identities and the pairing functional

**File status:** **Conditional** (all identities and “no hidden (|\Lambda|)” closures are **Established**; the only missing step is a **Conditional / open** coercive lower bound on the pairing functional).

**Scope:** This appendix is a **symbol-accurate ledger** of:

1. the diffusion generator (L_\Lambda) and carré du champ (\Gamma_\Lambda),
2. the Lyapunov choice (W_\Lambda=e^{\kappa V_\Lambda}) with (V_\Lambda=\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2),
3. the **exact drift identity** for (\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}),
4. the reduction of drift negativity to a **single pairing functional** (\mathcal P_\Lambda),
5. the explicit **local algebraic rewrites** of (\mathcal P_\Lambda) for the Wilson action.

This appendix does **not** claim any coercivity inequality. Any required coercivity statement belongs in `99_Open_Gaps.md` (not here).

## G.1 Reversible diffusion generator and carré du champ

### G.1.1 Configuration manifold and Gibbs law (finite volume)

Let (\Lambda) be a finite periodic lattice and
[
M_\Lambda := G^{E(\Lambda)}
]
with the product bi-invariant metric (g_\Lambda). Let (S_\Lambda:M_\Lambda\to\mathbb R) be a smooth action and
[
\mu_\Lambda(dU)\ \propto\ e^{-S_\Lambda(U)},d\mathrm{vol}*{g*\Lambda}(U)
]
the associated Gibbs measure. (For the Wilson action, see §G.2.3.)

### G.1.2 Generator

Define the reversible generator
[
L_\Lambda f \ :=\ \Delta_\Lambda f\ -\ \langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla f\rangle_{g_\Lambda},
\tag{G.1}
]
where (\Delta_\Lambda) is the Laplace–Beltrami operator and (\nabla) is the Riemannian gradient. This is the generator used in the Part 7 drift computation. 

### G.1.3 Carré du champ

For smooth (f,g),
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(f,g)\ :=\ \langle \nabla f,\nabla g\rangle_{g_\Lambda},
\qquad
\Gamma_\Lambda(f):=\Gamma_\Lambda(f,f).
\tag{G.2}
]
This is the carré-du-champ for the gradient-drift generator.

## G.2 The smooth plaquette proxy and global “badness” functionals

### G.2.1 Proxy on (G) and plaquette lift

Fix a unitary representation (\rho:G\to U(n)). Define the trace-defect proxy
[
\widetilde z(g)\ :=\ 1-\frac1n\Re,\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(g)),
\qquad 0\le \widetilde z\le 2,
\tag{G.3}
]
and its plaquette lift
[
\widetilde z_p(U)\ :=\ \widetilde z\big(U_p(U)\big),
\tag{G.4}
]
where (U_p(U)) is the plaquette holonomy. 

### G.2.2 Aggregates (\mathcal D_\Lambda), (V_\Lambda), and Lyapunov weight (W_\Lambda)

Define:
[
\mathcal D_\Lambda(U)\ :=\ \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \widetilde z_p(U),
\qquad
V_\Lambda(U)\ :=\ \sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)} \widetilde z_p(U)^2,
\qquad
W_\Lambda(U)\ :=\ e^{\kappa V_\Lambda(U)}.
\tag{G.5}
]


Basic pointwise bounds used for bookkeeping:
[
0\le \mathcal D_\Lambda \le 2|P(\Lambda)|,\qquad
0\le V_\Lambda \le 4|P(\Lambda)|,\qquad
V_\Lambda \le 2\mathcal D_\Lambda,
\qquad
\mathcal D_\Lambda \le \sqrt{|P(\Lambda)|},\sqrt{V_\Lambda}.
\tag{G.6}
]
These are simple consequences of (0\le \widetilde z_p\le 2) and (z^2\le 2z) on ([0,2]), plus Cauchy–Schwarz. 

### G.2.3 Wilson action relation

For the Wilson action (S_W(U)=\beta\sum_{p}\widetilde z_p(U)), one has the exact identity
[
S_W(U)=\beta,\mathcal D_\Lambda(U).
\tag{G.7}
]

## G.3 Drift identities: exact expansion and volume-uniform closure

### G.3.1 Diffusion chain rule (used repeatedly)

For a diffusion generator (L_\Lambda) with carré du champ (\Gamma_\Lambda), the standard scalar chain rule gives, for (\Psi\in C^2(\mathbb R)) and (V\in C^\infty(M_\Lambda)),
[
L_\Lambda(\Psi\circ V)
======================

\Psi'(V),L_\Lambda V
+
\Psi''(V),\Gamma_\Lambda(V).
\tag{G.8}
]
Specializing to (\Psi(s)=e^{\kappa s}) yields:
[
L_\Lambda(e^{\kappa V})
=======================

e^{\kappa V}\Big(\kappa L_\Lambda V+\kappa^2\Gamma_\Lambda(V)\Big),
\qquad
\frac{L_\Lambda W}{W}=\kappa L_\Lambda V+\kappa^2\Gamma_\Lambda(V).
\tag{G.9}
]


### G.3.2 Exact drift identity for (V_\Lambda=\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2)

Let (V_\Lambda=\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2) and (W_\Lambda=e^{\kappa V_\Lambda}). Then:
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}
=====================================

\kappa,L_\Lambda V_\Lambda+\kappa^2,\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda),
\tag{G.10}
]
and
[
L_\Lambda V_\Lambda
===================

2\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\widetilde z_p,\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p
;-;
2\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\widetilde z_p,\big\langle \nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla \widetilde z_p\big\rangle_{g_\Lambda}
;+;
2\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p).
\tag{G.11}
]
These are the exact identities recorded as Proposition 7.35 in Part 7. 

The decomposition (G.11) isolates:

* a Laplacian/Hessian contribution (2\sum_p \widetilde z_p,\Delta_\Lambda \widetilde z_p),
* the **pairing/drift term** (-2\sum_p \widetilde z_p\langle\nabla S_\Lambda,\nabla \widetilde z_p\rangle),
* a positive carré-du-champ contribution (2\sum_p \Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p)). 

### G.3.3 Volume-uniform control of all diffusion-generated terms by (\mathcal D_\Lambda)

Part 7 isolates a set of constants (depending only on ((G,g_G)), (\rho), and the lattice overlap constant (\nu)) such that:

* the Laplacian/Hessian term is bounded by (A_1,\mathcal D_\Lambda),
* the positive chain-rule term is bounded by (A_2,\mathcal D_\Lambda),
* the exponential carré-du-champ term (\Gamma_\Lambda(V_\Lambda)) is bounded by (A_3,\mathcal D_\Lambda).

These are packaged as
[
C_V:=A_1+A_2,
\qquad
C_\Gamma:=A_3,
\tag{G.12}
]
with explicit choices
[
C_V=8C_\Delta+8C_\nabla,
\qquad
C_\Gamma=64,\nu,C_\nabla.
\tag{G.13}
]


Consequently, Corollary 7.36 yields the key upper bound:
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}(U)
;\le;
\big(\kappa C_V+\kappa^2 C_\Gamma\big),\mathcal D_\Lambda(U)
;-;
2\kappa\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\widetilde z_p(U),\big\langle \nabla S_\Lambda(U),\nabla \widetilde z_p(U)\big\rangle_{g_\Lambda}.
\tag{G.14}
]


This is restated in Appendix I as:
[
\frac{L_\Lambda W_\Lambda}{W_\Lambda}(U)
;\le;
\big(\kappa C_V+\kappa^2 C_\Gamma\big),\mathcal D_\Lambda(U)
;-;
2\kappa,\mathcal P_\Lambda(U),
\tag{G.15}
]
where (\mathcal P_\Lambda) is defined below.

## G.4 The pairing functional (\mathcal P_\Lambda)

### G.4.1 Definition

Define the pairing functional
[
\mathcal P_\Lambda(U)
:=
\sum_{p\in P(\Lambda)}\widetilde z_p(U),\big\langle \nabla S_\Lambda(U),\nabla \widetilde z_p(U)\big\rangle_{g_\Lambda}.
\tag{G.16}
]
This is exactly the **only** term in (G.14)/(G.15) that is not already controlled by volume-uniform constants. 

### G.4.2 Universal identity: (\mathcal P_\Lambda = \tfrac12\langle \nabla S,\nabla V\rangle)

With (V_\Lambda=\sum_p \widetilde z_p^2),
[
\nabla V_\Lambda
=\sum_p \nabla(\widetilde z_p^2)
=\sum_p 2\widetilde z_p,\nabla \widetilde z_p,
\tag{G.17}
]
so
[
\mathcal P_\Lambda(U)
=====================

\frac12,\big\langle \nabla S_\Lambda(U),\nabla V_\Lambda(U)\big\rangle_{g_\Lambda}.
\tag{G.18}
]


This makes the structural issue explicit: drift negativity reduces to showing that the action gradient has **uniform positive alignment** with the “radial badness direction” (\nabla V_\Lambda).

## G.5 Wilson specialization: local carré-du-champ structure of (\mathcal P_\Lambda)

### G.5.1 Wilson identity: (\mathcal P_\Lambda) as a local double sum

For the Wilson action
[
S_W(U)=\beta\sum_{q\in P(\Lambda)}\widetilde z_q(U),
\tag{G.19}
]
one has
[
\nabla S_W=\beta\sum_{q}\nabla \widetilde z_q.
\tag{G.20}
]
Plugging this into (G.16) yields
[
\mathcal P_\Lambda(U)
=====================

\beta\sum_{p,q\in P(\Lambda)} \widetilde z_p(U),
\Gamma_\Lambda\big(\widetilde z_p,\widetilde z_q\big)(U),
\qquad
\Gamma_\Lambda(f,g):=\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle_{g_\Lambda}.
\tag{G.21}
]


### G.5.2 Locality

Each (\widetilde z_p) depends only on the four boundary links (\partial p), hence
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p,\widetilde z_q)\equiv 0
\quad\text{whenever (p) and (q) share no link}.
\tag{G.22}
]
This is the key locality input for any attempted coercivity argument. 

### G.5.3 Linkwise gradient decomposition (sum over links)

Decompose gradients into link-gradients (\nabla_\ell) (as in Part 3 / Part 7 linkwise calculus):
[
\Gamma_\Lambda(\widetilde z_p,\widetilde z_q)
=============================================

\sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}\left\langle \nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p,,\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_q\right\rangle_{\mathfrak g},
\tag{G.23}
]
and (\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p\equiv 0) unless (\ell\in\partial p). 

Combining (G.21)–(G.23) gives the **derived** link-sum form
[
\mathcal P_\Lambda(U)
=====================

\beta\sum_{\ell\in E(\Lambda)}
\ \sum_{\substack{p\in P(\Lambda):\ \ell\in\partial p}}
\ \sum_{\substack{q\in P(\Lambda):\ \ell\in\partial q}}
\widetilde z_p(U),
\left\langle \nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p(U),,\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_q(U)\right\rangle_{\mathfrak g}.
\tag{G.24}
]
**Status:** Established (algebraic rewrite from (G.21) and (G.23) plus support (\ell\in\partial p)). 

This makes the geometry of the missing coercivity input concrete: it is a statement about the sign/size of **cross-link correlations** among the gradients (\nabla_\ell \widetilde z_p), weighted by (\widetilde z_p).

## G.6 What remains conditional (and therefore excluded from this appendix)

The drift bound (G.15) becomes genuinely negative (in the Foster–Lyapunov sense) once one has a lower bound on (\mathcal P_\Lambda) in terms of (\mathcal D_\Lambda) (or a closely related averaged functional). Appendix I records three coercivity *targets*:

* **Strong linear target:** (\mathcal P_\Lambda \ge c_{\mathrm{pair}}\mathcal D_\Lambda - C_{\mathrm{pair}}). 
* **Quadratic-in-average target:** (\mathcal P_\Lambda \gtrsim |P|,\mathcal B_\Lambda^2 - C|P|) where (\mathcal B_\Lambda=\mathcal D_\Lambda/|P|). 
* **One-sided off-core target:** (\mathcal P_\Lambda \gtrsim \mathcal D_\Lambda) outside a core set (K_\Lambda(\varepsilon)={\mathcal B_\Lambda\le\varepsilon}). 

**None of these are proved in the current corpus.** This appendix does not restate them as lemmas. The missing statement must be tracked in `99_Open_Gaps.md`.
