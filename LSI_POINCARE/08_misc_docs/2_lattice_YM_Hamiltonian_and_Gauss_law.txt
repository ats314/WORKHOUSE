Title: Lattice Yang–Mills Hamiltonian and Gauss’s Law (Summary from Chat)

This document summarizes the key structures we discussed that are standard in Hamiltonian
lattice gauge theory for SU(3), focusing on the Hamiltonian and Gauss’s law constraint.


1. Degrees of Freedom on the Lattice

We consider a spatial lattice with sites x and directions μ = 1,2,3.

- Link variables:
    U_{x,μ} ∈ SU(3),
  live on oriented links from x to x+μ.

- Electric fields (canonically conjugate to the links):
    E_{x,μ}^a,  a = 1,…,8,
  taking values in the Lie algebra su(3).

The E_{x,μ}^a satisfy the SU(3) commutation relations and act as left/right invariant
vector fields on SU(3) in the Kogut–Susskind formalism.


2. Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian (Pure Gauge SU(3))

The standard Hamiltonian for pure SU(3) gauge theory on the lattice is:

    H = H_E + H_B,

with

    H_E = (g^2 / 2) Σ_{x,μ} E_{x,μ}^a E_{x,μ}^a,

    H_B = - (1 / 2g^2) Σ_{plaquettes p} [ Tr(U_p) + Tr(U_p^†) ].

Here:

- g is the gauge coupling;
- U_p is the ordered product of link variables around an elementary plaquette p;
- H_E is the electric (kinetic) energy;
- H_B is the magnetic (potential) energy, measuring the non-Abelian curvature via plaquettes.

This operator H acts on a Hilbert space of wavefunctionals ψ[{U}] over link configurations
equipped with the Haar measure on SU(3) per link.


3. Gauss’s Law Constraint and Physical Hilbert Space

Local SU(3) gauge transformations act on link variables as:

    U_{x,μ} → G_x U_{x,μ} G_{x+μ}^{-1},   G_x ∈ SU(3).

The generators of these gauge transformations at each site x are the Gauss operators G_x^a.

Physical states |ψ⟩ must be gauge-invariant:

    G_x^a |ψ⟩ = 0  for all x, a.

The physical Hilbert space is then:

    H_phys = { |ψ⟩ : G_x^a |ψ⟩ = 0, ∀ x,a } = ker G.

This constraint removes most naive low-energy degrees of freedom and is essential
for the physical spectrum (glueballs, etc.).


4. Hamiltonian Restricted to the Physical Subspace

While H acts on a larger kinematic Hilbert space, the physically relevant operator is
H restricted to H_phys:

    H_phys (as an operator) = H |_{H_phys}.

The energies E_n that define the spectrum and the mass gap come from solving:

    H_phys |ψ_n⟩ = E_n |ψ_n⟩,

with |ψ_n⟩ ∈ H_phys.

The vacuum |0⟩ is the lowest-energy solution; glueball states are higher-energy,
gauge-invariant excitations of this Hamiltonian.


5. Intuitive Role of Gauss’s Law in the Mass Gap

In Abelian (U(1)) theory, Gauss’s law still permits transverse, long-wavelength modes
that can have arbitrarily low energy → massless photons.

In SU(3), the combination of:

- Gauss’s law,
- non-Abelian commutator term in F_{μν},
- and the nonlinearity of H_B,

means that physical excitations are extended, loop-like objects (Wilson-loop-like),
and there are no arbitrarily soft, gauge-invariant excitations in the usual physical picture.

A rigorous proof that H_phys has a strictly positive spectral gap is exactly the mass-gap problem;
this document merely encodes the operator structure, not a proof.
