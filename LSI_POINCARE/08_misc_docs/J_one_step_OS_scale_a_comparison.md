# One-step OS/Dirichlet comparison via a boundary strip kernel and a scale-$a$ regularized Dirichlet form

This note extracts and packages a structural mechanism from the project: a **one-step transfer kernel** across an Osterwalder--Schrader (OS) reflection plane and a **mathematically viable comparison** between the one-step OS dissipation and a *bounded*, scale-$a$ regularized Dirichlet form for the configuration diffusion.

The key point is a boundedness obstruction:

* The one-step dissipation $\langle f,(I-K_a)f\rangle$ for a Markov operator $K_a$ is always bounded by $2\|f\|_2^2$.
* The raw gradient energy $\int|\nabla f|^2$ is unbounded on $L^2$.

Therefore a global inequality of the form
\[
\langle f,(I-K_a)f\rangle \gtrsim \int |\nabla f|^2
\]
cannot hold in general. The viable replacement is the **scale-$a$ form**
\[
\mathcal E^{(a)}_{\mathrm{conf}}(f,f):=\langle f,(I-P_a)f\rangle,
\]
where $(P_t)_{t\ge0}$ is the configuration diffusion semigroup. This is bounded and has an exact gradient representation.

---

## 1. Finite lattice with a time direction and the OS reflection plane

Fix a finite Euclidean space--time lattice
\[
\Lambda = \{0,1,\dots,T\}\times \Lambda_s,
\]
with time coordinate $t\in\{0,1,\dots,T\}$ and a finite spatial box $\Lambda_s\subset\mathbb Z^{d-1}$. Let $G=\mathrm{SU}(N)$.

A gauge configuration is
\[
U=(U_e)_{e\in E(\Lambda)}\in \Omega:=G^{E(\Lambda)}
\]
with the usual constraint $U_{e^{-1}}=U_e^{-1}$.

Let $\theta$ be reflection across the mid-plane between $t=0$ and $t=1$:
\[
\theta(t,x)=(1-t,x),
\]
and let $\Theta$ be the induced involution on observables (reflection of arguments + orientation reversal + complex conjugation).

Let $\mathcal A^+$ denote bounded cylinder functions depending only on the positive-time half (e.g. $t\ge1$ plus boundary links, depending on the chosen convention).

The OS form is
\[
(F,G)_{\mathrm{OS}}:=\int \overline{(\Theta F)(U)}\,G(U)\,d\mu(U),
\]
for a reflection-positive measure $\mu$.

## 2. Boundary variables and the one-step strip kernel

Let $E_s:=E(\Lambda_s)$ be the set of **spatial** edges in one time slice. Define the boundary configuration space
\[
\Sigma:=G^{E_s},
\]
thought of as spatial link variables on the time slice $t=0$.

Write $\sigma\in\Sigma$ for spatial links at time $0$ and $\sigma'\in\Sigma$ for spatial links at time $1$.
Let $U_0\in G^{\Lambda_s}$ denote the time-like links connecting $(0,x)$ to $(1,x)$.

### 2.1 Strip weight

Decompose plaquettes into negative-side, positive-side, and **straddling** plaquettes crossing the reflection plane.
Define a one-step strip weight of the schematic form
\[
W_a(\sigma,\sigma',U_0) := \Big(\prod_{p\in P_0} w(U_p(\sigma,\sigma',U_0))\Big)\,e^{-\frac12 S_{\mathrm{sp}}(\sigma)}e^{-\frac12 S_{\mathrm{sp}}(\sigma')},
\]
where $w(U_p)$ is a reflection-positive plaquette weight (e.g. Wilson) and $S_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is the purely spatial plaquette action on a slice. The half-weighting is the standard choice that makes the resulting kernel symmetric.

### 2.2 Strip kernel

Integrate out the time-like links to obtain a kernel on boundary slice variables:
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_a(\sigma,\sigma') := \int_{G^{\Lambda_s}} W_a(\sigma,\sigma',U_0)\,\prod_{x\in\Lambda_s} dH(U_0(x)).
}
\]
This kernel is nonnegative and gauge-invariant under time-slice gauge transformations.

### 2.3 Boundary measure and boundary operator

Define the boundary measure
\[
d\nu(\sigma):=Z_\nu^{-1}e^{-S_{\mathrm{sp}}(\sigma)}\,\prod_{\ell\in E_s} dH(\sigma_\ell).
\]
The half-weighting convention yields a symmetric integral operator on $L^2(\nu)$ whose kernel is obtained from $\mathcal K_a$ by the usual symmetrization.

## 3. OS compression to the boundary and the one-step dissipation

Let $\mathscr B$ be the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the boundary slice variables $\sigma$ at $t=0$.
For $F\in\mathcal A^+$ define the compression map (conditional expectation)
\[
(JF)(\sigma):=\mathbb E_\mu\big[F\mid\mathscr B\big](\sigma).
\]
A Markov property across the plane yields an isometric boundary realization:
\[
(F,G)_{\mathrm{OS}} = \int_{\Sigma} \overline{JF(\sigma)}\,JG(\sigma)\,d\nu(\sigma).
\]
Under this identification, one-step time translation induces a Markov operator $K_a$ on $L^2(\nu)$ (built from the strip kernel), and the one-step OS dissipation becomes the boundary Dirichlet form
\[
\boxed{
\langle F,(I-T_a)F\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{OS}}}
=\langle JF,(I-K_a)JF\rangle_{L^2(\nu)}
=:\mathcal E_{K_a}(JF,JF).
}
\]

## 4. The boundedness obstruction and the scale-$a$ replacement

For any Markov operator $K$ on $L^2(\nu)$,
\[
0\le \mathcal E_K(f,f)=\langle f,(I-K)f\rangle \le 2\|f\|_2^2,
\]
so $\mathcal E_K$ is a **bounded** quadratic form on $L^2$.

In contrast, the raw gradient form $\int |\nabla f|^2\,d\nu$ is unbounded on $L^2$. Hence a global inequality of the form
\(
\mathcal E_{K_a}(f,f)\gtrsim \int |\nabla f|^2
\)
cannot hold in general.

### 4.1 Configuration diffusion and the scale-$a$ form

Let $(P_t)_{t\ge0}$ be the configuration diffusion semigroup on $(\Sigma,\nu)$ generated by a reversible diffusion operator $L$ with carré du champ $\Gamma(f)=|\nabla f|^2$.
Define the **scale-$a$ Dirichlet form**
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E^{(a)}_{\mathrm{conf}}(f,f):=\langle f,(I-P_a)f\rangle_{L^2(\nu)}.
}
\]
This form is bounded: $\mathcal E^{(a)}_{\mathrm{conf}}(f,f)\le 2\|f\|_2^2$.

It also has an exact gradient representation (standard semigroup calculus):
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E^{(a)}_{\mathrm{conf}}(f,f)=\int_0^a \int_{\Sigma} |\nabla P_{t/2}f|^2\,d\nu\,dt.
}
\]
Thus $\mathcal E^{(a)}_{\mathrm{conf}}$ measures gradient energy after smoothing at time scale $\approx a$.

## 5. The viable one-step OS/Dirichlet comparison

### Theorem 5.1 (one-step OS vs scale-$a$ form)
There exists $c>0$ (independent of the lattice volume; depending only on the local plaquette weight class, $G$, $\beta$, and dimension) such that for all $F\in\mathcal A^+$,
\[
\boxed{
\langle F,(I-T_a)F\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{OS}}}
\ \ge\
 c\,\mathcal E^{(a)}_{\mathrm{conf}}(JF,JF).
}
\]

### Proof structure (locality + bounded overlap + finite-block inequalities)
The proof consists of three reductions.

**Step A (single straddling plaquette block).** Fix a temporal plaquette $p$ crossing the reflection plane. The strip kernel induces a local Markov operator $K_{a,p}$ on a finite block $\Sigma_p=G^{B_p}$ of boundary edges that influence $p$ after integrating out time-like links. Because the kernel density on this compact block is continuous and strictly positive, one obtains a uniform minorization (Doeblin) condition on $K_{a,p}$ and hence a local spectral gap:
\[
\mathrm{Var}_{\nu_p}(g)\ \le\ \frac{1}{\lambda_p}\,\langle g,(I-K_{a,p})g\rangle_{L^2(\nu_p)}.
\]
A group Poincaré inequality on the finite product $\Sigma_p$ gives
\(
\mathrm{Var}_{\nu_p}(g)\le C_p\int_{\Sigma_p}|\nabla g|^2\,d\nu_p
\).
Combining yields a local coercivity estimate
\[
\langle g,(I-K_{a,p})g\rangle\ \ge\ \frac{\lambda_p}{C_p}\int_{\Sigma_p}|\nabla g|^2\,d\nu_p.
\]

**Step B (scale-$a$ smoothing).** On a compact finite-dimensional block, semigroup smoothing can only reduce gradients. Hence
\[
\int_0^a\int |\nabla P_{t/2}g|^2\,d\nu\,dt\ \le\ a\int |\nabla g|^2\,d\nu.
\]
Thus the local coercivity controls the local contribution to $\mathcal E^{(a)}_{\mathrm{conf}}$ up to a factor absorbed into constants.

**Step C (bounded overlap).** Sum the block inequalities over all straddling plaquettes. Each boundary edge participates in at most $M(d)$ straddling plaquettes (a fixed dimension-dependent multiplicity). Therefore the sum of local gradient terms dominates the global scale-$a$ form with only an $O(1)$ loss, yielding the theorem.

---

## 6. Consequence: diffusion gap $\Rightarrow$ transfer gap (per-step)

If the diffusion generator $-L$ has spectral gap $\lambda_*>0$ on $L^2(\nu)$, then
\[
\mathcal E^{(a)}_{\mathrm{conf}}(f,f)\ \ge\ (1-e^{-a\lambda_*})\,\|f-\nu(f)\|_2^2.
\]
Feeding this into Theorem 5.1 yields a positive per-step gap for $K_a$ and hence a positive transfer gap for $T_a=e^{-aH}$, with the physical mass scale obtained by the standard relation
\(
 m(a)=\frac{\lambda_P(a)}{a}
\)
where $\lambda_P(a)=-\log\lambda_1(K_a)$ is the per-step contraction rate.
