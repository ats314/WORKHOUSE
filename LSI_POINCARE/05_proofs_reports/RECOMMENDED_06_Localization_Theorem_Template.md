# Localization to Global Spectral Gap Theorem Template

This note turns the “localization rescue” idea into something theorem-shaped.

The point: a **global** Bakry–Émery lower curvature bound on the full configuration space is often *impossible* (the infimum is destroyed by exponentially rare but catastrophically curved configurations).  
However, the dynamics and the measure typically live on a **core region** where curvature *is* good. If you can prove (i) a local Poincaré/LSI on that core and (ii) that excursions outside the core are **short** (or energetically expensive), you can still recover a **global spectral gap**.

---

## 0. Reversible diffusion / Dirichlet form setup

Let \((\Omega,\mu)\) be a probability space equipped with a reversible Markov generator \(L\) with invariant measure \(\mu\), and carré-du-champ \(\Gamma\).  
Assume the associated Dirichlet form is
\[
\mathcal E(f,f) := \int_\Omega \Gamma(f)\,d\mu,
\]
with domain \(\mathcal D(\mathcal E)\subset L^2(\mu)\).

Think: \(\Omega\) is a configuration manifold (e.g. \(\mathcal C_a\)), \(\mu\propto e^{-S}\,\mathrm{vol}\), and \(L=\Delta-\nabla S\cdot\nabla\) (Langevin).

---

## 1. Choose a “core” and a “bad complement”

Fix a measurable **core region** \(K\subset \Omega\) with \(\mu(K)>0\), and let
\[
A := K^{c}=\Omega\setminus K
\]
be the “bad” set.

We will assume two things:

1. **Inside \(K\)**: the measure is mixing well (local Poincaré / LSI).
2. **Outside \(K\)**: the process does not get “stuck” (quantitative return / exit-time control).

---

## 2. Hypothesis H1: local Poincaré or local log-Sobolev on the core

Let \(\mu_K\) be the normalized restriction:
\[
\mu_K(B) := \frac{\mu(B\cap K)}{\mu(K)}.
\]

### H1-P Local Poincaré on \(K\)
There exists \(\rho_K>0\) such that for all \(f\in \mathcal D(\mathcal E)\),
\[
\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_K}(f)\ \le\ \frac{1}{\rho_K}\int_K \Gamma(f)\,d\mu_K.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\mu(K)\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_K}(f)\ \le\ \frac{1}{\rho_K}\int_K \Gamma(f)\,d\mu.
\]

### H1-LSI Local log-Sobolev on \(K\)
There exists \(\rho^{\mathrm{LSI}}_K>0\) such that for all \(f\ge 0\),
\[
\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu_K}(f^2)\ \le\ \frac{2}{\rho^{\mathrm{LSI}}_K}\int_K \Gamma(f)\,d\mu_K.
\]

For a diffusion \(L=\Delta-\nabla S\cdot\nabla\), (H1-P) (and often (H1-LSI)) can be obtained from a **localized** Bakry–Émery bound on \(K\):
\[
\nabla^2 S \succeq \rho_K I \quad\text{on }K.
\]

---

## 3. Hypothesis H2: quantitative “escape” from the bad set \(A\)

Here is the cleanest functional-analytic form (it is exactly a **Dirichlet** spectral gap on \(A\)).

### H2-Dir Dirichlet Poincaré on \(A\)
Define the Dirichlet constant on \(A\) by
\[
\lambda_A\ :=\ \inf\left\{
\frac{\mathcal E(g,g)}{\int_\Omega g^2\,d\mu}\ :\ g\in \mathcal D(\mathcal E),\ g|_K=0,\ g\neq 0
\right\}.
\]
Assume \(\lambda_A>0\). Then for all \(g\in \mathcal D(\mathcal E)\) with \(g|_K=0\),
\[
\int_A g^2\,d\mu\ \le\ \frac{1}{\lambda_A}\,\mathcal E(g,g).
\]

Interpretation: \(\lambda_A\) is the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of \(-L\) on the “domain” \(A\) with killing upon hitting \(K\).  
Large \(\lambda_A\) means **fast exit / fast return to the core**.

### Exit-time version
Let \(\tau_K := \inf\{t\ge 0: X_t\in K\}\) be the hitting time of \(K\) for the stationary diffusion \((X_t)\) generated by \(L\).  
For many diffusions/domains one has the standard estimate
\[
\sup_{x\in A}\mathbb E_x[\tau_K]\ \le\ \frac{1}{\lambda_A}.
\]
So an **exit-time bound** \(\sup_{x\in A}\mathbb E_x[\tau_K]\le T\) implies \(\lambda_A\ge 1/T\).

### Capacity version
A convenient “\(1\)-capacity” definition on probability spaces is
\[
\mathrm{Cap}_1(A)
:=\inf\left\{\mathcal E(u,u)+\int u^2\,d\mu:\ u\in\mathcal D(\mathcal E),\ u\ge 1 \ \mu\text{-a.e. on }A\right\}.
\]
In many settings, \(\mathrm{Cap}_1(A)\) controls (or is controlled by) \(\lambda_A\) and \(\mu(A)\), so estimates of \(\mathrm{Cap}_1(A)\) can be translated into a usable lower bound on \(\lambda_A\).

---

## 4. The theorem: local Poincaré on \(K\) + Dirichlet exit from \(A\) ⇒ global spectral gap

### Theorem local to global Poincaré patching
Assume (H1-P) with constant \(\rho_K>0\) and (H2-Dir) with constant \(\lambda_A>0\).  
Then \(\mu\) satisfies a global Poincaré inequality:
\[
\mathrm{Var}_\mu(f)\ \le\ C_P\,\mathcal E(f,f)
\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal D(\mathcal E),
\]
with
\[
C_P\ \le\ \max\!\left\{\frac{1}{\rho_K},\ \frac{1+1/\mu(K)}{\lambda_A}\right\}.
\]
Equivalently, the global spectral gap \(\lambda_1\) of \(-L\) satisfies
\[
\lambda_1\ \ge\ \min\!\left\{\rho_K,\ \frac{\mu(K)}{1+\mu(K)}\,\lambda_A\right\}.
\]

In particular, if \(\mu(K)\ge 1/2\) then \(\mu(K)/(1+\mu(K))\ge 1/3\), so
\[
\lambda_1\ \ge\ \min\!\left\{\rho_K,\ \frac{1}{3}\lambda_A\right\}.
\]

---

## 5. Proof elementary variance decomposition

Let \(f\in L^2(\mu)\) with \(\int f\,d\mu=0\). Write
\[
m_K := \int f\,d\mu_K,\qquad m_A := \int f\,d\mu_A,
\]
where \(\mu_A\) is the normalized restriction to \(A\) (assume \(\mu(A)>0\); otherwise \(A\) is null and we are done).

A standard decomposition gives
\[
\mathrm{Var}_\mu(f)
=
\mu(K)\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_K}(f)\;+\;\mu(A)\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_A}(f)
\;+\;\mu(K)\mu(A)\,(m_K-m_A)^2.
\]

### Step 1: the core term
By (H1-P) and the normalized-to-unnormalized conversion,
\[
\mu(K)\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_K}(f)
\le
\frac{1}{\rho_K}\int_K \Gamma(f)\,d\mu.
\]

### Step 2: control of the bad-set variance term
Trivially,
\[
\mu(A)\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_A}(f)\le \int_A f^2\,d\mu.
\]
Define \(g := f\,\mathbf 1_A\). Then \(g|_K=0\), and (H2-Dir) yields
\[
\int_A f^2\,d\mu = \int g^2\,d\mu \le \frac{1}{\lambda_A}\mathcal E(g,g)\le \frac{1}{\lambda_A}\int_A\Gamma(f)\,d\mu,
\]
(using locality of \(\Gamma\) and \(\mathbf 1_A^2=\mathbf 1_A\) at the formal level; in a fully rigorous diffusion setting one uses smooth cutoffs approximating \(\mathbf 1_A\)).

### Step 3: control of the between-means term
Since \(\int f\,d\mu=0\), we have \(\mu(K)m_K+\mu(A)m_A=0\), hence
\[
m_A = -\frac{\mu(K)}{\mu(A)}\,m_K.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mu(K)\mu(A)(m_K-m_A)^2
=
\mu(K)\mu(A)\left(m_K+\frac{\mu(K)}{\mu(A)}m_K\right)^2
=
\frac{\mu(K)}{\mu(A)}\,m_K^2.
\]
But on \(A\), the \(L^2\) mass already contains the mean contribution:
\[
\int_A f^2\,d\mu \ \ge\ \mu(A)m_A^2
=\mu(A)\left(\frac{\mu(K)}{\mu(A)}m_K\right)^2
=
\frac{\mu(K)^2}{\mu(A)}\,m_K^2.
\]
So
\[
\frac{\mu(K)}{\mu(A)}\,m_K^2\ \le\ \frac{1}{\mu(K)}\int_A f^2\,d\mu
\ \le\ \frac{1}{\mu(K)\lambda_A}\int_A \Gamma(f)\,d\mu.
\]

### Step 4: collect bounds
Combine the three bounds:
\[
\mathrm{Var}_\mu(f)
\le
\frac{1}{\rho_K}\int_K \Gamma(f)\,d\mu
+
\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_A}+\frac{1}{\mu(K)\lambda_A}\right)\int_A\Gamma(f)\,d\mu.
\]
Thus,
\[
\mathrm{Var}_\mu(f)
\le
\max\!\left\{\frac{1}{\rho_K},\ \frac{1+1/\mu(K)}{\lambda_A}\right\}
\int_\Omega \Gamma(f)\,d\mu
=
C_P\,\mathcal E(f,f),
\]
which is the global Poincaré inequality. The spectral gap bound \(\lambda_1\ge 1/C_P\) follows by the standard variational characterization of \(\lambda_1\). \(\square\)

## 6. How this plugs into the Yang–Mills “obstruction → localization” narrative

In the SU\((N)\) lattice gauge setting:

- \(\Omega=\mathcal C_a=G^{|B_a|}\) (or a gauge-fixed quotient / slice),
- \(L\) is the Langevin generator with invariant \(\mu_a\propto e^{-S_{\mathrm{eff}}}\),
- the **global** Bakry–Émery curvature infimum is ruined by rare “bad” configurations (global obstruction),
- but on a **typical set** \(K_a\) (e.g. plaquettes bounded away from the Gribov horizon / large-action spikes), one may hope to prove a **localized** curvature bound:
  \[
  \nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{eff}} \succeq \rho_K(a)\,I \quad\text{on }K_a.
  \]

Then:

1. Use localized Bakry–Émery on \(K_a\) to get (H1-P) with \(\rho_K(a)\).
2. Prove (H2-Dir) for \(A_a=\mathcal C_a\setminus K_a\) by either:
   - an explicit **exit-time bound** for the diffusion from \(A_a\) to \(K_a\), or
   - a Lyapunov/drift condition outside \(K_a\) that implies return, or
   - a capacity estimate for \(A_a\).

If both succeed with constants uniform in volume, the theorem above converts them into a **volume-uniform global spectral gap**, without ever needing a global curvature lower bound.

---

## 7. What would upgrade this to a log-Sobolev localization theorem

Patching **LSI** (rather than Poincaré) typically needs stronger control of the complement than just \(\lambda_A>0\) — e.g. a Lyapunov condition giving exponential moments of \(\tau_K\), or a “defective LSI” plus tightening.

A realistic theorem template for LSI would look like:

- local LSI on \(K\) with constant \(\rho^{\mathrm{LSI}}_K\),
- drift/exit control outside \(K\) strong enough to prevent entropy from leaking into \(A\),
- conclude a global LSI with an explicit constant depending on the two ingredients.

But: for the mass-gap route, a **spectral gap** (Poincaré) is already the key “Gap” output, and is technically the easier localization target.
