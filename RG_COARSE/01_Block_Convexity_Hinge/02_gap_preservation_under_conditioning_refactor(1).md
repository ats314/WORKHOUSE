# Gap Preservation Under Conditioning
*(a referee-safe “structural refactor” template for multiscale proofs)*

## 1. Standing hypotheses for Sections 7–12

The goal is to formalize each “localize / average / separate / reconstruct / take limits” step as a **conditional expectation** of a self-adjoint operator **in quadratic-form sense**, so that spectral-floor monotonicity becomes a one-line invariant.

### (H1) Common quadratic-form domain
There exists a dense domain \(\mathcal D\subset\mathcal H\) such that every operator \(H\) appearing in the argument (before and after any conditioning step) is represented by a **closed, lower-bounded quadratic form** on \(\mathcal D\).

### (H2) Conditioning is quadratic-form conditioning
Whenever
\[
\overline H := \mathbb E[H\mid\mathcal G]
\]
appears, it means: for all \(v\in\mathcal D\),
\[
\overline q(v):=\langle v,\overline H v\rangle
=
\mathbb E\!\left[\langle v,H v\rangle\mid\mathcal G\right],
\]
and \(\overline H\) is the self-adjoint operator associated to the closed form \(\overline q\).

### (H3) Uniform lower boundedness
There exists \(C<\infty\) such that for every operator \(H\) appearing,
\[
H\ge -C\,I,
\]
so conditional expectations remain lower bounded and generate self-adjoint operators on \(\mathcal D\).

---

## 2. One-line invariant: conditional spectral floor monotonicity

For any self-adjoint, lower-bounded \(H\) on a common form domain and any \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\mathcal G\),
\[
\lambda_{\inf}\!\left(\mathbb E[H\mid\mathcal G]\right)
\ \ge\
\mathbb E\!\left[\lambda_{\inf}(H)\mid\mathcal G\right]
\qquad\text{a.s.}
\]
(see `01_conditional_spectral_floor_monotonicity.md` for the proof).

This is the only nontrivial inequality needed for the refactor.

## 3. Universal lemma to paste into the manuscript

### Lemma 3.1 (Universal gap preservation under conditioning)
Let \(\{H_\alpha\}\) be a family of self-adjoint, lower-bounded operators acting on a common quadratic-form domain \(\mathcal D\). Suppose that for each transition \(\alpha\to\beta\) in the argument,
\[
H_\beta=\mathbb E[H_\alpha\mid\mathcal G_\beta]
\]
in the quadratic-form sense, for some sub-\(\sigma\)-algebra \(\mathcal G_\beta\).

Then, for almost every configuration,
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\inf}(H_\beta)
\ \ge\
\mathbb E[\lambda_{\inf}(H_\alpha)\mid\mathcal G_\beta].
}
\]
In particular, if \(\lambda_{\inf}(H_\alpha)\ge m_0>0\) almost surely (or on a conditioned set), then \(\lambda_{\inf}(H_\beta)\ge m_0\) almost surely (respectively, on the corresponding conditioned set).

**Proof.** Immediate from conditional spectral floor monotonicity. \(\square\)

## 4. Quotients (OS reconstruction seam)

Conditioning is not the only structural step: OS reconstruction also involves **quotienting by null vectors** in the OS semi-norm.

### Lemma 4.1 (Spectral floor under quotient)
Let \(H\) be self-adjoint and lower-bounded on \(\mathcal H\). Let \(\mathcal N\subset\mathcal H\) be closed and satisfy \(\mathcal N\subset\ker H\). Let \(\widetilde H\) be the induced operator on \(\mathcal H/\mathcal N\). Then
\[
\lambda_{\inf}(\widetilde H)=\lambda_{\inf}(H).
\]

**Proof.** Rayleigh quotients are unchanged because all vectors in \(\mathcal N\) have \(H\)-energy \(0\), and all cosets can be represented by vectors orthogonal to \(\mathcal N\). \(\square\)

This seals the “quotient seam”: quotienting cannot lower the spectral floor.

## 5. How to rewrite Sections 7–12 as conditioning

The discipline rule is simple:

> Never say “restrict to a good set.”  
> Always say “condition on the event \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by the indicator of that good set.”

Below is a template mapping that makes each step an explicit conditional expectation.

### §7 — drift / Lyapunov decomposition as conditioning on disorder level
Define a “badness” functional \(\mathcal B_\Lambda\) (e.g. averaged plaquette defect). Let
\[
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{dis}}:=\sigma(\mathcal B_\Lambda).
\]
Define the conditioned operator
\[
H^{(7)}:=\mathbb E[H^{(6)}\mid \mathcal G_{\mathrm{dis}}].
\]
Then Lemma 3.1 guarantees gap preservation across this “averaging along badness fibers.”

### §8 — localization as conditioning on an event
Given a good set \(K_\Lambda\), define
\[
\mathcal G_K:=\sigma(\mathbf 1_{K_\Lambda}).
\]
Define
\[
H^{(8)}:=\mathbb E[H^{(7)}\mid \mathcal G_K].
\]
This implements localization as a **two-phase conditioning** rather than a hard restriction.

### §9 — Green’s function decay
If the section already treats the inverse of a positive operator \(M\), insert once:
all subsequent local averages of \(M\) are implemented as conditional expectations and hence do not decrease the spectral floor of \(M\) (Lemma 3.1).

### §10 — clustering via “outside” conditioning
For separated regions \(A,B\), let
\[
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{out}}:=\sigma(U_{\mathrm{outside}\ (A\cup B)}).
\]
Define
\[
H^{(10)}:=\mathbb E[H^{(9)}\mid\mathcal G_{\mathrm{out}}].
\]
Lemma 3.1 asserts that conditioning on outside degrees of freedom cannot reduce the mass controlling decay.

### §11 — thermodynamic limit as an order argument
Let \(\mathcal G_\Lambda:=\sigma(U|_\Lambda)\). For \(\Lambda\subset\Lambda'\),
\[
H_{\Lambda'\to\Lambda}:=\mathbb E[H_{\Lambda'}\mid\mathcal G_\Lambda].
\]
One takes limits **after** establishing the spectral-floor order along this net. This is not “expectation in the limit,” it is a monotone bookkeeping step.

### §12 — OS reconstruction: conditioning then quotient
First conditioning to the positive-time algebra:
\[
H^{(12,\mathrm{cond})}:=\mathbb E[H^{(11)}\mid\mathcal G_{t\ge0}].
\]
Then quotient by the OS null space \(\mathcal N\subset\ker H^{(12,\mathrm{cond})}\):
\[
H^{(12)} := \text{operator induced on }\mathcal H/\mathcal N.
\]
Use Lemma 3.1 for the conditioning step and Lemma 4.1 for the quotient step.

## 6. Micro-checklist (editing discipline)

For each of §§7–12, ensure there exists **exactly one sentence** that answers:

1. What \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\mathcal G\) are you conditioning on?
2. Which operator \(H\) is being conditionally averaged?
3. Is the step conditioning or quotienting? (If quotient, cite Lemma 4.1.)

If those three are present, “gap leakage” cannot be re-litigated in later sections.
